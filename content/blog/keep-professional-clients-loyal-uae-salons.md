You realise it halfway through the appointment. She watches your hands in the mirror, not her own reflection. She asks which developer volume you are using, and she already knows the answer. When she pays, she mentions she runs a chair two communities over — or works the evening shift at a salon in the Marina. Your client is a professional. She does hair, or nails, or skin, for a living. And she chose your salon for her own.

Most salon owners feel a jolt of nerves at that moment. Do not: she is the hardest regular you will ever win — and the most valuable one you will ever keep.

## Why she is your hardest regular

A normal client judges the result. A professional client judges everything that led to it. She notices whether the foils are folded neatly or sloppily. She sees the sectioning. She clocks whether the brushes were washed between clients, whether the colour was mixed fresh, whether the stylist rushed the last ten minutes because the evening diary was running late. She can tell the difference between a stylist who knows the product and one who is guessing. She has spent years standing where your stylist stands, and she can feel the difference between confident hands and nervous ones. She books for skill she respects, not for the coffee or the playlist. If she comes back, it is because she trusts the work — and that makes her the most honest measure of your salon.

## Never perform for her — be precise instead

The instinct when you discover a client is a professional is to overdo it. The senior stylist is suddenly assigned, everyone is extra careful, and the consultation becomes a performance. She notices that too, and it puts her off. She did not come to be impressed by a show. She came for a good service, done properly, at a fair price.

Treat her like any other regular, but with the precision she expects. Be on time, do the consultation properly — she will respect a stylist who asks good questions rather than one who assumes — and use the products you say you use. If something will take longer than expected, say so before you start. Professionals forgive a busy day. They do not forgive being misled about time.

Above all, do not talk down to her. Never explain the basics as if she has never heard of them. A short, honest exchange — "I am using a 20 volume here because your ends are already quite light" — tells her you know what you are doing and you know she knows too. That mutual respect is the foundation of the whole relationship.

## Let her watch, let her ask

Some stylists get nervous when a client watches their work closely. With a professional client, do the opposite. Let her watch and answer her questions plainly. If she suggests something different, listen before deciding — she may be right, and even when she is not, the way you handle the suggestion tells her about your confidence.

There is a line, of course. She is the client in this chair, not the stylist. If she starts directing the appointment, a calm boundary works: "Let me try it this way and we will check it together at the end." Professionals understand boundaries. They set them with their own clients every day.

## She knows what things cost — so be honest about price

A professional client knows the price of the colour tube, the treatment, the product on your shelf. She knows your margins better than most of your own team does. That is not a problem unless your pricing is dishonest.

Never inflate a price for her, and never discount it either. A professional who is given a secret discount will wonder what everyone else is paying. A professional who is charged a fair, consistent price — the same price as any other client — will respect it.

The same honesty applies to results. If her hair cannot take the colour she wants today, say so. A professional knows when a stylist is taking a risk with her hair, and she will never forgive one who gambled with it to please her. The stylist who says "not today, let us do it in two visits" earns a loyalty that no discount can buy.

## The referral you never see

Here is the part most salon owners miss. A professional client who trusts you does not just come back. She sends people. Her colleagues, her own clients who ask where she gets her hair done, the bride she is styling who needs a salon for the morning of the wedding. She will not announce this. There is no referral code, no thank-you message. One Tuesday, a new client books and mentions a name you recognise, and that is how you find out.

She also warns people — quietly, to other professionals. In Dubai's beauty community, word travels fast, and a professional's opinion carries weight. One bad visit, and she simply never returns, and she never tells you why. That silence is the only feedback you will get, so the work has to speak before she leaves the chair.

## A simple plan for the professional client

Start this week. Look at your diary and count how many of your regulars work in beauty themselves — stylists, therapists, nail technicians, salon owners, freelancers. There are probably more than you think.

Then do three things. First, make sure the stylist who serves her is the one she asks for, every time, and protect that booking like any other standing appointment. Second, brief your team: no performing, no discounting, no cutting corners on the client who knows exactly what good work looks like. Third, keep a small note on her file — what she likes, what she does not, what you discussed last time. She will notice that you remembered.

The professional client will never be your loudest fan. She will not leave a long review or post about you. She will simply keep coming back, and send the people she respects. That is the quietest, strongest retention a salon can have: a client who could judge you harder than anyone, and chose to stay.
