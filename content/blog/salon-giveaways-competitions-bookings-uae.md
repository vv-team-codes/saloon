A salon posts a giveaway, the comments fill up with tags, the follower count jumps — and then nothing. No new bookings, no new regulars, and within a week half the new followers have disappeared.

The design is the problem. A good giveaway puts your work in front of people who live near you and turns the excitement of winning into a booked chair.

## Why most salon giveaways fail

The usual failure starts with the prize. A generic prize — a gift card, a hamper, a gadget — attracts everyone and no one in particular. People who will never visit your salon enter, then leave the moment the winner is announced. Your engagement rate drops, and the algorithm stops showing your posts.

The second failure is the entry mechanic. When entry means following twelve accounts and tagging five friends, you filter for people who love free things, not people who want a haircut. Every extra step loses genuine clients and keeps only the professional competition-enterers.

The third failure comes after. The winner is announced, the salon moves on, and the hundreds who entered but did not win are never spoken to again. Ignoring them is the real waste.

## Pick a prize people actually want

A good prize is your best work, not a random object. Offer the service you are proudest of — a signature balayage, a bridal trial, a head spa ritual — and make the winner look and feel noticeably different, because that transformation is your advertisement.

Keep it specific and honest. Instead of "a free makeover worth AED 1,000", say exactly what is included: a consultation, a full-head balayage, a toner, a cut and a blow-dry, for one person, on a weekday. Specific prizes attract people who genuinely want that service.

One generous prize beats five small ones, and a quiet mid-morning slot gives the visit your team's full attention.

## Make entering easy — and useful

The simplest entry that works: follow your account, like the post, and tag one friend who would love the service. Anyone can enter in ten seconds, and the tag brings you someone like your existing clients — usually a real friend who lives nearby.

Then add one step that helps your business: ask entrants to message you on WhatsApp with the name of the prize, or to answer one question in the comments, such as which service they would choose. A WhatsApp message gives you a direct line to every entrant, and a comment answer tells you what your audience actually wants.

Do not make people follow partner accounts or share to stories as a condition of entry. Forced sharing annoys genuine clients and fills your list with people who will never book.

## Run it where your clients already are

In the UAE, that usually means Instagram first and WhatsApp second. Announce the giveaway with a short Reel showing the actual service — the colour going on, the head spa ritual, the finished result — rather than a static graphic. People enter for things they can picture themselves receiving, and video does that far better than a poster.

Put the full terms in the caption and a pinned comment: the prize, who can enter, the closing date, and how the winner will be announced. Keep entries open five to seven days — a month-long giveaway loses momentum; a one-week one creates urgency.

Launch on a Thursday or Friday evening, when people are scrolling and planning their weekend, and close it the following Thursday. Avoid your busiest diary days, so you have time to reply to entrants.

## Pick a winner people trust

Nothing kills a giveaway faster than a winner announcement that looks rigged. Decide the method before you launch — a random draw, a live draw on stories, or a judged winner — say it in the caption, and do exactly what you said, in public.

A live draw on Instagram stories, using a simple random picker on screen, takes two minutes and builds more trust than any caption. Announce the winner by name, tag them, and message them privately on WhatsApp within the hour to arrange their appointment.

If the winner does not respond within 48 hours, draw again — and put that rule in the terms from the start.

## Turn entrants into bookings

This is where a giveaway earns its keep. Every entrant who did not win is a warm lead: someone who wanted your service enough to enter. Within a day or two of the draw, send every entrant a short personal WhatsApp message: thank them for entering, and offer something real but modest, such as 20 percent off the same service for bookings within two weeks.

Send one message, not a drip campaign. One warm, personal message feels like a thank-you; three feels like a sales funnel. Make the offer easy to accept: name two or three real slots they can book, and let them reply to confirm.

Track it simply. Count how many entrants you had, how many messaged back, and how many booked. Ten bookings from two hundred entrants is a success. A thousand followers and zero bookings is not.

## Check the rules before you launch

Prize draws in the UAE are regulated. Before you promise a prize, check whether your giveaway needs a permit from the relevant authority in your emirate, and what conditions apply to advertising it and picking a winner. Confirm the current position rather than copying what another salon did — and get the winner's written permission before sharing photos of their treatment.

## A simple first giveaway plan

Pick one signature service and one clear prize, and write the terms in three short lines. Post a Reel on a Thursday evening, run entries for seven days, and reply to every entrant on WhatsApp as they come in. Do a live draw on stories, message the winner within the hour, and send every other entrant one personal message the next day with a modest, time-limited offer and two real slots. Then count the bookings.

That is the whole system: a prize worth wanting, an entry worth making, and a follow-up worth answering. Done this way, a giveaway becomes a week of genuine attention that ends with new clients in your chairs.
