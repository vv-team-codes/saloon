It is 9:40 on a Friday evening. A client messages your salon on WhatsApp: "Are you open now?" She is in a mall, her friends are waiting, and she wants a blow-dry before dinner. A clear answer in the next two minutes brings her through your door. Silence, a vague reply, or an answer that arrives tomorrow morning sends her to the salon next door.

Opening-hours enquiries look trivial. They are not. They are some of the warmest enquiries a salon ever receives, because the person asking has already decided to come. She wants a chair now, and how you answer decides whether that intent turns into revenue or evaporates.

## Why this one question matters so much

Most salon enquiries are about price, services, or availability next week. An opening-hours enquiry is different: it carries urgency. The client is already out, already dressed, already in your neighbourhood. In Dubai and across the UAE, clients message while they are in a mall, after work, or on their way home, and they expect the same instant answer they get from a food delivery app.

These messages also cluster exactly when your team is busiest: the evening rush between 6 and 9, Friday afternoons, and the hour before closing. That is when a slow or missing reply costs the most, because the client is deciding in real time. And there is a trust effect: a salon that answers "Yes, we're open until 10, and we have a chair at 9:45 — shall I hold it for you?" sounds organised and welcoming. One that replies the next morning with "Yes we are open" sounds like it was not really there.

## Answer the question, then add the booking

The most common mistake is answering only the question that was asked. "Yes, we're open till 10" is accurate and useless. It leaves the client to ask whether there is a free chair, for which service, at what time, and at what price — and every extra message she has to send is a chance for her to change her mind.

A good reply answers the question and moves the conversation forward in the same breath: confirmation, the closing time, a concrete slot, and a simple yes-or-no question.

"Yes, we're open until 10 tonight. We have a chair free at 9:45 for a blow-dry — shall I book it for you?"

One message, one decision. If she says yes, hold the chair and ask for her name. If the time does not work, offer the next one. The enquiry becomes a booking in two messages instead of eight. The same structure works when the answer is no: "We're closed now, but we open at 10 tomorrow and I can hold the 10:30 chair for you — shall I?" A closed sign is not the end of the conversation. It is the start of tomorrow's first booking.

## Know your real hours, not your posted hours

Many salons lose these enquiries because the hours on Google, Instagram, and WhatsApp do not match each other, or do not match reality. A client sees "open until 9" on Google, messages at 8:45, and is told you close at 8. She feels misled, even if nobody meant to mislead her.

Sit down once and make every listing agree, including the exceptions that catch UAE salons out: Friday hours, which often start later; Ramadan hours, which shift dramatically; public holidays; and mall hours, which can differ from high-street hours during festive seasons.

Write the exceptions somewhere your whole team can see them — a one-page sheet at the front desk and a pinned note in the team WhatsApp group. When a client asks "are you open on Friday afternoon?" the person replying should not have to guess. They should know.

## The after-hours messages are bookings too

A large share of opening-hours enquiries arrive when nobody is at the desk: late at night, early in the morning, and during lunch. A client who messages at 11 at night asking whether you open at 9 tomorrow is planning her morning around you.

Set up a WhatsApp greeting message that answers the question before a human ever sees it. It should state today's hours, tomorrow's opening time, and invite the client to leave her name and the service she wants. "Hi! We're open today until 10 pm, and tomorrow from 10 am. Leave your name and what you'd like booked, and we'll confirm first thing in the morning."

That message answers the client immediately, so she does not message three other salons while she waits, and it turns a vague enquiry into a concrete request your team can act on the moment they arrive.

## When the answer is "we're full tonight"

Sometimes you are open, but you cannot take her. The chair is full, the stylist she wants has left, or the service she needs takes longer than the time left before closing. This is where most salons lose the client entirely, with a flat "sorry, we're fully booked." Never end on a no — a client told "we're full tonight, but I can book you tomorrow at 11, or Thursday evening at 7 — which works better?" is still a client.

Keep two or three genuinely good alternatives ready. Tomorrow morning is often the best one, because the client who wanted tonight is clearly free and motivated. A quiet weekday slot is the second best, because it fills the hours you struggle to fill anyway. And if she really cannot come back, offer the waitlist: "We're full tonight, but if anyone cancels I'll message you straight away. Shall I put you first on the list?"

## A small question with a big return

"Are you open now?" is the shortest enquiry your salon will ever receive, and one of the most valuable. It comes from someone who is ready, nearby, and deciding right now. Answer it fast, answer it completely, and always end with a booking question. Keep your hours accurate everywhere they appear, let after-hours messages turn into tomorrow's bookings, and never let a full diary be the last word.

Do that consistently, and the messages that used to slip through the evening rush become one of the steadiest sources of new bookings your salon has — no advertising spend, no discount, just a good answer at the right moment.
