Every salon owner in the UAE knows the feeling. A regular you have seen for months, sometimes years, quietly disappears. No complaint, no goodbye. Then, a few weeks later, her name appears in the diary again. She tried another salon — and she came back.

That return visit is one of the most valuable moments in your business.

## Do not ask why she left — at least not yet

The instinct is to ask. "Where have you been?" "Did you try somewhere else?" Resist it. The first thing a returning client needs is a warm, normal welcome, not an interrogation. Greet her as if she had never left — her usual drink, her usual chair, her usual stylist if that stylist is free. The message you want to send is simple: you were missed, and nothing has changed.

There is a time to learn what happened, but it is not in the first five minutes. Many clients tell you themselves once they feel comfortable. Your job is to listen without defending or criticising the other salon. A client who hears you badmouth a competitor wonders what you say about her when she is not there.

## Look at what changed in her absence

Before she arrives, pull up her record. When was her last visit? What did she usually book? Which stylist did she see, and what products were used? A client who returns after a gap should never have to explain her whole history from scratch. Remembering her shade, her cut and her favourite chair makes the return feel like coming home.

If she went elsewhere because of something specific — a stylist who left, a price change, a long wait on a Saturday evening — check whether that thing has been fixed. There is no point welcoming her back into the same problem that pushed her away. If your Saturday diary is still a pile-up, say so honestly and offer a weekday slot with the same stylist, or a quieter evening time. Clients forgive a lot. They rarely forgive being promised a change that never happens.

## The service matters more than the speech

A returning client is judging you against fresh competition. She will notice the details: whether the consultation happens before the scissors come out, whether the colour is checked under proper light, whether anyone asks how the last cut grew out. This is not the visit to rush. Leave a little extra time in the diary for a returning regular, especially in the busy evening hours when UAE salons are at their fullest. A ten-minute buffer buys you the unhurried finish that makes a client decide she is home.

If her hair or skin shows it — a colour that needs correcting, a treatment that was overdone elsewhere — explain what you can do, what it will take and what it will cost before you start. Never promise to fix everything in one visit if it will take two. A client who has already been disappointed once is listening very carefully to promises.

## Offer something, but not a discount

It is tempting to mark the return with a discount, and it is usually a mistake. A discount tells the client her loyalty has a price, and it trains her to expect one every time she drifts away. What works better is a small gesture that costs little and means a lot: a complimentary deep-conditioning treatment with her colour, a free brow tidy with her facial, or first choice of the new season's appointment times before they open to everyone else.

The gesture should feel personal, not promotional. If she loves a particular mask or tea, have it ready. The point is not the value of the freebie. It is the proof that you noticed she was gone and you are glad she is back.

## Learn the lesson, then fix the leak

After the visit, take ten minutes with your team and ask one question: what did we learn? A returning client is free market research. She sampled the competition and came back, which means you are doing something right — but something pushed her to try elsewhere in the first place. Was it price, a stylist's schedule, a Saturday she could not get booked, or simple curiosity?

Log the reason if she shared it, in her own words, on her record. If three returning clients mention the same Saturday wait, that is a diary problem, not a coincidence. If two mention a price that surprised them, that is a communication problem.

When a regular goes quiet for longer than her usual gap, a short personal message — not a broadcast, not a voucher — can bring her back before she tries somewhere else. ## Make the next booking before she leaves

The single most important moment of a return visit is the last five minutes. Do not let her leave without a next appointment. Offer two specific dates that match her usual rhythm — "same time next Thursday, or the Saturday after?" — and book it while she is still in the chair. A client who walks out with a date in the diary is a regular again. A client who walks out with a vague "see you soon" is one busy week away from drifting off again.

If she hesitates, do not push. Ask what would make the next visit easier: a different stylist, a morning slot, a reminder the day before.

## A client who chose you twice is your best client

A regular who has never left is loyal by habit. A regular who left, compared and came back is loyal by choice — and that is stronger. She knows what the salon down the road charges and how long they keep her waiting. She chose you anyway.

Welcome her back warmly, deliver the best version of the service she remembers, learn whatever her absence can teach you, and book her next visit before she leaves. Do that consistently, and the clients who wander will keep finding their way home.
