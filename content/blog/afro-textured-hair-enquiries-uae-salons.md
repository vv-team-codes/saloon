It usually arrives on WhatsApp late in the evening, six words long: "Do you do Afro hair?" No photo, no service named, no appointment requested — just a question, and behind it a client deciding whether your salon is safe for her hair.

In the UAE, that message is more common than many owners realise. Dubai and Abu Dhabi are home to large African, Caribbean and mixed-heritage communities, and many clients with tightly curled hair have spent years being turned away or overcharged. When one messages you, she is asking whether you will treat her hair with knowledge and respect — and she will judge your answer in seconds.

## The question behind the question

Most salon enquiries are about price or availability. This one is about trust. A client with textured hair has often heard "yes, of course" from a salon that then struggled with her hair, rushed the appointment, or charged extra halfway through — so she tests the answer before she books.

The worst reply is a breezy "yes" with nothing behind it; the second worst is silence, or a reply two days later. The best reply shows you understand what she is asking: do you have someone who knows this hair, will you be honest about what you can and cannot do, and will the quoted price be the price she pays?

## The four-part reply that books

A strong first reply has four parts.

First, answer directly. If you have a stylist who works with Afro and tightly curled hair, say so and name them: "Yes — Amara specialises in Afro and coily hair." A named stylist beats any reassurance. If you do not, say so plainly.

Second, ask one smart question instead of twenty: "Is your hair natural, relaxed or in braids right now — and short, shoulder-length or longer?" That tells your stylist more than a long form.

Third, give a realistic price range and time. Textured-hair services often take longer than the same service on straight hair. "A cut and style with Amara is usually AED 180–260 and takes about 90 minutes" is honest and bookable; a vague "prices start from" is not.

Fourth, close with two specific slots and a photo request. "We have Thursday at 6pm or Saturday at 11am — a photo of your hair as it is now would help Amara plan." A photo is not a hurdle; it is how a good stylist prepares.

## Five questions before you promise anything

Before your team promises a result, they need five facts, collected in the first exchange: curl pattern and density; current state — natural, relaxed, transitioning, braids or weave; length and fullness; what she wants; and recent history, such as colour, heat damage or a bad cut. That stops the two most expensive mistakes: quoting 45 minutes for a two-hour service, and promising a result your stylist cannot deliver.

## Make the consultation the booking, not a barrier

For textured hair, a short consultation is not an upsell — it is part of the service. Fifteen minutes, in person or on video, lets your stylist see the hair dry and natural, feel its density and agree the result honestly. Many of the best textured-hair specialists in the UAE book it as a real diary slot, with a small fee that comes off the service.

Frame it that way and clients rarely object: "Amara likes a quick 15-minute consultation first — it's AED 50, and it comes off your appointment." That is professionalism, not hesitation. What clients object to is a salon that books a full colour and cut without ever seeing their hair.

## Price it honestly, by time and density

Nothing damages trust faster than a price that doubles at the till. Textured hair often needs more product, more time and more skill, and clients who wear it know that. What they will not accept is discovering it after the service starts.

So price by time and density, and say the range before she books. A cut for short, fine coils and a cut for long, dense coils are not the same job. If your booking system only allows one flat price, quote in the enquiry itself: "For your length and density, Amara would quote AED 220–280, confirmed at your consultation." That protects your margin — the stylist is paid for the real time the work takes — and it wins the client, because she has finally found a salon that prices her hair fairly.

## When the honest answer is no

Not every salon can do this work, and that is fine. What is not fine is saying yes anyway. A bad cut on textured hair is not a small mistake — it can take a year to grow out, and the client will tell everyone she knows.

If nobody on your team is trained for Afro or tightly curled hair, say so warmly and help her anyway: "We don't have a textured-hair specialist right now, so I don't want to book you in and get it wrong. House of Curls in our building does beautiful work with Afro hair — shall I send you their number?" A referral costs one appointment; a botched cut costs the client and everyone she warns.

## Train the team on one answer

The reply that wins this client cannot depend on who is holding the phone. Write one agreed reply, save it where the whole team can find it, and make sure everyone knows who your specialist is and when they work.

After the first visit, note curl pattern, density, products used and what worked. Next time she messages, your team can greet her by name and pick up where the last appointment ended. That is how a first enquiry becomes a regular — and where a good textured-hair stylist is hard to find, a regular is your best marketing.
