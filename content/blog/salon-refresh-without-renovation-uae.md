Your salon does not need to be falling apart for clients to notice it looks tired. The chairs are fine. The mirrors are clean. But the paint has dulled a little, the waiting chairs have seen better days, and the decor has not changed since the day you opened. Clients may not say anything about it. They simply feel it — and that feeling quietly shapes whether they book again, whether they recommend you, and whether they believe your prices are worth it.

In the UAE, that feeling matters more than most owners realise. A client in Dubai or Abu Dhabi has walked past dozens of salons in malls and on high streets. She has seen polished, light-filled spaces with plants, good lighting and a reception desk that looks like it belongs in a hotel. When she walks into yours, she is comparing — even if she does not mean to. A refresh is not vanity. It is part of the service.

## Start by seeing your salon through a client's eyes

The best place to begin costs nothing. Arrive one morning before opening, walk in through the front door, and sit where your clients sit. Look at what they look at while they wait. The scuffed skirting board by the entrance. The faded poster that has been on the wall for two years. The tangled charging cable by the waiting chairs. The plant in the corner that gave up months ago.

Then sit in the chair. What does the client see in the mirror? The ceiling? A storage shelf full of boxes? A window with a view of a car park? Clients spend most of their visit looking at the mirror and the wall behind it. That is the view that needs the most care.

Write down everything you notice, then sort it into three lists: things that cost nothing to fix, things that cost a little, and things that cost real money. Most owners are surprised how long the first list is.

## The free fixes come first

Before you spend a dirham, deal with the clutter. Salons collect things — spare stock, old magazines, half-empty product bottles, a broken hairdryer kept "just in case". Every visible pile tells a client the salon is not quite in control. Give everything a home behind a closed door. If a client can see it, it should be beautiful or it should be gone.

Next, clean what you have stopped noticing. Deep-clean the grout in the washroom, polish the mirrors until there is no haze, wipe the light fittings, and wash the windows inside and out. In the UAE's bright light, a dirty window is visible from the street. Fresh, clean surfaces make a room feel newer without a single purchase.

Then rearrange. Most salons were laid out on opening day and never changed. Move the waiting chairs so they face something pleasant rather than a blank wall. Give the reception desk a clear, uncluttered front. Create a small moment of welcome near the door — a tidy console, a vase, a framed print — so the first thing a client sees is deliberate, not accidental.

## Small spends that make the biggest difference

When you do spend, spend where the client's eyes and hands actually go. A fresh coat of paint on one feature wall costs far less than repainting the whole salon and changes the room completely. Choose a warm, calm colour that suits your lighting — soft sage, warm sand, or a deep muted green behind the reception desk. In the UAE's strong daylight, pale colours can look washed out by midday, so test a swatch on the wall and look at it at 10am, 3pm and 7pm before committing.

Lighting is the second best spend. If your salon still runs on harsh white tubes, swap a few for warm-toned bulbs in the waiting and styling areas. A client who looks good in your mirror feels good about your work. Add a floor lamp or two in the waiting area and the whole space softens. Keep the washbasin area brighter — clients want to see clearly there — and let the waiting area glow a little warmer.

Then the small touches: fresh cushion covers on the waiting chairs, a new rug at the entrance, matching towels folded neatly on display, and real plants. In the UAE climate, choose hardy indoor plants — snake plants, pothos, or a small palm — and water them on a schedule. One dead plant undoes ten living ones.

## What not to do

Do not copy another salon's look wholesale. The marble-and-gold interior that works in a Jumeirah villa salon may look wrong in a busy high-street shop in Sharjah. Your refresh should suit your clients, your prices and your space. A family salon does not need a luxury-hotel look; it needs to feel clean, warm and welcoming.

Do not spend on things clients never touch or see. A new storeroom shelving system is useful, but it will not bring a single booking. Spend where the client's experience happens first.

And do not wait for a slow season that may never come. The UAE salon calendar has its rhythms — quieter summer weeks, the December party rush, the Ramadan evenings — but there is always a quiet morning somewhere. A refresh done in small steps never needs the perfect week.

## A fresh space is a fresh start

Your clients choose your salon for your hands, your taste and your care. But they judge all of that through the room they sit in. A salon that looks cared for tells a client she will be cared for too. That is the whole point of a refresh: not a new look for its own sake, but a space that matches the quality of the work happening inside it.

Start this week. Walk in as a client, write your three lists, and fix the free things first. The rest can follow, one small step at a time.
