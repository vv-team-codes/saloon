Ask any UAE salon owner which week of the month is busiest and you will get the same answer: the last one. The Saturday at the end of the month is booked out days in advance. Then the new month begins, the chairs empty again, and the owner wonders where everyone went.

Nobody went anywhere. They were waiting to be paid. Across the UAE, most salaries land in the last few days of the month, and salon spending follows. A client who has been putting off a colour refresh or a facial all month books it the moment her salary arrives. It is the most predictable surge in the UAE salon calendar — yet most salons still treat it as a surprise.

The salons that make the most of payday week do not work harder. They plan for it: open the diary early, staff for the rush, message clients before the money lands, and use the busy week to fill the quiet one that follows.

## The pattern is the same every month

The surge has a shape. Enquiries climb about four days before payday, as clients check prices and ask about slots for the coming weekend. Bookings peak on the Thursday, Friday and Saturday of the last week. The busiest day is usually the Saturday closest to payday, when clients who work through the week finally have a free day and money in the bank.

None of this is bad news. A predictable surge is a gift. The problem is only that most salons plan the diary week by week, as if every week were the same. It is not, and the last week of the month deserves its own plan.

## Open the payday diary early

Two weeks before payday, release the extra slots. Add an earlier start and a later finish on the Thursday, Friday and Saturday of payday week. If you normally close at eight, stay open until nine on those three days. If you normally open at ten, open at nine.

Tell your regulars first. A short WhatsApp message two weeks ahead — "payday week slots are open, and Saturday the 29th is already half full" — gives your best clients first pick of the best times, and creates the gentle urgency that turns a maybe into a booking.

## Staff for the surge, not the average

A diary that is open longer is only useful if the team is there to work it. Payday week is the wrong week for annual leave, training days and quiet rotas. Put the strongest team on the floor for the last Thursday, Friday and Saturday, and stagger shifts so the evening peak is covered without anyone working a twelve-hour day.

This is also the week to bring in part-time cover if you have it. A freelance stylist or therapist for three days costs far less than the bookings you turn away when every chair is full and the phone keeps ringing. Agree the cover a month ahead, not the day before, and give them the diary in advance so they arrive knowing what the day looks like.

Protect the team while you do it. Build a proper break into every shift, even on the busiest Saturday, and keep the last appointment of the day short enough to finish on time. A tired team makes mistakes, and mistakes in payday week are remembered by clients who waited three weeks for their appointment.

## Message clients before the money lands

The salons that fill payday week fastest are the ones that message first. Three or four days before payday, send a short WhatsApp message to regulars who have not booked yet. Name the service they usually have, name two real slots, and ask one question: shall I hold one for you?

The message works because it arrives while the client is already thinking about payday. She is planning her weekend, her spending and her appointments, and your message lands in the middle of that planning. Sent a week earlier it is forgotten; sent on payday itself it arrives after she has already booked elsewhere.

Answer fast when she replies. Payday-week enquiries come in waves, often late at night as clients scroll after work. A reply that arrives in the morning arrives too late — the slot she wanted is gone, and so is she. ## Use the busy week to fill the quiet one

Here is the move most salons miss. Payday week puts more clients in your chairs than any other week of the month. Every one of them is a chance to fill the quiet first week that follows — if you ask at the right moment.

At checkout, when the client is happiest with her result, offer the next visit before she leaves. Not a vague "see you soon", but a specific date: "Your colour will need a refresh in four weeks — shall I put you in for the first Tuesday of next month, when the salon is quiet and you will have the chair to yourself?" Clients who book their next visit at the chair are clients who do not drift away in the first-week dip.

Keep a short list of the clients you saw in payday week and message them in the first week of the new month — not an offer, a reminder. "Your nails will be ready for an infill next week, and I have Thursday morning free." A personal message to a client you saw five days ago feels like care, not marketing, and it fills the chairs that payday week emptied.

## Start next month, this month

You do not need a new booking system to run payday week well. You need a calendar, a rota and a habit. At the start of each month, mark the last Thursday, Friday and Saturday. Open those days early, staff them properly, message your regulars three days before payday, and hold two slots back for the morning rush.

Then, when the busy week is over, do the second half of the job: book the next visits at the chair and message the clients you saw. The salons that grow in the UAE are not the ones with the busiest Saturdays. They are the ones whose busy Saturdays fill the quiet Tuesdays that follow — and payday week, planned properly, does exactly that.
