A message arrives on WhatsApp in the middle of a busy evening: "Are your stylists qualified?" It is a short question, and it is one of the most important enquiries a UAE salon will ever receive. The person writing it is deciding whether to trust your salon with her hair, her skin or a service that costs several hundred dirhams — and she has probably been disappointed somewhere else before.

In a city where a new salon opens on every high street and in every mall, clients have learned to ask. They have seen a balayage go wrong, paid for a keratin treatment that lasted two weeks, or watched a friend's colour turn brassy a day after leaving the chair. How your salon answers in the next few minutes decides whether that client walks through your door or keeps scrolling.

## What the question really means

"Are your stylists qualified?" is rarely a question about a certificate on the wall. It is a question about safety. The client is really asking: will the person touching my hair know what they are doing? Will they tell me honestly if something is not right for me?

That is why the worst reply is a flat "Yes, all our stylists are qualified." It answers the words but not the worry, and it gives the client nothing to hold on to. A defensive reply — a long list of certificates fired back as if the client had accused you of something — is just as bad. She was not accusing you. She was asking for a reason to choose you.

The best replies treat the question as a compliment. A client who asks about qualifications is a client who cares about quality, and quality clients are the ones who rebook, buy the home-care products and bring their friends.

## The first reply: four parts that build trust

A good first reply fits in one WhatsApp message. First, welcome the question: "Great question — we are glad you asked." Second, name something concrete, such as brand academy certificates or colour-specialist training. Third, connect it to her service: "Our senior colourist, Sara, has completed advanced balayage training and does most of our colour corrections." Fourth, offer a clear next step: a consultation with a named stylist, a duration and a price. That reply names a person, a skill and a next step — and specific is what trust is made of.

## Show the proof, don't just claim it

Clients believe what they can see. Make qualifications visible in small, natural ways. A photo of a certificate on the wall, shared once on your WhatsApp status or Instagram stories, does more than a paragraph of claims. A short stylist bio on your booking page — name, speciality, training, years of experience — lets a client choose with confidence before she even messages you. Before-and-after photos of real work, especially colour corrections, are proof that no certificate can match.

Keep it warm and human. "Sara trained in London and has been colouring hair in Dubai for six years" tells a client more than a list of course codes ever will. If a stylist is newly qualified, say so honestly and pair it with the safety net: "Aisha completed her colour training this year and works under Sara's supervision on every colour service." Clients respect honesty far more than they fear a junior stylist — as long as someone experienced is checking the work.

## When you cannot answer yes

Every salon has a service it is still building. Perhaps your team is new to a particular treatment, or your most experienced therapist is on leave. The temptation is to stretch the truth, and it is always a mistake. A client who discovers the stretch in the chair will never come back, and she will tell her friends why.

The honest answer costs nothing and often wins the booking anyway. "We are honest with you — our team is still building experience with that treatment, so we would start with a consultation and a patch test, and our senior stylist would supervise." Then offer the alternative you can stand behind. Clients remember the salon that told them the truth far longer than the salon that said yes to everything.

## The hidden question behind the question

Often, a qualifications enquiry is a price enquiry in disguise. A client comparing a 200-dirham cut with a 450-dirham cut is really asking whether the expensive one is worth it. Qualifications are the reason the price is what it is: training costs money, experience costs money, and a colourist who has corrected a hundred bad dyes will not make your hair the hundred-and-first.

So weave the value into the reply without lecturing. "Our colour services are priced for the consultation, the patch test and the senior stylist who does the work — and we include a follow-up check within a week." That turns a defensive question into a reason to book, without a single discount.

## Turn the enquiry into a consultation booking

The goal of the reply is not to win an argument about certificates. It is to book a consultation. End every qualifications reply with one concrete offer: a named stylist, a day and a time, a duration and a price. "Sara has a consultation slot on Thursday at 6 pm — 30 minutes, 50 dirhams, redeemable against your service. Shall I hold it for you?"

A consultation is the perfect answer to a qualifications question, because it lets the client judge the expertise herself. In thirty minutes she will know whether your stylist listens, explains honestly and understands her hair. No certificate can do that job.
