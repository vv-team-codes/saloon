She books six months after her last visit. Her hair is longer than she likes it, thinner at the temples than she remembers, and she has exactly two hours before the baby wakes up. She is a woman who has barely had a minute to herself in months, hoping, quietly, that someone will look after her for a change.

New mums are some of the most loyal clients a UAE salon can win. They live nearby, they need regular upkeep, and once they trust a salon they rarely leave. But that first visit back is fragile. Get it right and you have a regular for years. Get it wrong — keep her waiting, rush her, or make her feel like an inconvenience — and she will not complain. She will simply never come back.

## Start with the first message

The booking usually starts on WhatsApp, late at night, while the baby is feeding. The speed and warmth of that first reply matter more than any offer.

Answer the real question behind the message. She is asking whether the salon fits around a baby's unpredictable day, not just whether a slot exists. Offer two or three realistic options rather than a single take-it-or-leave-it time. Say how long the service genuinely takes, including processing and drying, so she can plan childcare honestly. If she needs to move the appointment, let her do it without a guilt trip — a mum's day changes by the hour, and a salon that understands that earns her trust before she ever sits in the chair.

## Time is the real luxury

For a new mum, the scarcest thing is not money. It is uninterrupted time. Design her visit around that fact.

Offer a realistic express option alongside the full service. A root touch-up with a blow-dry, a tidy-up cut, or a 45-minute facial can fit between feeds and naps in a way a three-hour transformation cannot. Be honest about what fits in the time she has, and do not book her into a long service she will have to abandon halfway through.

## Her hair has changed — say so kindly

Postpartum hair changes are real, common, and rarely talked about. Many new mums notice shedding around the hairline, a different texture, or hair that suddenly will not hold a style the way it used to. She may be worried about it and embarrassed to mention it.

Your stylist should raise it first, gently and without drama. A simple "lots of new mums notice the hairline changes — it's very normal, and here's what works well while it grows back" does more for her confidence than any treatment. Recommend honestly: softer layers that disguise regrowth, a colour that grows out gracefully so she is not back in three weeks, and home care she can actually manage with a baby in the house. Never sell her a miracle cure or a shelf of products she does not need.

If she asks about treatments while breastfeeding, be careful and clear. Say plainly what you know, what you are unsure about, and what is worth checking with her doctor — and never guess.

## Make room for the baby — or help her leave the baby at home

Some new mums bring the baby. Some would love an hour without the baby. Both are fine, and your salon should handle both without fuss.

If she brings the little one, a little planning goes a long way. A comfortable spot for the pram near her chair, a quiet corner for a feed, and a team that does not sigh when a baby cries will make her feel welcome rather than tolerated. A short appointment works best here — a trim, a blow-dry, a brow tidy — something that can pause and resume without drama.

If she has left the baby at home, protect that hour fiercely. Do not keep her waiting in reception, do not stretch a 60-minute service into 90, and do not fill the silence with small talk she did not ask for. Some new mums want to chat about anything except the baby. Others want to close their eyes for twenty minutes. Read her, and give her the version of the visit she actually came for.

## The goodbye that brings her back

Rebook before she leaves, but do it around her real life. Ask when the next six weeks look like, not just "same time next week?" Offer a standing slot at a quieter hour if that suits her, and make it easy to move it on WhatsApp when the baby has other plans.

A short message the next day — a simple hope that she enjoyed her hour, and the date of her next visit — costs nothing and lands warmly. And when she does come back, remember what you talked about. That continuity is what turns a one-off treat into a routine she protects.

New mums do not need a special package or a discount. They need a salon that understands their day has changed completely, and that treats their time, their body and their confidence with care. Welcome her back properly, and she will bring her friends, her sister, and eventually her daughter — and she will stay.
