Every UAE salon knows this message. It arrives on WhatsApp, usually in the evening, usually a week before a flight: "I'm going for Umrah next week — can you cut my hair before I go?" Sometimes it is a father booking for the whole family. Sometimes it is a man asking about a head shave for when he comes back. Sometimes it is a woman asking whether a trim after Umrah is something a salon can do at all.

It is a small enquiry with real weight behind it. How your salon answers decides whether the client trusts you with the booking — and whether they come back after they return. Handled well, one message becomes three bookings: the trim before travel, the tidy-up after, and the family.

## What clients are actually asking

The first question is the pre-travel trim: a neat cut a few days before departure, something tidy that lasts the trip, or a manageable style a woman can maintain herself for two weeks.

The second is the post-Umrah head shave. For men, shaving the head is part of completing the pilgrimage, and many prefer a barber at home rather than a rush job abroad. They ask whether you do it, how long it takes, and whether it needs an appointment.

The third is the women's trim after Umrah: a fingertip's length, done by a female stylist, in privacy. The fourth is the family booking: can you take four or five of us in one visit before Thursday's flight? That is a diary question as much as a hair question.

## Start with what the salon does — and what it doesn't

Honesty is what makes this enquiry different from an ordinary haircut. The rites of Umrah and Hajj happen in Makkah, not in your salon, and your team should never describe a haircut as part of the pilgrimage. What the salon does is prepare hair before travel and tidy it after return.

The first line of any reply should draw the line clearly: "We can cut and prepare your hair before you travel, and we do the head shave and the trim when you are back home. The rites themselves are completed in Makkah."

The second rule is just as firm: never give a religious ruling. If a client asks whether they must shave or whether a trim is enough, the honest answer is that it depends on their school of thought and they should check with their imam or scholar. Train everyone who answers WhatsApp on that line.

## The pre-travel appointment, done properly

Book the pre-travel cut two or three days before the flight, not the night before. Hair settles after a day, and a client who flies the next morning has no room to come back if something needs adjusting. When you confirm, suggest the earlier day: "Tuesday rather than Wednesday evening, so the cut has a day to settle before your flight."

Ask two questions before you confirm. For men: clippers or scissors, and how short? For women: are you travelling with your hair covered, and do you want a style you can manage yourself for two weeks without a blow-dry? The answers decide the cut, the time and the price.

This is also the moment to book the return visit. "Shall I hold a slot for the week you are back, for the shave and a tidy-up?" takes ten seconds and fills a chair that would otherwise sit empty while the client is away. Note the expected return week on the booking.

## The head shave and the trim after return

Offer the head shave as a named service with a fixed price and a fixed time — thirty minutes is honest — and make sure a male barber is available for male clients who ask for one. Shave, then follow with a warm towel and a light moisturiser or scalp oil, because skin that has spent two weeks in the Saudi sun is often dry and sensitive.

For women returning from Umrah, the trim is small but the privacy around it is not. Confirm that a female stylist will do it, in a private chair or screened area if she prefers, and that it takes only a few minutes. Do not turn a fingertip trim into a full service she did not ask for — offer a wash and blow-dry as a separate, clearly priced choice and let her decide.

Price both services plainly — clients compare prices quickly. A clear price in the first WhatsApp reply — "The head shave is AED 50 and takes 30 minutes" — removes the one reason a client messages three salons instead of booking with yours.

## The reply that books it

Most of these enquiries arrive on WhatsApp late in the evening, when the client has finally sat down to plan the trip. A reply that books the visit has four parts.

First, acknowledge the trip warmly. Second, answer the actual question with a specific service, a price and a time. Third, offer the return booking in the same message. Fourth, close with two real slots, not an open question: "Tuesday at 5 PM or Wednesday at 6:30 PM — which suits you better?"

The salon that replies like that at 10 PM wins the booking over the salon that replies at 10 AM the next morning with "Yes, we are open." That is also where a 24/7 AI receptionist like ZYNA earns its place: it can take the enquiry the moment it arrives and hold the slot before the client messages the next salon on their list.

Three things should never appear in a reply: a ruling on what the pilgrimage requires, a salon service described as part of the rites, or a rushed answer to a nervous first-timer. The same honesty applies to what the salon cannot do. If you do not offer a head shave, say so in one line and offer the nearest alternative — a number-one clipper cut before travel, or a trusted barber you can recommend. A clean, kind no keeps the client.

Umrah and Hajj enquiries are not a niche. In the UAE, they arrive all year, from every community. The salons that win them answer with warmth, specific prices, honest limits and a slot already held for the return. That is a small system to build — one reply template, one named service, one note on every booking — and it turns a single message about a haircut into a client who books with you before every journey.
