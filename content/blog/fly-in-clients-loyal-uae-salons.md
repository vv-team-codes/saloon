Dubai is a city people leave and keep coming back to. Some clients move to London, Riyadh, Mumbai or Singapore for work, yet their hair still belongs to a stylist in Jumeirah, Marina or Deira. They fly in twice or three times a year, land on a Thursday evening, and their first appointment is already in your diary for Friday morning. These fly-in clients are some of the most valuable regulars a UAE salon can have. They book long appointments, they rarely haggle, and they bring sisters, cousins and friends with them. But they are also the easiest regulars to lose, because a single missed message or a full diary on the one weekend they are in town can end a relationship that survived a move across continents.

## Start before she lands

A fly-in client does not decide to visit your salon when she arrives. She decides weeks earlier, usually while she is packing. That is your window. A short WhatsApp message about ten days before her usual travel season — "Are you coming to Dubai this month? Shall I hold your usual Friday slot?" — does two things. It tells her the chair is waiting, and it gives you first claim on her limited time before every other salon, dentist and dinner plan in her calendar fills it.

Ask her to send her flight details if she is comfortable sharing them. Not to track her, but so you can hold a slot that genuinely fits her arrival time.

## Book the whole trip in one conversation

A fly-in client is not coming for a single haircut. She is coming for everything she cannot get done properly at home: colour, a cut, threading, nails, a facial, maybe a blow-dry before a family wedding on Saturday night. Treat her visit as one plan, not a series of separate bookings.

In a single WhatsApp conversation, map out her days. Friday morning for colour and cut, with processing time used for a manicure. Saturday late morning for the facial, so she glows for the evening event. When a client sees her whole trip organised in one message thread, she stops shopping around. She has no reason to. You have already solved the problem she flew in with.

Be honest about what fits and what does not. If her dream schedule needs six hours and you only have four, say so early. A fly-in client would rather hear that on WhatsApp from home than discover it in your chair on her last day in Dubai.

## Remember everything, because she cannot remind you in person

A local regular pops in every three weeks, so small details get corrected naturally. A fly-in client arrives after four months away, and the last thing she wants is to explain her whole history again. Your notes are the relationship.

Before she lands, re-read her file: the exact colour formula, the length she likes, the stylist she trusts, the products that irritated her skin last time, the fact that she prefers the first appointment of the day because she hates waiting. Have the colour mixed and the chair ready before she walks in. When a client who lives abroad sits down and you already know her, she feels like she never left. That feeling is the whole reason she keeps flying back to you instead of finding someone closer to home.

## Make the visit worth the airfare

A fly-in client is spending far more than the price of a haircut to sit in your chair. She has paid for a flight, a hotel or a family stay, and a week of her time. The service has to feel like it was worth that effort.

Small gestures matter more than grand ones. A quiet chair away from the bustle. Her favourite drink ready without being asked. A realistic finish time, stated at the start, so she can plan the rest of her day. And never, ever keep her waiting because a local walk-in took her slot. She has two days in Dubai. Every hour in your waiting area is an hour she will not get back, and it is the fastest way to lose her to a salon in her own city.

## Stay in touch between trips, lightly

Four months is a long silence. A fly-in client who hears nothing from you between visits starts to wonder whether she is still remembered. But she does not want weekly broadcasts. Two or three thoughtful messages a year are enough.

Send a short note when her usual season approaches. Share a photo of a new service you think she would like, with a simple "thought of you" — no hard sell attached. And when she messages you from abroad with a question — "my colour is fading, what should I use?" — answer properly, with real advice, even though there is no booking in it for you today. That free, honest answer is what keeps you as her salon when she lands next time.

## Turn one fly-in client into three

Fly-in clients rarely travel alone. They come with a sister who needs a blow-dry, a mother who wants threading, a friend who has heard about your salon for a year and finally has a free afternoon. Make it easy for them to bring people. Offer to hold a second chair beside hers. When the friend has a good experience, she books her own visit next trip — and the circle grows.

## The test is simple

Ask yourself one question about every client who lives abroad: if she landed tomorrow, would your salon be ready for her, or would she have to chase you for a slot? If the answer is ready — slot held, notes re-read, whole trip planned — you have a client for years. If the answer is that she would have to message three times and hope, she will eventually find someone closer to home, and you will never know why she stopped coming.

Fly-in clients choose a Dubai salon the way people choose a home. Not because it is the nearest, but because it is where they feel known. Keep them feeling known, and the distance stops mattering.
