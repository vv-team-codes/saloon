"What's the difference between balayage and highlights?" It arrives on WhatsApp at 9pm, usually with a screenshot of someone else's hair. The client has already decided she wants a change. How you answer in the next few minutes decides whether she books with you, books the wrong service, or asks the salon down the road instead.

A client asking about price is still deciding whether she can afford you; a client asking about the difference between two services is deciding which one to book.

## Why this question is a booking waiting to happen

Most salons answer as if it were a quiz: explain the technique, list the steps, wait. The client reads a wall of text, feels no closer to a decision, and replies "ok thanks, I'll think about it."

Behind every "what's the difference?" are three questions the client rarely asks directly. Will it look good on me? How much upkeep will it need? And how much will it cost, in money and in time? Answer those three and the comparison becomes a recommendation.

## The three things every comparison answer needs

First, the result, in words the client can picture. Not "hand-painted freehand technique" but "soft, sun-kissed ends with no hard line as it grows out." Not "a builder gel overlay" but "stronger nails that last three to four weeks."

Second, the upkeep. This is the part salons most often skip. How often will she need to come back? A client who chooses highlights expecting balayage-level grow-out will be back in four weeks feeling misled, even if the work was perfect. Tell her now, while she is still choosing.

Third, the price and the time — as a range, not a single figure, and always attached to the next step. "Balayage is usually AED 450 to 650 and takes two and a half to three hours. Highlights start around AED 350. I can book you with Sara on Saturday at 11am and she will confirm the exact price once she sees your hair." A range is honest. A single number quoted blind is a promise you may not be able to keep.

## The three comparisons UAE salons hear every week

Balayage versus highlights is the classic. Highlights lift brighter from root to tip and need a touch-up every six to eight weeks. Balayage grows out softer and can stretch to ten or twelve weeks. Ask not "which do you prefer?" but "how often do you want to be back in this chair?" Her answer chooses the service.

Gel polish versus BIAB is the nail version of the same conversation. Gel polish is a colour service on the natural nail that lasts about two weeks. BIAB adds strength and structure, suits nails that split or peel in the air conditioning and hard water, and lasts three to four weeks. Mention the price difference and the removal time, because a client who books BIAB expecting a twenty-minute appointment will be late for her next one.

Classic versus volume lashes confuses almost every first-time lash client. Classic is one extension per natural lash, a natural, mascara-like look. Volume is a fan of finer extensions per lash, fuller and more dramatic. What helps is a photo of each on a real client, plus the fill cycle: both need infills every two to three weeks, and volume takes longer in the chair. Send the two photos side by side and the client will often choose in seconds.

## A reply you can copy

Here is a shape that works on WhatsApp for almost any comparison. Personalise the middle lines.

"Great question — they look similar in photos but they behave very differently. Balayage grows out softly, so you can go ten to twelve weeks between visits, and it is usually AED 450 to 650 depending on length. Highlights are brighter from the root and need a touch-up every six to eight weeks, from around AED 350. If you like a low-maintenance grow-out, balayage is the one. Shall I book you with Sara on Saturday at 11am or 4pm? She will confirm the exact price at your consultation."

Three sentences of explanation, one recommendation, one question with two real slots. That is the whole formula. Save it as a template, adapt the names, prices and slots each time, and have one owner review the templates monthly.

## What never to say

The first is "it depends" with nothing after it. It is true and it is useless. If it depends, say what it depends on, and ask the one question that settles it. "It depends on your hair length and how bright you want it — could you send me a photo of your hair in daylight?"

The second is trashing the other option. "Highlights are old-fashioned" does not make the client trust you. Compare honestly, including the genuine strengths of both.

The third is quoting one exact price for a service you have not seen. "Balayage is AED 500" sounds confident until the client arrives with waist-length hair and the real price is AED 750. She feels misled before the service even starts. Ranges protect you and the client.

## When the honest answer is "neither"

Sometimes the best answer is that neither service is right. The client asking about balayage versus highlights may actually need a colour correction first. The client choosing between classic and volume lashes may have natural lashes too fine for either and need a lash lift instead. Say so, kindly and quickly. "From your photo it looks like the old colour needs lifting first, so a straight balayage would come out patchy. A consultation with Sara on Saturday would let her plan it properly." That loses one booking and wins a client for years.
