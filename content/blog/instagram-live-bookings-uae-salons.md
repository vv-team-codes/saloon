Most UAE salons post Reels and run Stories. A smaller number do something far more direct: they go live on Instagram once a week and take bookings while viewers are still on screen. It costs nothing, takes half an hour, and reaches clients already scrolling in the evening.

A Reel is a performance; a live is a conversation. When a client watches her stylist answer a real question in real time, she stops wondering whether the salon is good and starts wondering whether she can get a slot this week.

## Why live video converts differently

A polished Reel can be filmed anywhere, by anyone, with enough editing, and UAE clients have learned to be sceptical. A live cannot be faked the same way. The light is the salon's real light, the stylist's hands are doing real work, and when someone asks whether a colour will suit dark hair in the Dubai sun, the answer comes straight away.

That honesty is the marketing. Clients who have watched a salon answer awkward questions live — about prices, patch tests, and what a treatment honestly cannot do — arrive trusting the team.

## Pick one format and repeat it

Salons that keep a live habit going pick one format and repeat it until clients expect it. Three work well in the UAE.

The first is the live question-and-answer session: thirty minutes, one stylist, questions from the comments and the salon's WhatsApp inbox. The owner collects five during the week and answers them on camera — and every question answered on live is one the salon will not answer ten times on WhatsApp.

The second is the live transformation: one real client, one service, filmed from consultation to mirror reveal. With the client's written permission, it is the most persuasive marketing a salon can make — proof, in real time, that the work is good. The third is the quiet tour: a slow walk through the salon on a calm morning, for new salons and for nervous first-timers who want to see the chairs before they book.

## The 30-minute weekly routine

A weekly live only survives if it is small enough to repeat: thirty minutes, once a week, at the same time. In the UAE, 8 or 9 PM works best, when clients are home scrolling and planning their weekend. Friday and Sunday evenings are both strong.

The day before, one Story announces the topic and time and invites questions. On the day, set-up takes five minutes — a phone on a tripod, the best-lit chair, a clean background, a charger plugged in. No script, just three talking points on a card out of shot.

In the last two minutes, the stylist names one real thing: two open slots this week, a service with space on Saturday, a package ending Sunday. "We have a colour slot open on Thursday at 6 PM — message us on WhatsApp and it is yours."

## What to say when someone asks "how much?"

Every salon live gets the same question, usually within five minutes: how much does it cost? The answer that works is a range, a reason and a next step. "A full balayage with us is usually between AED 450 and AED 650, depending on length. Message us a photo of your hair on WhatsApp and we will give you an exact price and a time." The viewer hears honesty, and the price talk happens in private, where it belongs.

The same rule applies to questions the salon cannot answer on camera — a medical question, a colour correction that must be seen in person, a treatment it does not offer. Say so, and offer the next step: "Come in for ten minutes on Saturday morning and we will look at it properly." Viewers remember the salon that said that far longer than the salon that guessed.

## Turn viewers into bookings the same evening

A live creates a wave of enquiries that arrives after closing. Someone watches the replay at 11 PM, wants the Thursday slot, and messages — then waits until morning, by which time she has messaged two other salons. The salons that win these clients answer first, even at midnight.

So the booking link should be in the bio before the session starts, and a saved reply with the week's open slots should be ready. The next morning, everyone who asked a question gets a short personal message. "Thanks for the question about frizz — we have a slot on Saturday at 11 if you want us to look at it." That message converts more viewers than any discount, because it proves the salon was listening.

## The mistakes that quietly kill a live

Most salon lives fail for fixable reasons. The first is irregularity: three lives in one week, then nothing for a month. Clients cannot build a habit around a salon that appears at random. One live a week, at the same time, beats a burst every time.

The second is talking to nobody. A stylist who stares at the camera and waits for questions loses the room in ninety seconds. Start with the questions already collected, greet people by name, and keep talking even when the viewer count is low — the replay often reaches ten times more people than the live.

The third is selling too hard. A live that is one long advertisement trains viewers to leave; a useful one trains them to stay, and the bookings follow. The fourth is bad sound — clients forgive shaky video, not audio they cannot hear. Film in the quietest corner, dryers off, and test the sound before going live.

## Your first four weeks

Week one is the question-and-answer session — no client, no model, just five questions from the inbox. Week two repeats it at the same time. Week three adds the concrete offer at the close. Week four, try a live transformation with a willing regular, and save the replay to a Highlight called "Live".

After a month, judge the habit by bookings, not viewer counts: WhatsApp enquiries during and after each live, how many became appointments, and how many were new clients. A live bringing three new bookings a week beats a Reel with ten thousand views and no bookings. Keep the format, keep the time, and let the audience grow one viewer at a time.
