Every week, UAE salons get messages from people who will never become regulars. A guest at a hotel on Sheikh Zayed Road wants a blow-dry before dinner. A visitor staying in a Marina apartment needs a manicure before her flight home. A bride's sister, in town for three days, asks if you can fit in a full glam session tomorrow morning.

These are tourist and hotel-guest enquiries, and they are some of the most valuable messages a salon receives. The client has money to spend, a deadline that cannot move, and almost no patience for a slow reply. She will book with the first salon that answers clearly — and forget the second one entirely.

## Why tourist enquiries are worth answering fast

A resident who messages you on Tuesday might book on Thursday. A tourist who messages you on Tuesday is choosing between you and the salon downstairs in her hotel. The decision window is measured in minutes, not days.

Tourists also spend differently. A one-time visitor does not need a loyalty programme or a membership pitch. She needs one excellent service, right now, at a price she understands before she walks in — and she often adds services on the spot because she will not be back.

The other reason to answer fast is reviews. A happy tourist leaves a Google review the same evening, often with photos — marketing you cannot buy, in a city where new residents search "salon near me" constantly.

## The four answers every tourist enquiry needs

Most tourist messages are short: "Hi, do you do hair? How much for a blow-dry? Can I come today?" The temptation is to reply with a price and wait. Instead, answer with the four things she needs to decide:

**What exactly, and how long.** Name the service, the realistic duration, and what is included. "Blow-dry with wash, 45 minutes, AED 120" beats "Prices start from AED 90" every time.

**Where exactly, and how do I get there.** Tourists do not know your neighbourhood. Give a landmark, not just an address: "Ground floor of the mall, next to the food court entrance" or "Five minutes' walk from the Marina metro, exit 2". Add parking information if you have it.

**Can I come today, and at what time.** Offer two real slots, not "come anytime". "Today at 4:30 or 6:00 — shall I hold 4:30 for you?" gives her something to say yes to. Vague availability kills tourist bookings because she is comparing you with three other salons at the same time.

**What do I need to know before I arrive.** One line is enough: whether a deposit is needed, whether you take cards, and whether she should arrive with clean, dry hair. Tourists appreciate this because they have no local knowledge to fall back on.

## The deposit question, handled honestly

Many salons hesitate to ask a tourist for a deposit. They worry it will scare her off. In practice, the opposite is true: a small deposit protects both sides, and tourists are used to paying deposits for hotels, tours, and restaurants.

The key is how you ask. "To hold your 4:30 slot, we take a AED 50 deposit by card link — it comes off your bill" is clear and fair. Frame it as holding the chair, not as a penalty. If she declines, offer the next walk-in window instead of arguing — a tourist who will not pay a deposit is telling you her plans are uncertain, and that is useful information too.

For high-value one-off services — bridal glam, full colour, a party package — a deposit is not optional. These appointments block a stylist for hours, and if she cancels at the last minute you cannot refill that chair with a regular.

## Working with hotel concierges

Some of your best tourist bookings will never come from the tourist at all. They come from the concierge desk. A concierge who trusts your salon will send guests to you all year, and the guest arrives pre-sold. Reply to concierge messages even faster than to guests, give them a named contact and a direct WhatsApp number, and deliver exactly what was promised. If you are near hotels, visit the concierge desks in person once, with a printed price list and a business card — a face beats a cold message. Keep a short "visitor menu" ready too: five or six services with prices and durations, sent as one message.

## The follow-up that brings a review, not a complaint

The visit ends, she pays, she leaves. Most salons stop there. A tourist is different: she has no reason to come back, so the follow-up has one job — make the experience worth reviewing.

Send one short message the same evening: "Hope you enjoyed your evening — thank you for visiting us today. If you loved your blow-dry, a quick Google review would mean the world to us." That is it. No discount offer for a next visit she will never make. One warm message, one small ask.

## When the honest answer is no

Sometimes the answer is no. She wants a 9 PM appointment and your last slot is 7 PM, or a service you do not offer. Saying no quickly and kindly beats a slow yes that falls apart — and a salon that was honest and helpful is the one she recommends to the friend who moves to Dubai next month.

## A simple system for tourist enquiries

You do not need new software to handle tourist enquiries well. You need a short, repeatable routine anyone on the team can follow: reply within ten minutes, even after hours, with the four answers; keep a visitor menu and a saved location message ready; take a deposit for anything over an hour, framed as holding the chair; send one same-evening follow-up asking for a review; and keep a list of the hotels and concierges near you, checking in monthly.

Tourist enquiries are a small part of most salons' days, but a large part of their reputation. Every visitor who leaves happy is a review, a referral, and marketing that keeps working long after her flight home. Answer fast, be specific, and treat the one-time visit like it matters — because to her, it does.
