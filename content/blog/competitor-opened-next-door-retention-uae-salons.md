A new salon has just opened next door. The launch offer is 50 percent off everything, and the queue on opening day stretches past your front window. Every salon owner in the UAE knows this moment, and the first instinct is usually panic — match the discount, hand out flyers, or brace for a quiet month.

Stop. Most clients do not leave a salon they like because a cheaper one opened nearby. They leave because the salon they liked slowly stopped making them feel noticed. A competitor opening next door is not the end of your business. It is a test of whether the loyalty you think you have is real — and a chance to make it stronger.

## Why most regulars stay put

Think about your own habits. Once you find a stylist who understands your hair, a therapist who remembers your skin, or a nail technician who never rushes you, switching feels like work. You would have to explain everything again, risk a bad result, and give up the comfort of being known.

New salons in Dubai, Abu Dhabi and Sharjah win curiosity visits. They rarely win loyalty on day one, because loyalty is built over months of small, consistent moments: the consultation that happens before the scissors come out, the colour formula written down so it is the same every time, a stylist remembering you are growing your hair out. None of that can be copied with a banner and a voucher.

## Do not panic-discount

The worst response to a competitor is a price war. A 50 percent discount trains your clients to think your normal prices were too high, and it pulls in bargain-hunters who will leave the moment the next salon undercuts you. Meanwhile, the regulars who were happy to pay your prices start wondering why they ever paid full price.

Hold your prices. Respond with value that costs you very little instead: a complimentary conditioning treatment with a colour service that week, a free brow tidy for regulars, or an extra ten minutes of head massage at no charge. These gestures say "we appreciate you" without saying "our prices were wrong."

## Talk to your regulars first

The week a competitor opens, your best move is a conversation, not a campaign. Send a short, personal WhatsApp message to your regulars — not a broadcast, a real message. Something simple: "We noticed a new salon opened nearby. We just wanted to say thank you for choosing us, and that your usual chair is always here for you."

That one message does two things. It tells the client you noticed them before they drifted, and it gives them a reason to feel good about staying. If a client does try the new place, do not take it personally. Many will. Most come back within two or three visits, because a first visit somewhere new is rarely as good as the hundredth visit somewhere familiar. When they return, welcome them warmly and never mention the absence.

## Make the experience impossible to copy

A new salon can copy your prices and your menu. It cannot copy your team knowing that Mrs. Ayesha likes her tea without sugar, that the client in chair three is growing out a fringe, or that the Saturday 11 o'clock regular likes silence and a magazine. Those details are your real defence, and they cost nothing.

Use the quiet weeks after a competitor opens to tighten the experience. Walk through your salon as if you were a first-time client. Is the greeting warm within the first minute? Is the waiting area calm or crowded? Does the stylist explain what they are doing? Small fixes matter more than any promotion, because clients comparing you with the new place compare the feeling, not the price list.

## Watch what your clients actually do

Not every client who tries the new salon is lost, and not every regular is safe. The warning signs are quiet: a client who used to book every three weeks stretches to five, a regular stops replying to confirmation messages, or a standing appointment is moved twice in a row. These are the moments to act — a personal message, a genuine check-in, an offer to adjust the time or the stylist.

Keep a simple weekly habit: look at who has not booked in twice their usual gap and reach out to five of them. A short message that mentions something real — "Your colour is due about now, and Sara has Thursday at 6 free if you like" — works far better than a generic offer. It shows you remember them as a person, not a diary entry.

## Win the second visit, not the first

If a competitor's opening brings curious new clients through your door too, treat them well — but remember the real contest is the second visit. The first is driven by curiosity and discounts; the second by whether the client felt looked after. So make the follow-up count: a thank-you message the same evening, a note of what was done and when the next visit should be, and an easy way to rebook.

## Turn the threat into a better salon

Six months after a competitor opens, most salons that survive are better than they were before — not because they got cheaper, but because the scare forced them to pay attention again: tighter service, remembered names, regulars no longer taken for granted.

Use the opening as a deadline, not a disaster: refresh the menu board, retrain the team on consultations, fix the small things you have been meaning to fix. Then let the work speak. Clients notice when a salon suddenly feels more cared for, and they tell their friends. Word of mouth in a UAE neighbourhood is worth more than any launch offer, because it comes from trust.

A new salon next door is a challenge, not a verdict. The clients who stay will stay because you earned them twice — once when they first chose you, and again when they had a reason to leave and did not.
