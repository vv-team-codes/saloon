Every January, the same thing happens in salons across Dubai and Abu Dhabi. The diary fills with faces you have not seen in months, alongside brand-new clients who arrive with a firm plan: this is the year they look after themselves properly. Hair every six weeks, nails every fortnight, skin on a real routine.

By March, most of them are gone.

It is not that they were unhappy. They simply never built the habit, and nobody in the salon helped them build it. January is the biggest retention opportunity of the year, precisely because the motivation is already there. Your job is to catch it before it fades.

## Start the conversation in December, not January

The salons that win January start working on it in December. While the festive rush is at its peak, they plant the seed with a short chat at the chair about what January could look like. Not a sales pitch. A plan.

"Shall we book your January colour now, so you start the year with it done?" is a very different sentence from "Would you like to rebook?" The first gives the client a decision she can say yes to in five seconds. The second asks her to think about her diary, her budget and her motivation all at once, which usually ends in "I'll message you later" — and she won't.

December is also the moment to reach clients who drifted away during the year. A short, personal WhatsApp message works better than any offer: "We missed you this year. If looking after yourself is on your list for January, your chair is here." Clients who return on a message like that return because they feel remembered.

## Turn a resolution into a routine, not a single booking

A resolution is vague. A routine is specific. The gap between the two is where salons lose January clients, because a single January booking is just a one-off visit with good intentions attached.

Agree the shape of the routine before the client leaves the salon. Three questions do the work. How often can she realistically come? Which services matter most to her? And what day of the week actually fits her life? A client who works in DIFC and can only come on Saturday mornings needs a different plan from a mum in JVC who is free on weekday mornings.

Write the answers down. Then book the next two visits, not just the next one. Two bookings in the diary turn a resolution into a commitment she can see. One booking is a hope.

## Price the routine honestly

January clients are often the most price-sensitive clients of the year. They have just paid for festive season spending, school fees are due, and the credit card bill is sitting on the kitchen table. A salon that pushes an expensive package in that first January visit will lose the client it just won.

Price the routine, not the visit. Show her what her three-month plan costs, broken down per visit, and let her see that the regular rhythm is cheaper than the occasional splurge she has been doing. A cut and colour every six weeks, a blow-dry in between, a facial once a month — priced as a rhythm, it feels manageable. If she hesitates, offer a smaller first step: a blow-dry and a brow shape this week, with the colour booked for next week.

## Use the quiet hours, not the Saturday rush

Here is a practical problem with January resolutions: everyone wants Saturday. Your regulars already own Saturday, and if the new January clients cannot get a Saturday slot, they will try once, fail, and never come back.

Steer January clients toward the hours you actually have. Weekday mornings, Sunday afternoons, the 11am slot that is always empty — these hours make a January routine sustainable, for the client and for the salon. Offer them honestly. "Saturday is fully booked for the next three weeks, but I can give you Tuesday at 10am every fortnight, same stylist, same chair. Shall I hold it?"

## Make the third visit the moment that matters

A client who visits three times is far more likely to become a regular than one who visits once or twice. The first visit is curiosity. The second is checking. The third is habit. So plan for the third visit deliberately.

At the second visit, mention something specific you noticed at the first: how her colour settled, or that her skin responded well to the facial. Clients stay where they feel known. A small note in the diary — "likes it quieter, no small talk, extra strong coffee" — costs nothing and is worth more than any loyalty card.

At the third visit, say it out loud. "You are one of our regulars now." People live up to the identity you give them.

## Keep the conversation going between visits

January resolutions die in silence. A client who hears nothing from her salon for five weeks will forget the plan she made, even if she meant it at the time.

A single WhatsApp message between visits is enough. Not a broadcast, not a generic offer, but something tied to her: "Your colour should be settling nicely now — if the ends feel dry in this weather, the mask we used is the one on your card." Clients in the UAE live on WhatsApp, and a salon that uses it thoughtfully feels attentive rather than pushy.

## Measure January properly

At the end of January, count the right number. Not how many new clients walked in — that number always looks good. Count how many of them have a second booking in the diary, and how many came back a third time by the end of February. That is your January retention rate, and it decides whether January was a busy month or a good month.

January will always bring a wave of good intentions through your door. The salons that keep them are the ones that turn a resolution into a routine, a routine into a habit, and a habit into a regular.
