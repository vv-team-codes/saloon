A salon move is exciting for the owner and unsettling for the client. She has a routine: she knows which mall entrance to use, where to park, which lift to take, and that your chair is on the left. One day the address changes, and everything she knows about visiting you has to be relearned. Whether she relearns it with you or drifts to the salon that opened nearer her home depends almost entirely on how you tell her — and in the UAE, that telling happens on WhatsApp.

Most salons announce a move badly: one Instagram post, a changed Google listing, and an assumption that the regulars will find them. But your regulars do not check your Instagram grid before their next appointment. WhatsApp is the only channel that reaches the people who actually book, in the same chat where they book every time.

## Tell them early, and tell them three times

A single announcement is not enough. Clients skim messages, forget addresses, and book from habit. Plan three messages over the move.

The first goes out two weeks before the move. It is good news, not admin: "We have some exciting news — from 8 December, we will be welcoming you at our beautiful new space in Al Barsha, just five minutes from our current salon." Include the new location pin in that very first message, while the excitement is fresh. People save pins; they do not go back and search for them later.

The second message goes out the day before the move, and it is practical. New address, new parking instructions, the nearest landmark, and one line on what has not changed: same team, same prices, same booking number. This is the message clients will actually open on the morning of their appointment, so keep it short enough to read in one glance.

The third message goes out on the morning of the first day in the new salon, with a photo of the new entrance or reception. It turns the move into an event: "We are open in our new home — come and see it."

## What every message must answer

A client reading your announcement is silently asking four questions, and every message should answer all four: where exactly, how do I get there, where do I park, and will my appointment still happen?

Where exactly means a WhatsApp location pin, not a typed address. Dubai and Abu Dhabi addresses confuse even long-time residents — a building name and a pin remove all doubt. Add one landmark sentence: "Next to the Spinneys, above the pharmacy." Landmarks are how people actually navigate here.

Parking is the question clients are too polite to ask twice, and the one that decides whether they arrive relaxed or flustered. State it plainly: free basement parking, validated mall parking for two hours, or street parking on the side road. If the new location has worse parking than the old one, say so honestly and give the workaround — a valet, a drop-off point, the nearest metro station.

And confirm what has not changed. Your regulars worry, quietly, that a new location means new prices, a new team, or a salon that has become too grand for them. One line settles it: "Same team, same prices, same number — just a brighter space."

## The bookings that straddle the move

The trickiest part is the diary. Some clients will have appointments booked at the old address for dates after the move. Message each of them personally — not with a broadcast — as soon as the new opening date is fixed. "Hi Sara, just to let you know your appointment on 10 December will now be at our new Al Barsha salon — I have sent you the pin. Everything else stays the same."

Personal messages matter here because a broadcast feels like an announcement, while a personal message feels like care.

If a client cannot reach the new location — she moved to the salon because it was next to her office, and now it is a twenty-minute drive — be honest and gracious. Offer her next two visits at the old price, help her find a good salon nearer her, or simply thank her. Some clients you will lose to distance no matter what you write, and a graceful goodbye keeps the door open if her circumstances change.

## The regulars who never reply

After the three messages, check who never opened or answered them. These are your at-risk regulars: the ones who will turn up at the old salon next month, find it closed, and assume you shut down. A short personal message a week after the move — "We have moved to Al Barsha and we miss you — shall I book your usual slot this Saturday?" — recovers more clients than any announcement ever will.

Pay special attention to clients who only ever booked by walking in. They have no WhatsApp thread with you, so they will never see the announcement. A printed card at the old door with the new address and a QR code that opens a WhatsApp chat catches them.

## Turn the move into a rebooking moment

A move is not just an admin event; it is the best natural reason to message every client you have. Use it. The announcement message can end with a soft invitation: "We would love to welcome you to the new space — shall I hold your usual Saturday slot for the opening week?" Clients who have been meaning to book suddenly have a reason to do it now.

The first week in the new salon is also the week to ask for the review and the referral. A client sitting in a bright new space is in a good mood, and a short WhatsApp message after her first visit — "Lovely to see you in our new home — if you have a moment, a Google review would mean the world" — lands better than the same message ever did at the old address.

Handled well, a move does not cost you regulars; it reminds them why they chose you. The salon they loved is the same people, the same care, in a better room — and the messages that told them so arrived in the chat where they already talk to you every week.
