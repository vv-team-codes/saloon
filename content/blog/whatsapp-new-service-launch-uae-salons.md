Every salon owner in the UAE knows the feeling: you research a new treatment, train the team, update the menu — and the diary stays empty. The service sits there for weeks, booked once or twice, while the chairs around it stay busy.

The problem is rarely the treatment itself. A new service needs a launch, and a quiet menu update is not a launch. Your clients cannot book something they do not know exists, and they will not try something they do not understand. In a market where most bookings start on WhatsApp, the launch should happen there too.

## Start with the clients it fits, not your whole list

The biggest mistake is telling everyone at once. A broadcast to the whole client list feels efficient, but most recipients are not the audience for this treatment, so they swipe past it — and the few who are see a message clearly sent to everybody.

Start by asking one question: who is this service actually for? A scalp ritual is for the client who complains about dryness in the air-conditioned months. A keratin treatment is for the client who fights frizz every morning. Go through your recent bookings and WhatsApp chats and make a short list of thirty to fifty clients who match. These are the people who get the launch first.

A message that speaks to her own hair or skin makes a client feel remembered, not marketed to — and that feeling is worth more than any discount.

## Tease before you announce

Two or three days before the launch, send the shortlist a short teaser: one message, one image, a hint of what is coming — a photo of the room being set up, or a line like "Something new for frizz-prone hair is arriving on Thursday — and our regulars get first pick of the slots."

A teaser creates curiosity, so the launch message is expected rather than random, and it shows you who is interested before you commit diary space: the clients who reply are your warmest leads. Keep it honest — never promise results you cannot deliver.

## The launch message that actually books

On launch day, send one clear message to the shortlist. It needs five things: what the treatment is, who it is for, how long it takes, the price or price range, and the slots available this week.

Here is the shape of it: a greeting by name, one sentence explaining the treatment, one sentence on who benefits most, then the practical details — "45 minutes, from AED 180, with Thursday 6 pm and Saturday 11 am open this week" — and a simple question: "Shall I hold one for you?" That final question turns news into a booking decision, and it is much easier to answer than "let us know if you are interested."

Send it in the early evening, when clients are scrolling after work — a midday message competes with work, an 8 pm message competes with nothing. And send it personally, not through a group or broadcast list that shows the client she is one of hundreds.

## Give the first week a shape

A launch without a deadline drifts. Decide in advance how the first week will work, and say so in the message. Perhaps the first ten bookings get a complimentary add-on, or you open four launch slots a day for the first week only. Scarcity only works when it is real, so do not invent limits — but a genuine first-week structure gives clients a reason to book now instead of "sometime."

A new treatment takes longer the first few times your team performs it, so leave honest buffers between the first appointments — nothing kills a launch faster than the first client waiting forty minutes while the stylist learns the timing.

## Ask the first clients for proof

The launch does not end when the first appointments are booked — it ends when the second wave books because the first wave told them to. After each of the first treatments, message the client that evening: how did it feel, and would she mind if you shared her before-and-after photo? A real photo from a real client is the strongest launch material you will ever have.

Keep the feedback in the same chat thread where the booking started — it becomes the template for your next launch. And if several clients say the same thing, listen and adjust before the second wave, not after it.

## What not to do

Do not send the launch to your entire list on day one. Do not discount heavily to force bookings — clients won on price expect that price forever. One gentle reminder two days later is enough for non-replies, and do not launch two new services at once.

Remember that WhatsApp is a conversation channel, not a billboard. If a client replies with a question, answer it — the launches that fill diaries feel like a conversation, not a campaign.

## A simple launch-week timeline

Put it together and the whole launch takes about ten days. Days one to three: pick the shortlist and prepare one good photo, the exact price and the timings. Day four: send the teaser. Day six: send the launch message to the shortlist in the early evening. Days seven to nine: fill the diary, answer questions personally, and protect the buffers. Day ten: follow up with the first clients, collect photos and feedback, and only then share the treatment with the wider list — now with real results to show.

It works because it respects how UAE clients book: they see something on WhatsApp in the evening, ask a question, get a straight answer, and book a slot that fits their week.
