It usually arrives on WhatsApp on a Tuesday evening, just after the school run. "Hi, can you host my daughter's birthday? Eight girls, ages nine to eleven. Next month. What can you do for them?" The parent has already looked at your Instagram and liked the idea. Now she is asking the only question that matters: can you make this work?

Most salons answer with a price and a date. That is a mistake. A pamper party is not one booking. It is eight small clients and eight sets of parents. Answer it well and you win a family.

## What parents are really asking

The words say birthday party. The worry underneath is different. The parent wants to know whether her daughter will feel special, whether the children will be safe and supervised, whether the salon can handle a group without the day falling apart, and whether the price will be clear before she commits.

So do not open with a price list. Open with reassurance and a question. "We love hosting birthday parties — the girls get their own pamper session and you get to relax. How many girls, what ages, and which Saturday were you thinking of?" That tells her you have done this before and are already planning her day.

## The five questions before you quote a price

Never quote a party price from the first message. You need five answers first, and each one changes the cost.

How many children, and what ages? Six seven-year-olds and ten fourteen-year-olds are two different parties. Younger children need shorter services and more supervision. Teenagers want real treatments and mirror time.

What does she imagine? Mini manicures and hair braiding is one party. A full hair wash, blow-dry and makeup trial is another. Ask her to describe the birthday girl's dream before you suggest anything.

Which day and what time? A Saturday afternoon is your busiest window and the hardest time to give a group eight chairs. A Friday morning or a weekday after school is often easier and calmer. Offer the quieter option honestly, and many parents will take it.

Will parents stay? Some drop off and collect. Others stay for the whole party. You need to know, because eight children plus eight adults will not fit in a salon built for six chairs.

Food and cake. Will she bring a cake? Does she expect you to provide drinks and snacks? Decide this now, not on the day.

Once you have the answers, your quote can be exact. "Eight girls, ages nine to eleven, Saturday 11 AM, two hours, mini manicure, hair braiding and a glitter finish each — AED 150 per child." Specific beats cheap every time.

## Pricing a pamper party honestly

The temptation is to price low to win the booking. Resist it. A party priced too low means rushed services, a stressed team and a birthday girl who can feel the hurry. Price it on what it really costs you: the chairs, the stylists, the products, the time the room is closed to other clients, and the clean-up afterwards.

Package it per child, not as one lump sum. Parents compare per-child prices easily, and it makes adding or dropping a guest simple. Include everything in that number — services, drinks and a small take-home gift — so there is nothing awkward to pay for at the end.

Take a deposit. A party is a big block of your diary, and a cancelled party a week before the date is a hole you cannot fill. A 50 percent deposit at booking, with the balance on the day, is fair and normal.

## Designing a party the diary can handle

A pamper party only works if the diary is designed for it. Eight children cannot all have a manicure at the same time in a salon with four nail stations. Plan the party in rotations: half the group starts on nails while the other half has hair braiding, then they swap, with the birthday girl first at every station. Nobody waits more than ten minutes.

Assign one stylist as the party lead. She greets the group, keeps the rotations moving, watches the clock and talks to the mother. Without a lead, everyone assumes someone else is watching the time, and the party runs over into your next booking.

Build in a thirty-minute clean-up buffer after the party, and block it in the diary the moment the deposit is paid. Keep the party in the quietest part of the salon if you can — a curtained corner gives the children their own world and gives your other clients their calm back.

## The grown-ups in the room

Children in a salon bring questions adult clients never raise. Answer them before the parent has to ask.

For children under twelve, ask for at least two adults to stay for the whole party. Parents expect this, and it protects your team.

Ask about allergies the day before, and use gentle, child-safe products for the youngest guests. Agree the photo rule in advance too: photos of the birthday girl and her friends only with each parent's permission, and no photos of other clients.

Finally, the birthday girl herself. She will remember whether she felt special. A small touch costs almost nothing: her name on a card at her chair, a flower in her hair, a few extra minutes on her braids.

## The follow-up that turns one party into a family

The party ends, the glitter is swept up, and the real value of the enquiry begins. Send the mother a message that evening, with a photo of the birthday girl if you have permission. "Thank you for choosing us — she looked wonderful today." A week later, offer the birthday girl a discounted blow-dry or manicure for her next visit.

Keep a note in your client records: the birthday girl's name, her birthday month, the friends who came. Next year, a message a month before the birthday — "Shall we book her party again?" — is the easiest rebooking you will ever make.

A birthday party enquiry is a family choosing your salon for the most important day in their child's year. Answer it with questions instead of a price list, price it honestly, design it so the diary survives, and follow up like you mean it.
