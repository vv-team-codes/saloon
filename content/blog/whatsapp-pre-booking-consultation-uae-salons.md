A client messages your salon on a Tuesday evening. She wants a balayage and asks for Saturday at 6 PM. Your front desk books her in. On Saturday the stylist learns the truth: henna six months ago, a home colour kit three weeks ago, hair never lightened. The balayage cannot be done safely in one sitting, the appointment runs an hour over, and the client leaves disappointed with a bill she did not expect.

That was not bad luck. It was a booking made without a consultation. In a UAE salon, where clients message on WhatsApp first, the cheapest fix is in the chat itself — a short WhatsApp consultation before the booking is confirmed. Five minutes of questions prevents most of the surprises that ruin a Saturday.

## Why the booking is where most visits go wrong

Most salon problems do not start in the chair. They start in the message thread, when a service is agreed before anyone knows what the client's hair or skin needs. A client asks for "highlights" when she means a full colour change. She books 45 minutes for a service that takes two hours.

The cost is not just one unhappy client. A wrong booking eats a chair that could have gone to the right client, pushes the next appointment late, and forces the stylist to disappoint the client or do work that should not be done. One wrong booking on a Saturday evening can spoil three appointments in a row.

## What a WhatsApp consultation actually is

A WhatsApp consultation is a short, structured exchange — four or five messages — between the first enquiry and the confirmed booking. Its only job is to answer one question: is this the right service, at the right time, for the right price?

It does not replace the in-chair check on the day. It simply makes sure the client arrives for the right service, with the right expectations, in a slot long enough to do the job properly. Use it for every new colour, correction, bridal, keratin or first-time treatment enquiry.

## The five questions that do the work

Keep the questions short and friendly, in the order a stylist would think.

First, what have you had done before? Ask about previous colour, keratin, henna, chemical straightening or lash work, and roughly when. Hair history is the biggest predictor of whether a service will work.

Second, what does it look like now? Ask for a photo in natural daylight, hair down, no filter. A photo in a dim bathroom tells you nothing; a photo by a window tells you almost everything.

Third, what do you want it to look like? An inspiration photo helps, but ask one follow-up: what do you like about this picture — the colour, the length, or the finish?

Fourth, is there anything we should know? Allergies, sensitive skin, pregnancy, or a previous reaction. One honest question protects the client and the salon. If the answer raises a flag, book a patch test or an in-person consultation instead.

Fifth, when do you need it ready by? A wedding on Saturday, a flight on Sunday, a work event on Monday — the deadline changes what is realistic. A transformation needing two visits cannot be squeezed into the night before a wedding.

## Turning the answers into a booking

The consultation only works if it ends in a clear booking, not a long chat. Once the stylist has seen the answers and the photo, the reply should name four things: the service, the time, the price range, and two specific slots. "That is a full balayage with a toner, about three hours, AED 450 to 550 depending on length. I have Thursday at 5 PM or Saturday at 11 AM — which suits you?" The client can say yes in one word.

If the answer is not what she hoped for, say so honestly and offer the closest alternative. "With henna in the hair, a full blonde lift is not safe in one sitting. We can do a gloss and a cut this week, then start lightening gradually — shall I book the gloss for Thursday?"

Keep the deposit in the same message, while the client is engaged. "We take a small deposit to hold the three-hour slot" — with a payment link — is far easier to accept now than tomorrow, and it protects the long slot you have carved out of a busy diary.

## When the answer is "not yet"

Some consultations should not end in a booking. A client with a recent reaction needs a patch test first. A correction from a bad colour elsewhere needs an in-person look before anyone quotes a price. A service the salon does not offer needs a kind referral, not a forced fit.

Salons worry that turning a client away loses the booking. In practice the opposite happens. The client told "let's do a patch test on Tuesday, then book you in properly" trusts the salon more than the one booked in blindly and disappointed. Write down what was decided — "patch test Tuesday, balayage after, henna history" — as a client note, so she never has to explain herself twice.

## Making it a habit

A WhatsApp consultation only works if the whole team does it the same way. One owner per shift answers new enquiries, and the first reply goes out within the hour during opening times. Save the questions as quick replies, and review them once a month.

The evening is the best time to run this well. Many UAE clients message after work, between 7 PM and 10 PM, when they are scrolling and planning their week. A consultation reply that arrives that evening, with a real slot attached, books the chair. One that waits until the next afternoon often finds the client has booked elsewhere.
