Every salon owner in the UAE knows the same Saturday story. The diary fills up by Thursday, the phone keeps ringing on Friday evening, and by Saturday morning you are turning people away. What stings most is not the walk-in you could not fit in. It is the regular — the client who has come every three weeks for two years — who messages on Friday night and finds nothing left.

She does not complain. She books somewhere else for that one Saturday, just this once — and sometimes, just this once becomes the new habit. Losing a regular to a full diary is one of the quietest ways a salon leaks revenue, because it never feels like a mistake.

Priority booking fixes this. Your most loyal clients get first pick of the busiest slots before those slots open to everyone else. Not a discount, not a free service, just early access. In a market where the best hours disappear fast, early access is genuinely valuable — and it costs you nothing to give.

## Why the best slots are the real reward

Salon owners often reach for discounts when they want to reward loyalty. A tenth visit free, twenty percent off a colour, a voucher on a birthday. Discounts work, but they train clients to wait for the next offer, and they eat margin every single time they are used.

Early access works differently. In the UAE, the most wanted salon hours are narrow and predictable: Saturday is the biggest day, followed by Thursday and Friday evenings, and the 5 pm to 9 pm window after work is gold. The days before Eid, National Day and wedding season are fought over weeks in advance. A regular who can lock in her Saturday 6 pm slot while everyone else is still deciding is getting something money cannot buy on the day: certainty.

That certainty is also what keeps her loyal. When a client knows her slot is safe, she stops shopping around and stops messaging three salons to see who has space. Loyalty, in practice, is often just the absence of friction.

## Who counts as a regular?

Priority booking only works if the line is clear. If everyone gets it, nobody values it, and the diary becomes a free-for-all again. Keep the rule simple and honest, and share it openly so clients understand what they are working towards.

A practical definition for most UAE salons: a client who has visited at least four times in the last six months, or who holds a standing appointment, or who has spent above a set threshold — say AED 1,500 — in the last quarter. You are rewarding frequency and consistency, not big one-off spending. Aim for priority status to cover roughly a fifth of your active client base — enough to fill the best slots reliably, and few enough that the status still feels special.

## How the window actually works

Open the priority window forty-eight hours before the general diary opens for a given week. The simplest version is a weekly rhythm: every Sunday evening, your priority clients receive a message with the following Saturday's available slots, and they can claim one before the diary opens to everyone on Tuesday.

Keep a small number of the very best slots back for the priority window — the Saturday late-afternoon and early-evening chairs, the Thursday evening colour slots, the pre-Eid appointments. A good balance is to hold back about a third of peak slots for priority clients and release the rest normally. One more rule: a priority slot that is not claimed by the time the general diary opens goes back into the open pool.

## The message that makes it feel special

How you tell clients about priority booking decides whether it feels like a perk or a policy. Never announce it as a rule change. Announce it as recognition.

Message the client personally, by name, on WhatsApp. "Aisha, you have been with us for over a year now, so from this week you get first pick of Saturday slots before they open to everyone." That one message tells her she has been noticed, gives her a concrete benefit, and gives her a reason to book now rather than later. Do the same for clients who are close to qualifying: "One more visit and you get first pick of Saturday slots" is a gentle nudge that turns an occasional client into a regular.

## What about new clients?

The worry every owner has is fair: if regulars take the best slots first, what is left for the new client who might become the next regular? The answer is that priority booking only touches a slice of the diary. Most of your week — weekday mornings, early afternoons, Sunday — stays open to everyone, and those are exactly the hours a new client can usually take.

In fact, the system gives you a better answer for new clients than "we are fully booked." Instead of a flat no, you can say: "Saturday is taken, but I have Thursday at 7 pm — and once you have visited a few times, you will get first pick of Saturdays too." That turns a refusal into an invitation, and it tells the new client exactly how the salon rewards loyalty.

## Start small, this week

You do not need software, a membership card or a new system to start. You need a list, a message and a habit. First, pull a list of your twenty most frequent clients from the last six months. Second, send them a personal WhatsApp message this week explaining that they now get first pick of the busiest slots. Third, set a weekly reminder — Sunday evening works well — to send the priority message with the following week's best slots before the diary opens to everyone.

Give it a month. Watch how many priority clients rebook within a week of their visit, and how many Saturday slots are filled by Wednesday instead of Friday. If both numbers rise, the system is paying for itself.

The most loyal clients a salon has are the ones who never ask for anything. Priority booking is how you give them something anyway, before they ever think of looking elsewhere.
