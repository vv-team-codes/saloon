It usually arrives in the evening: a WhatsApp message, a photo of a glossy balayage or a sharp layered cut, and five small words — "Can you do this for me?" Sometimes a follow-up — "How much?", "Are you free tomorrow?" — but often the photo is the whole enquiry.

For salons across Dubai, Abu Dhabi and Sharjah, this is now one of the most common ways a new client makes first contact — and one of the easiest to mishandle. A quick "Yes, come in" sets up a disappointed client; a slow reply sends her to the salon down the road; a blunt "No" loses someone who might have become a regular with a slightly different service.

Handling photo enquiries well deserves a proper place in your enquiry system.

## Why a photo enquiry is different from a price enquiry

A price enquiry is simple: the client wants a number. A photo enquiry is a bundle of unspoken questions. She is really asking four things at once: can you achieve this look, will it suit me, how much will it cost, and can I trust you with my hair?

So a one-word answer fails both ways: "Yes" creates an expectation the stylist must meet in the chair, while "No" closes a conversation that could have led somewhere good. Treat the photo as the start of a short consultation, not a booking to rubber-stamp.

Photo enquiries also deserve care because UAE clients bring dozens of hair backgrounds — different textures, previous colour, and heat damage from years in a hot, humid climate. The same photo can mean very different work on different heads of hair. A salon that asks smart questions first looks professional; one that does not looks careless, however good the stylist.

## The five questions to ask before you promise anything

You do not need a long interrogation. Five short questions, asked warmly, cover almost everything:

**1. Is this your hair now, or the goal?** Some send a photo of their own hair; others send a celebrity. Knowing which changes everything.

**2. What has your hair been through recently?** Colour, bleach, keratin, henna and extensions all change what is possible and safe. One message — "Has your hair been coloured or treated lately?" — prevents most chair-side surprises.

**3. How do you normally style it?** A cut that looks effortless in a studio photo may need forty minutes of styling each morning. A wash-and-go client needs to know that before she books, not after.

**4. When do you need it?** A full colour transformation the day before a wedding differs completely from the same service with two weeks to spare, and timing questions let you steer urgent clients toward realistic options.

**5. What do you like most about the photo?** The colour, the length, the volume, the fringe? Clients often love one element and would happily compromise on the rest — finding it gives your stylist room to adapt honestly.

Notice none of these questions is about price yet — it lands better once the client feels understood.

## How to say "not quite" without losing the client

This is where trust is built: replace the look, don't just refuse it.

Instead of "That won't work on your hair," try this structure: acknowledge, explain briefly, offer the closest beautiful alternative. For example: "That soft caramel blend is gorgeous. With your current dark base we would need two sessions to get there safely — but we can get you a rich chocolate melt in one visit, and build toward the lighter tones over your next two appointments." The client hears honesty, a plan, and a reason to return.

Photos also hide reality: studio lighting, filters and extensions make reference images more dramatic than real hair usually is. Naming that is not making excuses; it is expertise. Clients respect a salon that protects their hair more than one that promises anything to fill a chair.

When the answer genuinely is no, say so early and kindly, paired with something you can do. A client who leaves with a plan books; one who leaves with a flat refusal books elsewhere.

## From photo to chair: closing the enquiry properly

Once the questions are answered, end with three concrete things: a named service, a price range, and a time. "A layered cut with face-framing highlights, usually AED 250 to 350 depending on length — we have Thursday at 6:30 pm or Saturday at 11 am, shall I hold one?" That is a booking. "Yes, we can do it, call us to book" is not.

Most of these messages arrive in the evening in the UAE, often after closing. That is where many enquiries quietly die: a photo sent at 10 pm that waits until noon the next day has usually already been answered by a competitor. ## The mistakes that cost salons photo clients

A few patterns show up again and again. The first is quoting a price from the photo alone, then doubling it in the chair. The client feels tricked, even if the higher price was fair. Always give a range and explain what moves it.

The second is ignoring the photo. "What service would you like to book?" in reply to a carefully chosen photo tells the client nobody looked. Even "Beautiful choice, that warm blonde would suit you" changes the tone of everything after.

The third is letting the conversation drift. Enquiries left in chat for days with no appointment offered cool off fast. A client who sent a photo is already imagining the result — offer the appointment while that excitement is fresh.

## A simple system any salon can adopt

You do not need expensive software — just a habit. Save a reply template that thanks the client, names what you like about the photo, and asks the five questions. Keep your own before-and-after album to show what your team has achieved on similar hair. Reply within the hour during opening times and acknowledge automatically outside them.

Done well, a photo enquiry is more than a lead: it is a client telling you exactly what she wants before she has walked in. Treat it as a mini-consultation — curious, honest and quick — and more of those photos become filled chairs and returning clients.
