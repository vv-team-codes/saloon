Every UAE salon owner knows the message. It arrives ten minutes before the appointment, sometimes five minutes after it: "I'm outside." Sometimes it comes with a photo of a car park. Sometimes it is just "here". And in that small moment, the whole tone of the visit is decided.

Most salons treat it as an interruption — something to answer between clients, quickly, while holding a pair of scissors. But the calmest salons in Dubai and Abu Dhabi treat it as part of the service: the first moment of the appointment, and handling it well protects your diary, your team and your client's mood.

## The arrival message is a question, not an interruption

What the client is really saying is not just where she is. She is asking three quiet questions: am I in the right place, am I on time, and is someone expecting me? A fast, warm reply answers all three at once. A slow or missing reply answers them badly.

In a city where clients drive between mall parking levels, valet stands and high-street shopfronts, "outside" can mean three different places. A client circling a mall car park while her appointment time ticks away arrives stressed, and a stressed client is harder to please. The salons that get this right reply within a minute or two, every time, because they decided in advance who answers arrival messages and what the reply says.

This matters after hours too. A client messaging at 9 pm to ask where to park for a 10 am appointment tomorrow should not wait until morning for an answer. ## Answer the three questions before they ask

The best arrival replies are short and specific: exactly where to go, what to do when she gets there, and how long the wait will be. A message that says "Come in, we're on the first floor next to the pharmacy, ask for Sara at reception" is worth ten messages that say "ok".

Build your reply around three things: the entrance (which door, which floor, which landmark), parking (where to park, whether there is valet, what it costs), and the plan (come straight in, or wait five minutes while the chair is finished). Save those answers as a reply anyone on the team can send in seconds. If your salon is inside a mall, name the nearest entrance and the parking level, not just the mall name. If valet is free for the first hour, say so — clients remember the salon that made arrival easy, and they tell their friends about it.

## Early clients: turn waiting time into welcome time

In the UAE, clients often arrive early — sometimes twenty minutes early — because traffic is unpredictable and nobody wants to be late. An early client standing at the door while the team is mid-service is an awkward moment, and it usually ends with her waiting on a chair by the entrance, watching the clock.

A better response is a WhatsApp message that turns the wait into part of the experience. "You're early — welcome! Come in, there's a seat by the window and a karak on its way. Your stylist will be with you in about ten minutes." That one message welcomes her, gives her somewhere comfortable to be, and sets a realistic time so she is not wondering. Early clients who feel welcomed are far more likely to book again.

## Late clients: reply with a decision, not a question

The other side of the arrival message is the late one: "Sorry, stuck in traffic, five minutes away." This is where most salons lose money quietly. The stylist waits, the next client is kept waiting, and the whole evening diary slips by fifteen minutes.

Reply fast, and reply with a decision. "No problem at all — come straight in when you arrive. We can still do the full cut, and we'll finish by 7:15." That keeps the client, protects the next booking, and removes the guesswork. If the delay is longer and the full service no longer fits, say so kindly and offer the honest alternative: a shorter version today, or the full service at the next free slot. The key is that the client hears from you before she arrives — a client who has already been told what will happen walks in relaxed.

## The chair-ready message: your quiet queue manager

The most useful arrival message is not the one the client sends — it is the one you send her. A short "Your chair is ready, come on in" message, sent the moment the previous client leaves, is the simplest queue system a salon can run. It removes the two things that make waiting unpleasant: uncertainty and standing around.

Set a simple rule: the stylist signals the moment the chair is free, and the person on the desk sends the message within a minute. Do it for every appointment, and clients will start to expect it. When clients trust you will tell them when you are ready, they stop crowding the entrance, and the whole day runs calmer.

## A simple system the whole team can run

None of this works if it depends on one person remembering to reply. First, agree on who answers: one person owns arrival messages on every shift. Second, save four short replies: the parking and entrance directions, the early-client welcome, the late-client reassurance, and the chair-ready message. Third, set a reply-time rule: arrival messages are answered within two minutes, ahead of every other message in the inbox.

Fourth, keep the diary visible to whoever is answering. And finally, check it at the end of the day: did anyone wait without hearing from us? If yes, fix it tomorrow.

## The small details clients remember

Clients rarely remember the exact shade of a colour or the precise length of a cut. They remember how the visit felt — and the visit starts before the chair, with the message that says "we're expecting you, here's where to park, come in when you're ready." Get the arrival message right and the rest of the visit gets easier: the client walks in calm, the stylist starts on time, and the next client is not kept waiting. It costs nothing but a little planning: four saved replies, one person owning the inbox, and a chair-ready message sent on time.
