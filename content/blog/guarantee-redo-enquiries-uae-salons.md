It arrives on WhatsApp at 9:40 on a Tuesday night. "If my gel nails chip in a few days, will you fix them for free?" Or it comes before a first colour booking: "If the colour fades too fast, do you redo it?" It is one of the most awkward questions a UAE salon receives, because it sits right on the line between confidence and liability. Answer it well and the client books — because the question is not really about a free fix. It is about trust.

Most clients who ask about guarantees have been burned before. They paid full price somewhere else, the manicure lifted on day three, the balayage turned brassy in a week, and the salon shrugged. They are checking whether your salon stands behind its work before they hand over their money and their hair. Treat it as a buying signal, not an accusation, and your whole reply changes.

## What clients are really asking

Behind "will you fix it for free?" there are usually three quieter questions. The first is about quality: does this salon believe its own work lasts? The second is about fairness: if something goes wrong that is not my fault, will I have to pay twice? The third is about what happens next: if I come back with a problem, will I be treated well?

A reply that explains what is covered, for how long, and what the client should do if something goes wrong answers all three at once. That is why salons that handle this enquiry well have a written redo policy, even five lines long — so whoever answers WhatsApp at 10 pm does not invent an answer under pressure.

## A redo policy clients actually believe

The strongest policies share four features. First, a clear time window. Seven days for gel nails and lash lifts, ten to fourteen days for colour, works for most salons because it matches how long a good result should genuinely last. Second, a clear scope: the redo covers the same service, done by the salon, when the result has not held as it should. Third, honest conditions. A gel manicure that chipped because the client spent the weekend scrubbing a villa with no gloves is not the same as one that lifted on day two, and clients accept that when it is explained kindly.

Fourth, a clear next step. "Come back within seven days and we will fix it in the chair, no charge" is a promise a client can act on. "We will see what we can do" is not. Print the window on the receipt, mention it at the mirror, and repeat it in the WhatsApp confirmation. When the client already knows the policy, the question often never arrives at all.

## The four-part reply that turns the question into a booking

When the enquiry does arrive, a short structured reply works far better than a long defensive one. Start by welcoming the question. "Great question, and yes, we stand behind our work" costs nothing and instantly lowers the tension. Then state the policy plainly, in one sentence: "If your gel nails chip or lift within seven days, we fix them free of charge." Then add the condition and the next step in the same breath: "Just message us a photo and we will book you a fifteen-minute fix slot, usually the same day or the next."

Finally, close with the booking. "Would you like Thursday at 6 pm or Saturday at 11 am for the full set?" You removed the risk, showed her what happens if things go wrong, and gave her two real slots. That is a complete answer — and a sale.

## What never to promise

A guarantee enquiry is also the moment to avoid promises you cannot keep. Never promise a lifetime guarantee, a fix for work done by another salon, or a refund on the spot without seeing the result first. Each sounds generous in a message and becomes a problem the first time a client holds you to it. "We will fix our own work, free, within seven days" is strong precisely because it has edges. Clients trust a promise with edges more than a promise with none.

This enquiry often arrives alongside another one: "Can you fix what my old salon did?" That is a different question from "will you fix your own work?", and it deserves a different answer: a consultation first, an honest assessment, and a price for the correction. Mixing the two up is how a free redo policy quietly becomes free correction work for someone else's mistakes.

## When the fix is not free — and how to say so

Not every redo should be free, and clients know it. The rule that works is simple: if the salon's work did not hold as it should, the fix is free. If the client's choices changed the result — a different colour request a week later, damage from home products, a service the client asked for against advice — the fix is charged, but fairly, and the reason is explained before the work starts.

The worst version of this conversation happens at the till, after the fix is done, when the client expected it free and the salon expected to charge. The best version happens in the first message, before the booking. That is the real value of answering well: the awkward conversation never happens in the chair, in front of a full salon, on a Saturday evening.

## Make the guarantee work while you sleep

Guarantee questions arrive at all hours, and the 10 pm ones are often the most serious — a client who has just noticed a chip, or who is comparing three salons before booking tomorrow. A clear policy written down once, in the same words everywhere, means the enquiry gets a confident answer even when the owner is not replying. Keep it in your quick replies, on your service menu, and in the confirmation after every appointment.

A salon that answers with a calm, specific yes earns something no discount can buy: a client who books knowing she is safe. She will tell her friends, and she will come back — because the salon that stands behind its work is the one she trusts next time too.
