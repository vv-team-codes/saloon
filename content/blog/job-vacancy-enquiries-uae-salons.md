Every salon owner in the UAE knows the message. It arrives on the same WhatsApp number as your bookings, usually at the busiest moment of the day: "Hi, are you hiring? I am a hairdresser with five years' experience." Sometimes it is a voice note. Sometimes it is a CV in a PDF, sent at 11 PM. Sometimes it is three messages in a row from someone who has clearly messaged every salon in the building.

They are not bookings, and they are not spam either. Ignore them and you lose a good stylist you might need next month. Answer them badly and you waste twenty minutes of a shift. You do not need a human resources department to handle them well — just one agreed reply, one place to keep the details, and a rule that no job enquiry steals time from a paying client.

## Why job enquiries land on your booking number

In the UAE, a salon's WhatsApp number is its front door — on Google Maps, on Instagram, on the shop window and on the mall directory. A stylist looking for work does exactly what a client does: she searches for a salon she likes and taps the WhatsApp button. She does not know you have a separate email for careers, because most salons do not, and she does not know your receptionist is in the middle of a Saturday rush. All she sees is a green tick and a reply that never comes.

## The quiet cost of ignoring them

The most common response to a job enquiry is no response at all. The message is read, the receptionist means to reply later, and the day takes over. By evening it has slid down the chat list, and by the next morning it is buried under new bookings. The salon loses nothing visible, so the habit continues.

But there are two quiet costs. The first is talent. Good stylists are hard to find, and they are usually found through exactly these messages. A salon that replies to everyone keeps a warm list of people it can call when a stylist resigns, goes on leave, or when the diary needs a second pair of hands for party season. A salon that ignores everyone starts from zero every time it needs to hire, and pays for it in empty chairs. The second cost is reputation. The person who messaged you about a job is also a beauty consumer, with friends and colleagues who ask her where to get their hair done. A salon that leaves her on read has lost a potential advocate before she ever sat in a chair.

## A reply that takes thirty seconds

You need one agreed reply that any team member can send, and a rule that it goes out the same day. Something like this works well: "Thank you for getting in touch. We are not hiring right now, but we would love to keep your details. Could you send your CV and a few photos of your work to this number? We will message you if a chair opens up."

If you are hiring, the reply is even simpler: "Thank you for messaging us. Yes, we are looking for a [role]. Could you send your CV and a few photos of your work? Our manager will message you within two days to arrange a trial." Notice the promise of a timeframe. A candidate who hears nothing for two weeks assumes the answer is no, and by then she has taken another job. If you give a timeframe, keep it.

## Keep a simple talent file

The reply only helps if the details go somewhere, so keep a simple talent file — a folder on one phone, or a labelled chat in your salon's WhatsApp. The habit is this: when a job enquiry arrives, save the CV and the work photos in the folder, and write one line next to the name. "Aisha — colourist, six years, strong balayage photos, lives in Al Barsha." "Ravi — barber, four years, wants evenings only." That one line is what makes the file useful. When a stylist hands in her notice on a Tuesday, you do not need to post an advert and wait. You open the file, and you already have three names. Review it once a month and delete anyone who has stopped replying.

## When you actually are hiring

When a chair is open, the temptation is to interview everyone. Resist it. The same discipline you use for client enquiries applies here: qualify first, then commit time.

Ask for three things before any interview: a CV, three or four photos of real work on real clients, and one line about why they want to leave their current salon. The photos matter most. A CV can be polished; a photo of a lived-in colour or a clean fade cannot. If the work is not at your standard, say so kindly and move on. "Thank you for sending your work. We are looking for someone with more experience in [service] right now, but we will keep your details." That is a complete answer, and it costs you one minute.

## When you are not hiring

Most of the time, you are not hiring, and that is when the system matters most. The reply should be honest and warm, and it should never pretend a job might exist when it does not. "We will keep your CV on file" is only fair if you actually keep it on file. If you do not, say so plainly: "Thank you for thinking of us. We are not hiring at the moment and we do not keep a waiting list, but we wish you well."

## Keep the booking line for bookings

There is one risk to watch. If job enquiries crowd out client messages, your booking line is doing two jobs and doing both badly. The fix is small. Add one line to your WhatsApp greeting or Google profile: "For job enquiries, please message us with the word JOBS and attach your CV." It does not stop the messages, but it makes them instantly recognisable, so the receptionist can file them in seconds and get back to the client who wants a Saturday appointment.
