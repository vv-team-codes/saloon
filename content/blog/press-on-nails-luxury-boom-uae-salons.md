It is 9 in the evening and a message lands on your salon's WhatsApp: "I have a wedding tomorrow and my nails are a disaster. Can you fit me in tonight?" The diary is full and a full set of extensions takes two hours you do not have. A year ago, that enquiry ended with an apology. Today, more UAE salons answer differently: a custom-fit set of luxury press-on nails, applied in twenty minutes, that looks like it took two hours.

Press-ons used to be cheap, one-size-fits-all plastic nails. The new generation is hand-painted, shaped and sized to the individual client, made from quality gel or acrylic, and designed to be worn for a week or two — or removed and reused. For a UAE salon, they fill a gap no other nail service fills: beautiful nails, tonight, without a two-hour appointment.

## What luxury press-ons actually are

A luxury press-on set starts with measurement, not a box. The salon measures the exact width of each of the client's ten nails, in person or from a sizing kit she uses at home, then builds the set on those measurements — hand-painted, with chrome, French tips, 3D details or the quiet, clean finish popular here.

The client wears them with adhesive tabs for a day or two, or with nail glue for one to two weeks. Good sets can be soaked off, cleaned and worn again. A full set of builder-gel extensions is a two-hour, premium appointment; a regular manicure is quick but plain. Press-ons offer the look of the first at something close to the time and price of the second.

## Why the UAE is ready for them

Three things about the UAE market make press-ons a natural fit. The first is the events calendar. Dubai and Abu Dhabi run on weddings, gala dinners and photoshoots, most of them decided at short notice. A client who finds out on Thursday that she is attending a wedding on Saturday has no time for a long appointment — but she has twenty minutes for a press-on fitting.

The second is the travelling client. UAE residents fly constantly, and press-ons are the easiest beauty fix for a trip: no drying time, no appointment before the flight, and a set that can be applied in the hotel room. Salons already sell take-home sets the day before clients travel, and that sale takes five minutes at the till.

The third is the sheer number of clients who want beautiful nails but cannot commit to extensions — because of work, weak nails, or simply not wanting to sit still for two hours. Press-ons give all of them the look for the weekend, to take off on Sunday night.

## Three ways salons are selling them

The salons doing this well offer press-ons in three forms. The first is in-salon application: the client books a 20 to 30 minute slot, the technician fits and applies a ready-made set from the salon's display, and she leaves with finished nails. The slot is short, the product cost is low, and the chair turns over quickly — and it fills the awkward half hour between a colour appointment and a blow-dry that would otherwise sit empty.

The second is the custom order. The client sends her nail measurements and a photo of the design she wants, and the salon makes the set over a few days. She collects it or has it delivered, and applies it herself with the kit provided. Custom orders cost more because they are one of one, and they keep earning: the same client orders a new set for every big occasion, and often a second set as a gift.

The third is the ready-to-wear display: a small stand near the till with finished sets in popular sizes, so clients who came in for a blow-dry pick up a set for the weekend. It costs almost nothing to run, and it advertises the service to everyone who walks past.

## Pricing them honestly

Press-ons fail when salons price them like a cheap afterthought or like a full extension set. The honest middle is what works: in-salon application of a ready set is typically priced around a premium manicure, while a custom hand-painted set costs more — it carries hours of a technician's work and a design no one else owns. A reusable set is worth more than a single-use one.

Say what the price includes: a set that lasts one to two weeks with glue and can be reused is a different product from a set held on with tabs for one evening. Clients who feel they were told the truth come back. Clients who were promised three weeks and got three days do not.

## The diary advantage nobody talks about

For the salon owner, the quietest benefit is what press-ons do to the diary. A technician who completes a press-on application in twenty minutes can serve three clients in the hour one extension set would take — and that matters most on the UAE's busiest evenings.

Press-ons also bring in clients who would never book a nail appointment — the blow-dry regular trying a twenty-minute set, the bride adding nails because it takes twenty minutes, not two hours. Each is a new nail client who may come back for the full menu.

## What to get right before you start

First, the sizing has to be right. A press-on a millimetre too wide looks wrong in a photograph and feels wrong on the hand, and one bad fit costs you the client's trust. Measure properly, and keep a sizing card on file for every custom-set client.

Second, do not oversell how long they last: glue-on sets last a week or two with care, tabs a day or two. Say that plainly, in writing, at the point of sale.

Third, train one person to own it. Press-ons look simple, and that is exactly why they go wrong when everyone dabbles and nobody is responsible.
