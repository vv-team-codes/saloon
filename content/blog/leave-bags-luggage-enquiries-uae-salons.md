It is one of the smallest questions a salon ever receives, and one of the most revealing. A client messages on WhatsApp: "Hi, I'm coming straight from the mall — can I leave my shopping bags with you while I get my hair done?" Or she arrives at the door pulling a cabin suitcase behind her, a flight to catch in four hours.

The answer matters more than it looks. She is not really asking about a shelf. She is asking whether she can relax and whether her things will be safe — and whether asking makes her look fussy. Get the answer right and you win a booking, and often a regular. Hesitate, and she books somewhere that sounds more certain.

## Why this question comes up so often in the UAE

The pattern is very local. Many UAE salons sit inside malls or on high streets next to them, so clients arrive mid-errand, carrying shopping bags, a gym kit, or a child's school bag. Others are hotel guests who checked out in the morning and have a flight in the evening, with a suitcase they cannot leave anywhere else.

Clients arrive carrying their day with them. A salon that has thought about where those things go feels organised. A salon that has not shows it at once.

## The four-part reply that books the appointment

The best replies follow the same shape, on WhatsApp, on the phone, and at the door.

First, a warm yes with a boundary. "Yes, of course — we have a spot for your bags." A yes without a boundary sounds vague; a boundary without a yes sounds like a refusal. Together they sound confident.

Second, name the actual place. "We have a locked cabinet behind reception for handbags, and a shelf in the staff room for suitcases and shopping bags." Specificity turns a polite answer into a trustworthy one.

Third, give the valuables advice before she has to ask. "We'd suggest keeping your passport, phone and wallet with you in the chair — there's a small tray by the mirror for them." This is honest, it protects the salon, and clients read it as care rather than caution.

Fourth, close with the booking, not the bag. "So shall I hold the 6.30 slot for you on Thursday? You can come straight from the mall." The bag was never the point. The appointment is.

## Offer what you honestly have

A locked cabinet or a cupboard with a key is the gold standard, and it is worth having one even in a small salon — a single lockable drawer behind reception covers most handbags and laptop bags. A staff-room shelf works for suitcases and shopping bags, as long as it is out of the walkway and not blocking a fire exit.

Never promise more than you have. Do not say bags are "in a locked room" when they are on an open shelf, and do not say "we watch them all the time" when the receptionist steps away for an hour. Clients forgive a modest setup. They do not forgive discovering the truth mid-appointment.

Keep the offer consistent across the team, too. If one stylist says yes to a suitcase and another says the salon cannot take luggage, the client hears a salon that does not know its own rules. Agree the answer once and write it into your quick replies.

## State the limits kindly, at booking time

Every salon needs a limit, and it should be stated before the client arrives, not after something goes wrong: the salon can look after bags with reasonable care, but it cannot take responsibility for valuables left unattended. The kind version belongs in the booking message, not on a sign at the door. "Just a quick note — we have safe storage for your bags, and we recommend keeping passports, phones and jewellery with you during your visit." Said at booking time, it reads as helpful.

The same applies to oversized items. If a client asks about two large suitcases and a pram, be honest about what fits. "We can take one suitcase comfortably — the second one would block the walkway." A client who hears an honest limit before she arrives will plan around it.

## Three small habits that make it effortless

Label the bag. A tag with the client's name and appointment time takes five seconds and removes every moment of doubt at the end of the visit, when three similar black tote bags sit in a row.

Give the job to one person per shift. Whoever is on reception owns the bags for that shift — takes them, labels them, and returns them. When everyone owns it, nobody does, and bags get forgotten in corners.

Do the return check at checkout. Before the client pays, the receptionist asks, "Have you got everything with you?" It catches the forgotten shopping bag before the client is halfway to the car park, and it is the last impression the client takes home.

## Turning a yes into a booking

A confident answer to the bags question is a marketing asset. Clients who have to plan their whole day around an appointment book less often. Clients who know the salon will look after their things book more easily — including the awkward flight-day and lunch-break slots other salons lose.

It also belongs in your listing. A single line in your Google Business Profile or Instagram bio — "bags and luggage welcome" — answers the question before it is asked, and it is the kind of detail that makes a new client choose you over the salon next door.
