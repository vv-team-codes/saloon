A regular client looks at the finished glam in the mirror and says something you probably hear more often than you used to: "Can you show me how you did that?" She does not want another booking. She wants the skill.

Across the UAE, salons are answering with a new service: the one-to-one makeup lesson. The client books an hour with an artist, learns a look on her own face, and leaves with a face chart and the confidence to recreate it.

## Why clients want to learn, not just be done

First, occasions have multiplied. A Dubai resident might attend a wedding, a work dinner and a brunch in a single weekend, and booking full glam for every event is expensive. Clients want one look they can do themselves in twenty minutes.

Second, new residents arrive every month with a drawer full of products bought in another country and no idea which survive the UAE climate. A lesson that sorts her existing kit, keeps what works and replaces what melts by midday is worth more than a new palette she will never use.

Third, brides have changed the brief. A bride still books her wedding-day makeup, but she also wants to do her own touch-ups between the ceremony and the reception. Salons that teach the bride before the wedding become the salon she trusts afterwards.

## What a lesson actually looks like

A good lesson is not a makeover with commentary. It is a working session, usually 60 to 90 minutes, and it follows a simple shape.

It starts with a kit audit. The artist empties the client's makeup bag onto the counter and sorts it into three piles: keep, replace and stop using. This takes ten minutes.

Then the artist does one side of the face and the client does the other, step by step. Each step is demonstrated once, then repeated by the client while the artist corrects the hand. The client holds the brush the whole time. If the artist does the whole face, it was a makeover, not a lesson.

The session ends with two things the client takes home: a face chart with the steps in order, and a short product list with prices. Photos of each stage, taken on the client's own phone, make the lesson easy to repeat at home.

Group lessons work too, especially on quiet weekday mornings. Three or four clients, one artist, one look — a soft evening eye or a five-minute office face.

## Pricing it without undercutting your glam services

The most common mistake is pricing a lesson like a discount makeover. A lesson is a different product: the client pays for knowledge and a repeatable skill she will use dozens of times. Price it between a consultation and a full glam service.

In practice, UAE salons charge roughly the price of a blow-dry and a brow shape for a one-to-one session, and a little less per person for a group of three or four. The lesson itself does not need to be the profit centre. The profit comes from what follows: the two or three products the client buys because she has just used them on her own face, and the next booking she makes because she now trusts your artist.

Bundle the lesson with a small kit and the numbers improve further. A starter set — the foundation, the brush and the lip colour from the lesson — sold at the chair while the client can still feel the result on her skin converts far better than the same products on a shelf. Keep the bundle to three items. One rule protects the rest of your menu: never discount a full glam booking to push a lesson, and never let a lesson run into a glam slot. Lessons belong in quiet hours, when chairs would otherwise sit empty.

## Who books lessons — and when

Four kinds of clients book first. Brides and bridesmaids want touch-up skills and a honeymoon look. New residents want a kit sorted and a routine that survives humidity and air conditioning. Busy professionals want a five-minute face for early meetings.

Timing matters. December party season is the strongest window: clients want to look good at three events a week and cannot book glam for all of them. The weeks before Ramadan are another, as women plan their Eid looks. Promote lessons four weeks ahead of those peaks, not during them.

## Turning one lesson into a regular

A lesson is a first visit with a difference — the client leaves feeling capable, not just pretty. That feeling is what brings her back, if the salon follows up. The follow-up is a single WhatsApp message the next evening: a photo of her face chart, one tip for the step she found hardest, and an invitation to book a refresh lesson in six weeks.

Refresh lessons are the retention engine. A seasonal refresh — a lighter summer base, a deeper winter lip — gives the client a reason to return every few months.

The lesson also feeds the rest of the menu. A client who has learned her base will book a facial to keep the skin underneath it healthy. A client who has learned her eye look will book a lash lift so mornings get easier.

## How to start this week

You do not need a new room, new stock or a launch campaign. Pick your strongest makeup artist and one quiet morning slot. Write a short description — what the client learns, how long it takes, what she takes home — and put it on your WhatsApp catalogue and your booking page. Offer the first five lessons to regulars you know by name; their feedback will shape the format faster than any planning meeting.

Set two rules from the start. First, the client always holds the brush. Second, the artist always ends with the face chart and the product list, written down.
