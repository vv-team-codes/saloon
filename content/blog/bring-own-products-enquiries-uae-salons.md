It arrives on WhatsApp late at night, usually in one line: "Can I bring my own hair colour?" Sometimes it is a box of colour bought on holiday, sometimes a shampoo from a supply shop, or a bottle of keratin a friend recommended. The client is not trying to be difficult. She has a reason — an allergy, a favourite brand, a tight budget — and she is asking before she books, which is exactly the kind of enquiry a UAE salon wants to hear.

And yet most stylists groan when they read it. Say yes without thinking and you take responsibility for a product you did not choose and cannot guarantee. Say no too bluntly and a client who was ready to book walks away. The answer matters, and it is worth having one ready.

## Why clients ask in the first place

Most clients who ask fall into one of four groups.

The first group has sensitive skin or a past reaction. A client who once had an itchy scalp after a colour service is not trying to save money. She is trying to feel safe, and the product she trusts is the one that never hurt her. The second group is loyal to a brand she has used at home for years and wants the professional result with the product she already knows.

The third group is watching the budget and assumes supplying her own colour will cut the price. The fourth group simply does not know how salons work — she may be new to Dubai or new to professional colour. None of these clients is wrong for asking.

## The honest reasons salons hesitate

A good reply starts with being clear, inside the salon, about why the answer is usually no. Professional colour is not the same as the box on a supermarket shelf. The colour a stylist mixes is chosen for that client's hair: its current shade, its condition, the result she wants, and what has been done to it before. A box colour bought abroad may be a different strength or tone, or made for hair that has never been coloured. Put it on the wrong base and the result can be patchy or brassy — and the client will blame the hands that applied it, not the product she brought.

Then there is responsibility. If a client brings her own product and has a reaction, whose product caused it? The salon cannot check the batch, the storage, or whether the box is genuine. In a busy Dubai salon, a stylist also cannot spend twenty minutes researching an unfamiliar product before starting.

None of this means the answer must always be no. It means the answer should be honest and consistent, with a real alternative attached.

## A reply that keeps the booking

The best replies have the same shape: acknowledge the reason, explain the policy in one sentence, and offer the next step. No long apology, no lecture, and no leaving the client with nothing to do next.

For a client with a sensitivity concern: "Thank you for letting us know — we take that seriously. We do not use products brought from outside because we cannot guarantee their safety or the result, but bring the product to a free consultation and we will check its ingredients together and find a professional product that suits you." She hears care, not rejection, and has a clear next step.

For a budget-driven enquiry, honesty about pricing works better than a discount: "Our colour price covers the consultation, application and finish, so bringing your own colour does not change the price much. We can show you options at different price points, so you get the result you want within your budget." Many clients are surprised to learn the product is a small part of the price, and that conversation alone often closes the booking.

For a brand-loyal client, check whether the salon can order or stock that brand. If the product is professional-grade and the salon can source it, saying yes to the brand while keeping the application in the stylist's hands is a genuine win. If not, suggest the closest professional equivalent and offer a patch test first.

## When saying yes is actually fine

There are situations where accepting a client's own product is perfectly reasonable, and a salon that says yes gracefully earns loyalty. A client who brings her own shampoo because of a medical condition is an easy yes — the product touches the hair for minutes, and the stylist controls everything else. A client who brings a styling product she loves for the blow-dry is another easy yes.

The line is usually drawn at chemical services: colour, bleach, keratin, and perms. Those are the services where the product decides the outcome, and where the salon's guarantee would be meaningless if the product came from outside. Having that line written down stops the awkward moment where one stylist says yes and another says no to the same client.

## What to do when the client pushes back

Some clients will push. "The salon near my office lets me bring my own colour." The temptation is to argue, or to give in to keep the booking. Neither helps. A calm reply works better: "Every salon has its own policy, and ours is to use products we know and can guarantee, so we can stand behind your result." Clients respect a salon that stands by its standards.

If the client still insists, let her go kindly. "You are welcome to come for a cut or a blow-dry any time, and if you ever want to try our colour, we would love to have you." A graceful no keeps the door open. A resentful yes closes it.

## Turn the question into a consultation

The smartest salons treat this enquiry as a gift. A client who asks about bringing her own product is thinking carefully about her hair, her skin, and her money. The salon that answers her well — quickly, honestly, and with a real alternative — earns a kind of trust that a discount never buys.

A clear answer, given fast and kindly, turns a tricky enquiry into a booked chair.
