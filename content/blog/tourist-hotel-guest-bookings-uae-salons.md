Every year, millions of visitors arrive in Dubai and Abu Dhabi for holidays, business trips, weddings and stopovers. Many of them want exactly what your salon sells: a blow-dry before dinner, a manicure before a desert safari, a fresh fade before a conference, or glam before a wedding they flew in for. Yet most UAE salons market almost entirely to residents.

## Why tourists are worth chasing

A tourist is not a regular, and that is precisely the point. She is not comparing your price with the salon in her neighbourhood. She is comparing you with doing nothing, or with the hotel spa that charges three times your rate. She books on short notice, decides fast, and pays for convenience without haggling. A visitor with a dinner at 8 pm and an empty afternoon will happily pay full price for a 4 pm blow-dry, because the alternative is going to dinner with hotel-room hair.

Tourists also leave the kind of review money cannot buy. A glowing Google review from a visitor in London or Mumbai is read by the next visitor searching from her hotel room. And because visitors travel in groups, one good visit often turns into two or three chairs filled the same afternoon.

## Be findable in the moment they search

Nobody plans a salon visit from home three weeks before a holiday. The search happens in the moment, usually from a hotel room or a mall bench, and it almost always starts with the same phrase: "salon near me".

Your Google Business Profile is your shop window for this audience. Keep your hours accurate, including evening hours, because tourists search after dinner too. Upload real photos of your work, your chairs and your team, not stock images. Make sure your services and starting prices are visible, and that the WhatsApp button works — a visitor who cannot see a price or message you in one tap will simply tap the next listing. Add landmarks to your description: "two minutes from Mall of the Emirates" means far more to a visitor than a street name she has never heard of.

## Win the concierge and the front desk

The single most valuable relationship a salon near a hotel district can build is with the people behind the front desk. Concierges and reception staff are asked every day where guests can get their hair or nails done, and they recommend the places they trust. That trust is earned, not bought.

Start with the three or four hotels closest to you. Visit in person, introduce yourself, and leave a simple printed menu with honest prices and a map. Offer the concierge team a complimentary service so they can recommend you from experience rather than from a leaflet. Agree a simple arrangement — a small commission per referred booking — and keep it in writing. Then make it effortless: one WhatsApp number, instant confirmation, and a guest who is looked after from the moment she arrives.

## Build a visitor-friendly menu

A tourist does not want your membership plan, your ten-visit package or your loyalty card. She wants one excellent service, delivered today, at a price she understands before she sits down. Build a small visitor menu around that truth.

Lead with express services that fit a holiday schedule: a 45-minute blow-dry, a 30-minute express manicure, a 20-minute brow tidy, a one-hour bridal-guest glam package. Put the duration next to every service, because a visitor planning her day around a 6 pm dinner needs to know she will be out by 5. Show prices in dirhams, clearly, with no surprises at the till. Keep a few same-day slots open every day, especially in the late afternoon and early evening, when visitors are getting ready to go out.

## Answer fast, in her language, on WhatsApp

The visitor's enquiry usually arrives on WhatsApp, in English, and often outside your working hours. She is in a different time zone, she is planning her evening, and she will message three salons at once. The first clear, helpful reply wins the booking.

Answer the four questions she is really asking in a single message: can you do it, when, how much, and where exactly are you. Send the location pin, the nearest landmark and the parking note together, so she never has to ask a follow-up.

Speed matters most after hours, when your team has gone home and the enquiries keep coming. For a visitor deciding between three salons at 11 pm, an instant answer is often the deciding factor.

## Turn one visit into a review that travels

A tourist's review is worth more than a resident's, because it is written for other tourists. Ask for it at the right moment — at the mirror, when she is smiling at the result, not in a message three days later when she is already home. A small card at the reception desk with a QR code that opens your Google review page makes it a ten-second job, and most happy visitors will do it gladly.

With permission, a quick before-and-after photo becomes content that speaks directly to the next visitor scrolling your Instagram from her hotel. Tag the location, not the person, and keep the caption simple: the service, the time it took, and the price range.

## A simple 30-day starter plan

Start small and measure what happens. In week one, tidy up your Google Business Profile, add real photos and prices, and check that your WhatsApp button works. In week two, visit the three nearest hotels and offer the concierge team a complimentary service. In week three, put together your visitor menu — five express services with durations and prices — and train the team on the one-message reply. In week four, add the review QR code at reception and ask every visitor client for a review at the mirror.

Track three numbers for a month: how many enquiries mention a hotel or a short stay, how many become bookings, and how many reviews they leave. The UAE welcomes the world through its airports every single day, and a salon that is ready for the visitor in the chair does not need to chase the market — the market walks in, phone in hand, looking for exactly what you do.
