Walk into a busy Dubai salon on a Thursday evening and you will see something that would have looked odd five years ago: a client standing in a pop-up tent, being misted head to toe, while the blow-dry chairs run at full speed. The spray tan has arrived in the UAE — and it is quietly becoming one of the most profitable add-on services a salon can offer.

The logic is simple. Clients in the Emirates want the look of a tan without the reality of the sun. A wedding on Saturday, a holiday on Monday, a photoshoot for a new job — every one of those moments looks better with an even, golden glow. But the midday sun here is not a tanning bed. It is harsh, it burns fast, and it leaves the patchy, uneven colour that no bride wants in her photographs. A professional spray tan gives the same result in twenty minutes, indoors, with no sun damage at all.

## Why the demand is real — and growing

Three forces are pushing the trend. The first is the wedding calendar. The UAE's winter wedding season runs from November through March, and brides are booking beauty timelines months ahead. A spray tan now sits on that timeline alongside the trial, the facial and the final hair appointment — usually two days before the event, when the colour has settled and looks most natural.

The second is the event economy. Dubai and Abu Dhabi host galas, award nights, product launches and shoots almost every week. Clients who attend them want to look polished in photographs, and a spray tan is the fastest way to look like the best version of yourself under bright lights and camera flashes.

The third is skin awareness. Clients who once lay by the pool for hours now understand what that costs their skin. Salons that already sell SPF and skin-health routines are hearing the same question from a new angle: how do I get the colour without the sun? A spray tan is the honest answer.

## What the service actually involves

A spray tan is not a machine you plug in and forget. The treatment starts with a short consultation — skin tone, the depth of colour the client wants, and the event it is for. The stylist then applies a fine mist of DHA-based tanning solution with a handheld spray gun, working in sections so the colour is even. The whole appointment takes about twenty to thirty minutes, and the client leaves with a guide for the next eight hours: no shower, no gym, no tight clothing, loose dark clothes only.

The result develops over six to eight hours and lasts five to seven days with good aftercare. That short lifespan is actually a selling point. It makes the spray tan a repeat service, tied to the client's calendar — every wedding, every holiday, every big night out becomes another booking.

## The mistakes that ruin a spray tan — and your reviews

The service has one unforgiving quality: a bad tan is visible to everyone. Orange palms, streaked ankles, a tide mark at the wrist — clients notice all of it, and they photograph it.

The first is skipping the consultation. A fair-skinned client who asks for the darkest shade will look unnatural, and no amount of blending will fix it. Good salons show a shade chart, ask about the event, and recommend one shade lighter than the client expects.

The second is rushing the preparation. The tan only looks as good as the skin underneath it. Clients need clear pre-care instructions at booking: exfoliate the day before, no moisturiser or deodorant on the day, shave at least a day ahead. Salons that send these instructions on WhatsApp the evening before the appointment see far fewer patchy results.

The third is treating aftercare as an afterthought. The client who showers two hours after her appointment and blames the salon is a client who was never told the rules clearly. A printed aftercare card, handed over with the booking for the next visit, costs almost nothing and prevents most of the damage.

## Pricing it honestly

Spray tans in the UAE typically sit in the range of a premium blow-dry — affordable enough to be an impulse add-on, valuable enough to be worth the salon's time. The smartest pricing structure is simple: one price for a full-body tan, a lower price for a half-body or top-up, and a package of three for clients with a season of events ahead.

Resist the urge to discount it heavily. A spray tan sold cheap reads as a cheap service, and clients who choose on price alone are the ones most likely to complain about the result. Instead, bundle it: a tan with a blow-dry and a manicure the day before the wedding, priced as one event package. The client gets a simpler decision, and the salon fills three chairs with one booking.

## Adding it to your menu without disrupting the salon

Start small. One trained staff member, one tent in a quiet corner, and a two-week trial with your existing clients. Offer it first to the clients you already know — the bride whose trial you are doing, the regular who has a gala next week. Their before-and-after photos, shared with permission, are better marketing than any paid post.

Training matters more than equipment. A one-day certification course covers the technique, the shades and the safety rules — including when not to tan a client, such as broken or freshly waxed skin. Build the treatment into your diary with a real slot and a real buffer, so it never collides with a colour appointment running late.

And keep the conversation going after the appointment. A WhatsApp message the next morning — how did the colour settle, is there anything to adjust next time — turns a one-off treatment into a standing event-season booking.
