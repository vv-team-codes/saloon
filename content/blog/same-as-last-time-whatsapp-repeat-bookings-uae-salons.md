Every salon owner in the UAE knows the message. It arrives on WhatsApp at 9pm, or during the Friday lunch rush: "Hi, can I book same as last time?"

It is the best message a salon can receive. The client is not shopping around or asking for prices. She wants the same cut, the same colour, the same stylist, the same feeling she walked out with last time. All she needs is a quick yes and a slot in the diary.

Yet this is one of the easiest bookings to lose: a slow reply, a wrong guess about what "last time" was, or a price surprise on the day can send a loyal regular elsewhere. The salons that win it treat "same as last time" as a process, not a guess.

## Why this message matters

A repeat booking is the cheapest booking your salon will ever win: no advertising spend, no reel to produce, no discount to offer. In Dubai or Abu Dhabi, where a regular visits every four to six weeks for a cut or every eight weeks for colour, these quiet messages are the backbone of the diary. Lose a few each month and the empty chairs show up on Tuesday mornings first, then everywhere. It is also a compliment: the last visit was good enough to repeat exactly — she is asking you to remember her.

## The trap: guessing what "last time" was

The most common mistake is replying from memory. A stylist is sure the client had a balayage, so that is what goes in the diary — but on the day it turns out she had full highlights and a toner, forty minutes longer and more expensive. Now the diary runs late, the bill does not match the price in her head, and the stylist is rushing.

Memory fails because the person answering WhatsApp is often not the person who did the service — and the client herself may not remember the service name. So the first rule is simple: never guess. Check the record before you reply.

## Step one: find the client before you answer

Keep a short note for every client: name, last visit date, services, stylist, colour formula or shade, price paid, and anything worth remembering. A shared note in the booking software, or even a notes app, does the job if the whole team uses the same one.

When the message arrives, look the client up first, then reply — it takes ninety seconds: "Of course, Sara — last time was a cut and blow-dry with Mariam on 4 October. Shall I book the same for Thursday at 6pm?" A specific reply proves the salon remembers her and removes the back-and-forth.

## Step two: confirm the details that can change

Even with a good record, three things are worth confirming in the same message.

First, the stylist. If her usual stylist is on leave or fully booked, say so now. Offer the closest alternative by name: "Mariam is off on Thursday, but Lina does the same cut."

Second, the price. If it has changed, say so on WhatsApp, not at the till: "Just so you know, the price is now AED 180, it was AED 160 last time." A surprise at checkout costs bookings; honesty rarely does.

Third, the time. A colour service that took two hours last time still takes two hours — if the diary only has ninety minutes free, offer the next slot that genuinely fits.

## Step three: handle the gaps honestly

Not every message has a neat record behind it. The client may be new to your notes system, her last visit may predate it, or her stylist may have left. This is where most salons either guess or go silent, and both are worse than asking.

Ask one or two short questions, and make them easy to answer: "Was it the cut with the layers, or the bob?" A photo helps too — ask for one from the week after her last visit, when the style had settled.

For colour, one rule is non-negotiable: if the formula is not in the notes, do not promise to recreate it from a photo alone. Book a short consultation or patch test first — "I want to get your shade exactly right, so let's do a quick ten-minute check before the colour day." Honesty about colour beats a confident guess that turns out wrong.

## Step four: one message that books it

Once the details are confirmed, the reply should do the whole job in a single message: service, stylist, day, time, price, and the way to confirm. Something like: "Done — cut and blow-dry with Mariam, Thursday 6pm, AED 180, about an hour. Shall I hold it for you? Just reply yes."

It works because it asks for one small action. A client facing three separate messages — service, time, price — often replies to none until tomorrow, and tomorrow the slot may be gone.

## Step five: turn the easy rebook into a habit

A client who books "same as last time" is telling you her rhythm: every five weeks, Thursday evening, same stylist. Make the next booking before she has to ask. At the chair, while the result is fresh, offer it: "Same time in five weeks? I can hold Thursday the 18th at 6pm for you now." Most regulars say yes — it removes a job from their list.

## The small system behind the easy reply

None of this needs expensive software — just three habits: one shared record per client, updated at the end of every visit; one owner for the WhatsApp inbox on each shift; and a short list of saved replies for the common cases, so the team answers in one voice.

Review the notes once a month and fix the gaps before they become a guessing game on a busy evening. The message "same as last time" is a gift: the client chose you once, and she is choosing you again. Remember her properly, confirm the details honestly, and make the next booking the easiest message she sends all week.
