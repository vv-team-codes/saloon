A message arrives on a busy afternoon: "Hi, we are six people, we need hair and makeup on Saturday morning before a wedding. Can you do it?" It sounds like a wonderful booking. Handled badly, it becomes a week of scattered messages, a half-confirmed diary, and a Saturday morning that runs an hour late.

Group enquiries differ from ordinary bookings: one person asks on behalf of several people, each with different services and levels of certainty. Treat it like a single appointment and the details will unravel. Treat it like a small event and it becomes one of the most profitable bookings of the week.

## Why group enquiries go wrong

Most group enquiries fail for the same three reasons. First, the headcount is never pinned down. A group of "about five or six" becomes four on the day, or seven, and the staffing plan collapses either way. Second, the services stay vague. "Hair and makeup" could mean blow-dries for three people and full bridal styling for one, which take completely different amounts of time. Third, nobody owns the communication. Messages arrive from three different phones, prices get quoted differently to different people, and the salon ends up negotiating with the whole group at once.

In the UAE these risks are higher because group occasions are frequent and time-critical: weddings, Eid gatherings, and engagement parties often involve sisters, cousins, and friends getting ready together. A late finish makes a whole group late for an event that cannot move.

## The first reply should capture five things

The goal of your first reply is not to close the sale. It is to gather the five facts that make a real plan possible: the date and the time everyone must be finished, the number of people, the service each person wants, any service that needs extra time such as bridal hair or full makeup, and a single contact person for the group.

Ask for these in one friendly message, not five separate questions across the day: "Wonderful, we would love to look after your group. To reserve the right stylists and timing, could you share the date, the time you need to leave, how many people, and roughly what each person would like?" When a detail is undecided, give it a deadline: "Just confirm the final headcount by Wednesday evening so we can hold the chairs."

Ask kindly that all changes come through one person. It prevents the classic situation where one friend cancels, another adds a service, and the salon hears about neither until Saturday morning.

## Price it as a package, not a guess

Group clients usually ask for a price before they commit, and a vague answer loses them. Build the quote from the confirmed services, not from the headcount alone. List each service, its price range, and the total for the group, so the organiser can see exactly what is included. If some prices depend on hair length or a consultation, say so plainly and give the range.

A small group discount, or a complimentary blow-dry for the organiser, is a genuine incentive. A deep discount that leaves stylists rushed and underpaid is not. A clear written quote also protects you: when the group arrives, nobody can claim they were promised something different.

## One organiser, one timeline

Once the services are known, work backwards from the finish time. If the group must leave at 1 pm and the longest service takes ninety minutes, that person starts first, not last. Share a simple timeline with the organiser a day or two before, showing who sits where and when. That alone removes most of the morning's anxiety.

Build in buffer time. Groups run late: a taxi is delayed, or a hairstyle takes longer than expected. Ten to fifteen minutes of breathing room between the last appointment and the departure time is far cheaper than rushing the final client or making the group late. Tell the organiser the timeline includes that buffer, so it reads as professionalism rather than slowness.

## Staffing and deposits

Assign named stylists to named clients in advance, matching skill to service: your senior stylist handles the bride or the most complex style, while reliable team members handle blow-dries and simpler makeup. Confirm availability with the team before you confirm it with the client, and brief them the day before on who is coming, what each person wants, any allergies, and the finish time.

Deposits are the most effective tool for group bookings, and they should be explained as protection for the group as much as for the salon. A deposit per person, or a single deposit from the organiser, confirms that the chairs are genuinely held. Without one, a salon can block out a whole Saturday morning for a group that quietly books elsewhere. Set the terms in writing at booking: the deposit amount, the deadline for final headcount, and what happens if numbers drop. Clients accept firm terms far more easily when they hear them early, in a calm message, than when they are surprised by them at the desk.

Agree payment on the day in advance too: will the organiser settle the whole bill, or will each person pay separately? Splitting six payments at the end of a busy morning, with everyone in a hurry to leave, is a recipe for confusion. Decide it before the first chair is filled.

## On the day, and after

Greet the group together, confirm the finish time, and introduce each person to their stylist. Offer water or coffee while clients wait and a place for bags and outfits. If the schedule slips, say so early and offer a solution: "We are ten minutes behind, so we will start your makeup while your hair sets." Silence, followed by a rushed finish, does not keep confidence intact.

A group booking should not end at the door. A thank-you message that evening, addressed to the organiser, costs nothing and often produces the next booking. Keep notes on what worked: which stylist combinations ran smoothly and how long each service really took. Over a few months, group bookings stop feeling like emergencies and start feeling like a service your salon is known for.

Group enquiries reward salons that are organised, clear, and calm under pressure. Capture the details early, quote honestly, staff deliberately, and protect the booking with a deposit.
