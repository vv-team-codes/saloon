Every salon owner in the UAE has heard the same complaint. A client returns for a colour refresh three weeks after her last appointment and the shine is already gone. Her blonde has turned brassy, her brunette looks flat, and her hair feels dry no matter how much conditioner she uses. She assumes the colour was wrong, or the products are not good enough. In many cases, neither is true — the problem is the water coming out of her shower.

Most tap water in the UAE is desalinated, and it carries a heavy load of minerals — mainly calcium and magnesium — along with chlorine. Over time, those minerals settle on the hair shaft and build up layer by layer, leaving hair dull, coated, and unwilling to hold colour. It is one of the most common hair problems in the country, and until recently most salons treated it as a mystery rather than a service opportunity. That is changing fast: hair detox treatments are becoming one of the most requested add-ons on UAE salon menus.

## Why UAE water is so hard on hair

Desalination makes the water safe to drink, but it does not make it gentle on hair. The mineral content is high, and in a hot climate where clients wash their hair more often, the build-up happens faster. A client washing her hair four or five times a week picks up far more mineral deposits than one washing twice a week in a soft-water city.

Minerals do their damage quietly. They coat the cuticle, so light bounces off unevenly instead of giving that smooth shine. They also sit between the hair and any colour applied afterwards, which is why a fresh colour can fade in two weeks instead of six. Blonde is hit hardest: mineral deposits, especially iron and copper traces, push blonde tones towards orange and brass.

## The signs your clients are already seeing

Most clients will not walk in and say "I think I have mineral build-up." They describe symptoms, and a good stylist learns to recognise them. Hair that feels waxy or coated even straight after washing is a classic sign. Colour that fades unusually fast is another. Blonde that turns brassy within a fortnight, curls that have lost their bounce, and products that seem to stop working are all clues.

The consultation is where this trend starts. A stylist who spots the signs can suggest a detox before booking a full colour correction. Clients are often relieved to learn there is a cause — and a fix — rather than a fault with their hair or the salon's work. That honesty builds trust, and trust turns a one-off detox into a regular booking.

## What a hair detox treatment actually does

A hair detox is a deep-cleansing treatment built around chelating ingredients, which bind to mineral deposits and lift them off the hair and scalp. It is not the same as a clarifying shampoo: a clarifying wash removes surface product build-up, while a proper detox targets the mineral layer underneath, using professional-strength products in the salon.

A typical session takes 30 to 45 minutes. The stylist applies the detox product, lets it work, rinses thoroughly, and follows with a conditioning or glossing step to seal the cuticle back down. Hair feels lighter, looks shinier, and takes colour far more evenly — which is why many salons now detox before any major colour service.

Honesty matters here. One detox will not undo years of build-up or repair damage already done, and clients with heavy build-up may need two or three sessions spaced a few weeks apart. Salons that set that expectation clearly get better reviews than those promising a miracle in a single visit.

## How salons are packaging it

The smartest salons in the UAE are not selling detox as a one-off fix. They are building it into the menu in three ways.

First, as a standalone add-on. A client books a cut or blow-dry and adds a 30-minute detox for a modest extra fee — an easy yes at the chair for clients who already complain about dullness or fast-fading colour.

Second, as a pre-colour prep step. Detox before colour is becoming standard in colour-focused salons, because the result is visibly better and lasts longer. When a client sees her colour still fresh at week five instead of week two, she comes back.

Third, as part of a bundle. Detox plus gloss, detox plus a scalp treatment, or a monthly detox-and-blow-dry package all work well for clients who wash daily. Bundles also smooth out quiet weekday hours, since a detox is short, fits between longer services, and does not need a senior stylist.

## The home-care conversation

A detox works best when the client keeps build-up down between visits, and that is where retail comes in. Chelating shampoos, used once a week, help maintain the result, and some salons also recommend shower filters, which cut the mineral load before the water reaches the hair. Clients appreciate these honest recommendations because they can feel the difference at home.

This is also where a salon earns repeat visits without discounting. A client who buys her chelating shampoo from the salon and gets a gentle reminder when her next detox is due has a reason to return every four to six weeks. That rhythm — detox, maintain, detox again — fills a diary steadily.

## A small service with a big payoff

Hair detox is not a dramatic trend — there are no viral transformations driving it. Its strength is simpler: it solves a real, everyday problem that almost every client in the UAE has, whether she knows it or not. It is quick to deliver, easy to explain, and it makes every other service on the menu work better.

For salon owners, it is also a reminder of where the best new services come from. They do not always arrive from overseas trends or trade shows; sometimes they come from listening to the same complaint for the tenth time and realising the water, not the work, is the problem. The salons that act on that insight first are the ones booking out detox slots weeks ahead — and keeping the colour clients who used to drift away.
