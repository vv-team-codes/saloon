It arrives on WhatsApp at 9 PM, usually with a screenshot attached: "I bought this voucher online — can I use it at your salon?" The deal is for a full balayage and blow-dry, or a 90-minute facial, at half the price you charge. The client has already paid someone else. She just needs you to say yes.

This is not the same as a gift voucher enquiry. A gift voucher is yours: you set the value, the terms and the margin. A third-party voucher was sold by a deal site, often at a steep discount. How you answer decides two things at once: whether you protect your prices, and whether a new client ever comes back at full price.

## First, find out exactly what she bought

Never answer with a yes or a no until you know which voucher it is. Deal-site vouchers in the UAE come in many forms: a specific service at a named salon, a generic credit, a package from a platform like Cobone or the Entertainer, or a voucher bought for a salon with a similar name to yours. Clients rarely read the terms.

Four questions settle it, and they can all go in one short reply: which site did she buy it from, what is the voucher code, which service does it cover, and how long is it valid? Asking these is not being difficult — it is the only way to give her a correct answer.

If the voucher is not for your salon at all — it happens more often than owners expect, usually with a similar name — say so quickly and kindly, and help her find the right salon.

## The three honest answers

Once you know what she holds, there are really only three answers, and each one needs to be given with confidence.

The first is yes, with conditions. You do accept the voucher — and here is what it covers, what it does not, and how to book it. The conditions matter more than the yes: which days it can be used, whether it covers the full service, and whether an upgrade is available at a stated price.

The second is not this voucher, but here is what we can do. Perhaps you stopped listing on that platform months ago, or the voucher expired, or it covers a service you no longer offer. Offer the nearest equivalent at a clear price — "that facial is AED 280 with us, and I can book you in tomorrow at 6 PM" — and many voucher holders book anyway, because they have already decided they want the treatment.

The third is a plain no, given well. Some salons never list on deal sites, and that is a legitimate business choice. Say it once, warmly, and do not apologise for your prices: "We don't work with deal sites, because we price our services so we can spend the full time on every client." A confident no keeps your value intact.

## The real question behind the voucher

A voucher enquiry is rarely only about the voucher. The client is testing whether your salon is worth full price, and she is using the deal as a low-risk way to find out.

That is why the worst response is a slow, grudging yes. Voucher clients who are squeezed into the last slot of the day, rushed through a shortened service, or made to feel like a burden never return at full price.

The salons that make deal sites work treat a voucher booking exactly like a full-price booking — same stylist, same time, same care — and then use the visit to win the second one. A voucher client who leaves happy already knows your location, your team and your standard. A small, honest offer at checkout — "your next visit, the same facial is AED 280, and I can book you in four weeks from now" — converts a one-off deal hunter into a regular far more reliably than any discount could.

## Set the rules before the next voucher arrives

Most voucher trouble comes from having no policy at all, and deciding case by case while a client waits on WhatsApp. Decide the rules once, write them down, and make sure everyone on the team gives the same answer.

Start with the basics: which platforms, if any, do you accept, and for which services? Many salons accept vouchers only for services with spare capacity — a facial on a quiet weekday afternoon, for example — and never for the Saturday evening diary. Add blackout rules for peak times, a clear expiry policy, and one upgrade rule: anything above the voucher value is charged at the stated menu price, agreed before the appointment, never in the chair. Then cap the volume: two or three voucher slots a week, in quiet hours, so the deal site fills empty chairs instead of slowly leaking your margins.

## The reply that books the client

A good reply to a voucher enquiry has the same shape as any good enquiry reply: fast, specific, and ending with a booking.

"Thanks for sending that over — I can see it is the 90-minute facial from Cobone, valid until the end of the month. Yes, we accept it. It covers the full facial, and you can book any weekday before 5 PM. Shall I book you in for Tuesday at 3 PM? If you would like the 24k gold add-on, that is AED 60 extra."

Notice what that message does. It proves the salon checked the voucher, states the terms plainly, gives a real slot, and offers an upgrade without pressure. The client does not need to ask a second question, and the booking is one tap away.

A voucher client is still a client — one who found you, chose you, and is already halfway through the door. Answer her well and she may be the cheapest new client you ever win. Answer her badly and she costs you far more than the discount ever did.
