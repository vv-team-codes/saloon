It usually arrives as one short WhatsApp message: "Do you do henna?" Sometimes there is a photo of an intricate bridal design attached, sometimes just those five words. It looks like the simplest enquiry a salon can receive. It is not.

Behind that question sit five real questions the client has not asked yet: what designs do you offer, how much will it cost, how long will it take, will it be ready in time for my event, and is it safe for my skin? The salons that win henna bookings answer all five before the client has to ask them. The salons that lose them reply "Yes, we do" and wait — and the client moves on to the next salon.

Henna demand in the UAE runs on the occasion calendar: before Eid, through the winter wedding season, around Diwali and National Day, and on Thursday and Friday evenings. A salon that handles these enquiries well does not just fill a chair; it often books a group, a bridal party, or a client who returns for every festival.

## What the client is really asking

She has an event, and the henna is part of getting ready for it. That changes the whole reply.

She wants to know whether you do the style she has in mind — a simple Arabic design for Eid is a different job from full bridal henna. She wants the price, and she is comparing it with two or three other salons. She wants to know how long it will take and whether the colour will be deep enough on the day, which depends on when she comes in. And if she has sensitive skin, or a bad experience elsewhere, she wants reassurance that what goes on her skin is safe.

## The four-part reply that books the henna

The strongest henna replies follow the same shape every time, and the team can learn it in one sitting.

First, confirm the service warmly and name the styles you offer: "Yes, we do henna — simple Arabic designs, full traditional bridal, and party henna for groups." Naming the styles tells the client she is in the right place and helps her picture what she wants.

Second, ask the four questions that decide the booking: which occasion is it for, which design style does she like, how many hands or people are involved, and which day is the event? The event date matters most, because henna needs time to develop. A Wednesday message for a Saturday wedding is easy; a Saturday-morning message for a Saturday-evening party needs an honest answer about what is realistic.

Third, give a price range and a time, not a single figure and a shrug. "Simple Arabic designs start from AED 80 per hand and take around 45 minutes; full bridal henna starts from AED 450 and takes two to three hours." A range is honest and ends the price ping-pong that kills so many enquiries.

Fourth, end with two specific slots. "We have Thursday at 5 PM or Friday at 11 AM — which suits you better?" A client who has to reply with a day and a time is already halfway booked. A client told "let us know when you want to come" is a client who forgets.

## Timing: the 48-hour rule every client should hear

This is the part most salons never explain, and it causes the most disappointment. Henna does not reach its deepest colour straight away. The stain develops over 24 to 48 hours, so the best day for henna is one or two days before the event — not the morning of it.

Say it plainly: "For the deepest colour on Saturday, the best time is Thursday evening or Friday morning." A client who comes in too late and gets a pale stain blames the artist, even though the timing was the problem.

For bridal henna, book a proper appointment with a named artist and a realistic finish time — not squeezed between two hair appointments.

## The safety question you must answer honestly

Sooner or later a client will ask whether your henna is safe, or whether you use black henna. This is the one enquiry where a wrong answer is not just a lost booking — it is a real risk to the client.

Natural henna gives a reddish-brown stain and is generally well tolerated. So-called black henna, which stains dark almost immediately, often contains a chemical dye that can cause serious skin reactions, and no reputable salon should pretend otherwise. If a client asks for black henna, say you use natural henna, the colour deepens over a day or two, and offer a small patch test first.

Never promise a product is safe for everyone, and never give medical advice.

## Pricing henna without the back-and-forth

Henna pricing goes wrong when it is vague. "It depends" is not an answer a client can plan around, and every extra message she has to send is another chance to drift away.

Use three clear bands in every reply: simple designs per hand, traditional full hands, and full bridal including feet. Add the one thing clients always forget to ask: whether the price includes both hands or one. Most misunderstandings about henna prices come down to exactly that.

For groups — a bride with her sisters, a henna night, friends before Eid — quote per person and say how many artists will be there. Take a deposit, because a group that has paid something shows up.

## After the booking: the message that protects the result

The enquiry does not end when the client books. A short message the day before does more for the result than anything else the salon sends: come with clean, dry hands, no lotion or oil, loose sleeves you do not mind staining, keep the paste on as long as you can — overnight if possible — and avoid water on the design for the first few hours.

It is the difference between a deep, rich stain and a pale one. The salon that explains aftercare in advance gets the credit; the salon that says nothing gets the complaint.
