It is 9:40pm. The salon closed an hour ago, and a WhatsApp message lands: "Can I wash my hair tonight?" The client had a full colour this afternoon and is standing in her bathroom, shampoo in hand, deciding right now. A reply tomorrow morning is too late — she will have washed it, the colour will have faded, and she will blame the salon.

Aftercare enquiries are the second half of the service. The appointment ends at the chair, but the result is decided in the hours after — in the shower, at the gym, in the pool, under the Dubai sun. How a salon answers decides whether the treatment lasts, whether the client trusts the salon, and whether she books again.

## The questions that arrive after closing time

Every UAE salon recognises them: "Can I wash my hair tonight?" after colour or keratin. "Can I swim tomorrow?" after nails or lashes. "Can I go to the gym?" after a facial. "Can I go in the sun?" after waxing.

The UAE makes these questions sharper. Clients here swim, use the sauna and steam room, and work out in air-conditioned gyms before stepping into 40-degree heat. Hard water, chlorine, salt water and humidity all work against fresh colour, keratin and nails. A client who would wait a week to swim in a cooler climate is in the pool the next morning here — so the aftercare answer has to fit the life she actually lives.

## Why the answer matters more than the treatment

A treatment that fades in a week feels like a bad treatment, even when the work in the chair was perfect. The client does not separate the two: she remembers the price, looks in the mirror, and decides whether it was worth it. If the colour washed out because she shampooed the same evening, she will blame the salon, not her timing — and she will not come back.

The reverse is just as true: a clear, fast answer at 10pm makes a client feel looked after. She follows the advice, the colour holds, and the salon earns a trust no discount can buy.

## The four answers every salon should have ready

Most questions fall into four groups.

For colour: no washing for 48 to 72 hours, lukewarm water after that, and a sulphate-free shampoo. Add the UAE pool warning: chlorine strips fresh colour faster than anything else.

For keratin and smoothing, the first 72 hours are the treatment: no washing, no tying the hair back, no tucking it behind the ears, no sunglasses on the head. Say it plainly at the till, and repeat it in the late-night reply.

For nails and lashes, the first day is the fragile one: no soaking, no steam room, no long hot shower for 24 hours, and no picking at a lifted edge. Gel lifts faster in heat and humidity, so warn the client heading to the sauna the morning after a full set — before she goes, not after.

For skin and waxing, the rule is 24 to 48 hours of calm: no sun, no gym sweat, no pool, no perfumed creams on freshly waxed skin, and no exfoliating a fresh facial. Be specific: "skip the 7am class tomorrow" lands better than "avoid heat".

## Reply fast, even at 10pm

The hardest part of aftercare is timing: the question arrives when the client is about to do the thing she is asking about. The fastest salons keep the four answers above as saved replies, so anyone can send the right one in seconds. When nobody is on shift, a 24/7 AI receptionist for salons like ZYNA can answer the common questions instantly and flag the unusual ones for the morning team.

Speed is not carelessness: a fast wrong answer is worse than a slow right one. If the question is unusual — a reaction, swelling, a burn, a colour gone wrong — never guess. "Let me check with your stylist first thing tomorrow and I will call you" is honest. Inventing a home remedy is not.

## The three-line reply that works every time

The best replies follow one shape: the direct answer first — yes or no, plainly — then the reason in one line, then what to do instead, or when it becomes safe.

So instead of "Don't wash your hair", try: "Wait until Thursday morning if you can — the colour is still settling and hot water lifts it faster. If you need to freshen up tonight, dry shampoo at the roots will do." The client gets the rule, the reason and a way through the evening.

## Turn the answer into the next booking

Every aftercare message is also a rebooking opportunity. She is thinking about her hair, nails or skin right now — the warmest lead of the week. So end the reply with the next step, gently and specifically: "Your colour will want a gloss in about four weeks — shall I hold you a Thursday 6pm, your usual time?" That is not a sales push; it is the salon looking after the result it just created.

Two habits make this work. First, write the aftercare note on the client's file — what she had, what she was told, what she asked — so the next stylist knows the story. Second, check in once, briefly, a few days after a big treatment: "How is the colour settling?" is a five-second message that turns a one-time client into a regular.

## When the aftercare already went wrong

Sometimes the message is a confession, not a question: "I washed it." "I went swimming." The worst response is blame — the client already knows. What she needs is the fix, usually small: a gloss to revive faded colour, a repair treatment, a free check of lifting nails. Offer the fix, book it, and note it for next time. A client rescued well after her own mistake becomes more loyal than one whose visit went perfectly — she has seen how the salon treats her when things go wrong.

Aftercare enquiries look like small messages. They are the moment the client decides whether the money was worth it, whether the salon actually cares, and whether she will be back. Answer them fast, answer them honestly, and answer them with the next booking in mind.
