Ask any therapist in a Dubai or Abu Dhabi salon what clients complain about most, and the answer is rarely the hair or the nails. It is the eyes: dark circles no concealer quite hides, puffiness after a late night, fine lines that appeared over one summer. For years, the answer was a shrug and a facial, with a dab of cream at the end. That is changing: across the UAE, salons are carving out dedicated eye-area treatments — short, focused services for the skin around the eyes — and they are booking out fast.

## Why the eyes became the UAE's most-booked complaint

The eye area is the thinnest skin on the body, and it shows tiredness first. In the UAE, tiredness has plenty of help. Evenings run late: dinners at nine, mall trips after work, events that start when the heat breaks. Screen time is high, sleep is short, and air conditioning dries the skin for hours a day. Add frequent flying, and you have a city of people whose eyes look older than they feel.

Clients are not asking for a miracle; they want someone to take the thing that bothers them every morning seriously. A salon that offers a named eye treatment, with a clear price and a realistic promise, wins that trust.

## What an eye-area treatment actually is

A proper eye treatment is not a facial with the word "eye" added to the price list. It is a focused 20 to 30 minute service. It starts with a gentle cleanse and a proper look at the skin: the therapist checks whether the problem is pigmentation, puffiness, dryness or fine lines, because each needs a different approach. Then comes the treatment itself — cooling massage to move fluid from the under-eye area, a targeted mask or patches, and a serum chosen for the actual concern.

Some salons add a device step, such as a small microcurrent or LED handpiece for the eye area, but the treatment works without one. What matters is that it is a dedicated slot with a named result, not an afterthought. Clients can feel the difference, and they rebook the service designed for them.

## Three ways to put it on the menu

The salons doing this well offer it in three tiers. First, the express add-on: fifteen minutes tacked onto a facial, a blow-dry or a manicure, priced low and filling a gap in the diary. Second, the signature eye facial: a standalone 30-minute treatment with its own price, and the tier that earns the margin — for the client who books specifically for her eyes, before a wedding or a holiday, or simply because she is tired of looking tired. Third, the course: four to six sessions booked together, spaced a week or two apart, with a modest saving on the single-session price, progress reviewed at the third visit.

Pricing should stay honest. In most UAE salons the express add-on costs about the same as a lunch and the signature treatment roughly what a good blow-dry does. Never price it like a medical treatment — it is not one, and the moment the price suggests a clinical result, the client expects one.

## Where it fits in the diary

The eye treatment is diary-friendly. The express version slots into the gaps between longer appointments — an ideal use of a chair that would otherwise sit empty at 11 in the morning. The signature treatment fits the evening peak, when clients come in after work wanting to look fresh for the night ahead. It also pairs naturally with occasion bookings: a bride's trial or a pre-flight glow treatment. Just keep the add-on to fifteen minutes — the moment it runs over, it eats the next appointment, and the next client pays for it.

## The honest limits — and why stating them sells more

Here is the part that separates the salons that keep eye-treatment clients from the ones that lose them after one visit: honesty about what the treatment cannot do. Some dark circles are genetic. Some puffiness is a medical matter. Some fine lines are simply the face a person has lived in. A good therapist says that plainly, in the first consultation, before taking a dirham.

Screen properly, too: anyone with an eye infection, a recent eye procedure, or a skin condition around the eyes should be referred to a doctor, not booked in. Turning one client away kindly is worth more than ten treatments sold badly.

## Selling it without filters

The marketing almost writes itself, because the result is visible in a way most treatments are not. A before-and-after photo of a real client, same light, no filter, is worth more than any slogan. Clients in the UAE spot a filtered photo quickly, and one dishonest image does more damage than ten honest ones do good.

Late-night enquiries are a big part of this trend, too: clients browse at midnight and message at 1am, asking whether the eye facial would help and when the next slot is. Salons that answer the next morning lose some of them; the question has cooled by then. ## A four-week starter plan

If your salon does not offer an eye treatment yet, a month is enough to test it. Week one: train two therapists, agree on the tiers and prices, and write the consultation script, honest limits included. Week two: offer the express add-on free to ten regulars and ask what they noticed. Week three: put the signature treatment on the menu, message the clients who have asked about their eyes before, and photograph the first real results. Week four: check the numbers — add-ons taken, signature treatments booked, clients asking about the course.

If a third of the clients offered the add-on take it, and the signature treatment books a handful of times a week, the service has earned its place. If not, the problem is usually the offer — the price, the name, or the moment it was suggested.

The eye area is a small patch of skin, but it carries a large share of how people feel about their face. A salon that treats it with skill, honesty and a fair price answers the question clients ask in the mirror every morning — and gives them a reason to come back to the chair that answered it.
