A client buys a course of six laser sessions in your Dubai salon. She comes for the first one, glowing and excited. She comes for the second. Then life gets in the way, the third session never gets booked, and three months later she has quietly disappeared. The treatment was never the problem. The diary was.

Multi-session courses — laser hair removal, facial programmes, microneedling, chemical peels, keratin top-ups — are some of the most valuable bookings a UAE salon can win. A client who commits to six sessions is worth six times a walk-in — and far more likely to become a regular. Yet most salons book these courses one session at a time, so every gap is a chance for the client to drift away. The fix is simple: treat the whole course as one booking, planned at the very first visit.

## Why treatment courses fall apart

A single appointment has one point of failure: the day itself. A course has five or six. Between sessions there is a gap of two, four or six weeks, and in that time a client in the UAE travels, gets busy at work, forgets, or finds another salon closer to her new apartment. If nothing is booked, there is nothing to remind her and nothing for your team to follow up on.

There is a second problem. Treatments in a course are not interchangeable. A laser session needs a specific gap so the hair is in the right growth phase. A facial programme builds on the last session. If the gap stretches from four weeks to ten, results stall and the client starts to doubt the course. When results stall, clients blame the treatment, not the diary — and they do not come back for the next course.

## Book the whole course at the consultation

The moment to book a course is the moment the client says yes — at the consultation, while she is sitting in the chair and the plan is fresh. Offer her the full series there and then: all six dates, written down, with the gaps set by the treatment, not by her calendar. Clients almost always say yes at this point, because the plan feels real and organised. Asked three weeks later over WhatsApp, the same client will stall.

Work the dates out with her, not for her. Show her the spacing the treatment needs — four weeks between laser sessions, two weeks between facials — and fit those dates around her real life. A client who travels every summer, a mum whose school-run mornings are impossible, a professional who can only come after 6 pm: build the course around those facts now, and the course will survive them later.

## Keep the same stylist on the same client

A course is a relationship as much as a treatment. The therapist who did the first session knows how the client's skin reacted, which settings were used, and what she was happy or unhappy with. Handing session four to a different pair of hands breaks that thread, and the client feels it even if she cannot name it.

Block the course against one named therapist from the start — the same name on all six appointments. If that therapist takes annual leave, plan around it before the course begins — shift one session, or arrange a handover where the covering therapist reads the notes first. A course should never arrive at a session to find a stranger.

## When a session gets missed

Even the best-planned course will lose a session. A client falls ill, a flight is delayed, a meeting runs over. What matters is not that it happened, but what happens in the next 24 hours.

Treat a missed session as a same-day task. Message the client that evening with two concrete replacement dates, both inside the treatment window, not an open question about when she might be free. An open question gets an open-ended answer — usually silence. Two dates get a choice, and a choice gets a booking.

If the client has missed two sessions, stop messaging and call. A short, warm call says the salon noticed and cares, in a way a third WhatsApp message never will. Use the call to rebook, not to lecture. If the gap has already stretched too far, say so honestly and agree a revised plan — perhaps an extra session at a reduced price.

## Deposits, reminders and the diary

A course that is paid for in full is a course that gets finished. A course paid session by session is a course that can quietly end after session three. You do not need to demand full payment to get the benefit: a deposit for the course, or payment for the first two sessions together, gives the client a reason to return and gives the salon a reason to chase her.

Reminders should follow the course, not the single appointment. Send the usual confirmation a day before each session, but add a second message after each session that names the next one: "See you on 14 December for session three — same time, same therapist." That small sentence turns an isolated visit into a story with a next chapter.

In the diary, mark course appointments differently from one-offs — a colour, a tag, a note — so the front desk sees at a glance which chairs are holding a course together.

## Know your completion rate

You cannot improve what you do not measure. Once a month, count how many courses started three months ago were finished on schedule. If the number is below seven in ten, the diary is leaking clients — and the leak is almost always in the gaps, not in the treatment room.

Look at where courses break. Is it always session three? Is it always clients who booked around school holidays? The pattern will show exactly what to fix — booking the full course earlier, tightening reminders, or protecting the same-therapist rule.

A completed course is worth far more than six appointments: a client who saw real results, trusts your salon, and is ready to buy the next course or bring a friend. Book the whole course on day one, guard the gaps, and the results will take care of themselves.
