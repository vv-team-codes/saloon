It usually arrives as a voice note, late in the evening: "Hi, my sister's birthday is next month and I want to book the whole day for her — hair, nails, facial, everything. Can you do that?" Sometimes it is a bride, sometimes a group of friends, sometimes a tourist with one free day in Dubai.

A full-day pamper enquiry is not a normal booking. A normal booking is one service, one chair, one slot. This is a whole day of your diary, several stylists, and a sequence of services that have to fit together, for a client who has been looking forward to this for weeks. Handled well, it is one of the best bookings a salon can take — high value, and the kind of day that turns into photos, reviews and new clients. Handled badly, it is a day of running late. The difference is the conversation that happens before anyone picks up a pair of scissors.

## Ask five questions before you quote a price

The most common mistake is to answer with a price. The client asked "can you do the whole day?" and the salon replies with a number, and then both sides spend a week negotiating a day neither of them has designed. Ask five questions first, and the price becomes easy.

First, what is the occasion? A birthday, a bridal morning and a girls' day out are three different days. Second, how many people? A day for one woman is a slow sequence; a day for four friends needs two or three stylists working in parallel. Third, which services are non-negotiable? Ask her to name the two or three she cares about most. Fourth, what time does the day start and end? A client who arrives at 10 AM and needs to be at dinner by 7 PM has nine hours. Fifth, roughly what budget? A range tells you whether to design around a signature facial or a simple blow-dry and manicure.

These five answers are the whole plan. With them, you can reply with services, a start time, a finish time and a price, all in one message.

## Build the day in the right order

A full day has a natural rhythm. Put the long services first: colour, keratin and deep treatments belong in the morning, while the client is fresh and processing time can be used for something else. Facials and massages belong in the middle of the day. Hair styling, makeup, nails and the final mirror moment belong last, so she walks out looking finished.

Build in breaks, and say so in the plan: a lunch break — an hour, away from the chair — and two shorter pauses. Some salons partner with a cafe next door, or keep sandwiches and karak on hand. It costs very little and changes how the whole day feels.

Be honest about the finish time. If the services add up to seven hours and she arrives at 11 AM, she will not be finished by 5 PM once breaks are included. Say that now, not at 4 PM when she is checking her watch.

## Price the day, not the parts

Adding up every service separately usually produces a number that shocks the client, even though she would have happily paid it. Present one price for one day, with the services listed underneath. If the individual prices add up to AED 1,800, a day price of AED 1,500 feels like a genuine saving — and it is, because a full day fills chairs that might otherwise sit empty.

Say what is not included, and be clear about the deposit. A full day is a big commitment of your diary, so a deposit of around half, collected when the day is confirmed, is fair. Explain it simply: the deposit holds the chairs and the stylists for the whole day, and it is refundable up to a date, such as seven days before.

## Protect the rest of the diary

A full-day booking can quietly eat a salon's best day. One client, one chair, one stylist from 10 AM to 6 PM on a Saturday is a chair that cannot serve six other clients. Weekdays are the right home for a full-day package, when chairs are quieter. On a Saturday, price it to match what the chair would otherwise earn, or build it around two stylists working in parallel.

Assign one person to own the day — usually the receptionist or manager — who keeps the schedule, watches the clock and tells the client what is next.

## The reply that books the day

The reply writes itself. Four parts, one message: warm acknowledgement, the five questions, a suggested shape for the day, and a clear next step. Something like this: "We would love to plan a full pamper day for your sister! To design the perfect day, could you tell me how many people are coming, which two or three services matter most, and what time she would like to start? A typical pamper day runs from 10 AM to about 6 PM, with a lunch break, and starts from AED 1,500 including hair, facial, nails and makeup. Once I know her favourites, I will send you the full plan and hold the date with a deposit."

If she replies, send the full plan within a day: services in order, times, breaks, the price, the deposit and the cancellation date. If she goes quiet, follow up once, two days later, with the plan attached: "I have put together a pamper day for your sister — shall I hold the date?"

## When to say not this day

Not every full-day enquiry should become a full-day booking. If she wants a Saturday in December during party season, be honest that the salon cannot give one client the whole day that week, and offer a half-day package, a weekday, or the same services split across two visits. If her budget is far below what the day costs, say so kindly now, with a smaller version of the day she can afford.

A full-day pamper enquiry is really a compliment: out of every salon in Dubai, she chose yours for the day that matters most to her. Treat the planning with the same care as the services, and she will leave glowing, tell her friends, and book the next one before she reaches the door.
