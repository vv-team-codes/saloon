Every salon owner in the UAE knows the pattern. For most of the year the diary follows a familiar rhythm: quiet weekday mornings, a busy Saturday, and the evening rush after work. Then Ramadan approaches, and the whole calendar bends. Evenings come alive after iftar, and the final ten days before Eid turn into the busiest stretch of the year.

The salons that make the most of Ramadan are not the ones that work the hardest during it. They are the ones that plan for it months ahead, while the diary is still calm. November is the right time to start. Here is how to plan a Ramadan season that fills chairs without exhausting the team or discounting the business away.

## Start With the Diary, Not the Discount

Most salons begin Ramadan planning with an offer: a percentage off, a bundle, a flash deal. That is the wrong starting point. The real constraint during Ramadan is not demand — demand will arrive on its own. The constraint is capacity: how many chairs, how many stylists, and how many hours you can honestly staff in the final week.

Open your diary early and block the shape of the season before you sell a single package. Decide now which nights you will stay open late in the last ten days, and which of those nights are henna nights. Put your strongest stylists on the Eid-week rota first, and protect at least one rest day per person in the final fortnight. A tired team in the last three days before Eid makes mistakes that are expensive to fix when every chair is full.

Plan the diary for the last ten days as though it will be fully booked, because it will be, and leave two short buffer slots each evening for walk-ins. Then design offers that fit inside the hours you actually have.

## Build Packages Around Real Ramadan Routines

Ramadan clients do not buy random services. They buy a sequence. In the first two weeks many women book a fresh cut or colour and a facial, getting the groundwork done while the pace is still gentle. In the last week the demand shifts to finishing touches: threading, henna, manicures, blow-dries and the final Eid-morning styling slot.

Design packages that follow that sequence instead of fighting it. A "Ramadan ready" package in week one — cut, colour refresh and a facial — is easy to sell because it matches what clients are already thinking about. A henna-and-finishing package in the final days sells itself, but only if the slots exist. Price them as a small saving on the combined services, not a deep discount — regulars will book them because they are convenient, not because they are cheap.

Think about families too: many clients book for a mother, a sister or a daughter at the same time, and a family package with staggered start times is one of the easiest ways to fill a quiet afternoon.

## Market Early, and Market Gently

Ramadan marketing that starts in the final week is too late and too loud. The salons that fill their Eid diary do their talking in the weeks before Ramadan begins, when clients are calm and receptive. A short WhatsApp message in early February — "Ramadan hours are now open for booking, and Eid-week slots go first to regulars" — does more than any last-minute discount blast.

Keep the tone respectful and practical. Clients are fasting and spending their evenings with family, so messages sent after iftar get read and mid-afternoon ones get ignored. One thoughtful message a week is plenty: your Ramadan hours, the henna night dates, the last day you can guarantee an Eid booking — each ending with one clear next step, such as replying with a preferred day.

Update the basics too: your Google Business Profile should show the Ramadan hours, your WhatsApp greeting message should mention them, and your price list should be current. ## The Eid Week Is a Different Business

Treat the final week before Eid as a separate operation with its own rules. Deposits matter more than ever: a no-show on an ordinary Tuesday costs you an hour, but a no-show on the night before Eid costs you a slot that ten other clients wanted. Ask for a deposit on every Eid-week booking, and say so plainly when the client books. Regulars understand this; they have been turned away themselves in previous years.

Keep a live waitlist for the final three days. When a cancellation comes in — and several will — message the waitlist in order, one client at a time, with a fifteen-minute window to confirm. Done well, this fills nearly every cancelled chair.

Stop taking new service enquiries that need a consultation in the final three days. A client who needs a colour correction or a first-time bridal trial on the eve of Eid is a risk to the whole diary. Offer them the first slot after Eid instead, and book it on the spot. You protect the team, and the client still leaves with a plan.

## After Eid, Keep the Momentum

The season does not end on Eid morning. The week after Eid is when the smartest salons quietly win the next three months. Clients who loved their Eid look are warm, happy and ready to rebook — if you ask. A short message two or three days after Eid, thanking them and offering their usual slot in three weeks, converts at a rate no advertising campaign can match.

It is also the time to settle the numbers: which packages sold, which nights filled first, how many waitlist clients converted. Write it on one page and keep it for next year — Ramadan comes every year, and a plan that improves a little each time eventually runs itself.

The salons that do best in Ramadan are not the loudest or the cheapest. They are the ones that decided in November what their March would look like, told their clients early, protected their team, and treated the final week with the seriousness it deserves. Start now, while the diary is calm, and this Ramadan can be your salon's best season yet.
