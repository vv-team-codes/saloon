It usually arrives at 10:40 in the morning: "Sorry, I missed my 10 o'clock. Can I come at 12?" The chair sat empty, and now everything depends on the next message you send.

Most salons answer in one of two ways, and both lose the client. The cold reply — a flat "you missed your appointment, please rebook" — makes her feel like a nuisance. The over-generous reply squeezes her in and quietly teaches her that missing an appointment has no consequences. The salons that keep these clients do something in between: warm, clear, and always ending with a real booking.

## Why the reply matters more than the missed appointment

A missed appointment is rarely a sign that a client does not value your salon. In the UAE, life moves fast — school runs overrun, meetings shift, and traffic on Sheikh Zayed Road turns a twenty-minute drive into an hour. The client messaging you afterwards is good news: she is still trying to come. The clients you have already lost are the ones who never message at all. That is why the reply is the real recovery moment. If she feels understood and leaves with a confirmed time, the missed appointment becomes a small story, not a reason to try the salon down the road.

## The four-part reply that works every time

The best replies follow the same shape.

First, acknowledge without scolding. "No problem at all, it happens" costs nothing and changes the whole tone. Never write "you didn't show up". The client already knows, and embarrassed clients do not rebook.

Second, give her one concrete option, not an open question. "I can fit you in tomorrow at 11:30 with Sara, or Saturday at 4:00 with Mariam — which suits you better?" is a reply that books. "When would you like to come?" starts another three-message exchange that often ends in silence. Always offer two real slots, with a named stylist.

Third, state the policy in one calm sentence. "We do ask for a small deposit to hold the new slot" is fair and clear — clients accept rules they understand.

Fourth, confirm in writing: day, time, service and stylist in one message, and ask her to reply with a yes. That turns a friendly chat back into a booking.

## What to say when the day is fully booked

When there genuinely is no space left today, be honest and fast. "Today is fully booked, I'm sorry — the earliest I have is tomorrow at 1:00, or I can put you first on the list if anything opens up this evening." A client who missed her appointment is already feeling guilty; a reply that arrives four hours later feels like a door closing. A reply within ten minutes, even with bad news, feels like a salon that cares.

Make the waitlist offer properly. Do not just say "I'll let you know". Say what will happen: "I'll message you by 5 PM if a slot opens, and if not, your tomorrow booking stands." In busy UAE salons, freed chairs in the evening rush are gold — a client on standby fills them fastest.

## Deposits, second chances and the line you should not cross

Every salon owner eventually faces the client who misses appointments more than once. The second time, warmth alone is not enough. A deposit policy helps here — not as a punishment, but as a shared commitment. A client who pays a small deposit to rebook is far more likely to arrive.

The wording is everything. "To hold your new slot, we take a small deposit of AED 50, which comes off your bill" lands very differently from "you will have to pay a deposit because you missed your appointment". The first is a normal process; the second sounds like a fine. Keep it modest and the same for everyone.

Be clear about the line. One missed appointment is an accident. Two is a pattern worth a gentle conversation at the chair: "Shall we pick a time that usually works better for you?" Three, with no deposit and no apology, is a client costing you real money — and it is fair to say, kindly, that her bookings will need a deposit from now on.

## A small system that stops the same message arriving every week

Good replies are not enough if the same message arrives five times a week. The salons with fewer missed appointments have fixed the cause, not just the reply.

Start with the reminder. A confirmation message the evening before catches most forgetfulness; a second reminder on the morning catches the rest. In the UAE, a message between 5 and 6 PM the day before, and a short one around 9 AM on the day, reaches clients when they plan their day.

Then check your diary for the pattern. Are most missed appointments on Saturday mornings, when the school run collides with the salon visit? A five-minute look at the week's missed appointments, every Monday, shows the pattern. Fix it — move the reminder, or ask for a deposit on Saturday peak slots — and the messages slow down on their own.

Keep a note on the client's record too. Not a black mark, just a fact: "Missed 12 Nov, rebooked 13 Nov, deposit taken." When she messages again, any member of staff can see the history and reply consistently.

## The quiet follow-up that brings her back

A client who missed and rebooked is more fragile than a normal booking. A short message the evening before — "Looking forward to seeing you tomorrow at 11:30, Sara has your colour ready" — reminds her and tells her she is expected, not merely tolerated.

If she does not rebook, do not let the conversation die. One message the next day is enough: "No worries about yesterday — would you like me to hold a slot for you this week?" No guilt, no pressure, one open door. About half of those clients will take it.

Missed appointments will never disappear. But they do not have to mean lost clients. A warm first sentence, two real options, a clear policy and a confirmed booking — that is the whole reply. Get it right, and the message that starts with an apology ends with a client in your chair.
