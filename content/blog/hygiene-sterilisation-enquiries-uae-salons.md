It usually arrives as one short WhatsApp message, often late in the evening: "Do you sterilise your tools?" Sometimes it is softer — "Is it clean there?" or "Do you use new files for nails?" It is not an accusation. It is a buying signal from a client who is already halfway to booking.

Behind that question sit four worries she has not spelled out: will I be safe here, do you reuse tools, is the linen fresh, and has anyone had a problem here before? She may be a first-timer choosing between three salons, a mum booking for her daughter, or someone who picked up an infection elsewhere and never forgot it. Salons that win these clients answer the worry properly. The ones that lose them reply "Yes, of course" and nothing more — and the client, quite reasonably, does not believe them.

Hygiene questions are more common in the UAE than many owners realise. Clients here come from dozens of countries with different expectations, and many have read stories about salon infections. Nail clients ask most often, because files, buffers and cuticle tools touch skin directly. Hair clients notice combs, brushes and capes, and facial clients ask about beds, bowls and spatulas. A good answer costs nothing. A bad one costs a client you never met.

## What the client is really asking

She wants three things: a straight yes, a concrete detail that proves it, and a reason to trust you more than the salon she messaged five minutes ago.

The detail is what separates a convincing answer from a hollow one. "We sterilise everything" is a claim. "Our metal tools go through an autoclave after every client, and we open a fresh sealed pouch in front of you" is something she can picture. "Nail files and buffers are single-use — one per client, then thrown away" tells her exactly what happens to the tool that touches her skin.

Match the answer to the service she asked about. A nail client wants to hear about files, buffers, cuticle nippers and pedicure liners. A hair client wants clean combs, brushes and capes, and colour bowls washed between clients. A facial client wants fresh headbands, clean beds and spatulas that never go back into the jar. One generic answer for every service sounds like a script. A specific one sounds like a salon that does the work.

## The four-part reply that turns the question into a booking

The strongest hygiene replies follow the same shape every time, and the whole team can learn it in one sitting.

First, answer warmly and directly, and thank her for asking. "Great question — yes, and I'm happy to explain how." A salon that bristles at a hygiene question is telling the client exactly what she feared.

Second, give the specifics. Name the method and the moment: "Our metal tools are sterilised in an autoclave after every client, and we open the sealed pouch in front of you. Nail files and buffers are single-use — a fresh set for every client." If you use a UV steriliser or a disinfectant solution instead, say so plainly and name the product.

Third, offer proof without being asked. "You're welcome to see the steriliser when you come in." A photo of sealed tool pouches, sent on WhatsApp, settles the question faster than any paragraph. Keep one or two such photos saved on the salon phone.

Fourth, close with a booking, not a lecture. Offer two real slots: "Would you like Thursday at 6 pm or Friday at 11 am for your manicure?" The client asked about hygiene because she was deciding whether to book. A reply that ends without a next step leaves the decision open, and an open decision is usually a lost one.

## What never to say

"Of course we're clean" with no detail answers a specific question with a slogan. "Don't worry about it" dismisses the worry instead of answering it. Getting defensive ends the conversation on the spot.

Never claim more than you do. Do not call your process medical-grade or hospital-standard unless it genuinely is, and do not claim a certification you do not hold. And never answer with a price — the client asked about safety, not cost, and a reply that jumps straight to "manicure AED 120" tells her you did not hear her at all.

## Make hygiene visible before anyone asks

The best hygiene answer is the one the client never has to ask for, because she can already see it. Keep the steriliser and the sealed tool pouches where clients can see them. Open the fresh towel and the sealed pouch in front of the client, every time, and wash hands visibly before starting a service.

Put the same evidence where new clients look before they message. A photo of a clean, set-up station on your Google Business Profile and on Instagram answers the question for the client who never asks it out loud — she simply books with the salon that looks careful.

Train the team on one agreed answer, so the client who messages at 10 pm and the one who asks mid-service hear the same facts in the same calm tone. A saved reply on the salon phone keeps everyone consistent — and a 24/7 AI receptionist like ZYNA can carry that same accurate answer to clients who message after hours.

## When the answer is not perfect yet

Not every salon has an autoclave, and honesty about that is better than a lie. "We disinfect our metal tools in Barbicide after every client, and we use single-use files and fresh linen for every client" is a good, honest answer — far stronger than claiming a process you do not have. Then fix the gap: a steriliser is one of the cheapest trust-building purchases a salon can make. If a client ever reports a problem after a visit, take it seriously, offer to look into it and to make it right.

## The quiet payoff

Clients who ask about hygiene are the clients you most want. They are careful, they notice detail, and when they choose you they stay — because they chose you for trust, not for a discount. They are also the clients who tell their friends, in exact detail, why your salon is the safe one.

So welcome the question. Answer it with specifics, show the proof, and close with a time. The client who asked "Do you sterilise your tools?" was giving you the easiest booking you will get all week.
