Most salon owners in the UAE have a complicated relationship with the word "free". Give something away and the wrong people queue up. Give nothing away and a new client who has never sat in your chair has no reason to choose you over the salon next door. The answer is not to avoid free offers. It is to design one properly.

A free taster is a short, real taste of your best work — a ten-minute blow-dry, a brow tidy, a hand massage. It costs you fifteen minutes of a stylist's time in an hour you could not sell anyway. In return, a stranger walks out knowing exactly how good you are. Done well, it is the cheapest new-client marketing a salon can run.

## Pick a taster that shows your best work

The biggest mistake is offering the taster that is easiest for you rather than the one that sells your salon. A quick trim shows almost nothing. A client who has just had a beautiful blow-dry walks out of your door and straight into a mall full of people who can see the result. A brow tidy changes a face in five minutes. A hand massage lets a client feel your standard of care without any commitment at all.

Choose one taster, not five. One clear offer is easy to explain on WhatsApp, easy to put on a poster in the window, and easy for your team to deliver the same way every time. The taster should be the service you are proudest of, the one that makes a first-time visitor think: "I want the full version of this."

## Put tasters in the hours you cannot sell

A free taster should never take a chair from a paying client. That is the rule that keeps the whole idea profitable. Offer tasters only in the hours your diary already leaves empty — the Tuesday morning, the weekday lunch hour, the first hour after opening. In a Dubai mall salon that might be the 11 AM slot before the lunchtime wave. In a community salon it might be the quiet Wednesday afternoon.

When you put the taster in a dead hour, the real cost drops to almost nothing. The stylist is already there. The chair is already empty. What you are really spending is attention, and attention in a quiet hour is not scarce.

Be honest about the timing when you advertise it. "Free blow-dry tasters, Tuesday to Thursday, 11 AM to 1 PM" is a real offer. "Free blow-dry" with no time attached is an invitation to turn up on Saturday at 6 PM and be disappointed.

## Make the taster feel like a real appointment

A taster fails when it feels like a freebie. The client is squeezed in, rushed, and sent on her way with a price list. She leaves remembering the rush, not the result.

Treat the taster like a real appointment. Book it in the diary with a name and a phone number. Send the same confirmation message you send a paying client. Greet her properly, offer the same welcome drink, and give the stylist the full fifteen minutes without interruption. The point of the taster is to show her what a normal visit feels like.

Take one honest photo of the result — with permission — and send it to her on WhatsApp the same evening. She now has something to show a friend, and you have a reason to message her again without it feeling like a sales pitch.

## The follow-up that turns a taster into a regular

Most salons lose the taster client in the forty-eight hours after the visit. She liked the salon, she meant to come back, and then life happened. A single well-timed message closes that gap.

Send one message the next day. Thank her by name, mention the service she tried, and offer two specific slots for the full version — "Thursday at 5 PM or Saturday at 11 AM" — instead of asking her to pick a time. Two concrete options are far easier to answer than an open question.

Do not send a second message if she does not reply. One gentle follow-up is good service. Three is pressure, and pressure is exactly what a new client does not want from a salon she has visited once.

## The rules that stop tasters being exploited

Every salon owner worries about the same thing: the same person coming back for free treatment after free treatment. Three simple rules solve it.

First, one taster per person, ever. Say so kindly at booking: "The taster is a one-time introduction, so you can try us out." Clients accept this easily when it is stated upfront.

Second, book tasters in advance only. No walk-in tasters. A booking means you have a name and a number, and it means the client has committed a small amount of effort, which filters out almost all of the people who would take advantage.

Third, never offer the taster to your existing regulars as a freebie. A regular who hears about a free blow-dry will quite reasonably ask why she is paying. Keep tasters strictly for people who have never visited. Your regulars get loyalty in other ways — priority booking, a remembered preference, a small gesture on their birthday.
