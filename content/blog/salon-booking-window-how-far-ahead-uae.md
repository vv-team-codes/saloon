Every salon owner in Dubai knows two opposite complaints. The first comes from a regular who tried to book three weeks ahead and was told the diary was not open yet. The second comes from a diary full of appointments made two months ago, half of which will be moved or forgotten by the time the day arrives.

Both problems have the same cause: the booking window — how far into the future clients are allowed to book. Get it right and the diary fills steadily, with fewer gaps and fewer surprises. Get it wrong and you spend every week chasing confirmations or turning away clients who wanted to plan ahead.

## Why the booking window matters

A booking is a small commitment. The longer the gap between booking and appointment, the weaker that commitment becomes. Life intervenes: travel plans change, work shifts move, a school event appears. In the UAE, where many clients travel often and family schedules shift with school terms, a booking made eight weeks out is a guess, not a plan.

At the same time, clients who like to organise their lives want to book ahead. A working mother in Dubai Marina plans her week on a Sunday evening. A regular who gets her roots done every five weeks wants her next slot secured before she leaves the chair. If your diary only opens a week ahead, these clients cannot plan with you.

## The sweet spot: two to four weeks

For a typical ladies' or unisex salon in Dubai or Abu Dhabi, a booking window of two to four weeks works best. Two weeks is enough for regulars to plan their next visit. Four weeks is enough for clients who like to book a month ahead without stretching so far that plans go stale.

Start with three weeks if you are unsure. Watch what happens over a month. If clients keep asking to book further out, extend it to four weeks. If you spend too much time chasing confirmations for bookings made weeks ago, pull it back to two.

The right window also depends on your services. A salon that mostly does cuts, blow-dries and express treatments can run happily on two weeks. A salon with a heavy colour book, bridal work or long treatments like keratin needs longer, because those clients plan further ahead.

## When a longer window genuinely makes sense

Some bookings deserve a longer horizon. Bridal trials and wedding-morning bookings are the obvious ones: a bride planning her wedding will book her hair and makeup months ahead, and turning her away because the diary is closed is a lost booking you will not win back.

The same applies to standing slots, the same time every week or fortnight. Once a regular has a standing appointment, the window stops mattering for her, because her slot is already protected. Group bookings and bridal parties belong here too, since they need coordination across several stylists and are usually arranged weeks in advance.

Keep these exceptions deliberate rather than letting the whole diary drift open. Keep the standard window at three or four weeks, and open longer dates only for the bookings that need them. That way the diary stays honest: most of what you see is real, and the far-future bookings are the ones that were always going to hold.

## The hidden cost of opening too far ahead

A diary open two or three months ahead looks successful. Chairs appear full, weekends look booked, and the owner feels calm. But a full diary is not the same as a full salon. Every appointment booked eight weeks out carries a higher chance of moving, and every move costs your front desk time.

There is a second cost owners rarely notice. When regulars can book months ahead, they block the best slots early: Saturday mornings, Thursday evenings, the popular stylist. New clients who try to book for next week find nothing available and go elsewhere. You end up with a diary that is full on paper but loses you the new clients who would have become regulars.

Then there is the no-show problem. A client who booked two months ago is far more likely to forget than one who booked last Tuesday. If your no-show rate climbs whenever the diary is open far ahead, the window is the first thing to check.

## How to change your window without chaos

Pick a date to make the change and tell the team first, so everyone gives clients the same answer. Then announce it the same way you would announce anything else: a short message on WhatsApp, a line on your booking page, a mention at the chair.

A practical rhythm works well for many UAE salons. Open the diary in weekly blocks, every Monday, for the week that starts three weeks from now. Clients get used to the rhythm quickly, and your front desk gets one small weekly task instead of a constant stream of "when can I book?" messages. If you use online booking, set the window in the system itself so the rule enforces itself.

## Plan longer for Ramadan, Eid and National Day

Some weeks in the UAE calendar deserve a longer window. Before Ramadan, clients book evening appointments weeks in advance, because the hours after iftar become the busiest of the year. Before Eid, the diary fills faster than any other time, and salons that open early capture the rush while others are still deciding. The same applies to the National Day weekend and the winter wedding season, when bridal parties and event bookings land early.

For these periods, open the diary six to eight weeks ahead, announce it once, and let demand do the rest. Then return to your normal window when the rush passes.

## A simple rule to start with

Open your diary three weeks ahead, protect the exceptions that need longer, and review the window once a quarter. Ask two questions. Are clients asking to book further out? And are bookings made more than a month ahead actually turning up? If the first answer is yes, extend the window. If the second answer is no, shorten it.

Your booking window is a small, quiet decision that shapes how full your chairs are and how calm your front desk feels. Get it right and the diary starts working for you instead of against you.
