It usually arrives as a short WhatsApp message, often late in the evening: "Can I get a VAT invoice for today's appointment?" Or the client asks at the till, card already in hand, "Can I have a proper receipt for this, please?"

Many salon owners freeze at that moment. It feels like an admin question, maybe even a suspicious one. It is neither. A client asking for a VAT invoice is telling you something useful: she is organised, she probably expenses her beauty spend or claims it back, and she intends to come back. How you answer decides whether that trust grows or quietly dies. The reasons clients ask are almost always good ones: a tourist who can reclaim VAT through the refund scheme, a corporate client whose company reimburses her visits, a regular who keeps careful records, or a gift buyer who paid for someone else's appointment. None of them is trying to catch you out — they are asking for something a well-run business should be able to give in minutes.

## What a proper UAE tax invoice actually shows

A tax invoice in the UAE is not just a till receipt with your logo on it. It needs your salon's legal name and address, your Tax Registration Number (TRN), a unique invoice number, the date, and an itemised list of the services with the price of each. It must show the VAT amount separately — 5% on the taxable services — and the total the client paid.

If your salon is not VAT-registered, you cannot issue a tax invoice — but you can issue a simple receipt with your salon name, the date, the services and the amount paid. That is honest and usually enough for a client who just wants a record. The mistake is to stall, to say "we will send it later" and never do, or to hand over a handwritten note with no details at all.

Know which of the two documents your salon can issue before anyone asks. Print a small template, keep it at the till, and make sure every member of the team knows where it lives. A request that takes thirty seconds to fulfil builds confidence. One that takes three days of chasing destroys it.

## Answer in one message, not three

Most of these enquiries arrive on WhatsApp, often after hours, and the reply matters as much as the document. The client is not asking for a policy lecture. She wants a yes, a time, and a detail or two.

A reply that works sounds like this: "Of course — I can send your tax invoice on WhatsApp within the hour. Could you confirm the name it should be under, and is it for today's facial and blow-dry?" That one message says yes immediately, gives a time, and asks for the only two details you actually need.

If the invoice needs the company name rather than the client's own name, ask for it now. If the client needs it in a particular format — a PDF, for example — offer the PDF without being asked. Small courtesies like that are what turn a one-time tourist into a client who books every time she lands in Dubai.

## When you genuinely cannot issue one

Sometimes the honest answer is no, and a clear no is far better than a vague maybe. A salon below the VAT registration threshold cannot issue a tax invoice. A salon that took cash with no record cannot invent a receipt for a visit from three months ago. Say so plainly, in the same message, and offer what you can: a receipt for today's visit, or a stamped confirmation of the booking and the amount.

## Tourists and VAT refunds — say only what is true

Be careful with the tourist refund question, because it is easy to overpromise. Your salon can issue a correct tax invoice. It cannot guarantee a refund, and it should never claim to. The refund is handled at the airport through the official Tax Free system, and it depends on the tourist's purchases, her paperwork and the scheme's rules.

The right answer is simple: "Here is your tax invoice with our TRN. You can present it at the Tax Free counter at the airport." That is accurate, helpful, and it protects you. Do not tell a client she will "definitely get the VAT back," and do not fill in refund forms on her behalf.

## Make it a system, not a favour

The salons that handle this well do not treat each request as a one-off favour from the owner. They have a system: one person per shift who knows how to issue the invoice, a template saved on the till computer or the salon phone, and a rule that every invoice goes out within the same day.

Keep a simple log — date, client name, invoice number, amount. It takes seconds and it saves hours when a client messages two weeks later asking for a copy. ## A receipt is a reason to return

Here is the part owners miss: the invoice is not just paperwork. It is a touchpoint. A client who receives a clean, professional invoice within the hour remembers a salon that is organised. A tourist who gets one without asking twice books again on her next trip and tells the friend she is travelling with. A corporate client who can expense her visits without chasing paperwork chooses your salon over the one down the road, month after month.

Treat the request as a compliment, not an interruption. Answer fast, issue the right document, and file the record. The client asked for proof that your salon is a proper business. Give it to her — and she will give you her loyalty in return.
