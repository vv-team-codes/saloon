Every salon owner in the UAE knows the same quiet problem. Your services are good, your team is skilled, yet some weeks the diary has gaps that no amount of posting the same haircut photo will fill. Clients scroll past it because they have seen it before. What they have not seen is a reason to book this week, rather than next month.

A seasonal menu gives them that reason: a small, limited-time set of treatments built around the season your clients are living in — the dry winter air, the summer heat, the festive calendar. It gives you something genuine to announce and a deadline that makes clients decide now instead of someday.

## Why a seasonal menu sells better than a discount

Discounts train clients to wait: a twenty percent off post this month teaches them to ask what the offer is next month, and the clients you win on price are the first to leave when another salon goes cheaper. A seasonal menu does the opposite — it sells on novelty and timing, not on a lower number.

A winter hydration ritual or a summer cooling head massage is worth booking because it exists only for a few weeks and answers a problem the client feels right now: tight, dry skin in December, or heavy, sweaty hair in July. The price can stay at full value because the value is obvious — the client is comparing how her skin feels today with how it could feel after the treatment, not with the salon down the road.

It also protects your quiet hours. Offer the limited-time service first in the slots you most need to fill — Tuesday mornings, Sunday afternoons — before opening it at peak times. Clients who want it will take the quiet slot, and your Saturday evening diary stays free for regulars.

## Start with the season your clients are in

The best seasonal menus come from the UAE calendar, not from a trend spotted online.

Winter, from November to February, is hydration season. The air is cooler but dry, and clients arrive with tight skin, static hair and chapped lips. A deep-hydration facial, a hot-oil hair ritual and a rich hand treatment fit naturally here, and a blow-dry and glow package suits party season.

Summer, from June to September, is the opposite problem. Clients want cooling, lightness and frizz control: a scalp-cooling treatment, a lightweight hydrating facial and a keratin top-up for humidity-proof hair.

Then there are the occasions. The weeks before Eid, Diwali, Christmas and National Day are the busiest beauty weeks of the year, and a festive menu — henna and glow packages, party hair, family booking bundles — lets you sell the preparation rather than just the appointment.

Pick one season and one problem, and build around it. A menu that tries to cover everything ends up saying nothing.

## Build the menu: three to five services, one hero

Keep it short: three to five services is enough for a client to choose quickly and for your team to deliver well. More than that and it becomes your normal price list with a festive heading.

Give it one hero service — the treatment the whole menu is built around. It should be the most photogenic, the most seasonal, and the best margin. Everything else supports it: a shorter express version, a paired service that completes the result, and a home-care product the client takes away.

Price each service honestly. Work out the product cost, the stylist's time and the slot it occupies, then set a price you can defend without a discount. If you want an incentive, make it a bundle — hero treatment plus the express add-on for a combined price that is still profitable — rather than cutting the price of the hero itself.

## Name it and photograph it like a product

A seasonal menu needs a name, because a name is what clients remember and repeat. "The Winter Glow Ritual" or "The Summer Cool-Down" is enough. Say it the same way everywhere, and put it on every message, post and price card for the season. Photograph the hero treatment in your own chairs, with a real client or team member, and use that one image everywhere so the menu is recognisable at a glance.

## Launch it on WhatsApp first

Your best launch channel is the one your clients already answer: WhatsApp. Announce the menu there before you post it anywhere else, because your existing clients are the easiest bookings you will ever make.

Send it in three waves. First, a WhatsApp Status with the hero photo, the name, the price and the end date, posted in the late afternoon when clients are scrolling after work. Second, a short broadcast message to clients who had the related service recently. Third, a personal message to your most regular clients, offering them the first quiet-hour slots.

Each message should end with one clear next step: a day, a time and a way to book. "The Winter Glow Ritual is available until 15 February — reply with a day and I will hold a slot for you" will book more chairs than a beautiful post with no instruction. ## Measure it, then retire it on time

A seasonal menu only works if it ends. Put the end date on everything from the first announcement, and when the date arrives, take the menu down — even if it is selling well. Scarcity is the whole point.

Track four numbers while it runs: how many clients booked the hero service, how many of those were new, how many quiet-hour slots it filled, and how many clients rebooked a regular service afterwards. Keep the notes. Next year the same season will come around again, and a menu that worked once — refreshed with a new hero and a new name — is the easiest marketing you will ever repeat.

The salons that fill their chairs through the quiet months are rarely the ones shouting loudest. They are the ones giving clients a fresh reason to book, at the moment the season gives them one. Build the menu, name it, launch it to the clients who already trust you.
