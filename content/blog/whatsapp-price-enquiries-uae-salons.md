Open WhatsApp on any salon phone in Dubai or Abu Dhabi and you will see the same first message again and again: "How much is balayage?" Or a haircut, a facial, a bridal package. The client has not asked about availability or location. Price comes first, and how you answer in the next few minutes often decides whether that person ever sits in your chair.

Many salons lose this enquiry without realising it. The reply comes hours later, or it says "DM for prices", or it drops a long price list with no context. The client, comparing three salons at once, simply books the one that answered clearly first.

## Why price is always the first question

For UAE salon clients, price is a filter, not a negotiation tactic. A newcomer does not know whether a haircut costs 80 or 300 dirhams, and a regular wants to know whether your salon sits in their usual range before investing time in a consultation. Clients also compare quickly: they message two or three salons in the same evening, especially before a wedding or a holiday, and the salon that replies with a clear, friendly answer earns the visit.

There is another reason price comes first: uncertainty. A balayage on short hair is a different job from a balayage on long, previously coloured hair, and clients ask the price because they want reassurance there will be no surprise at the till. Your answer should give them that reassurance.

## The three replies that lose the booking

The first losing reply is silence. A price question left unanswered overnight is usually a lost client, because the client has moved on by morning. This is especially true of evening enquiries, which arrive in a rush after work, when clients finally have time to plan their weekend appointments.

The second is "DM for prices" or "price on consultation". It sounds exclusive, but to a client comparing options it reads as evasive. They want a number they can plan around, and if they have to work for the price, they will ask a salon that gives it freely.

The third is the full menu dump: a screenshot of a forty-service price list with no guidance. The client asked about one service and received homework, and will not reply asking which line applies to them.

## Answer with a range, not a single number

The strongest habit is to answer every price question with a range, plus one short sentence explaining what moves the price within that range. For example: "Balayage starts at AED 350 for short hair and goes up to AED 550 for long or thick hair, including wash, toner and blow-dry." That one sentence gives a number, explains the variation, and states what is included.

Ranges protect you too. A flat quote given over chat can trap you when the client arrives with hair that needs twice the product and time. A range, stated upfront, lets you confirm the exact price at the consultation without the client feeling misled. But the range must be narrow enough to be useful: 200 to 900 dirhams is not an answer, it is another question.

## Ask two questions, no more

Before finalising the price, ask at most two quick questions that genuinely change it — hair length and previous colour for colour services, skin concern and previous experience for facials — in the same message as the price range, not as a separate interrogation.

This is where many salons go wrong: they send a questionnaire. Two questions show expertise; ten questions feel like friction. If the answer truly needs an in-person look, say so plainly: "For an exact price we would need a quick 5-minute consultation, which is free, and we have slots today at 4 pm or tomorrow at 11 am."

## Anchor the price to what the client gets

A number on its own invites comparison with the cheapest salon in the area. A number attached to value invites a different comparison. Mention, briefly, what the price includes — the consultation, the products, the blow-dry, the aftercare advice — and the stylist level if your salon prices by seniority, because a client paying for a senior colourist judges the price differently.

## Always end with a next step

Every price reply should end with one easy action. Offer two specific appointment times rather than asking "when would you like to come?" A client choosing between Tuesday at 6 pm and Wednesday at 11 am is already halfway booked. If you use online booking, send the link with the time options, so the client can finish the job in one tap.

If the client goes quiet after your price, follow up once, the next day, with a short nudge: "Just checking whether you would like me to hold a slot for the balayage this week." One follow-up recovers a surprising number of bookings; three follow-ups annoy people.

## What about price questions after hours?

A large share of price enquiries arrive when the salon is closed or the front desk is busy with clients in the chair. Evening and late-night scrolling are peak enquiry times in the UAE, and a price question sent at 11 pm is often from someone ready to book tomorrow. If nobody answers until the following afternoon, that booking has usually gone elsewhere. ## Keep a note of what people keep asking

Price enquiries are also free market research. If ten people a week ask about a head spa or a bridal package you do not list, that is demand telling you something. Note the most-asked prices each week, update your replies and price list to match, and keep one shared set of ranges so every team member quotes the same numbers. When two staff members quote different prices for the same service, clients notice, and trust drops fast.

Think of the price reply as the front door of your salon. A clear range, two smart questions, a brief note on what is included, and a concrete next step: that is a welcome. Clients in the UAE have plenty of salons to choose from, and they will choose the one that makes booking feel easy. Answer the price question well, and you will win not just that booking, but the client who comes back and brings a friend.
