Your salon already posts. Reels of transformations, Stories of the day's work, a grid full of good hair. The likes arrive, the comments are kind, and yet the diary on Tuesday afternoon still has empty chairs. The problem is not your content. It is that watching is passive. A follower can admire your work for a year and never once feel involved in it.

A social media challenge changes the relationship. Instead of asking people to watch, you ask them to do something small for seven days. Each day has a prompt, each entry is tagged, and each participant shows up in front of their own friends. A challenge turns quiet followers into a visible crowd — and the crowd into bookings. It costs almost nothing: a prize, a hashtag, and about an hour of attention a day.

## Pick a challenge your clients can actually finish

The most common mistake is building the challenge around your services. Nobody joins a seven-day advert. People join something that makes them feel good and looks easy from the outside. Pick one theme — seven days of glow, a hair-health week, a self-care reset — and design seven prompts that each take under two minutes.

Day one decides everything. If the first task feels like homework, nobody comes back for day two. So make day one the easiest thing on the list: a bare-faced selfie in morning light, a photo of a hair wash, a glass of water on the bedside table. Once someone has posted once and been reposted by your salon, finishing the week becomes a matter of pride.

## Write all seven days before you launch

Never invent the prompts as you go. Write the whole week in one sitting, then check each day against a simple test: could a busy mum do this between school drop-off and work, and could a client do it without buying anything? If the answer is no, rewrite the day.

A simple structure works well. Days one to three are easy home habits — cleanse, protect, hydrate. Day four is the salon day: a visit, a mini treatment, or a photo from the chair. Days five and six build on the result — the glow, the shine, the finished look. Day seven is the reveal: a before-and-after side by side, with the participant's own words about what changed. That final day is your best marketing asset, because it is a real client saying a real thing.

Launch on a Sunday evening, when the scroll is heaviest, and run Monday to Sunday. Publish the rules in a single post and pin it: how to enter, the hashtag, the closing time, and the prize. One hashtag, carrying your salon's name, so every entry is findable in one place. Announce the winner live on Stories the following Monday, while the week is still fresh in everyone's mind.

## The prize should book a chair, not give one away

Do not give away a year of free services. A prize that big attracts people who will never pay, and it teaches your real clients that your work should be free. The winner gets one signature treatment, in one chair, on a weekday slot you choose.

The smarter prize is the one every finisher gets. Offer everyone who completes all seven days a small reward tied to a booking — an express add-on free with their next visit, or a weekday blow-dry at a set price, valid for fourteen days. The deadline matters: without one, the reward sits in a screenshot folder forever. With one, it becomes a fortnight of bookings after the challenge ends. Film the winner's prize visit, with their permission, and you have the opening post for the next challenge.

## Make joining effortless

Every extra step halves your entries. Entry should be one action: post, tag the salon, add the hashtag. That is all.

Two details matter in the UAE. First, allow entry by WhatsApp as well as by public post. Many clients here value privacy and will happily send you a photo while refusing to post it themselves. Accept the WhatsApp entry, repost it only with permission, and you double your pool without anyone feeling exposed. Second, repost every entry to your Stories the same day it arrives. People join when they see other people joining. Your own team should take part too — the salon's feed should look like it is in the challenge, not just running it.

## The follow-up is where the bookings live

A challenge that ends with a thank-you post wastes the warmest leads a salon will ever get. These are people who spent a week thinking about their skin or their hair, in public, with your salon's name attached. Message every finisher personally within two days of the final day. Thank them by name, mention one thing you noticed about their entries, and offer their reward with two real slots attached.

Keep a simple list as entries arrive: name, number of days completed, and any service they mentioned. That list is the whole point of the exercise. The challenge creates the demand; the follow-up catches it.

## What to measure, and what to ignore

Count five numbers and ignore the rest. How many people entered. How many finished all seven days. How many of the finishers booked within two weeks. How many of those bookings were new clients rather than regulars. And what the whole thing cost, including the prize. A challenge with thirty finishers and twelve bookings is a success. A challenge with three thousand likes and no bookings is a nice week on Instagram and nothing else.

Run the format once a quarter and change the theme each time. The structure stays the same, so each run gets easier, and your followers start to recognise it. After the third one, clients will ask when the next challenge starts — and that is the moment a marketing idea becomes a salon tradition.
