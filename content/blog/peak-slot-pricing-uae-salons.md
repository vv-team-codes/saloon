Every UAE salon owner knows the shape of the week. Saturday is booked wall to wall, and weekday evenings from 6 PM fill up fast, because that is when working clients are finally free. Tuesday at 11 AM? The chairs sit empty.

But those two slots are not the same product. A Saturday 6 PM haircut and a Tuesday 11 AM haircut take the same scissors, time and skill — but one is scarce and the other is plentiful. Charging the same price for both leaves money on the table at the weekend and chairs empty on weekdays. Peak-slot pricing fixes both at once.

## Why peak slots are genuinely worth more

The honest reason is demand. In the UAE, Saturday is the busiest salon day, and the evening rush from 6 PM to 9 PM is the busiest part of most days. Clients who want those hours compete for limited chairs. When demand is high and supply is fixed, the price should reflect that — as airlines, hotels and restaurants have done for years.

Peak hours are also your most expensive hours to run: your best stylists are on shift, the salon is fully staffed, and every minute at 7 PM on a Saturday could have gone to another paying client. A quiet Tuesday morning costs far less in lost opportunity.

## The principle: price the slot, not the service

Peak-slot pricing does not mean raising all your prices. Keep your standard price for most of the diary and add a small premium only to the hours clients fight over.

A workable structure: your standard price applies to weekday mornings and early afternoons, and a modest premium — AED 10 to AED 25 on a typical service, or roughly 10 to 15 percent — applies to weekday evenings after 6 PM and to Saturdays. Or keep it simpler still: one peak price and one off-peak price on the service menu. Keep the premium small. It must be large enough that some clients choose a quieter hour instead, and small enough that nobody feels punished for booking on a Saturday. A gentle nudge is the goal, not a penalty.

## What it does to your diary

The first effect is the obvious one: more revenue from hours that were already full. Twenty Saturday appointments at a AED 15 premium is AED 300 of extra income from a day that was already booked out — at no extra cost.

The second effect changes the business. Some clients will look at the peak price and decide Tuesday at 11 AM suits them after all. That is not a lost booking; it is a booking moved to an hour that was earning nothing. Over a month, a handful of moved appointments each week turns dead weekday mornings into paying hours. Pair the premium with calm, unhurried off-peak visits, and clients have a reason to move.

Third, when peak slots cost slightly more, clients treat them more seriously — a Saturday 6 PM booking is one a client is less likely to forget or cancel late. That quietly reduces the no-shows that hurt most: the ones in hours you could have sold twice over.

## Protect the regulars first

The fastest way to ruin this idea is to spring it on the clients who keep your salon alive. Regulars should hear it from you first, personally, with a grace period. A simple rule: regulars with a standing appointment keep their current price for two months, and the new peak price applies to new bookings after that. Tell them at the chair. One honest sentence is enough: the busiest hours carry a small premium, so weekday mornings stay at the usual price.

## Announce it honestly, everywhere it matters

Peak pricing only works if clients know about it before they book. The worst version is a client who discovers the premium at checkout, feels tricked, and never comes back. Put both prices on your printed and WhatsApp service menu: standard and peak. When a client asks for a Saturday evening, the reply states the price in the same message as the slot. Give the team one sentence: peak hours carry a small premium because they are the hours everyone wants.

After-hours enquiries matter too. Many clients message late at night, asking for a Saturday slot and its price. If nobody replies until the morning, they have already messaged the salon down the road. ## Mistakes that kill the idea

A few traps catch almost every salon that tries this. First, making the premium too big: a 30 or 40 percent jump turns a nudge into a wall, and clients will simply go elsewhere. Start small. Second, hiding the price. If the premium only appears at checkout, or only on some services, clients will assume the worst. Every service, every channel, the same two prices. Third, forgetting to review it. After a month, check the numbers: how many appointments moved to quiet hours, how Saturday revenue changed, and whether any regulars complained. If nothing moved, the premium is too small. If clients are angry, it is too big or was communicated badly.

## A 30-day starter plan

Week one, decide the shape: which hours count as peak, and the premium. For most UAE salons, that means Saturday all day and weekday evenings from 6 PM, at AED 10 to AED 25 per service. Week two, tell your regulars first, face to face, with the two-month grace period, and update the printed menu, the WhatsApp service list and the confirmation wording. Week three, go live for new bookings, with every reply stating the peak price alongside the slot. Week four, check the diary: how many appointments landed in quiet hours, and what clients said. If the week is flattening — fewer empty Tuesday mornings, a Saturday queue that is busy but not bursting — the idea is working.

Your diary is your most valuable asset, and its hours are not all equal. Pricing them as if they were is the quietest leak in most UAE salons: full on Saturday, empty on Tuesday, with nothing moving clients between the two. A small, honest peak premium gives clients a reason to choose the quiet hour, gives your busiest hours the value they already have, and gives your week a shape that pays all the way through it.
