It is one of the first questions a new client asks on WhatsApp, usually before price or availability: "How long will it last?" She might mean a balayage, a set of lashes or a keratin treatment. Whatever the service, she is really asking one thing: is this worth my money?

How a UAE salon answers decides whether she books — and whether she comes back happy or leaves a disappointed review. A vague promise wins the booking and loses the client. An honest range, explained well, builds the trust that turns a first visit into a regular one.

## Why she asks before she books

Three reasons lie behind the question, and a good reply speaks to all of them.

First, value. A treatment that lasts eight weeks is a very different purchase from one that fades in two, even at the same price.

Second, planning. A bride asking about a facial wants to know when to book it before the wedding; a client flying home for the summer wants to know whether her colour will survive the trip. The question is often about a date on her calendar, not just the service.

Third, fear. She may have paid elsewhere for a treatment that faded in days, and she does not want to be disappointed again. When she asks how long it lasts, she is asking whether she can trust your salon.

## The golden rule: ranges, never guarantees

No honest salon can promise an exact number of weeks. Hair grows at different speeds, skin reacts differently, and life in the UAE — strong sun, humidity, sea water, chlorinated pools and hard tap water — shortens almost every beauty result. A reply that pretends otherwise is a complaint waiting to happen.

Answer with a range, and name the two or three things that move a client toward its long end. "Balayage usually lasts eight to twelve weeks. You will get closer to twelve with a sulphate-free shampoo, cooler water and a toner at around week six."

That reply sets a realistic expectation, shows expertise, and quietly books the next visit — the toner — before she has even had the first one.

## Honest ranges your team should know

Your whole team should give the same honest answer for the services you offer most — keep these ranges as a guide and adjust them to your own products.

A full colour or root touch-up generally holds for four to six weeks, limited by regrowth rather than fading. Balayage and lived-in colour stretch further — eight to twelve weeks — because the grow-out is softer by design. A keratin or smoothing treatment usually lasts three to five months, depending on wash frequency and heat styling.

A full set of lash extensions needs a fill every two to three weeks as natural lashes shed; a client who sleeps on her face will be back sooner, and saying so upfront is a kindness. Gel and BIAB nails typically hold for three to four weeks before growth at the cuticle gives them away. Brow lamination and lash lifts last around four to six weeks; a facial glow is brightest for the first week, which is why timing matters for events.

Use your own numbers if your salon's experience differs — clients remember a salon that told them the truth.

## The four-part reply that books

The best replies follow the same shape every time, whatever the service.

One, give the range. Two, name what affects it in her life — swimming, sun, washing habits, heat styling. Three, say what your salon does to make it last longer, whether a bond treatment or a specific aftercare product. Four, close with two real slots.

"Keratin usually lasts three to five months. It lasts longest washing two or three times a week with a sulphate-free shampoo — we include one with every treatment. Would you like Thursday at 6 or Saturday at 11? We will book your first aftercare check while you are here."

Notice the message never promises five months. It gives a range, explains why, and ends with a booking.

## What never to say

Never promise the top of the range as a guarantee — "it will last six months" — because the one client it lasts four months for will feel cheated. Never blame the client when a result fades early; ask how she cared for it, and offer a fix before she has to ask.

Never answer with a single bare number. "Four weeks" tells her nothing about why, and it invites comparison with the salon down the road that promised six. And never use the question to push an expensive product she does not need — recommend one aftercare product, explain what it does, and let her decide.

## Aftercare is part of the answer

Often the honest answer to "how long will it last?" is "it depends on what you do after you leave the chair." That is not a dodge — it is the truth, and a service opportunity if you handle it well.

Send a short aftercare message on WhatsApp the same evening: what to avoid for 48 hours, which product to use, and when to come back. Clients who receive that message look after their results better and come back sooner. A client who got twelve weeks instead of ten by following your advice will tell her friends — and so will the one who got four weeks and no guidance.

## Log the question — it is a rebooking engine

Longevity enquiries are also a diary tool. Every time a client asks how long something lasts, you learn when she expects to be back — note it on her record, and the next appointment is already half-booked.

When a lash client leaves, agree the fill date before she reaches the door. When a colour client leaves, put the toner date in the diary. The question she asked before booking becomes the reason she returns on time — and regulars who return on time are the steadiest revenue a salon has.
