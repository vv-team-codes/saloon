A salon owner in Dubai once told me she spent AED 3,000 on Google Ads in a month and got almost nothing back — just a report full of clicks and a lighter bank balance. Her conclusion was that Google Ads does not work for salons. Her conclusion was wrong. Her campaign was wrong. There is a big difference, and it is the difference between paying for attention and paying for bookings.

Google Ads works for UAE salons because of one simple fact: people search for a salon exactly when they want one. Nobody Googles "balayage near me" on a Tuesday afternoon for fun. They are ready to book, and the ad puts your salon in front of them at the moment they decide.

## Start With Search, Not Display

Google sells many kinds of ads. Most are wrong for a salon. Display ads are the banners that follow people around the internet; YouTube ads play before videos. These suit big brands with big budgets, but a small salon does not need to remind the whole internet it exists. It needs the ten people in its neighbourhood who are searching right now.

Search ads are the short text ads that appear above Google's results when someone types a query. They are the only place to start. Set up one campaign, in English, targeting your city within five to ten kilometres of your salon. That radius matters. A client in Sharjah is not going to drive to your salon in Dubai Marina for a haircut, and every click from outside your area is money wasted.

Then choose your keywords with discipline. Bid on phrases that signal intent: "salon near me", "balayage Dubai", "men's haircut JLT", "keratin treatment Abu Dhabi", "bridal makeup Dubai". Avoid broad words like "beauty" or "hair" on their own — they attract curiosity, not bookings, and curiosity clicks still cost you money.

## Your First AED 1,000: Where It Actually Goes

Think of your first AED 1,000 as a test budget, not a marketing budget. Its job is to answer one question: does a click from Google turn into a booking at your salon, and at what cost?

Start with AED 30 to 40 a day, five days a week. That keeps the campaign alive long enough to learn something, and it caps your risk at a few hundred dirhams if something is misconfigured. In most UAE salon markets, a click costs between AED 2 and AED 8, so AED 1,000 should buy roughly 150 to 400 clicks. If none of them books, you have learned something important about your offer or your landing page — and you have learned it cheaply.

Here is the part most salon owners skip: do not send people to your homepage. A person who searched for "keratin treatment Dubai" should land on a page about keratin treatment, with the price range, the time it takes, a photo of real work, and a WhatsApp button they can tap in one second. Every extra click between the ad and the booking is a chance to lose them.

Timing matters too. Your ads do not need to run at 3am. In the UAE, salon searches spike in the late morning, again around lunchtime, and hardest in the early evening when people finish work. Schedule your ads for the hours your salon is open and staffed to answer. A click at 11pm that nobody answers until 10am the next day is a booking that went to your competitor.

## Measure Bookings, Not Clicks

Google will happily report impressions, clicks and click-through rates. None of those pay your rent. The only number that matters is cost per booking: how many dirhams of ad spend did it take to produce one confirmed appointment? To know that, connect the click to the chair: send every ad to a WhatsApp chat, ask each new client "How did you hear about us?", and keep a daily tally. Ten enquiries from Google, four bookings, AED 350 spent — that is AED 87.50 per booking. If your average client spends AED 250 and returns four times a year, that is very good business.

Google's automated suggestions will keep nudging you to raise your budget and broaden your keywords. These suggestions are designed to increase Google's revenue, not yours, so review every recommendation before accepting it.

## When Google Ads Is the Wrong Spend

Google Ads is not right for every salon at every moment. If your salon is already fully booked at peak evening hours, buying more clicks for those same hours is pointless — you would be paying to turn people away. Spend instead on filling your quiet hours: Tuesday mornings, weekday afternoons, the long stretch after the weekend rush.

It is also the wrong spend if nobody answers the phone. An ad that generates a WhatsApp message at 7pm is worthless if the reply arrives at 10am the next day, because by then the client has booked somewhere else. Before you spend a single dirham, make sure someone on your team owns enquiries for every hour your ads run. ## A Sensible First Month

Here is a simple plan for your first four weeks. Week one: one search campaign, one service you do brilliantly, a five-kilometre radius, AED 30 a day. Week two: check which keywords brought real enquiries and pause the ones that only brought clicks. Week three: add a second service page if the first one is converting. Week four: sit down with your tally and work out your cost per booking. If it is lower than the value of a new client to your salon, keep going and raise the budget slowly. If it is not, you have spent AED 1,000 to learn that your money works harder somewhere else — and that is a cheap lesson.

Google Ads will not save a salon with no reviews, no clear prices and nobody answering the phone. But for a salon that has those basics in place, it is one of the most honest forms of marketing available: you pay only when someone searches for exactly what you offer, in your area, at the moment they want it. Spend small, measure bookings, and let the numbers decide whether you spend more.
