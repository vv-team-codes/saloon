It is 9 PM and your salon closed an hour ago. A message arrives on WhatsApp: "Hi, I think I left my gold earring at your salon today. It was my mother's. Please can you check?" The client is worried, and she is messaging you instead of the salon down the road because she trusts you with the question. How you answer in the next ten minutes matters more than the earring itself.

Lost-property messages arrive more often than most salon owners realise. Earrings slip off during a wash, a watch comes off before a facial, a phone slides between the chair and the wall. The client usually notices at home, hours later, and WhatsApp is the first place she turns. The salons that handle these messages well do not just return belongings — they earn a kind of trust no discount ever buys.

## Reply fast, even when you do not know yet

The worst reply to a lost-property message is silence, and the second worst is a vague promise to check tomorrow. The client is already imagining the item gone forever. A fast reply that says you are on it changes everything, even before you have searched.

If the salon is still open, reply straight away: "Thank you for letting us know — I will check your chair and the wash area right now and message you back in ten minutes." Then actually do it, and message back in ten minutes even if the news is bad. If the salon is closed, reply anyway: "Thank you for messaging — the salon is closed now, but I will check first thing in the morning and message you by 9:30. Can you tell me which chair you were in and what the earring looks like?"

That second message does three things. It gives a specific time for the next update, so the client is not left refreshing her phone. It asks for the details you need to search properly. And it shows the message was read by a person who cares, not left on blue ticks overnight.

## Ask for the photo and the chair

Before anyone searches, you need two things: what the item looks like, and where the client was sitting. Ask for both in the same message, and make it easy for her to answer.

"Could you send me a photo of the other earring, or one like it? And do you remember which chair you were in, and whether you had a wash or a colour today?" A photo is worth more than any description. "Small gold earring" could be twenty different things in a salon drawer. A photo of its pair, or even a similar one from the internet, tells your team exactly what to look for.

Keep the questions to two or three at most. A client who is already upset does not want a form to fill in. She wants to feel that someone is helping, and every extra question delays the search.

## Search properly, then answer honestly

A proper search takes five minutes and follows the item, not the room. Check the chair itself, between the cushions and under the seat. Check the wash basin and the floor beneath it. Check the trolley where the stylist kept her tools and the hooks where the client hung her abaya. Earrings roll. Phones slide. Watches hide under towels.

If your salon keeps a lost-property box or a simple log, check that too — and say so in your reply. "I have checked your chair, the wash area and our lost-property box" tells the client the search was real.

Then answer honestly, whatever you found. If you found it, say so with a photo: "We found it — here is a photo. It is safe in an envelope with your name on it at reception." A photo of the found item is the moment the client's worry disappears, and it costs you ten seconds.

If you did not find it, say that plainly and say what happens next. "I have checked your chair, the wash area and the lost-property box, and I am sorry, I could not find it. I have asked the morning team to check again before the first client, and I will message you by 11 AM." Never promise it will appear, and never blame the client for losing it.

## Make collecting it effortless

When the item is found, the collection should be the easiest part of the whole story. Offer the client two or three options in the same message: collect it at reception any time, have it waiting at her next appointment, or have it delivered with her next home visit if she books one. Let her choose, and do not make her chase you for it.

If the item is valuable — gold jewellery, a watch, a wallet — keep it sealed in an envelope with her name and the date on it, somewhere only the team can reach. When she collects it, ask her to confirm it is hers before she leaves, and note the collection in your log. That small bit of care protects the client and protects your team from any later confusion about what was handed over.

## The small system behind the good reply

None of this needs a complicated system. It needs three habits. First, one person per shift owns lost property — usually the receptionist — so a message never sits waiting for "someone" to check. Second, a simple log: date, client name, item, where it was found, and when it was collected. A notebook by the till is enough. Third, a short saved reply for the first response, so the front desk never stares at a worried message wondering what to say.

The saved reply can be as simple as: "Thank you for letting us know — I will check right now and message you back within ten minutes." Adapt it to the moment and add the client's name.

A lost-property message is a small test of your salon, and most clients will never tell you they are running it. They are watching to see whether you care about something that is already paid for and already finished. Answer fast, search properly, and tell the truth — and the client who came back for an earring will come back for everything else too.
