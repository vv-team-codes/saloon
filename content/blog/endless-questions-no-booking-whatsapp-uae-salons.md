Every UAE salon owner knows this client. She messages on WhatsApp on a Tuesday evening: "How much is a balayage?" You reply with the price range. She asks which stylist does it, how long it takes, whether it suits dark hair, and whether she can come on Saturday. You answer everything, patiently, over two days. Then she goes quiet.

She was not being rude, and she was not wasting your time on purpose. She was stuck — and every extra answer you gave her made the decision feel bigger instead of smaller. This is one of the most common ways WhatsApp enquiries die in UAE salons, and it is also one of the most fixable.

## Why some clients ask and ask and never book

Understanding the reason behind the questions changes how you reply. Most endless enquirers fall into one of four groups: the comparer, messaging three or four salons at once and looking for a reason to choose you; the nervous client, whose every question is really "Will this be safe with you?" after a bad experience elsewhere; the one waiting for permission, who needs to check with her husband, her mother, or her own diary before committing; and the genuinely unsure client, who asks about balayage, then highlights, then a gloss, using your answers to work out what she actually wants.

They need a person to take the decision off their shoulders, not another ten facts.

## The mistake most salons make

The natural instinct is to answer every question as it arrives, one message at a time, for as long as the client keeps asking. It feels like good service. In practice, it does the opposite. Each new answer gives the client a new thing to think about and a new reason to delay, and a long back-and-forth trains her to treat your WhatsApp like a search engine — free and with no commitment attached. By the fifth exchange, the conversation has drifted so far from booking that neither side remembers how to get back to it.

There is a second cost: your front desk spends real minutes on a conversation that may never convert, while paying clients wait and new enquiries sit unread. In a busy UAE salon, especially during the evening rush, that time is worth real money. The fix is not to answer less. It is to answer differently — with a recommendation instead of more information.

## The three-answer rule

Answer up to three questions, then guide the conversation toward a decision. The third answer should always end with a recommendation and two specific slots: "Based on what you have told me, the soft balayage with Sara would suit your hair best. It takes about three hours and costs AED 450 to 550. I have Thursday at 6 PM or Saturday at 11 AM — shall I hold Thursday for you?"

That message shows you listened, gives a clear recommendation and a price, and asks a small question — Thursday or Saturday — instead of the big one: "Do you want to book?" If she asks a fourth question, answer it in the same shape: one short answer, the recommendation, then the two slots. The conversation should narrow toward a booking, not widen into a consultation.

## Ask one question back

The most powerful move in a long WhatsApp enquiry is also the simplest: ask the client one good question. Most endless enquiries stall because the salon never learns what the client actually wants.

Good questions are specific and easy to answer: "What look are you hoping for — lighter ends, or an all-over change?" "When do you need it done by?" "Have you had colour before?" One answer tells you more than ten of hers. And if she needs it before Friday, she is not casually browsing — she is buying.

## When she needs to check with someone else

The client who says "let me check with my husband" is not rejecting you. She is telling you exactly what stands between her and a booking, and the worst thing you can do is reply "okay, let me know" and wait.

Instead, make it easy for her to say yes later. Send a short summary she can forward: the service, the stylist, the price range, and the two slots you offered. A message she can show someone else does the selling for you. Then agree a moment to follow up: "Shall I message you tomorrow at 11 AM once you have checked?" That gives you permission to return without feeling pushy. Most salons lose these clients not because the client said no, but because nobody ever followed up.

## The polite close

Some enquiries will never convert, and that is fine. After three helpful answers, a recommendation, and one follow-up, you have done your job. The last message should leave the door open without chasing: "No rush at all — the Thursday 6 PM slot is yours if you would like it, just message me and I will hold it for you. We are here whenever you are ready."

Do not send a fourth or fifth message asking "any update?" Clients in the UAE get dozens of business messages a week, and a salon that chases looks desperate — not the one a nervous client trusts with her hair.

Keep a simple note of these conversations — service, price, date. If she messages again in a month, you will remember her, and that memory alone often wins the booking.

## Make it easy for the next one

The best salons quietly prevent endless enquiries. A WhatsApp service menu with honest price ranges and real photos answers half the questions before they are asked, and a greeting message — "tell us the service and the day you have in mind, and we will send you the price and the nearest slots" — sets the shape of the conversation from the first message.

Train the whole team on the same habit: answer, recommend, offer two slots. When every reply ends with a next step, the client never has to wonder what to do next — and wondering is what keeps her asking instead of booking.

Your WhatsApp inbox is full of clients who want to book. Some just need you to decide for them, kindly and clearly, and hold the chair while they say yes.
