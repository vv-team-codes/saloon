Every salon owner in the UAE pays for marketing: Instagram ads, Google listings, influencer posts, printed flyers. Yet the most powerful advert most salons own is one they already paid for and then forgot about — the shopfront itself. Your window, your sign and the few metres of pavement outside your door work every hour of the day, including the hours you are closed. The question is whether they are working for you or against you.

In Dubai and Abu Dhabi, a huge share of salon discovery still happens on foot. Someone finishes lunch in the mall, walks past your window on the way to the car park, and makes a decision in about five seconds. Someone new to the neighbourhood walks past your high-street salon every evening for a month before finally coming in. These are not people who searched for you. They found you because you were there, visible and inviting.

## Your window is a free advert — use it like one

Most salon windows show everything or nothing: a cram of faded posters and a half-dead plant, or frosted film that hides the salon entirely. Both waste the best advertising space you own.

The windows that work show real work, clearly. One or two strong images of actual results — a balayage, a bridal look, a fresh fade — printed large and replaced every few weeks, will do more than a dozen small posters. Real photos matter: stock images look generic, while your own work tells a local story. Keep the glass clean, keep the view into the salon open enough that people can see a calm, professional room, and resist the urge to fill every centimetre. Empty space is what makes the one thing you do show stand out.

Change the display with the seasons. A small National Day touch in December, a bridal theme in the winter wedding months — these cost almost nothing and signal that the salon is alive and current. A window that never changes becomes invisible to the people who pass it every day.

## Signage that answers three questions

A passer-by who likes what they see will ask three questions before they ever reach for the door: what do you do, how much does it cost, and are you open now? Your signage should answer all three without them having to walk in and ask.

The name and the service need to be readable from across the street. In a mall corridor or a busy high street, that means large, high-contrast lettering and nothing clever that cannot be read at a glance. If your salon does ladies' hair, gents' grooming or both, say so plainly — a surprising number of people walk past a salon for months assuming it is not for them.

Price should not be a mystery. You do not need a full menu on the wall, but a small, tidy board with starting prices for the three or four most-booked services — cut, colour, blow-dry, manicure — removes the biggest reason people hesitate. A client who already knows the rough cost walks in as a buyer, not a browser. The same board can carry your opening hours, including the late evening hours that matter so much in the UAE.

## Light, cleanliness and the five-second test

Stand across the street from your own salon at 7 pm, when the evening rush begins, and look at it the way a stranger would. Is the sign lit? Is the entrance obvious? Does the light inside look warm and welcoming, or harsh and tired? In the UAE's long, bright evenings, a salon with a dark sign effectively closes itself to everyone walking past.

The five-second test is simple: in five seconds, a stranger should be able to tell what you are, that you are open, and that the place looks well run. Clients judge the quality of the cut by the quality of the room they walk into, and that judgement starts outside.

## The pavement is part of your salon

On a high street, the pavement outside your door is the most underused marketing space a salon has. A neat A-board with one clear message — today's availability, a new service, a last-minute opening — can pull in the person who was only passing by. Keep the message to one line and change it daily; an out-of-date board is worse than no board at all.

In a mall, the rules are tighter, and that is exactly why they matter. Work within them: a well-lit unit, a clear display and a team that greets people at the threshold. Some of the busiest mall salons in Dubai win their walk-ins simply because the stylist at the front desk looks up, smiles and says hello to people passing by.

## Make the walk-in effortless

A person who has decided to come in should become a client within a minute. That means someone at the front ready to greet them, a clear answer to "can I book today?", and a simple way to secure the slot. If the diary is full, offer the next real opening and take their number — a walk-in turned away with nothing is a walk-in lost to the salon next door.

And remember that the shopfront works after closing time too. Plenty of people notice a salon on their evening walk and message it when they get home. If your sign carries your WhatsApp number or a scannable code, that late-night curiosity becomes a morning booking. ## Measure it like any other marketing

Because shopfront marketing feels free, most owners never measure it. Fix that with one small habit: ask every new walk-in how they found you, and write the answer down. A simple tally on the reception desk, kept for a month, will tell you more than any analytics dashboard. If a third of your new clients came from the street, your shopfront is your best marketing channel, and it deserves the same attention as your Instagram account.

Review the window monthly and the signage quarterly. Small, regular updates keep the salon looking alive to the people who pass it every day — and those people, once they finally walk in, are the most loyal clients you will ever have. They chose you from the street, not from a feed, and that kind of choice sticks.
