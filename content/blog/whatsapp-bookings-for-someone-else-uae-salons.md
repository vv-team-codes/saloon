It happens every week in salons across the UAE. A WhatsApp message arrives: "Hi, I want to book a hair colour for my mum." Or: "Can I book a facial for my wife on Saturday?"

The person messaging is not the person sitting in the chair. That small difference changes almost everything about the booking — the questions you need to ask, the details you need to record, and the mistakes you need to avoid. Handle it well and you win two clients at once. Handle it badly and you get a no-show or a wrong service.

## Why this booking is so common

In the UAE, booking for someone else is part of everyday life. A husband books a spa afternoon for his wife's birthday. A daughter books a haircut for her mother who does not use WhatsApp much. A friend books a bridal trial as a gift. During wedding season and around Eid, these bookings multiply.

Most salons treat these messages like any other enquiry. That is where the trouble starts: the person messaging cannot answer questions about the other person's hair, skin, allergies or preferences, and will often guess rather than say they do not know. A guessed answer becomes a wrong booking, and a wrong booking becomes a wasted chair.

## The five details you need before you confirm

You need five things before you lock in a third-party booking. Ask for them in one message, so the booker can answer in a single reply.

First, the name of the person coming in, spelled correctly. Second, her mobile number, so you can confirm directly with her. Third, the service she actually wants, not the one the booker thinks she wants. Fourth, anything about her hair or skin that changes the service — previous colour, sensitive skin, allergies. And fifth, who is paying, and how.

A message that collects all five sounds like this: "Of course, we'd love to look after your mum! Could you share her name and number so we can confirm with her directly? Which service would she like, and has she had colour or any treatment recently? And would you like to pay now or at the salon?"

That one message gets you the facts, shows the booker you are organised, and opens the door to confirming with the real client — the most important step here.

## Always confirm with the person in the chair

Never rely only on the booker. Once you have the real client's number, send her a short, warm confirmation of her own.

"Hi Sara, your daughter Aisha has booked you in for a haircut and blow-dry with Mariam on Saturday at 5 PM. It takes about an hour and costs AED 120. Does that still work for you? Just reply YES to confirm."

That message prevents the three most common failures: the client who never knew she was booked, the client who wanted a different service, and the client who turns up at the wrong time. It also gives you a direct line to the person in the chair, so your reminders reach the right phone.

Some clients will tell you they would rather all messages go through the person who booked. Respect that, but still ask for the real client's number for the day itself — a running-late message needs to reach the person travelling to you, not the person who booked from her office.

## Settle the payment question before the day

Money is where third-party bookings get awkward. The booker assumes the salon will charge the client. The client assumes the booker already paid. Nobody pays, and the front desk is left negotiating at checkout.

Decide the rule in advance and state it in your first reply: whoever books pays, unless they tell you otherwise. Offer the booker a payment link on WhatsApp at the time of booking, especially for gifts and bridal trials, where the person in the chair should never be handed a bill for her own present. If you take deposits, take them from the booker when the appointment is made — chasing a deposit from someone who did not make the booking is a fast way to lose the appointment.

## Three traps to avoid

The first trap is the allergy question. A booker will almost never know about the other person's allergies or sensitivities, and will often answer "no, she's fine" without checking. Never accept a second-hand answer on health questions — ask the real client directly, in your confirmation message, and note the answer on her record.

The second trap is the wrong service. "She wants highlights" from a daughter can turn out to be a full balayage, a root touch-up, or a cut the mother never asked for. When the booker is unsure, offer a consultation first, so she can decide the colour with the stylist on the day. Fifteen minutes of consultation saves a two-hour service nobody wanted.

The third trap is the wrong person arriving — a booking made for one Sara and a different Sara walks in. Confirm the full name in your message to the real client, and check it against the diary when she arrives.

## Turn one booking into two clients

Here is the part most salons miss. A third-party booking is not one client — it is two. The booker chose your salon and trusted you with someone she cares about. That is a warmer lead than any advertisement will ever bring you.

Thank the booker after the appointment. A short WhatsApp message — "Your mum looked lovely after her colour today, thank you for trusting us with her" — costs nothing and often brings the booker in for her own appointment within weeks. If the gift was for a birthday, note the date: next year, a message a week before it is one of the easiest rebookings a salon can win.

The person in the chair matters just as much. She arrived because someone else chose you, so give her the same careful consultation and honest finish you would give any new client, and ask her at the mirror whether she is happy. A client who was gifted an appointment and loved it becomes a regular on her own terms.

None of this needs complicated systems — just a short checklist on the diary (booker name, client name, client number, service, payment) and the habit of confirming with the person in the chair.
