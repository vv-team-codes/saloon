It arrives as a screenshot, usually late in the evening. A rival salon's price list, circled in red, with a short message underneath: "They do balayage for 350. Why is yours 550?"

Every salon owner in Dubai knows this message. The client is not being rude. She likes your salon, or she would not be messaging you at all. She is asking you to give her a reason to stay, and what you write back in the next few minutes decides whether she books with you or with them.

The worst replies are the two most tempting ones: matching the price, or explaining at length why you are worth more. Matching it teaches every client that a screenshot earns a discount, and the next one will bring a cheaper screenshot. A long defence sounds like an argument, and nobody books a relaxing treatment after an argument.

## Read what she is really asking

A competitor's price list is almost never just about money. She wants to know three things: is the cheaper service actually the same service, will she be looked after if something goes wrong, and is your price fair?

Most of the time she has not read the rival list carefully. The cheaper balayage may exclude toner, a cut, or the blow-dry, or be a first-visit offer. Your reply should help her see that for herself, without ever attacking the other salon.

## Reply fast, in three short parts

Speed matters more than length. She sent the screenshot because she is deciding right now, probably comparing two chats on her phone. A reply within ten minutes keeps you in the decision. A reply the next morning usually arrives after she has booked elsewhere.

Keep the reply to three short parts. First, thank her: "Thanks for sharing that — it's a fair question, and I'm glad you asked before booking." Second, state what your price includes: "Our balayage at 550 includes the consultation, toner, a cut, and the blow-dry, with a senior colourist." Third, end with a low-pressure next step: "If you'd like, I can hold a consultation slot on Thursday evening and we can look at your hair before you decide anything."

Notice what is missing: no apology for the price, no mention of the rival, and no discount. Just a clear list of what she gets and an easy next step. If she still wants the cheaper option, she will go — and that is fine. A client who books only on price will leave the same way, the next time someone is cheaper.

## Compare what is included, not the price

The moment you start comparing your price with theirs, you have accepted their framing: that the two services are the same and only the number differs. They usually are not the same, and your job is to show that gently.

Ask one or two honest questions instead of arguing. "Does their price include toner and the blow-dry?" or "Is that with a senior colourist, or a junior?" Many clients will check the rival's fine print themselves after that and come back on their own.

Keep a short list of what your headline services include, ready to paste, and make sure everyone who answers WhatsApp uses the same wording. When the answer changes depending on who is on shift, clients notice and trust drops. If the rival genuinely is cheaper for the same service, say so honestly and move on to what is different. "They are a good salon. With us you get the same senior colourist every visit, a quiet chair, and a free toner refresh within two weeks." Some clients will still choose price. The ones who choose you after that reply are the ones who stay.

## Offer a smaller step, not a smaller price

When a client hesitates over price, the answer is rarely a discount. It is a smaller commitment: a consultation, a gloss, a fringe trim, or a single service instead of a package. Let her experience the salon before she commits to the full price.

This works especially well in the UAE, where many clients are new to the city and trying salons for the first time. She has no history with you and no reason to trust a 550 dirham service from a stranger. A 15-minute consultation or a blow-dry at your normal price gives her something real to judge.

Never invent a one-time discount on the spot. A discount offered in a panic becomes your real price in the client's mind, and next visit she will expect it again. If you run genuine offers — a first-visit package, a weekday rate, a bring-a-friend deal — they should be planned, written down, and the same for everyone, not invented in a chat at 9pm.

## Keep a record, and learn from it

Every price-comparison message is free market research. Note which service the client compared, which salon she named, and what price she quoted. After a month, patterns appear. If ten clients compare your keratin price but nobody compares your haircut price, your keratin price is the one to look at. If the same rival appears every week, that rival is your real competition.

Review these notes with your team once a month, not to panic about prices, but to check whether your own pricing still makes sense. And protect the team: price messages are stressful for junior staff, who often panic and offer discounts they are not allowed to give. Write the reply templates together, agree on who may offer what, and make it a rule that nobody invents a price on WhatsApp.

## The reply that keeps her

The client who sends a competitor's price list is not your enemy. She is a client who likes you enough to ask. Answer quickly, answer kindly, explain what your price includes, and offer her a small first step instead of a discount.

She may still book with the cheaper salon. That is not a failure. The salon that holds its prices, treats price questions with respect, and keeps its regulars happy does not need every client. It needs the right ones — the ones who come back and send their friends. Those clients are never won by a price war. They are won by a calm, honest reply on WhatsApp.
