It usually arrives on WhatsApp in the evening, from a client with one free Saturday and a long list of plans: "Can I do colour and keratin on the same day? I have a wedding next week and I only have Saturday free." It is one of the most common combination-treatment enquiries UAE salons receive, and one of the easiest to get wrong. Say yes too quickly and you risk a faded colour and a rushed result. Say no too bluntly and she books both services somewhere else.

In the UAE the question comes up constantly. Humidity and hard water push clients toward keratin smoothing, while sun, sea and swimming pools fade colour fast, so the same client often wants both fixed in a single visit. The salons that win these enquiries explain the honest answer clearly, offer a better plan in the same message, and make booking that plan effortless.

## Why clients ask for both at once

Most clients who ask this are not being difficult. They are being practical. A working woman in Dubai or Abu Dhabi may only have one free morning a week, and two separate salon visits feel like a luxury she cannot afford. A bride with a wedding on Saturday wants everything finished in one trip.

There is also a simple misunderstanding behind the question. To a client, colour and keratin are both "hair treatments" that happen in a salon chair, so it seems natural to stack them like a facial and a manicure. She does not know that they work in opposite directions: colour opens the hair, while keratin seals it.

## The honest answer, in plain language

The safe answer in most cases is that the two services belong on two different days, in the right order. Colour first, then keratin a week or two later, is the order most stylists recommend, because keratin applied over fresh colour helps seal it in and can even add shine. The reverse order needs a longer wait: colour applied too soon after keratin often does not take evenly, because the coating is still doing its job of keeping things out.

That is the full explanation a client needs. No chemistry lecture, no brand names. One or two sentences in everyday language, then straight to the plan. A reply like "We usually do colour first and keratin a week later, because the keratin seals the colour in and makes it last longer" answers the question and quietly sells the second visit at the same time.

There are exceptions, and honesty means naming them. Some express formulas can sit closer together, and a stylist who has seen the hair may judge differently. That is exactly why the reply should end with a consultation, not a flat rule quoted from a price list.

## The reply that books two appointments

The best replies to this enquiry have four parts, and they fit in a single WhatsApp message. First, acknowledge the deadline: "A wedding next week, of course, let's get you sorted." Second, give the honest answer in one line. Third, offer the two-visit plan with real dates, not a vague suggestion: "Colour this Saturday at 11, keratin next Saturday at 10 — that gives the colour a week to settle and the keratin will lock it in." Fourth, hold both slots while she decides, so the plan feels real rather than theoretical.

Offering real times matters more than offering a discount. A client who asked for one visit because she is short on time does not need a cheaper price; she needs to see that two visits can still fit her week. Naming the second appointment before she has even agreed to the first is not pushy when it is framed as protecting her result.

Combination questions usually arrive late at night, when the client is planning her week and the salon is closed. ## Protecting the diary, and the client

There is a business reason behind the honest answer, and it is worth understanding before anyone on the team is tempted to say yes. Colour and keratin together can take five to six hours in one chair. That is a full Saturday morning for one client, with no room for a delay or a walk-in. If the result disappoints, the complaint is about a whole day lost, not one service.

Two visits are better for the diary and better for the client. The colour visit is unhurried. The keratin visit starts with hair the stylist has already seen, so the consultation is shorter and the result is more predictable. A client who leaves happy after her colour is far more likely to keep her keratin appointment than one talked into a marathon session she quietly regretted.

## When the answer really is no, and what to offer instead

Some combinations genuinely cannot be done close together, and the reply should say so plainly. Fresh bleach and a keratin treatment in the same week is a combination most stylists will refuse, because the hair simply cannot take both. In those cases, the worst reply is a soft yes that turns into a problem at the basin.

The right move is a clear no plus a genuine alternative in the same message. Offer a gloss or a deep-conditioning treatment for the event this week, and book the keratin for the safe date. The client came in asking for a result, not a specific chemical, and a salon that protects her hair earns more trust than one that takes her money and hopes for the best.

## Log the enquiry, because it is market research

If your salon hears this question once a week, that is useful information. It means clients want a combined result and are willing to pay for it, even if the safe version takes two visits. Some salons respond by creating a named two-visit package with one price and both appointments booked together. Others add a simple line to their WhatsApp quick replies so every team member gives the same honest answer, at midnight as well as at midday.

Either way, the enquiry itself is a gift. A client who asks whether two services can be combined is a client who already trusts you with both of them. Answer honestly, offer the two-visit plan with real times, and hold both slots.
