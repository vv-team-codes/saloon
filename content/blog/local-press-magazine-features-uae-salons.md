Every salon owner in the UAE knows the feeling of paying for attention. You boost a post, you run an ad, and you hope a booking follows. Yet there is a kind of attention that costs nothing and carries more weight than any advert: a feature about your salon in a local magazine, newspaper or lifestyle website.

## Why a press feature beats another advert

An advert tells people you exist. A press feature tells people why you matter. When a reader in Dubai Marina or Jumeirah sees your salon mentioned in a magazine she already trusts, that trust transfers to you.

A feature also keeps working long after publication. A printed magazine sits on a coffee table for weeks, and an online article keeps appearing in Google searches for months.

## What journalists actually want

The first mistake salon owners make is pitching the wrong thing. A journalist does not want to write about your new opening hours or your latest discount. She wants a story her readers will enjoy, so before you write a single email, find the story inside your salon.

Maybe you are the stylist who moved to Dubai ten years ago and built a salon from one chair. Maybe your team speaks six languages between them and serves clients from forty nationalities. Maybe you specialise in a treatment nobody else in your neighbourhood offers, or your head spa service has a waiting list three weeks long.

## Finding the right publications

Start close to home. Beyond the big national papers, the UAE has a lively local media scene: community magazines, neighbourhood guides, lifestyle websites, expat blogs and free publications that land in apartment lobbies across Dubai, Abu Dhabi and Sharjah. These are the publications that actually write about small businesses, because local businesses are their bread and butter.

Make a short list of five or six that your own clients would read.

Do not overlook the smaller outlets. A feature in a community magazine with a few thousand loyal readers in your own area can fill more chairs than a mention in a huge publication read by people who live an hour away.

## Writing a pitch that gets answered

Journalists are busy and their inboxes are full, so keep your pitch short. Three short paragraphs are enough. The first line should say what the story is, in one sentence. The second paragraph should give two or three interesting details that prove the story is real. The third should say why now — a seasonal hook, a milestone, a trend — and offer a photo and a short interview.

Be specific. Instead of writing "we are a lovely salon with great services", write "our salon in Al Barsha runs a ladies-only morning that has become the busiest slot of the week, and we would love to show your readers what happens there".

## Be ready when they say yes

The worst outcome is not a rejection. It is a yes that you are not ready for. Before you start pitching, prepare three things. First, two or three good photographs of your salon, your team and your work, taken in natural light.

Second, know your numbers. How long have you been open? How many clients do you see in a week? How long is the wait for your most popular treatment?

Third, decide what you want the reader to do next. Make sure your Google Business Profile is complete, your WhatsApp number is easy to find, and your booking link works. When the article goes live, new readers will search for you within minutes. This is also where a 24/7 AI receptionist like ZYNA earns its keep: enquiries sparked by a press feature often arrive late at night, and a salon that answers instantly keeps the booking a slower rival would lose.

## Turn one feature into months of marketing

A single feature should not be a one-day event. Share the article on your Instagram and WhatsApp Status the day it appears, and again a few weeks later for clients who missed it. Print a copy and frame it near reception, where waiting clients will read it. Add "As featured in" to your website and your Google Business Profile, and send the link to your regulars with a short personal message thanking them for being part of the story.

## What not to do

Do not pay for a feature that pretends to be editorial. Some publications sell "features" that are really adverts dressed up as articles, and readers can tell the difference. If someone asks you to pay, judge it as advertising, not editorial.

Do not send a generic press release to fifty people at once. A mass email with no personal touch and no real story gets deleted. Five thoughtful, personal pitches will do more than fifty identical ones.

## Start this week

You do not need a budget, an agency or a media contact to begin. This week, pick one story from your salon and write it down in three sentences. Find three local publications your clients read and note the name of the right editor. Send one pitch, with a photo attached, before the weekend. If it is ignored, send one more pitch the following week to a different publication. Keep going.
