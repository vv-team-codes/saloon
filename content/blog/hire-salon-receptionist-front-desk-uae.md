Walk into a busy salon in Dubai on a Thursday evening and watch the front desk for ten minutes. The phone is ringing, WhatsApp messages are arriving, and a client at the counter is asking about prices. In the middle of it all, the owner is trying to answer a message and greet a walk-in.

Most salon owners in the UAE do this job themselves for far too long, telling themselves that nobody can represent their salon like they can, or that a receptionist is a luxury for bigger salons. But the logic is backwards: the salon stays stuck at the same size because the owner is trapped behind the desk. Hiring a front-desk receptionist is one of the cheapest growth moves a UAE salon can make — it buys back the owner's time and stops bookings leaking away.

## Why the front desk is a growth role

Every booking that is missed, delayed or answered badly is revenue lost, and the front desk is where those bookings are won or lost. A client who messages at 9 pm and hears nothing until the next morning has often booked somewhere else by then. A walk-in who is greeted warmly and offered a real slot becomes a regular; a walk-in who is waved at vaguely becomes a one-time visitor.

A dedicated receptionist changes this because answering clients becomes someone's actual job. She watches the diary, answers quickly and follows up on unanswered messages. And when the owner is no longer tied to the desk, she can do the work only an owner can do: building relationships with regulars, training the team and planning the next promotion.

## What the job really covers

Before you write a job ad, write down what the role covers. First, she owns the diary: booking, moving and cancelling appointments, keeping gaps realistic and making sure two clients are never promised the same chair. Second, she owns the first reply: a warm, fast answer with a price range and a real slot. Third, she owns the follow-ups: confirming tomorrow's appointments and nudging regulars who have not rebooked. Fourth, she owns checkout, taking payment smoothly and rebooking the client before she leaves. Fifth, she keeps the front of house calm, greeting clients by name. It is a full job, and it is the job that holds the salon's revenue together.

## When you know it is time to hire

The signals are easy to spot. You are missing messages because you are with clients. You have double-booked or forgotten to confirm a booking. Regulars have mentioned that nobody answered their WhatsApp. Or you have stopped doing the things that grow the business because the desk eats your whole day.

Try the one-week check: note every hour you spend on front-desk work instead of on clients or on growing the business. If it is more than ten hours a week, you are already paying the cost of not having a receptionist — in lost bookings and lost growth instead of a salary.

## What to look for beyond a friendly smile

Friendliness matters, but it is not enough. Look for someone calm under pressure, because the 7 pm pile-up will test that every day. Look for someone organised, who can hold a diary in her head and still notice the small things, like a client's usual stylist. Look for someone who writes clearly, because most client contact in the UAE happens on WhatsApp, and a muddled message loses a booking.

Language matters too. A receptionist who can switch between English and at least one other language common among your clients will answer more people well. Hire for attitude and reliability over a salon CV, and teach her the salon part.

## Pay, hours and the evening problem

The hardest practical question is hours. UAE salons are busiest in the evenings and on weekends, which is exactly when a receptionist is most needed — and exactly when good staff least want to work. Be honest about this in the job ad rather than surprising a new hire in week two. Offer a fair, clear shift pattern, such as rotating morning and evening shifts.

Pay should reflect that the role earns its keep. A receptionist who saves even two or three bookings a week has usually covered her salary, and a good one does far more. Many owners add a small monthly bonus tied to something the receptionist controls, such as the rebooking rate. Keep the bonus simple and visible, and pay it on time.

One gap no single hire can cover is the late night. Clients message at 10 pm, at midnight and on Friday mornings. ## Train them in two weeks, not two months

A receptionist does not need months of training, but she does need a proper first two weeks. Give her a written list of your prices, services and policies, so she never has to guess an answer in front of a client. Sit with her through a full day at the desk, including the evening rush, and let her hear how you handle a price question, a reschedule and a complaint. Then let her run the desk while you watch, and correct gently after the client has gone.

The most useful training tool is a short list of standard replies. Write the answers to the ten questions clients ask most — price, availability, location, parking, service times, stylist availability — and keep them within reach. Good front desks are built on good answers, not on memory.

## Measure it: three numbers

Once your receptionist is in place, measure whether the hire is working. Three numbers tell you almost everything: response time for the first reply to a WhatsApp message or call, enquiry-to-booking conversion, and the rebooking rate — how many clients leave with their next appointment already booked.

Check these once a week with your receptionist, and celebrate the wins in front of the team. A receptionist who can see that her fast replies turned into real bookings will keep answering fast.

Your front desk is the first thing a client meets and the last thing she remembers. Staff it properly and it stops being the bottleneck that holds your salon back — and starts being the engine that fills your chairs.
