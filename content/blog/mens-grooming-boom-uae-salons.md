Walk into a busy Dubai salon on a Thursday evening and you will notice a sizeable share of the chairs filled by men, and they are not just in for a quick cut. They are getting beard sculpts, facials, hair colour to cover grey, manicures, and full grooming packages — and rebooking before they leave.

Men's grooming has quietly become one of the fastest-growing revenue lines for UAE salons. The drivers are easy to see: a large population of professionals who meet clients face to face, a social calendar built around weekend gatherings and weddings, and a climate that punishes neglected skin and hair.

Yet many salons still treat male clients as an afterthought — a barber chair in the corner and a price list that stops at "haircut". The salons winning this segment design for men deliberately, from the first enquiry to the fourth visit.

## What male clients actually want

The first mistake is assuming men want the same experience as women, only shorter. In practice, male clients value speed, clarity and predictability above all else: exactly what a service includes, how long it takes, and what it costs — before they sit down. A vague "grooming package" with no breakdown is a reason to walk away, not a reason to book.

They also want low-friction booking. Most male clients in the UAE book on WhatsApp, often late in the evening after work, and they expect a fast answer. A salon that replies the next morning has often already lost the booking to a competitor who answered at 10 pm. Finally, men are loyal once trust is established — but that trust is usually attached to a person, not a brand. A client who trusts his barber will follow him across town. Proper client notes — preferred cut, beard length, skin sensitivities, products used — let any skilled team member deliver the same result, which protects the relationship when the regular barber is on leave.

## Services that sell beyond the haircut

The haircut remains the anchor, but the growth is in everything around it. Beard design and hot-towel shaves are the obvious starting point, especially for clients who maintain a beard as part of their professional image. Grey blending — subtle colour that softens grey without looking dyed — has become a quiet bestseller among men who want to look rested rather than "coloured".

Skin services are the next frontier. Facials marketed to men work best when framed around a problem, not a luxury: deep cleansing after a week in Dubai's heat and humidity, de-tanning after a beach weekend, or a quick pre-event refresh before a wedding or a big meeting. Express formats of 30 to 45 minutes suit male clients far better than two-hour rituals, and they fit neatly into lunch breaks or the gap before an evening out.

Then there is the package. A monthly grooming membership — one cut, one beard tidy and one facial, at a fixed price — turns an occasional visitor into a regular. It smooths out quiet weeks, gives the client a reason to return on schedule, and makes revenue predictable. Keep it simple: one price, one clear list of inclusions, no expiry traps.

## Designing a space men feel comfortable in

Space matters more than most owners expect. A man who has to wait in a row of styling chairs surrounded by hair dryers and colour bowls may not come back, even if the cut was excellent. The fix does not require a separate branch or a big renovation. A dedicated corner with its own seating, a slightly different playlist, and a waiting area that feels calm rather than busy is often enough.

Timing helps too. Male demand often spikes in the early evening, when office workers head straight to the salon before dinner plans. Protecting a couple of chairs for male grooming during the 6 pm to 9 pm window keeps walk-ins from being turned away at exactly the hour they are most likely to arrive.

Staffing is the other half of the equation. A men's grooming offer only works if the team can deliver it, so invest in training for beard work, grey blending and men's facials, and make sure at least two team members can cover each service.

## Pricing without apology

Men's services are often underpriced in the UAE, partly because owners compare them to a basic barber shop down the road. But a salon offering is not a barber shop offering: it includes consultation, premium products, a comfortable environment and skilled staff, and pricing should reflect that.

A good structure is three tiers: an essential cut, a cut-plus-beard combination, and a full grooming session that adds a facial or scalp treatment. The middle tier is where most clients land, and where the salon makes its margin. Discounting the top tier to fill chairs usually backfires — it trains clients to wait for offers.

One pricing trap to avoid is the "free" add-on. A complimentary beard trim with every cut sounds generous, but it quietly tells clients the beard work has no value. Charge for it, even modestly, and clients will treat it as a service worth returning for.

## Keeping them coming back

Retention for male clients follows a simple rhythm: remind, rebook, recognise. A short message a few days after the visit — how the cut is settling, a reminder of the beard oil used — keeps the salon in the client's mind. Rebooking at the chair, before the client leaves, is even more powerful: "Same time in three weeks?" converts a one-off visit into a habit.

Recognition is what turns habit into loyalty. Remembering that a client prefers a skin fade over a taper, dislikes strong fragrances, or has a wedding coming up is the difference between a salon and a barber he happens to visit. Small notes, kept consistently, compound over months into a relationship competitors cannot easily break.

The men's grooming boom in the UAE is not a passing trend. It reflects a lasting change in how men think about appearance, professionalism and self-care. Salons that treat male clients as a proper segment — with clear services, honest pricing, a comfortable space and a plan for the second visit — will capture a growing, loyal and high-frequency customer base.
