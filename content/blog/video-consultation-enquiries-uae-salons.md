A message lands in your WhatsApp at 9:40 one night: "Hi, can we talk on video first before I book?" The client is thinking about a colour correction, a bridal package, or a first visit. She is not being difficult. She is being careful.

Video consultation enquiries are growing fast in UAE salons. Clients here come from dozens of countries, many are new to Dubai, and plenty are spending real money on a service they cannot undo. A short video call costs you almost nothing and answers the one question every enquiry is really asking: can I trust this salon?

## Why clients ask for a video call first

Most requests come from one of four places: distance — a client in Abu Dhabi or Sharjah, or a tourist in a Dubai hotel, does not want to drive across town just to ask a question; a big decision, like a colour correction or bridal package; a bad experience somewhere else; or language, because speaking is easier than a long WhatsApp thread.

None of these are objections. They are buying signals: a client who asks for a video call has already chosen you over the other salons in her search results. She just wants one reason to commit.

## The reply that books the call

Speed matters more than polish. Reply within the hour if you can, and never answer with a flat yes that leaves the client to organise everything herself. Offer the call, name two specific times, and tell her what to bring.

Try this: "Of course — a quick video call is a great idea for colour work. I can do tomorrow at 11:30 or 4:00, about ten minutes. Have a photo of your hair in daylight and one of the look you like ready. Which time suits you?"

That message says yes immediately, gives two concrete times, sets a short duration, and asks for two photos, turning a vague chat into a real consultation. End every such reply with a question, because a message that ends in a question gets an answer.

If the client asked late at night, do not leave her waiting in silence. A short holding reply helps: "Yes, happy to do a video call — I will send you two times first thing in the morning." Then send them first thing.

## How to run a ten-minute video consultation

Keep it short and structured. Ten minutes is enough. Start with the client's own words: what she wants, what she has now, and what she is worried about — then listen. For hair, ask her to hold the phone near a window so you can see her colour in natural light. For bridal, ask about the dress, the venue, and the time she needs to be ready.

Then give one clear recommendation, not three options. Confidence sounds like a single plan: the service, the time, and the price range. "I would do a full balayage with a toner, about three hours, between AED 650 and 800. I have Thursday at 5:00 or Saturday at 11:00." That is a consultation that ends in a booking.

Finish by writing it down. Tell her you will send a short WhatsApp summary — service, price range, time, and the two slots — then send it within ten minutes of hanging up, while the conversation is still warm.

## What never to promise on video

A video call is a consultation, not a guarantee, and the honest limits build trust. Phone cameras change colours, bathroom lighting flatters everyone, and a photo of a celebrity's hair does not show you the hair underneath.

Say so plainly: "On camera your colour looks warm, but I will only know the true tone when I see it in the salon." Clients respect that. What they do not forgive is a promise made on a small screen that the chair cannot keep.

The same honesty applies to timing. If a transformation needs two visits, say so on the call, not at the till. If a treatment is not right for her, say so and suggest what is.

## Turning the call into a booking

The consultation is only useful if it ends in a diary entry. The most common mistake is ending with "think about it and let me know." Most clients will not. Decide the next step together, on the call.

Offer the slot while you are still talking: "Shall I hold Saturday at 11:00 for you? I can hold it until this evening, and a small deposit confirms it." A hold with a deadline is fair to both sides. If she needs to check with family or work, agree when you will message her again.

If she does not book on the call, follow up once the next day with something useful: "Here is the summary again, and Saturday 11:00 is still open if you would like it." One good follow-up is service; three is pressure.

## The practical setup

Video consultations only work if someone owns them. Decide now who takes the calls and when — usually one senior stylist or the manager, in two short windows a day, late morning and mid-afternoon. Publish those windows so clients stop asking for calls at 8:00 on a Saturday when every chair is full.

Take the call from a quiet corner with good daylight, not from the middle of a noisy salon floor. Keep a one-page note for the team so every consultation covers the same ground: current state, desired result, allergies, price range, duration, and two slots. ## When to offer the salon visit instead

Not every enquiry belongs on video. A client choosing between two shades of blonde, or asking about a simple trim, does not need a call — a fast WhatsApp answer with a price and two slots is better. A client asking about something you cannot judge on a screen is better served by a short in-salon consultation.

The rule is simple: video when the decision is big, the client is far away, or trust is the barrier. Text when the answer is short and the booking is obvious.

Video consultation enquiries are not a burden. They are clients telling you exactly what they need before they spend money with you. Answer fast, offer two times, keep the call to ten honest minutes, and end with a slot held and a summary sent.
