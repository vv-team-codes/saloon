Most salons treat a move as an admin task. They change the address on Google, stick a notice on the old door and hope for the best. A few regulars drift away, the new neighbourhood takes months to notice you exist, and the diary quietly empties. It does not have to go that way. A relocation is one of the best marketing moments a salon ever gets — a genuine reason to contact every client, a story worth sharing, and a fresh start in a new area. Handled well, a move can leave you with more clients than you had before.

## Tell your regulars first — before the sign goes up

Your regulars should never learn about your move from a stranger, a closed door or a Google listing. Tell them personally, and tell them early — at least three weeks before moving day, and again in the final week.

The message should answer the three questions every client asks in the first five seconds: where exactly, how far is it from the old place, and is parking easier or harder? Send the new location pin on WhatsApp, name the nearest landmark, and say how long the drive takes from the old salon. If the new spot is a five-minute drive away, say so plainly — most clients imagine a move is further than it is.

## Make the move a story, not an announcement

A plain "we have moved" post is easy to scroll past. A move told as a story is not. Clients love a behind-the-scenes journey, and a relocation gives you weeks of genuine content.

Share the empty new space before the mirrors go up. Film the first delivery of chairs, the paint going on the wall, the team choosing the new reception desk. Ask followers to vote on small decisions — the colour of the feature wall, the name of a new treatment room. Every vote is a client who now feels part of the new salon before she has ever visited it.

On opening day, do a proper reveal. A short video walkthrough, a few photos of the finished space, and one honest line about why you moved — more light, a bigger colour bar, parking that actually works. Clients respond to honesty far more than to polish.

## Win the new neighbourhood in the first two weeks

Your old neighbourhood knew you. Your new one does not, and the first fortnight decides how fast that changes. Start before you open.

Walk the street. Introduce yourself to the shops next door — the café, the pharmacy, the gym, the boutique. Leave a card offering their staff a first-visit discount and ask if you can leave a few on their counter. A salon that arrives as a friendly neighbour gets recommended far faster than one that arrives as a stranger behind a closed door.

Update every listing the same week you move, not a month later. Your Google Business Profile, your Instagram bio, your WhatsApp Business address, your booking link and your website all need the new pin and the new parking note. Check the listings yourself on your own phone, as a client would.

Then give the new neighbourhood a reason to walk in. A "new neighbours" offer for the first month — a free blow-dry with any colour, or a complimentary brow shape with a first facial — works because it is tied to the move and it has an end date. Put it on a small sign in the window, on your WhatsApp Status and on a leaflet in the building lobby. Keep it simple: one offer, one month, one clear way to claim it.

## Don't let enquiries slip through the cracks

Moving week is the worst possible time to miss messages. Clients are asking where you are, whether you are open, whether their usual stylist is still there — and they are asking on WhatsApp at 10 pm while your team is surrounded by boxes. A 24/7 AI receptionist for salons and beauty businesses in the UAE, like ZYNA, can keep answering those "where are you now?" messages and taking bookings while the whole team is busy with the move, so no enquiry dies in the chaos.

Make one person on the team the "move enquiries" owner for the first month. Their job is simple: every message about the new location gets a pin, a parking note and a booking option in the same reply. Speed matters more than polish here — a client who gets an answer in two minutes books; one who waits until morning often books elsewhere.

## Turn moving week into bookings

The week of the move, your diary will look messy. Use that. Offer your regulars a "last week at the old salon" slot and a "first week at the new salon" slot in the same message. Clients who book both give you a full final week and a full opening week, and the salon never feels empty in either place.

On the first Saturday in the new location, run a small open morning. Coffee, a look around, a free hair consultation or a mini hand massage for anyone who pops in. It costs very little, it fills the room with exactly the people you want to meet — the neighbours — and it gives you photos and video of a busy new salon to share for weeks afterwards.

Finally, measure the move honestly. Four weeks after opening, count three numbers: how many regulars from the old location have rebooked, how many new clients came from the new neighbourhood, and how many enquiries mentioned the move. If the regulars followed you and the new area is booking, the move worked.

A salon move is not a disruption to survive. It is a launch, and you get to do it with something a brand-new salon does not have: clients who already love you. Tell them early, tell the story well, welcome the new neighbours properly, and the new address becomes the best marketing your salon ever had.
