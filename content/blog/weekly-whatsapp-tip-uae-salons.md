Open the WhatsApp of almost any UAE salon client and you will see the same pattern: her salon only messages when it wants something — a discount, an empty chair, a reminder about tomorrow. Useful, but every one is about the salon, not about her.

Now imagine a different message landing on a Sunday evening. No offer, no ask. Just one useful thing: why her colour faded faster this month, or the mistake that makes frizz worse in Dubai humidity. She reads it, tries it, and thinks of your salon. That is the weekly tip — one short, genuinely useful message a week that keeps your salon in her mind without sounding like an advert.

## Why teaching beats selling on WhatsApp

Clients guard their WhatsApp — it is where family talks and friends send photos. A salon that only shows up to sell slowly trains the client to ignore it, or to mute it. A salon that shows up with something useful becomes the expert she trusts.

That trust is what fills chairs. When a client has tried your advice at home and it worked, your salon is the first name that comes to mind when she next needs a cut or a colour. She needs to be reminded that you know what you are doing. A weekly tip does that quietly, for five minutes of your time.

## What counts as a tip — and what doesn't

A good tip answers a question she has already asked in your chair. In the UAE, those questions are wonderfully specific: why does my blonde turn brassy so fast, or my hair feel dry even though I condition it? The answers live in the local climate — hard water, chlorine, strong sun, air conditioning. Your team answers these questions ten times a week already — a tip simply writes one down and sends it to everyone who would benefit.

What a tip is not: a disguised offer. "Book a keratin treatment this week and get 20% off" is an advert wearing a friendly hat, and clients can tell. A real tip helps even if she never books anything because of it — and that absence of a sales pitch is exactly what makes her trust the next message too.

## Steal your tips from real client questions

You do not need to brainstorm tips — your clients hand them to you every day. Keep a small notebook at reception and write down every question a client asks during her visit. By Friday you will have a dozen — pick the one you heard most often. That is next week's tip.

The best tips solve a small, everyday problem: rinsing chlorine out after a swim, why hot water fades coloured hair, how to make a blow-dry last an extra day in August humidity. The smaller and more practical the advice, the more likely she is to try it.

## The format: short, specific, one photo

A WhatsApp tip is not a blog article. Keep it under six lines, in your own voice: the problem she recognises, the fix in plain words, and one line that keeps the door open. For example: "Rinse coloured hair lukewarm, not hot, and it holds for weeks longer. Already faded? A gloss takes twenty minutes."

One photo helps, but only if it is real — a quick photo of the product or a before-and-after from your own salon beats any stock image. The whole message should take ten seconds to read and feel written for her.

## When to send it

Sunday evening between 7 and 8 PM is the sweet spot in the UAE: the weekend is ending and people are planning their week. A tip that arrives then feels like a thoughtful note, not an interruption.

Avoid Friday midday, when many clients are at prayer, and late evenings after 9 PM, when a salon message feels intrusive. Never send a tip on the same day as another message — a reminder, an offer, a confirmation — or the two blur into one. And never send more than one a week: the moment your number becomes the salon that forwards things, clients mute it. One message a week, at the same time, becomes a small habit she starts to expect.

## Who gets it

A weekly tip only works if it goes to people who want it. Start with your regulars — clients who come every three to six weeks and already trust you. When a new client books, ask one question at the end of her visit: "Would you like our one tip a week on WhatsApp? Just hair and skin advice, no offers." Most will say yes.

Keep the lists small and grouped by service — colour tips for colour clients, skin tips for facial clients, nail tips for nail clients. A mistargeted tip teaches her to ignore the next one. Thirty people who read every message are worth far more than three hundred who mute you.

## How a tip quietly becomes a booking

A tip never asks for a booking, but it creates them anyway. A client reads your hard-water tip and books a detox. A client tries your frizz advice, it half works, and she books a consultation to finish the job. None of them responded to an offer. They responded to being helped.

The replies matter too. A tip always gets replies — a thank you, a follow-up question, a photo of her own hair. Answer every one the same day. Those replies are the warmest enquiries a salon ever receives: a client talking to you because she wants to, not because you chased her. ## A four-week starter plan

Start this Sunday with a month of tips from the questions UAE clients ask most. Week one: hard water and the rinse that helps. Week two: the lukewarm-water rule for colour. Week three: skin, sun and air conditioning. Week four: when nails need a break from gel. Write each in under six lines, with one real photo. Watch which tip gets the most replies — your next month of tips starts there.

The salons that win in the UAE are not always the ones shouting loudest. They are the ones clients trust. One useful tip a week, to the people who asked for it, builds that trust faster than any discount ever will. Start this Sunday, keep it short and honest.
