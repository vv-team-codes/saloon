During Ramadan, the rhythm of a UAE salon changes completely. The quiet hours move, the busy hours move, and the client in your chair at three in the afternoon has not eaten or drunk anything since dawn. She is not being difficult. She is fasting — and the salons that notice that, and plan for it, are the ones she comes back to long after the month ends. A long colour appointment that feels relaxing in October can feel endless in Ramadan. Caring for her well costs almost nothing: ask one question at booking, slow the pace, and be ready for sunset.

## Ask at booking — once, and kindly

The whole experience starts with one question, asked when the appointment is made: "Would you prefer a daytime slot, or one after Iftar?" Note her answer on the booking, and never ask again. Nobody wants to explain that she is fasting to three different people across one visit.

A daytime client will usually arrive tired, fitting the salon around work or family. An evening client has eaten, but she is arriving into your busiest hours. During Ramadan, many enquiries arrive late at night, when families are awake and planning. ## Slow the daytime appointment down

A fasting client tires faster, and she will rarely say so. Build that into the visit instead of waiting for her to ask. Keep the room cool — the step from a hot Dubai afternoon into strong air conditioning is a shock to the body, and a fasting client feels it more.

Shorten where you can. A cut and blow-dry that normally runs ninety minutes can often be done in sixty if the stylist skips the long chat and the extra mirror checks. For long services, offer to split the visit: colour one day, cut and finish another. Check in quietly, and check in early — "Are you comfortable?" after the first ten minutes means far more than the same question at the end. And know the one thing never to do: never offer a fasting client water, tea or coffee during the day. Save the hospitality for sunset.

## Iftar at the chair

Sooner or later during Ramadan, sunset will fall in the middle of a long appointment. This is the moment clients remember — for years. Keep a small tray set aside every evening: dates, water, and a glass. Know the Maghrib time for that day, and watch the clock yourself, so the client does not have to. When the time comes, pause the service for two minutes, hand her the dates and the water, and step back.

It costs almost nothing, and it is the single most talked-about gesture a salon can make in Ramadan. Clients tell their sisters, their mothers and their group chats about the salon that broke their fast with them. For clients booked right at Iftar time, ask at booking whether she would like to break her fast at the salon or arrive after she has eaten at home.

## Move the heavy work to the evening

Not every service suits a fasting body. Colour, keratin and long treatments ask a lot of a client who has had no food or water all day. Wherever the diary allows, steer those services to after-Iftar slots, when she has eaten and her energy is back. Keep the daytime diary for the lighter work: blow-dries, trims, brows, threading and quick facials.

This is also a diary decision, not just a kindness. A client who feels faint halfway through a four-hour colour service costs you the rest of the evening: the service stops, the chair sits empty, and the team scrambles.

## Prayer, privacy and the quiet chair

Fasting clients often want to pray during a long appointment, and many will not ask twice. Make it easy the first time. If your salon has a quiet corner or a small room, keep it tidy and available through the evening, with space to wash before prayer. Mention it once during the consultation — "the quiet room is free whenever you need it" — and leave it there.

The same applies to the chair itself. A fasting client who is tired wants a calm chair, not a chatty one. Read her cues: many fasting clients want quiet, a phone to scroll, and a stylist who works without a running commentary.

## When a fasting client feels unwell

Most fasting clients are perfectly fine. But a long, hot appointment can leave someone light-headed or headachy, and the team needs to know what to do without panicking. Stop the service calmly, sit her up slowly, and put a cool cloth on her neck. A few minutes of still air fixes most episodes.

If she is clearly unwell, offer water — and mean it. Health comes before the fast, and every client knows that. If she faints, or does not recover quickly, call 999 and stay with her. Then follow up the same evening with a short WhatsApp message. Not a sales message — a human one.

## The team is fasting too

None of this works if the team is running on empty. In most UAE salons, several stylists will be fasting alongside their clients. Look after them with the same attention: shorter shifts where the diary allows, a real break at Iftar with dates and a proper meal, and a rota that does not put a fasting stylist on the longest service of the day. A tired team cannot deliver a calm experience. Protect the team's energy and you protect the client's experience.

## A simple Ramadan checklist

None of this needs a meeting or a budget. Write it on one page and put it at the front desk before the month begins: ask the daytime-or-evening question at booking; keep the Maghrib time on the diary each day; keep dates and water ready every evening; offer the quiet room once; split long services across two visits where you can; and check in early, not late.

Ramadan is the month when UAE salons are at their busiest and their clients are at their most stretched. The salons that win are not the ones with the biggest offers. They are the ones that noticed the woman in the chair had not eaten since dawn — and made the visit feel easy anyway.
