It is Saturday at 6 PM in your Dubai salon. The colour client in the chair needs twenty more minutes, the blow-dry after her is running over, and the next client is sitting in the car park. Nobody has told her anything. She watches the minutes pass and starts scrolling for another salon. By the time you message her, she has already decided.

This happens every week. A diary that runs ten minutes behind by midday can be forty minutes behind by evening, because the peak hours are stacked tight. The delay itself is rarely the problem. Clients understand that a colour can take longer than planned. What they do not forgive is silence. A client who is told will wait. A client who is left guessing will walk.

## Tell them before they ask

The rule is simple: the moment you know a client will wait more than fifteen minutes, message her. Do not wait until her appointment time has passed. A message that arrives twenty minutes before her slot says "we respect your time". The same message sent ten minutes after she was supposed to be in the chair says "we forgot about you".

In practice this means the front desk needs a trigger, not a feeling. The moment a stylist says a service will finish late, the front desk checks who is affected next. In a busy UAE evening diary that might mean three messages in ten minutes — far cheaper than three empty chairs next Saturday.

## The four-line delay message

A good delay message is short, specific and honest. It has four parts: what is happening, how long the wait will be, what you are doing about it, and a real choice for the client.

Here is a message you can copy and adapt: "Hi Sara, this is [salon name]. Your 7 PM appointment with Mariam is running about 20 minutes late because the colour before you is taking longer than planned. We have your chair ready and Mariam will be with you as soon as she finishes. If you would rather come at 7:30, or move to tomorrow at 11 AM, just reply and we will change it — no charge."

Notice what it does not do. It does not apologise five times or blame the previous client. It gives a real time, a real reason and a real option. The client can decide, and that feeling of control is what keeps her from cancelling in frustration.

## Honest time, not hopeful time

The biggest mistake salons make with delay messages is underestimating the wait. The front desk says fifteen minutes because it sounds better than thirty, and then the client waits forty. That second wait feels like a lie, even when nobody intended to mislead her.

Always quote the longer, honest figure. If you think it will be twenty minutes, say twenty-five. If the chair is ready after fifteen, she is pleasantly surprised. If you promise fifteen and it takes thirty, you have spent the goodwill your first message bought. An honest time also lets her plan — she can stop for coffee or arrive a little later instead of sitting in the car park.

## Give them a real choice

A delay message that only says "we are late, please wait" is not a message. It is an instruction, and tired clients do not like instructions. Always offer a genuine alternative: a later slot the same evening, a priority slot tomorrow, or a different stylist who is free now.

This is where knowing your diary matters. Check the real diary before sending the message — never offer a tomorrow slot that is already full. If another stylist can do the service well, name her. "Lina is free at 7:15 and can do your blow-dry" is a solution. "Let us know what you would like to do" is not, because it pushes the work back onto the client.

Be careful with the alternative you offer, though. Never push a client onto a stylist she has never met for a service she is particular about, like a colour correction or a bridal trial. For those, the honest offer is a later time with her own stylist, or a rescheduled appointment with a small gesture — a complimentary blow-dry at her next visit.

## First-timers need the message most

Not every waiting client is the same. A regular who has been coming to you for a year will forgive a delay if you tell her early and treat her well when she arrives. A first-time client has no reason to forgive anything. She chose you from a dozen salons on Google Maps, she is nervous already, and a silent forty-minute wait is the whole story she will tell about you.

That is why first-time clients should get the delay message first, and the warmest version of it. Name her stylist and her service, and add one line that shows you remember her: "We have your consultation notes ready and Mariam is looking forward to meeting you."

## Make it a team habit

Delay messages only work if they happen every time, and that means they cannot depend on one person's memory. Write the rule down: fifteen minutes or more behind means a message goes out, sent by whoever is on the front desk, using the salon's shared WhatsApp number so the client can reply in the same thread.

Keep a short template saved as a quick reply, with blanks for name, time and stylist. When a client arrives after a delay, the stylist should know about it before she greets her — a one-line diary note lets her welcome the client properly. A drink on arrival and a genuine thank-you cost almost nothing and are remembered far longer than the delay.

## The follow-up that turns a delay into loyalty

The work is not finished when the client leaves. That evening, send one short WhatsApp message: "Thank you for your patience today, Sara. We know your time matters and we are sorry you had to wait. We hope you love the result — let us know how it settles over the next few days." No offer, no ask. Just thanks.

Clients remember how a salon handles a bad moment more than the bad moment itself. A salon that communicates honestly when things slip earns more trust than one that is never late but never speaks.
