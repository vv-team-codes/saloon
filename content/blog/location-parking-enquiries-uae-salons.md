It usually arrives in the evening. A WhatsApp message from a number you do not recognise: "Where are you located?" Sometimes it is even shorter: "Location please." Sometimes a follow-up lands before you can reply: "Is there parking?"

It is tempting to treat this as a small question. It is not. A person who asks where you are has already decided your work is worth the trip. How you answer decides whether that warm enquiry becomes an appointment or quietly disappears.

## Why this enquiry is warmer than it looks

Most salon enquiries are about price or availability. A location enquiry is different: the person is no longer comparing you with five other salons. They are picturing the trip — whether they can get to you after work, find parking, and arrive without stress.

That is why a slow or vague reply is so costly. Answer three hours later with only a dropped pin and the moment has passed; answer quickly, with everything they need in one message, and you remove the last reason not to book.

## Answer the three questions hiding inside "where are you?"

Behind every location enquiry there are three real questions — answer all three, even if the client only asked one.

First, where exactly are you? Not just the area — the building, the floor, the entrance, the landmark next door. In Dubai and Abu Dhabi, a mall name alone is not enough: a big mall has several entrances, and the wrong one can mean a ten-minute walk through the wrong wing.

Second, how do I get there? A short line each: the nearest metro station and exit, the taxi drop-off point, the parking level.

Third, will it be easy when I arrive? Clients rarely ask this out loud, but they are wondering whether parking will be a struggle and whether they will get lost. A calm, specific answer removes that worry before it forms.

## Landmarks beat addresses

A street address looks professional, but it does not help a client already in the car. In the UAE, people navigate by landmarks: the mall entrance near the food court, the tower opposite the park, the salon above the pharmacy.

Build a one-line directions sentence and use it everywhere: "We are on the first floor of Marina Mall, entrance C near the food court, next to the pharmacy." Then add the map pin — the pin does the navigation, the sentence does the reassuring.

Keep the same directions on your Google Business Profile, your Instagram bio link, and your WhatsApp business profile. When the directions are identical everywhere, clients stop double-checking and start booking.

## Send the pin, the photo, and the parking answer together

The best reply is one message that contains everything: a dropped pin alone leaves questions, a typed address alone leaves doubt, but together they close the loop.

Try this shape: a greeting, the directions sentence, the live location pin, one line about parking, and a booking question. "Hi! We are on the first floor of Marina Mall, entrance C near the food court. Here is our location. Parking is free for three hours on level P2, and the lift from P2 comes straight to our floor. Would you like Thursday at 6 pm or Friday at 11 am?"

That message answers the question, removes the parking worry, and moves the conversation to a booking in the same breath.

## Talk about parking before they ask

Parking is the hidden deal-breaker in UAE salon enquiries. A client in Dubai Marina, JLT, or Downtown knows parking can eat twenty minutes of their evening — and if your reply says nothing about it, they assume the worst.

So say it first. Free or paid, which level, how long the validation lasts, whether valet is available, whether street parking is realistic at 7 pm. If parking is genuinely difficult near you, say so honestly and offer the alternative: the nearest metro station or the taxi drop-off.

## Turn the answer into a booking in the same message

The biggest mistake salons make is answering the question and stopping there. The client asked where you are. You told them. The conversation ends. Nobody books.

Always end with a next step. Two specific appointment options work better than an open question: "Would you like Thursday at 6 pm or Friday at 11 am?" is easy to answer, while "When would you like to come?" is a decision the client has to make from scratch — and many will put it off.

If the client is asking late at night, they may be planning for tomorrow, and a quick reply with two slots for the next day often converts on the spot. This is also where a 24/7 AI receptionist for salons and beauty businesses in the UAE earns its keep: it can answer the location question, send the pin and the parking details, and offer real slots at midnight, when your front desk is closed.

## When your salon is genuinely hard to find

Some salons sit in new buildings or on upper floors where the map pin lands in the wrong place. Fix what you can and warn about the rest. Stand outside your salon and confirm the pin is on the right building and entrance — a pin one street off sends clients on a loop, and many will give up rather than call. Add a short "finding us" note to your booking confirmation, and keep a saved reply for tricky cases like two towers with the same name. When the whole team gives the same directions, clients stop getting confused by different answers.

## Make it a system, not a favour

Location enquiries arrive several times a day, and the quality of the answer varies with whoever is on the phone. So turn it into a small system: save a quick reply with your directions sentence, the entrance photo, and the parking line, and make sure the whole team uses the same version. Add the directions to your WhatsApp business profile, your Google listing, and every booking confirmation, and check monthly that the pin and parking details are still current.

And for one week, note how many location enquiries turn into bookings. If the number is low, the problem is rarely your location — it is usually the reply: too slow, too vague, or ending without a booking question. Fix the reply and the conversion follows.

A client who asks where you are is telling you they want to come. Give them a clear path, remove the parking worry, offer a time — and most will walk through your door.
