In Dubai, almost every client has a flight coming up. A weekend in London, a family visit to Manila, a business trip to Riyadh. For UAE salons, that constant travel is quietly becoming a reliable source of bookings — because more clients want to look good before they board, and to land looking like they never spent seven hours in dry cabin air.

The pre-flight appointment is not new, but the way salons treat it is. Smart salons now package travel-ready treatments as a named service: a short menu chosen for flying, timed around departure, sold as a ritual before every trip.

## Why the pre-flight client is different

A client booking before a flight is not shopping for a treat. She has a deadline, a fixed departure time, and one fear: arriving looking tired. Cabin air is dry, sleep is broken, and a long-haul flight shows on skin, hair and nails within hours. What she wants is a result that survives the journey — hydrated skin, tidy nails, hair that still looks styled when she lands.

That changes what she books. She does not want a treatment that needs recovery time, and she does not want to experiment. A facial that leaves redness, a brand-new hair colour, or a peel that needs days of sun protection are all wrong the day before a flight. What she wants is safe, proven and finished: a hydrating facial, a fresh manicure, a smooth blow-dry, shaped brows. It also changes when she books — appointments cluster in the evening before departure, so keeping a few late slots flexible wins the client who decides at lunchtime that she flies tomorrow night.

## What belongs on a travel-ready menu

The best travel-ready menus are short. Five or six services, each with a clear reason it suits flying.

A hydrating facial comes first. Hours of recirculated cabin air pull moisture from skin, so a deep-hydration facial the day before a flight is the most useful treatment a traveller can book. Keep it gentle — no strong exfoliation, no extractions that leave marks, nothing that needs recovery.

Nails come second. A fresh manicure or pedicure lasts the whole trip, needs no maintenance, and photographs well on holiday. Gel or builder gel suits travellers because it survives luggage, swimming pools and two weeks of wear.

Hair is third. A blow-dry on the morning of departure is a small luxury with a big payoff — the client boards looking polished and arrives with hair that still holds its shape. For longer trips, a gloss or root touch-up a few days before departure keeps colour fresh for the whole holiday. The rule is timing: colour and chemical treatments belong two or three days before the flight, never the night before.

Brows and lashes round out the menu. A brow shape, a lash lift or a tint means one less thing to think about while travelling, and these fit neatly into the same visit as nails or a blow-dry.

## Timing it right: the 48-hour rule

The salons doing this well give every pre-flight client the same simple guidance: the two days before a flight have their own order. Colour and chemical work two to three days out. Hydrating facials, nails, brows and lashes in the final 24 to 48 hours. Blow-dry on the morning of departure if the salon opens early enough, or the evening before.

Put that guidance on the menu itself, not just in conversation. A client who can see "best 48 hours before your flight" next to a treatment understands why you are recommending it, and books it at the right time instead of the wrong one.

The diary side is simple. Pre-flight bookings are predictable once you start tracking them: Thursday evenings and Friday mornings before weekend departures, and the evenings before school-holiday flights.

## Packaging it: the "Fly Fresh" treatment

A named package turns scattered services into a product clients ask for by name. The simplest version is a two-service combination — hydrating facial plus manicure, or blow-dry plus brow shape — priced as a package with a small saving against booking separately. "Fly Fresh" works; so does "Travel Ready" or "Holiday Ready". The name matters less than the fact that it exists as one bookable thing.

Price it for the traveller's reality. She is already spending on a holiday, so the package should feel like a sensible part of the trip, not a luxury add-on. The margin comes from the combination: two services in one visit, one chair, one appointment, and a client who rebooks the same package every time she flies.

## Winning the booking — and the return visit

The pre-flight client finds you through timing, not advertising. The enquiries arrive late at night — a client packing at 10 p.m. realises she wants an appointment tomorrow, and messages then. Salons that answer within minutes win that booking; salons that answer the next morning often find she has already booked elsewhere. A 24/7 AI receptionist like ZYNA can take those late-night enquiries, check the diary and confirm a slot while the client is still packing, which is exactly when the decision is made.

Beyond the late-night enquiry, the marketing is mostly about being visible where travellers already are. Expat community groups and building WhatsApp groups light up before school holidays and long weekends, and a short post — "flying this weekend? Our Fly Fresh package still has Thursday evening slots" — reaches exactly the right people at exactly the right moment.

The smartest salons treat the pre-flight visit and the post-flight visit as one service, not two. The client who books before she flies is the same client who needs a hydrating facial when she lands, a colour refresh after two weeks of sun, and a manicure removal before her next trip. Offer the return appointment at the pre-flight visit, while she is still in the chair, and book it for the week she is due back. It takes thirty seconds, and it doubles the value of every traveller who walks through the door.

In a city where everyone flies, the salon that thinks in departures and arrivals has an advantage the salon down the road does not. What is new is naming the moment, timing the menu around it, and answering the enquiry when it arrives — usually late at night, the evening before a flight.
