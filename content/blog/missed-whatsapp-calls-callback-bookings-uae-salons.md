Your hands are covered in colour, the client in your chair is mid-consultation, and the salon phone lights up. A WhatsApp call. You cannot answer, and by the time the colour is processing, the call is gone. That caller was ready to book — she had chosen your salon and reached out at the exact moment she wanted an appointment. Then she heard nothing back.

This happens in almost every UAE salon, every day. Between 5 PM and 9 PM, the phone rings most and gets answered least. The person who called simply moves on to the next salon on her list and books there instead — and you never even know she existed.

## Why a missed call is not a small thing

A missed WhatsApp call is different from a missed message. A message waits in the inbox and can be answered an hour later. A call is a person asking for your attention right now, and when nobody picks up, the silence itself is an answer. In a WhatsApp-first market like the UAE, an unanswered call feels personal: the client wonders whether the salon is closed or whether she should bother trying again.

Salon owners usually underestimate how many calls they miss. The phone only shows the calls someone happened to notice, not the ones that rang while the whole team was busy or after closing. Over a month, a busy salon can easily miss fifty or more. If even a third of those callers would have booked, that is real money walking past the door every week.

## The ten-minute callback rule

The fix is not hiring another receptionist. It is a simple rule the whole team follows: every missed WhatsApp call gets a callback or a message within ten minutes while the salon is open — short enough that the client still remembers why she called, long enough that a stylist can finish a rinse.

The callback does not need to be a phone call. A short WhatsApp message is often better, because the client may be at work, driving, or sitting in another salon's waiting room. Something warm and specific works best: "Hi, this is Aisha from Glow Salon in Jumeirah — sorry we missed your call. Would you like to book a cut and colour this week? We have Thursday at 6 PM or Saturday at 11 AM." It names the salon, apologises briefly, and ends with two real slots. The client only has to pick one.

If she does not reply within a couple of hours, send one gentle follow-up the same evening: "Just checking in — those two slots are still open if you would like one. Happy to hold one for you for an hour." Then stop. A third message starts to feel like pressure, and a client who feels chased will not come back.

## Keep a missed-call log, not a memory

A callback rule only works if someone owns it. In most salons, the missed call belongs to nobody. The receptionist assumes a stylist saw it. The stylist assumes the receptionist will handle it. Nobody does. The answer is a small daily habit: one person per shift owns the phone, and the missed-call list in WhatsApp is checked every hour, on the hour.

It takes less than a minute. Open WhatsApp, tap the calls tab, and look at the missed calls. Anyone not yet called back gets a message straight away. At the end of the day, the shift owner does one final sweep before closing.

## After-hours calls need an answer too

Missed calls do not stop at closing time. In the UAE, clients often browse salons late at night and call at 10 PM or even midnight. Nobody expects a person to answer at that hour, but they do expect something. A greeting message that says when the salon opens and invites them to leave a message changes everything. Better still, a short automated reply confirming the salon saw the call and will ring back first thing in the morning turns a dead end into a warm start.

First thing in the morning is not a suggestion. It is a promise. The first job of the day, before the diary is opened, is to return every call missed overnight. A cheerful morning message with two slots often books them before 9 AM.

## When the callback goes unanswered

Sometimes you call back and nobody picks up. That is normal, and it is not a failure. Leave a short voice note rather than another missed call: "Hi, this is Sara from Lumiere Salon in Marina — sorry we missed your call earlier. We have a few slots open this week for balayage, so just message us back whenever suits you." A voice note is harder to ignore than a text. If she still does not reply, leave it there. She has your number, she knows you tried, and many clients come back days later when their schedule clears.

One habit to avoid is calling back from a personal number. The client saved your salon's business number, and a call from an unknown personal number looks like a wrong number or spam. Keep every callback on the salon's WhatsApp Business number, so the client sees the salon name and the conversation stays where the whole team can see it.

## Make it a team system, not a favour

A callback system only survives if it is written down and shared. Put it on the wall near the reception desk in three lines: check missed calls every hour, reply within ten minutes, sweep once more before closing. New staff learn it on their first day. The salon owner checks the log once a week, not to catch people out, but to see whether the system is working. Teams keep doing what gets noticed.

The salons that win in the UAE are not always the ones with the biggest advertising budget. They are the ones that answer. Every missed call is a client who chose you first. A ten-minute callback, a message with two real slots, and a morning sweep of the overnight calls will recover bookings you never knew you were losing.
