Walk down any salon corridor in a Dubai mall and you will pass ten businesses offering the same cut, colour and facial. So how does a client choose? Most of the time, she does not choose a salon. She chooses a person she already feels she knows.

That is the quiet shift in UAE salon marketing. The owners growing fastest are not the ones with the biggest ad budget. They are the ones who put their own face to the business — answering questions on camera and letting clients meet them before they walk through the door.

## Why Clients Book a Person, Not a Logo

A salon visit is intimate. A client hands over her hair or her appearance before a wedding, a job interview or a family gathering. That takes trust, and trust is hard to build from a logo or a stock photo of a model who has never set foot in your salon.

When a client has watched the owner explain how she fixes brassy blonde in hard water, the first visit stops feeling like a gamble. She arrives comfortable, knowing who will greet her.

This matters even more in the UAE, where so many clients are new to the country and choosing a salon from scratch. A newcomer in JVC, Motor City or a Sharjah neighbourhood does not have a neighbour to ask. She searches, she scrolls, and she picks the salon that feels like a real person runs it. A faceless page competes on price. A page with a real owner competes on trust — and trust wins the booking.

## What "Being the Face" Actually Looks Like

Becoming the face of your salon does not mean dancing on TikTok or filming yourself all day. It means showing up, briefly and consistently, where your clients already look.

The strongest version is simple: the owner, in her own salon, talking about her work in her own words. A sixty-second clip answering one real question a client asked that week. A photo with a bride after the morning trial. None of this needs a studio, a script or a professional crew — a phone, decent window light and a steady hand are enough.

The mistake is thinking you need to be polished. Clients respond to honesty, not production value. A video where the owner explains why she refused to bleach a client's damaged hair earns more bookings than a glossy advert, because it proves something no discount can buy: this salon will tell you the truth.

## Five Simple Formats That Work

Start with formats that are easy to repeat, because consistency beats cleverness. The first is the one-question answer. Every week, take the most common question your team heard — "Will keratin survive the humidity?" — and answer it on camera in under a minute. These clips also become your best WhatsApp replies.

The second is the transformation with a voice. Before-and-after photos are everywhere, so they no longer stop the scroll. What stops it is the owner explaining, in thirty seconds, what she saw and what she did. "She came in with three years of box dye. Here is how we got her back to healthy hair." That is a story, not a picture.

The third is the honest behind-the-scenes moment. Show the colour mixing, the chair being prepared, the team arriving early on a Saturday. Clients love seeing the care behind the visit — and it justifies your prices.

The fourth is the myth-buster. The UAE market is full of confident claims — miracle treatments, instant results. A calm owner correcting one myth a week positions your salon as the honest voice in a noisy market.

The fifth is the community moment. A thank-you to a regular of five years, a team member's anniversary, the National Day decorations going up. These posts do not sell a service. They sell belonging, and belonging turns a one-time client into a regular.

## A Weekly Rhythm That Fits a Working Owner

The reason most owners quit this is time. You are cutting hair, managing staff and answering WhatsApp at midnight, so a plan that needs two hours a day will die in week two.

One hour a week is enough. Block thirty minutes on a quiet weekday morning — Tuesday or Wednesday, when the diary is lightest — and record three short clips back to back, answering three questions clients asked that week. Post one on Tuesday, one on Thursday and one on Saturday, when weekend clients are deciding where to book. Spend the rest of the hour replying to comments and messages, because the reply is where the booking happens.

## If You Would Rather Not Be on Camera

Not every owner is comfortable on camera, and that is fine. The face of the salon does not have to be yours alone. A senior stylist who loves explaining her work can take the lead, with your name and your standards behind them.

If nobody on the team wants to film, start smaller. A voiceover on a video of the work, with the owner speaking but not appearing, still builds familiarity. A written post in the owner's voice — "Here is what I told a client this morning" — still beats a generic flyer. The goal is that a client feels she knows someone at your salon before she books.

There is also a simple test for whether this is working. After a month, ask every new client one question at the chair: "How did you find us?" If the answer starts to be "I saw your video," the face is doing its job. Track it in a notebook for a few weeks and you will know exactly which kind of clip brought the most bookings.

## The Salon People Already Know

Salon marketing in the UAE will keep getting noisier. More salons will open and more discounts will flash across your clients' screens. The salons that keep growing through that noise will be the ones clients feel they already know — the owner whose advice they trust and whose face they recognise before the first hello.

You do not need a marketing agency to start. You need a phone, one honest answer a week and the patience to keep showing up.
