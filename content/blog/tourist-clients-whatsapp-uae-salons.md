She lands at Dubai International on a Thursday afternoon, checks into a hotel in JBR or Downtown, and by Friday evening she is scrolling through salon profiles on her phone. She has three days in the city and one free afternoon. She messages three salons on WhatsApp. The one that replies first, clearly, with a price and a time, gets her.

Tourist clients are one of the most overlooked segments in the UAE salon business. Dubai and Abu Dhabi welcome millions of visitors every year, and many of them want exactly what your salon sells: a blow-dry before a dinner, a manicure between sightseeing stops, a facial after a long flight. They are not looking for a salon for life. They are looking for a salon for today.

## The tourist enquiry looks different

A regular client's message is usually short: "Same as last time, Thursday 6?" A tourist's message is a bundle of questions in one go. "Hi, I'm staying at a hotel on JBR, can I come tomorrow for a blow-dry and manicure? How much and how long? Do you take card?"

That message tells you everything about how to win her. She does not know your area, your prices, or your diary. She may be messaging from a foreign number or hotel Wi-Fi with no local SIM, and she may be writing at 1 AM your time because it is still evening where she flew from. She has almost certainly messaged two or three other salons in the same ten minutes.

The salon that replies the next morning with "Please call us for prices" has already lost her. She booked somewhere else while your team was asleep.

## Speed is the whole game

With tourist enquiries, reply time is the deciding factor. A visitor with forty-eight hours left in the city cannot wait for a reply. If your first message lands within a few minutes, you are in the running. If it lands the next day, you are answering a client who has already had her hair done.

This is where the after-hours gap hurts most. Tourist messages peak in the late evening, when visitors are back at the hotel planning tomorrow. A salon that only answers during working hours hands those bookings to whichever competitor replies first. ## Answer the four questions before she asks them

A tourist enquiry almost always hides the same four questions. Answer all four in your first reply and you will stand out from every salon that replies with a question of its own.

First, the price. Give a range, not a "prices start from" that hides the real figure. "Blow-dry AED 120–180 depending on length, manicure AED 90" is an answer she can decide on. A vague reply sends her to the next salon.

Second, the time. Tell her how long the service takes and which slots are actually free tomorrow. "Takes about 45 minutes, we have 2 PM and 4:30 PM open" beats "What time suits you?" every time.

Third, the location. An address alone means nothing to someone who arrived yesterday. Give her a landmark, the nearest metro station, and whether there is valet parking or a mall entrance she should use. A WhatsApp location pin in the same message removes the last excuse not to come.

Fourth, payment. Many visitors do not carry local cash. Say it plainly: cards accepted, Apple Pay and Google Pay accepted, no cash needed. Ask for her hotel name when she books and write it in the appointment note, so the front desk can greet her by name if she messages again. Send one short confirmation the evening before, or the morning of, with the time, the service, the price and the pin. If she writes in another language, reply in hers if you can, or keep your English simple, with times and prices as numbers.

## Sell the one-visit service, honestly

This is where honesty earns you more than a hard sell. A tourist leaving on Sunday is not a candidate for a six-session treatment plan. Recommend the services that fit a holiday: a blow-dry, an express facial, a manicure and pedicure, braids, henna, a quick colour refresh. These are services with a visible result in one visit, and they are exactly what she came in for.

If she asks for something that genuinely needs more than one visit, say so. "That colour correction needs two appointments and you would need to be here next week, so I would suggest the gloss and blow-dry instead." That answer costs you a bigger sale and buys you something worth far more: a client who trusts you and writes about it.

Do not push a loyalty card or a membership at a tourist either. She will not be back next week, and offering one makes it obvious you did not read her message.

## Turn one visit into a review and a return booking

A happy tourist is worth more than one appointment. Visitors write reviews on the flight home, in the taxi to the airport, and in the hotel lobby while they wait for check-out. A five-star Google review from a visitor carries weight precisely because she had no reason to be loyal to you. She chose you from a search, had a good experience, and said so in public.

Ask for that review while she is still smiling, not three days later when she is home and has forgotten your salon's name. A short WhatsApp message as she leaves, with a direct link to your Google profile, is enough: "Thank you for coming in today. If you enjoyed your visit, a quick review here would mean the world to us." Most happy clients will tap the link.

Then plant the seed for the next trip. "Next time you are in Dubai, message us before you fly and we will hold your favourite slot." Add her to your client notes with the service she had and the stylist she liked. When she messages again in six months, and many do, you can greet her like a returning regular instead of a stranger.

Dubai's visitors are already searching, already messaging, and already deciding within minutes. The salons that win them are not the ones with the biggest advertising budget. They are the ones that reply first and make a stranger feel like a regular for a single afternoon.
