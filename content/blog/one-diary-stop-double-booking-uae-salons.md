A client walks in at 6 pm for a balayage she booked on Instagram. The chair is already taken — by a client who booked the same slot on WhatsApp an hour earlier. Now the front desk has to disappoint one of them in front of the other.

This is one of the most common appointment mistakes in UAE salons. A typical Dubai salon takes bookings from five places at once: WhatsApp, Instagram, phone, walk-ins, and an online booking page. Without one diary that all five feed into, two people will eventually be given the same chair.

## Why double-bookings keep happening

The evening rush makes it worse. Between 5 pm and 9 pm, most UAE salons are at their busiest, and the person answering enquiries is often the same person blow-drying a client or greeting someone at the door.

So a stylist glances at a WhatsApp message, says "yes, 6 pm is fine," and means to write it down later. Meanwhile the receptionist, who never saw that message, confirms 6 pm to a walk-in. There was simply no rule that said a booking does not exist until it is in the diary.

The second cause is the diary itself. Many salons run two diaries without meaning to: a paper book at the desk and a phone full of messages. A diary only works if it is the only diary.

## The rule that fixes it: nothing is booked until it is in the diary

Every salon that has solved this runs on one rule, worth writing on the wall at reception: a booking does not exist until it is in the shared diary. Not when the message is read, but when the name, service, stylist, start time and end time are in the one place everyone checks.

The person answering a message does not confirm a time from memory. They check the diary first, then reply. It adds thirty seconds, and it saves the salon from telling a client who has already arrived that her slot belongs to someone else.

Walk-ins are treated the same way: a walk-in told "yes, we can take you now" should have her name in the diary before the cape goes on.

## One diary, one owner per shift

A shared diary only works if someone owns it. One named person per shift is responsible for it. Everyone else can look at it, but only the diary owner writes in it — or confirms new entries, if the team uses a shared booking app.

This sounds rigid, but it frees the team up. Stylists focus on the client in their chair, while the diary owner watches the diary, the messages and the phone together.

## Answer in the order the messages arrived

When several enquiries arrive at once, the order matters. First come, first served means the first message that arrived, not the first one someone happened to read.

This is where salons quietly lose bookings. A client messages at 4 pm asking for 7 pm Friday. The message sits unread while the team is busy. At 5 pm, another client asks for the same slot, gets a faster reply, and takes it.

Scan all inboxes in order at regular intervals — WhatsApp, Instagram, missed calls, then the booking page — and answer the oldest first. When a time is taken, offer the two nearest alternatives in the same message. A client offered "7 pm is taken, but I can offer 6:15 or 8 pm" will usually book. A client who gets "sorry, fully booked" will go somewhere else.

## Hold the slot while you confirm

Many double-bookings happen in a small gap: the minutes between offering a time and the client confirming it. A stylist offers 6 pm to one client on WhatsApp, and while waiting for her reply, the receptionist offers the same 6 pm to a walk-in.

The fix is a pencil hold. The moment a time is offered, it is marked in the diary as held, with the client's name. If she confirms, the hold becomes a booking. If she does not reply within fifteen minutes for a same-day slot, the hold is released and the time goes to the next person.

## Build the diary around real service times

Double-bookings are not always two clients in one chair. Sometimes one client is booked for a service that takes longer than the slot allows, quietly pushing every appointment after her late.

The diary needs honest durations. Every service should carry its real time — including processing time for colour and a few minutes to tidy the station between clients. When the diary reflects reality, the team stops choosing between rushing a client and keeping the next one waiting.

## When a double-booking still happens

Even with a good system, it will happen occasionally — a message answered on a personal phone, a new team member who did not know the rule. What matters is what happens next.

Tell the affected client early, not when she arrives. A message sent an hour before is an inconvenience; the same news at the door is an insult. Offer a real alternative in the same message: a different stylist at the same time, a slightly later slot, or a priority booking the next day. Clients forgive mistakes far more easily than they forgive being kept waiting without an explanation.

Then fix the cause. Ask one question: which step in the system failed? Was the booking confirmed before it was written down? Was there no diary owner on that shift? Every double-booking is a clue, and the salon that reads the clue stops it happening again.

## Make the system easy enough to keep

The best diary system is the one the team actually uses on a busy Saturday. One diary, one owner per shift, no confirmation before the entry exists, and pencil holds while clients decide — that is the whole system. A new team member can learn it in ten minutes.
