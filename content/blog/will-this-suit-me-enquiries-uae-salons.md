Every salon in the UAE gets this message, usually late at night, usually with a photo attached: a celebrity haircut, a glossy copper balayage, a friend's new fringe. And underneath it, five small words: "Will this suit me?"

It looks like a simple question. It is actually the most important enquiry a salon receives, because the answer decides whether the client books, whether she leaves happy, and whether she ever comes back. Say yes to everything and you book a service that disappoints. Say no too bluntly and you lose a client who was ready to spend.

## Why she is really asking

Behind "will this suit me?" there are three quieter worries. The first is money: a full colour or a big cut in Dubai is not a small spend, and nobody wants to pay it twice to fix a mistake. The second is regret, because a bad colour correction takes months to grow out. The third is trust. She is really asking whether this salon will tell her the truth, or just take the booking.

## The four-part reply that works

A reply that converts follows the same shape every time.

First, acknowledge the look. "That copper is gorgeous, and I can see why you love it" costs nothing and tells her you looked at the photo properly.

Second, ask three quick questions: hair type and texture, current colour history, and how much upkeep she is happy with. "Is your hair naturally dark or already coloured? How often do you swim? Are you happy to come back every six weeks, or would you rather have something that grows out softly?" Three questions are enough. Ten feels like a form.

Third, give an honest steer. This is the part most salons skip, and the part that builds the booking. If the look will work, say why: "With your warm skin tone and dark base, that copper will sit beautifully, and we would build it in two sessions so it does not go brassy." If it will not work as pictured, say so kindly and offer the version that will: "A full platinum blonde would need a lot of upkeep in this humidity, but a softer lived-in blonde around the face would give you the same brightness with half the maintenance."

Fourth, close with a safe next step: a fifteen-minute consultation or a strand test. "Come in on Thursday evening and we will test a strand first, so you see the real colour on your hair before we commit."

## The UAE details that change the answer

Suitability is never just about face shape. In the UAE, the climate is part of the consultation, and clients respect a salon that says so plainly.

Humidity changes how hair behaves. A sleek, straight style that looks effortless in a photo may need daily heat styling here, and a client who swims or works out most days will fight it. Hard water and pool chlorine fade colour faster than most clients expect, so a vivid copper or an ash blonde needs an honest maintenance conversation, not a surprise at the second visit. Sun matters too: colour fades faster in strong sun, and a client who spends weekends outdoors will need a gloss refresh sooner.

## When the honest answer is no

The hardest suitability enquiry is the one where the right answer is no. She wants a look her hair cannot hold, a treatment her skin cannot take yet, or a style that needs more upkeep than her life allows.

Saying no well has a simple shape: name what you love about the idea, name the one real obstacle, then offer the closest alternative in the same sentence. "I love the idea of the fringe, and with your curl pattern a soft curtain fringe would frame your face beautifully. A blunt fringe would need straightening every morning in this humidity, and I do not want you fighting it. Shall we book the curtain fringe instead?"

Never end on the no. A reply that ends with "we can't do that" sends her to the salon down the road that will say yes and do it badly. A reply that ends with a better option keeps her with you.

## The client who insists

Sometimes the client hears the honest answer and still wants the original look. Confirm what she wants, name the trade-off once, and let her choose with her eyes open. "We can absolutely do the full blonde. Just so you know, with your natural base it will need a root touch-up every four to five weeks." Write the agreement in the client notes. Clients who choose with full information rarely complain.

## Make it a team habit, not a talent

In most salons, one stylist answers these enquiries brilliantly and everyone else wings it. That is a problem, because the enquiry that arrives at 10 pm on a Friday goes to whoever is holding the phone.

Write down the salon's suitability answers for the ten looks clients ask about most: the hair or skin type each suits, the upkeep it needs in the UAE climate, and the alternative when it does not fit. Keep the list where the front desk can see it. Save the good replies too, so a new team member can give the same honest answer as the owner within her first week.
