Walk into a Dubai beauty conversation this year and it will not be long before someone mentions a drip. Not a facial, not a peel — a vitamin IV drip, taken in a comfortable chair while the bag slowly empties, sold as hydration, glow and a reset after a long week. Wellness clinics have offered them for years. Now salons are asking: should we offer them too?

The demand is real. Clients in the UAE live fast, travel constantly, and move all day between air conditioning and desert heat. Dehydration, jet lag and tired skin are everyday complaints, and a treatment that promises to help with all three in under an hour is an easy sell.

## What an IV drip actually is

An IV drip delivers fluids, vitamins and minerals directly into the bloodstream through a small cannula in the arm. The popular beauty versions are built around hydration, vitamin C, B vitamins and glutathione, sometimes with a "glow" or "energy" label on top. A session usually takes 30 to 60 minutes, and the client sits in a recliner while it runs.

That is the appeal, and also where salons need to slow down. An IV drip is a medical procedure, not a beauty treatment: a needle, a vein and a substance going into the body. That single fact changes everything about how a salon can offer it.

## The licence question comes first

In the UAE, a salon licence does not cover IV infusions. A beauty salon cannot simply add drips to its menu the way it would add a facial. IV therapy sits under health regulation, with the rules set by the health authority in each emirate — the Dubai Health Authority in Dubai, the Department of Health in Abu Dhabi, and the Ministry of Health and Prevention in the northern emirates.

That does not mean salons are locked out. It means the route is a partnership, not a purchase. The model that works is a licensed medical professional — a doctor or a registered nurse working under a licensed medical facility — delivering the treatment inside the salon, or the salon referring clients to a partner clinic. Some salons set up a separate medical licence for a treatment room. Others host a weekly clinic day, where a partner's nurse runs a half-day of appointments in a private room.

Whichever route you take, get it in writing before you take a single booking: check your trade licence activity codes and the health authority's requirements, and ask your partner to show their own licence and insurance. A client who asks "who is doing this?" should get a name, a qualification and a licence — not a shrug.

## Why clients are asking for it

The demand makes sense once you see who is asking. Frequent flyers landing in Dubai after a long-haul flight want to feel human again before a wedding or a work event. Brides in the final week before the wedding want their skin to look rested. Clients who have just finished a course of facials or laser ask what else they can do for their skin from the inside.

Winter is the strongest season. The UAE's wedding and party calendar peaks between November and March, when clients are most willing to try something new before a big occasion. A drip fits neatly into the pre-event routine: a glow facial on Monday, a drip on Wednesday, the event on Friday.

## Honest expectations are the whole service

This is where salons either earn a repeat client or lose one. An IV drip can genuinely help with hydration, and many clients report feeling more energetic for a day or two. What it cannot do is transform skin overnight, replace sleep, or undo a month of dehydration in forty-five minutes. A client who expects a miracle will be disappointed, and a disappointed client tells her friends.

The salons doing this well set expectations at the consultation, before any needle appears. They explain that the glow effect is subtle and short-lived, that results vary, and that a drip works best as part of a routine — sleep, water, skincare — not instead of one. They screen out the clients who should not have a drip at all: pregnant clients, people with kidney or heart conditions, anyone on medication that interacts with high-dose vitamins. The screening is not a formality. It is the service.

Pricing follows the same honesty. In Dubai, a single beauty drip typically sits between AED 400 and AED 900, with courses priced lower per session. Price for the medical partner, the private room and the screening — and say so.

## How it fits the diary

An IV drip is a surprisingly good diary fit, because it uses time salons already waste. A session takes 30 to 60 minutes, needs one private room and one chair, and the client is mostly sitting still. It slots into quiet mid-morning hours, the gap between colour processing and rinse, or the early evening before the rush — and it does not need a stylist.

The smartest salons start small: one room, one partner, one half-day a week — usually a Thursday or Friday, when clients prepare for the weekend. They offer it first to regulars, through a personal WhatsApp message rather than a poster, and they track one number: how many clients rebook. If rebooking is strong, they add a second half-day.

## A small test before you commit

If you are tempted, run a four-week test first. Week one, find your partner and agree the split, the room and the screening form. Week two, tell your twenty best regulars personally and take six bookings. Week three, run the sessions and watch the room. Week four, ask every client one question — would you book this again? If most of them say yes, you have a service.

Beauty trends come and go in the UAE faster than anywhere else, and not every one belongs on your menu. IV drips are different only in one way: the demand is real, the margins are good, and the clients are already yours. Whether they belong in your salon depends on one decision — whether you will offer them properly, with licensed hands and honest words, or not at all.
