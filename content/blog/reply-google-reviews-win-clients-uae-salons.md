A woman in Dubai Marina searches "salon near me" on her phone. Your salon appears, with a 4.6-star rating and forty-odd reviews. She does not just read the stars. She reads what clients wrote — and then she reads what you wrote back. That second part is the part most salon owners ignore, and it is where the booking is often won or lost.

A review is a client's opinion. Your reply is your advert. It is public, it is permanent, and it speaks to the next person deciding whether to trust you with her hair, her skin or her wedding morning. In a market as competitive as the UAE, the way you answer reviews can matter as much as the services themselves.

## Your replies are the real advert

Think about how clients choose a salon here. Many are new to Dubai and have no friend to ask, so they search, compare and read. A glowing review says one client was happy. A thoughtful reply from the owner says the salon cares, listens and stands behind its work. That is what a stranger is really looking for.

The same applies to the reply nobody wants to write. A one-star review with no response reads as a salon that does not care, or does not even notice. A calm, honest reply underneath it reads as a business run by adults. Future clients will judge you far more on the reply than on the complaint.

## The five-star reply: thank them like a person, not a template

The easiest replies to get wrong are the good ones. A copy-paste "Thank you for your kind words!" under every five-star review looks like nobody read it. Clients notice, and so do the people reading along.

Reply to what the person actually said. If she praised her balayage, name it. If she mentioned your stylist by name, thank them both. If it was her first visit, say you hope the second one is even better. Two or three sentences is enough — specific, warm, human.

This is also a gentle marketing moment. A reply that mentions the service she loved — "so glad the keratin treatment held up in this humidity" — tells the next reader exactly what you are good at, in the client's own words.

## The one-star reply: your most valuable marketing moment

A bad review feels personal, especially when you know the full story and the client left half of it out. The instinct is to defend yourself, or to say nothing at all. Both are mistakes.

Your reply is not written for the angry client. She has already decided how she feels. It is written for the hundreds of people who will read it afterwards. They are asking one question: if something goes wrong at this salon, how will I be treated?

So keep it short, calm and specific. Thank them for the feedback. Say plainly what happened, without blaming them. Say what you have done about it. Then invite them back — not with a freebie, but with a real conversation: "Please message us on WhatsApp so we can make this right."

Never argue in public, never reveal private details about the client, and never blame a staff member by name. If the review is fake, flag it to Google — but reply anyway, briefly and politely, because the report can take weeks and the review is visible today.

Here is the surprising part: a salon with a few honest one-star reviews and graceful replies often converts better than a salon with a perfect score and silence. A perfect score looks bought. A handled complaint looks real.

## The three-star middle: where honest fixes win trust

Three-star reviews are the most useful ones you will ever get, because they usually contain a real, fixable point wrapped in a fair verdict — a lovely cut, but a forty-minute wait. These are gifts: a client telling you exactly what to improve, for free.

Reply by naming the point, not dodging it. "You're right, the wait on Saturday evening was too long — we've added buffer time between evening appointments so it doesn't happen again." That sentence does two jobs at once: it shows the reviewer you listened, and it tells every future reader that the problem is already fixed.

## The reply routine: fifteen minutes, twice a week

Replying to reviews does not need to become a daily chore. It needs a routine that survives a busy week. Set aside fifteen minutes, twice a week — say, Monday morning before the first client and Thursday evening after the last one. Open your Google Business Profile, answer everything new, and move on.

Assign one person to it. In most UAE salons that is the owner or the front-desk lead, because the replies need to sound like the salon, not like a different person every time. Give them three or four example replies to learn the tone from, and a simple rule: anything angry or complicated gets passed to you before it is posted.

Never reply when you are angry; a reply written at midnight after a long day will read exactly like one. Sleep on the difficult ones, and answer them fresh in the morning. And never copy another salon's replies — clients in the UAE read reviews across many salons before choosing one, and identical wording stands out immediately.

## From review reply to booking

The final step is the one most salons skip: closing the loop. A good reply ends with a next step — for a happy client, "we've saved your usual slot for next month, just message us on WhatsApp to confirm." Every reply is a small invitation, and invitations are what turn readers into bookings.

This matters most after hours. Many clients read reviews late at night, when your salon is closed and your phone is on silent. They will message you on WhatsApp at 11 pm, and if nobody answers until the next afternoon, they will have booked somewhere else by then.
