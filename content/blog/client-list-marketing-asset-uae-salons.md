Every salon owner in the UAE knows the feeling: you spend money on Instagram ads and boosted stories, and the chairs still sit empty on a Tuesday afternoon. Meanwhile, sitting quietly in your phone or booking book, there is a list of hundreds of people who already trust you with their hair, skin and nails. They have paid you before. They just need a reason to come back this week.

That list is your best marketing asset. Not because it is big, but because it is warm. A stranger who sees your ad needs convincing from zero. A past client needs a nudge. A well-timed message to your own clients will almost always beat a cold advert on cost per booking, and it costs you almost nothing to send.

## Start with three simple groups

Sort your client list by one thing: when each person last visited. Most salons find the same three groups.

The first group is your regulars, the people who came in the last six weeks. They are your backbone. They do not need persuading; they need convenience — a reminder before their usual gap runs out, or a quiet weekday slot held in their name.

The second group is the quiet ones, clients who came two or three months ago and then stopped. Nothing went wrong — life got in the way, or another salon caught their eye for one visit. These are the most valuable people on your list, because winning one of them back costs far less than finding a brand-new client, and they already know your standard.

The third group is the one-timers, people who came once, months ago, and never returned. Do not write them off. Some moved, some were tourists, and some simply never got a reason to come back. A single well-chosen message can bring a surprising number of them through the door, especially if their first visit went well.

## What to send each group

The mistake most salons make is sending the same message to everyone. A blanket discount trains your best clients to wait for a sale. Targeted messages work because they feel personal, and personal messages get answered.

For your regulars, keep it simple and useful. "Hi Sara, it's been five weeks since your last balayage — I have your usual chair free on Thursday at 6pm if you'd like it." That is service, not marketing — and it is the kind of message clients in Dubai reply to, because it saves them messaging you first.

For the quiet ones, lead with memory, not money. Mention what they had last time and offer one specific thing. "Hi Mariam, you had the keratin treatment with us in August — how has it been holding up in the humidity? We have a quiet slot on Wednesday morning if you'd like a refresh." If you add a small gesture, make it a service upgrade, not a price cut.

For the one-timers, give them a reason that is about them, not about you. "Hi Anna, you visited us for a cut in September — we have just added a 30-minute express facial that fits into a lunch break, and I thought of you." One message, one idea, one easy reply. If they do not answer, leave it.

## Timing that respects the UAE week

When you send matters as much as what you send. The working week runs Monday to Friday for many clients, with the weekend on Saturday and Sunday, and evenings are when salons are busiest. Your messages should land when clients are actually planning their week.

Sunday evening is one of the best times to reach people — they are looking at the week ahead, and a message about a Tuesday slot can be booked on the spot. Mid-morning on a weekday works well too. Avoid anything after 9pm, and Friday mornings, when many clients are with family or at prayer.

The other timing rule is about your own diary: message people about quiet hours, not peak ones. If your Thursday evening is already full, point regulars toward the Tuesday afternoon gap instead. Every chair you fill at 11am is revenue that would otherwise have been lost.

## Small offers that protect your prices

Targeted marketing does not mean discounting. The salons that do this best rarely cut their prices at all. They use value instead: a small extra, a convenient time, a service the client has not tried yet. A ten-minute head massage with a colour service, or priority booking for regulars during the busy December party season. None of these touch your headline prices, and all of them give the client a reason to say yes now rather than later.

## Keep it welcome, and keep it legal

One caution matters more than all the tactics. Only message people who have given you their number in the course of doing business with you, and always make it easy to stop — a simple "Reply STOP if you'd rather not hear from us" costs you nothing and protects your reputation. Never add numbers from a bought list, and never message someone who has asked you not to. In a market as connected as the UAE, where clients talk and reviews travel fast, one pushy campaign can undo years of good service. And keep it to once a month for quiet clients, once a week at most for regulars, and only when you have something genuinely useful to say.

## A simple weekly routine

Here is the whole system. On Sunday evening, find the three emptiest hours in next week's diary. Pick the clients who would fit those hours — regulars due for their usual service, quiet clients whose last visit matches. Send each of them one short, personal message naming the service and the time. On Monday morning, reply to everyone who answers within the hour. By Tuesday, those empty hours should be filling up.

It works because it is built on something no advert can buy: a real relationship. Your competitors can copy your prices and your Instagram feed. They cannot copy the fact that you remember Sara likes her balayage every five weeks.
