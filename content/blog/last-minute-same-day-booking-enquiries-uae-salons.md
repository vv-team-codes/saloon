It usually arrives at the worst possible moment. The salon is mid-rush, and a WhatsApp message lights up the phone: "Hi, can I come today? I need a haircut before tonight."

Last-minute enquiries are a daily reality for UAE salons. Plans change quickly, and an event or a sudden trip can make a salon visit urgent. Handled well, these messages are pure opportunity: a client who wants to come today is already halfway to booking. Handled badly, they become double-bookings and a diary that falls apart by evening.

The difference is a small, clear routine for same-day requests, so whoever picks up the phone gives the same calm, honest answer every time.

## Why last-minute enquiries are different

A normal booking enquiry has slack in it. If Thursday is full, you offer Friday, and nobody is disappointed. A same-day enquiry has no slack. The answer is either yes, a near alternative, or a polite no.

That urgency changes what the client values. They do not need a long menu of services in the first reply. They need three things: whether there is a slot, when exactly, and with whom. It also changes the cost of a slow reply: a client asking about today will message another salon within fifteen minutes if no one replies.

## Speed matters more than perfection

The first reply to a same-day enquiry should go out within minutes, even during busy hours. It does not have to be a full answer. A short holding message works well: "Thanks for messaging! Let me check today's diary and I will confirm within ten minutes." The client feels heard, and the enquiry does not drift to a competitor.

The mistake is leaving the message unread while the team finishes the current rush, then replying an hour later with an offer nobody needs anymore. Assign this job clearly: one named person per shift watches the inbox. A same-day message is never left for "later".

## Check the diary before you promise

The most damaging same-day mistake is promising a slot that does not exist. It happens easily: someone glances at the diary, sees a gap, and confirms, only to discover that the gap was a lunch break or a colour processing window.

Before confirming anything, check three things: the stylist's real availability, the true length of the service, and the buffer around it. A forty-five-minute haircut cannot be squeezed into a thirty-minute gap, and a colour service needs its processing time counted, not just the chair time.

This is why the diary must be the single source of truth. If bookings live partly in a notebook, partly in WhatsApp and partly in someone's head, same-day promises will go wrong. One diary, updated the moment a booking is made, makes fast replies safe.

## Offer the closest honest alternative

Very often, the exact slot the client asked for is gone. That is not the end of the enquiry. Most same-day clients are flexible about the details, even if they are not flexible about the day. Offer the closest honest alternative: a different stylist, a slightly different time, or a shorter version of the service that fits the gap.

The key word is honest. "We have 4:30 with Sara, or 6:00 with Layla" is useful. "Come anytime, we will fit you in" is not, because it sets up a long wait and a frustrated client watching booked clients go ahead of her.

If the service itself cannot be done today, say so plainly and offer the real alternative. A client who wanted full highlights today might accept a root touch-up today and a full appointment later in the week.

## Hold the slot, briefly

A same-day offer has a short shelf life. If you tell a client "we have 4:30 free" and she replies an hour later, that slot may be gone, and now you have disappointed her twice. Give every offer a short, clear expiry: "I can hold 4:30 for you until 2:30, just let me know."

Write the hold in the diary, not just in the chat, with the client's name and expiry time. If the client does not confirm by then, release it. An untracked hold becomes a phantom booking that blocks a real one.

## When today is genuinely full

Some days, the answer really is no. The diary is full, and squeezing one more client in would hurt the clients who already booked. Saying no well is a skill, because a same-day client who is treated kindly today often becomes a planned booking tomorrow.

Thank them, be specific, and offer the next real option: "Today is fully booked, but I have tomorrow at 11:00 with Sara, or Thursday at 6:30. Would either work?" Add them to the waitlist for today in case of a cancellation. A client who hears "we are full, but here is exactly what we can do" feels looked after.

Never overbook to avoid saying no. One overbooked afternoon creates late-running appointments and unhappy regulars, all to win a single visit from someone who may never return.

## Turn a one-off into a next booking

Last-minute clients are often new clients, and new clients who have a good urgent experience tend to come back. Before they leave, make the next booking easy. If they came for an event, note it. If they liked a particular stylist, record that preference. Offer a specific next appointment rather than a vague "see you soon".

## A simple same-day reply routine

Put it together and the routine is short enough to teach in ten minutes. Reply within minutes, even if only to say you are checking. Check the real diary before promising anything. Offer the closest honest alternative when the exact request is gone. Hold the slot briefly and mark it in the diary. When today is full, say so kindly and offer the next real option plus the waitlist. Record the client's details so a one-off visit becomes a relationship.

Salons that run this routine stop dreading the "can I come today?" message. The diary stays calm, and clients learn that this is a salon that answers fast and tells the truth. That reputation travels fast.
