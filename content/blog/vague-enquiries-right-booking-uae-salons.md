Every UAE salon owner knows this message. It arrives on WhatsApp late in the evening, sometimes with no greeting and no detail at all: "Hi, I want to do something with my hair." Or: "I need a facial, which one is good?" Or simply: "What do you recommend?"

There is no service named, no price range, no date, and often no photo. The client knows she wants to feel better when she walks out, but she cannot say what that means yet. These vague enquiries are some of the warmest leads a salon ever receives — the client has already chosen your salon, she just needs help choosing the service — and they are also the easiest to lose. A reply that dumps a full price list on her, or asks "which service would you like?" back, usually ends the conversation. A reply that guides her, calmly and in a few steps, usually ends in a booking.

## The four questions that narrow it down

A good reply welcomes the client, asks one easy question, and offers a clear next step. You only need four questions, asked one or two at a time, and in this order.

First, ask about the occasion or the goal: "Is it for a special occasion, or just a refresh for yourself?" This one question splits the world in two. An occasion means a deadline, and deadlines change everything about what you recommend. A refresh means the client is open to suggestion and the diary matters less.

Second, ask about the last time: "When did you last have this done, and how did it feel?" A client who coloured her hair three weeks ago needs something different from a client who has never coloured it.

Third, ask about what she wants to avoid: "Is there anything you definitely don't want — for example, a big change, or anything with a lot of upkeep?" Vague clients are often much clearer about what they do not want than what they do. A woman who says "nothing too short, nothing high maintenance" has just given you a usable brief.

Fourth, ask about timing only when you are close to recommending: "Would you prefer morning or evening, and is there a day that suits you best?" Asking about timing early feels pushy. Asking it after you have understood her needs feels like service.

## Turn the answers into one clear recommendation

Once you have two or three answers, stop asking and start recommending. Name one service, not three. Say what it does, how long it takes, and what it costs — all in the same message, in plain language. "Based on what you've told me, I'd suggest our gloss and blow-dry. It adds shine and softness without changing your colour, takes about 45 minutes, and it's AED 220. We have Thursday at 6 pm or Saturday at 11 am — would either suit you?"

Notice the structure: a reason, a time, a price, and two concrete slots. The client does not need to ask a follow-up question to book. She can simply reply "Thursday 6 pm" and the conversation is done. This is the moment vague enquiries turn into confirmed appointments, and it works because the client feels understood, not sold to.

If you genuinely need to see something before recommending — the current state of a client's hair, for example — ask for one photo, and say why: "Could you send a quick photo in daylight? It helps me check whether the colour you want will work with your current shade." Clients send photos gladly when they understand the reason.

## Keep a note, and the second visit gets easier

Every vague enquiry that becomes a booking should leave a trace. A line in the client notes — "likes low-maintenance colour, prefers evenings" — turns a stranger into a known client. Next time she messages, your team can greet her with something specific instead of starting from zero. That is how salons win the whole beauty routine of a client who first arrived not knowing what she wanted.

It also helps to keep a short list of the questions that worked. Over a month, patterns appear: the clients who ask "what do you recommend?" in Dubai tend to cluster around a few real needs — a refresh before a holiday, a fix for something another salon did, a first visit after moving to the UAE. When your team recognises the pattern, the first reply gets faster and better, and fewer of these warm enquiries drift away.

## The reply speed that matters most

Vague enquiries are especially sensitive to speed. A client who knows exactly what she wants will wait for a reply, because she has already decided. A client who does not know what she wants is still deciding — and if your reply takes four hours, she may have asked another salon in the meantime. During opening hours, aim to answer within minutes, even if the answer is only a warm acknowledgement and one easy question: "Of course, happy to help. Is it for a special occasion or just a treat for yourself?" That first reply holds the conversation open until your team can give her the full recommendation.
