Every UAE salon owner knows the moment. The timings change — Ramadan hours, an Eid closure, a National Day holiday, a refit week, a mall shifting its opening times — and within a day, the messages start arriving. "Are you open today?" "What time do you close?" "I came at 7 and you were shut." A client who drove to your salon and found a dark shopfront rarely books again that week.

Changed hours are not a small admin task. In a WhatsApp-first market like the UAE, they are a client-experience moment, and most salons handle them badly: a single Instagram story, a note taped to the door, a message sent the night before. The fix is simple: announce every hours change on WhatsApp, in the right order, at the right time, with a booking attached.

## Start with the clients who already have bookings

When your hours change, the first people who need to know are not your followers. They are the clients with appointments inside the affected window. A woman booked for a 9pm colour on the first night of Ramadan timings, or a bride whose trial falls on your Eid closure day, needs a personal message, not a broadcast.

Go through the diary first and list every affected booking. Then message each client individually, before any public announcement. The message has four parts: the change, the reason in one line, the new time you are offering, and a single tap to confirm. "Hi Sara, just a quick note — during Ramadan we close at 5pm, so your 7pm colour on Thursday moves to 4pm with the same stylist. Does 4pm work for you? Reply yes and it's done."

That order matters. Clients forgive a changed time far more easily than arriving to find the salon closed. Handle the diary first, and the rest of the announcement becomes easy, because you already know which slots are genuinely free.

## Announce early, in one message, to everyone else

Once the booked clients are sorted, tell the rest of your client list. Two weeks ahead is the sweet spot for a planned change like Ramadan hours or a holiday closure. For a refit or an unplanned closure, tell them as soon as you know — even if the details are still rough, a message that says "we're closed this Sunday, back Monday at 10am" beats silence.

Keep the announcement short and complete. Clients need five things: the dates affected, the new hours, when normal hours resume, whether bookings are still open for the days around the change, and how to book. "We're open 10am to 5pm during Ramadan, closed on Eid day, and back to normal hours on the second day of Eid. Evening slots before Ramadan fill fast — reply here to book."

Send it on WhatsApp, where your clients actually read messages, and pin it in your WhatsApp Business profile and as a Status update so latecomers find it without messaging you. A pinned post on Instagram helps, but it should never be the only place the news lives, because your most valuable clients — the regulars who book by WhatsApp — may never see it.

## Turn the announcement into bookings

A hours-change message is also a booking opportunity, and smart salons use it as one. The days just before a closure fill fast, because clients rush to get in before the break. Say so plainly. "We close for Eid on Friday. Our last slots on Thursday are 10am, 1pm and 4pm — reply with a time and we'll hold it for you." Scarcity works best when it is honest and specific, and a closure is the most honest scarcity a salon ever has.

The same applies the other way round. When you reopen after a break, or extend hours for a busy season like the Dubai Shopping Festival or the winter wedding rush, announce it with a booking link and a clear first day. "We're back Monday at 10am, and the diary is open now."

One caution: do not send the announcement to people who never asked to hear from you. Use your regular client list, the people who have booked or messaged before. A hours-change message sent to a cold list reads as spam. A hours-change message sent to your own clients reads as good service.

## What to do when someone still turns up at the wrong time

No announcement reaches everyone. Someone will arrive at 6pm on a day you closed at 5pm, or on the Eid day itself. How your team handles that moment decides whether the client comes back.

First, never make the client feel foolish for not knowing. They missed the message; that is not their fault. Second, give them something concrete before they leave: a slot the next day, a priority booking for the morning you reopen, or a small gesture like a complimentary blow-dry with their rescheduled service. Third, check their number is on your WhatsApp list before they go, so the next announcement reaches them.

Keep a short script at the front desk for exactly this situation, so whoever is on duty gives the same warm, useful answer. The script needs only three lines: an apology, the new hours, and the offer. Anything longer sounds defensive.

## Make every future change a five-minute job

The salons that handle hours changes well do not improvise each time. They keep a simple system. One person owns the announcement. There is a saved template for a hours-change message, with the dates and times as blanks to fill in. The client list is segmented — regulars, clients with bookings in the affected window, and everyone else — so each group gets the right version of the message. And the diary is checked before the announcement goes out, so no message ever promises a slot that does not exist.

That system takes an hour to set up once and saves a frantic evening every time your hours change. Clients remember how a salon treats them when something changes. A clear message, sent early, with a booking attached, tells them the salon is organised and respects their time. A missed message tells them the opposite.
