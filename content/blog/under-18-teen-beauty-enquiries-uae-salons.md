It usually arrives on WhatsApp late in the evening: "Hi, my daughter is 15 and wants lash extensions for her birthday. Can you do it?" Or a teenager messages directly: "Do you do acrylics for 16-year-olds?" These enquiries are now a regular part of the week for salons across Dubai and the UAE. The person asking is often not the person who will sit in the chair, the person paying is a parent who may never visit, and the answer touches on safety, consent and trust in a way a price question never does.


## Why teen enquiries are different

Most enquiries follow a simple pattern: the client wants a service, you quote a price and a time, she books. A teen enquiry has a third person in the room — the parent — and a real question underneath the request: is this safe for my child, and is your salon the right place for it?

Teenagers in the UAE see the same beauty trends as adults and want the same looks. What they lack is the experience to judge what is safe for younger skin and nails. That is where a salon earns its reputation — by being the adult in the conversation, not just the seller.

## Know your line before the message arrives

The worst time to decide your age policy is while you are typing a reply. Sit down with your team once and agree, in writing, which services have an age limit and what it is. Cover the services teenagers ask for most: lash extensions, acrylic and gel nails, hair colour and lightening, spray tans, waxing, and semi-permanent treatments.

Simple services such as a manicure, a haircut or a blow-dry are fine at any age with a parent's knowledge. Chemical and semi-permanent services — colour, keratin, lash extensions, acrylics, brow lamination — need a parent's consent and, for younger teens, a parent present in the salon. Write it down so every stylist gives the same answer.

## The four-part reply that keeps the booking

Answer in four parts: welcome the question warmly, give a clear answer on the service, explain the condition simply — parental consent, a parent present, or a consultation first — and close with a concrete next step: a time, a price range and the stylist's name.

Here is how that sounds: "Thank you for asking — we'd love to look after her for her birthday. Lash extensions are fine from sixteen with a parent's written consent, and we always do a short consultation first. We have Saturday at 4 pm with Aisha, and a classic set is AED 180 to AED 220. Shall I hold the slot?"

## When the answer is no — and how to say it

Sometimes the honest answer is no — a thirteen-year-old asking for acrylics, or a fourteen-year-old wanting full bleaching — and a refusal protects both the teenager and your salon. Say no the way you would want someone to say it to your own child: kindly, briefly and with an alternative. "We don't do acrylics under sixteen because young nails are still developing and the removal can damage them. What we can do is a beautiful gel manicure with a short, natural shape — it looks just as polished and it keeps her nails healthy. Shall I book that for Saturday instead?"

Never make the teenager feel embarrassed for asking. And never say yes to something you know is wrong for a young client simply because the parent is pushing. A damaged nail bed or a bad reaction is not worth one booking.

## When the answer is yes — keep the parent in the loop

When the service is suitable and the parent agrees, keep the parent in the loop at every step. Send the confirmation to the parent's number, not only the teenager's. State the price, the duration and any aftercare clearly, so there are no surprises at the till.

Take patch tests seriously too: teenage skin can be more reactive, and a first-timer should be patch-tested even when she is in a hurry for a party on Saturday. Explain it as care, not bureaucracy: "We do a quick patch test for every first-timer — it takes a minute and it means the big day goes exactly as planned."

## The services teens ask for most

Manicures and pedicures are the most common first request, often for a birthday or a school event, and they are the easiest yes. Hair colour is the trickiest — teenagers ask for balayage and highlights they have seen online, and the honest answer often involves a consultation, a longer appointment and a price that surprises the parent. Lash extensions are popular but need the most careful consent conversation, because they require maintenance and gentle aftercare a younger client may not expect.

For each of these, recommend the gentlest version that delivers the look: a soft gloss instead of full bleach, a classic lash set instead of volume, a gel manicure instead of acrylics. The teenager gets the result she wanted and the parent sees a salon that put her daughter first.

## Make your age policy visible before they ask

Most teen enquiries could be answered before they are even sent. Add one short line to your WhatsApp greeting message, your Instagram highlights and your booking page: which services welcome younger clients, and that a parent's consent is needed for chemical services.

If you use a booking system or an AI receptionist for after-hours messages, make sure it knows the policy too — a 10 pm enquiry from a teenager deserves the same careful answer as a 10 am enquiry from her mother. ## Turn one teen visit into a family of clients

The real prize in a teen enquiry is not one appointment — it is the household. A teenager who has a good first salon experience brings her mother next time, then her sister, then her friends. Note the family connection in your client records and follow up like any new regular: a short message the next day, and a reminder when the lashes or colour need attention. Answer with warmth, hold your line with kindness, and keep the parent close — and the fifteen-year-old asking about lashes today becomes the client who books with you for the next ten years.
