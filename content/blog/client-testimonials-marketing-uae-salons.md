A woman in Dubai is choosing between three salons. All three have good photos. All three list similar prices. What tips her toward one of them is rarely another glossy picture of a blow-dry. It is a sentence from a client who sounds like her: "I have thick, frizzy hair and every salon in Dubai made it worse — until Sara here." That one line does more work than a month of polished posts, because it answers the question every new client is really asking: will this salon understand someone like me?

Testimonials are the most underused marketing asset in UAE salons. Owners spend real money on ads, influencers and photo shoots, then let the most persuasive words in the building walk out the door unrecorded. A happy client says something wonderful at the mirror, the stylist smiles, and nobody writes it down. The fix is a small habit, repeated every week, that turns those moments into bookings.

## Ask at the moment of delight, not a week later

The best testimonial is collected in the seconds after the mirror reveal, when the client is still looking at the result and feeling good about it. A follow-up message sent three days later gets a polite "thanks, it was lovely" — if it gets anything at all. The emotion has cooled and the detail has gone.

Train the team to listen for the moment. When a client says "I love it" or "this is the best my hair has looked in years," that is the cue. The stylist can simply say, "That means so much — would you mind if I wrote that down, or would you say it on camera for thirty seconds?" Most clients say yes, because being asked feels like a compliment. Asking warmly, in the moment, is the whole system — everything else is just organising what you collect.

## Make it easy, specific and honest

A testimonial works when it is specific. "Great salon, highly recommended" persuades nobody, because it could describe any business in the city. "I came in with box-dyed hair and they fixed the colour in one visit without damaging it" persuades the exact client who is searching for colour correction right now.

Help clients be specific without putting words in their mouth. Two gentle prompts are enough: what were you worried about before you came in, and what changed? If a client is shy on camera, a typed message on WhatsApp is just as good — screenshot it with her name and first name only, or anonymised if she prefers. If she is happy on camera, thirty seconds on a phone is plenty: her face, her hair, her own words. The roughness is the proof. A testimonial that looks like an advert reads like an advert, and clients in the UAE have seen enough of those.

## Always get permission, and write it down

Consent is not a formality here; it is the whole foundation. Never post a client's photo, video or words without her clear agreement, and never assume that because she was happy in the chair she wants her face on your Instagram. Ask plainly, record the yes in your client notes, and tell her exactly where it will appear — your Google profile, your WhatsApp status, your website. If she says yes to WhatsApp but no to Instagram, respect the boundary exactly. One careless post can undo years of trust, and in a city where salons live on reputation, trust is the asset you are actually marketing.

## Put each testimonial where it can actually book

Collecting testimonials is only half the job. A folder of kind words on your phone books nobody. Each one needs a place where a deciding client will see it.

Start with your Google Business Profile. A new client searching "salon near me" in JLT or Deira sees your star rating first and your reviews second — and a review that mentions a named service, a named stylist and a real outcome reads completely differently from a generic five stars. Reply to every review personally, and quote the best ones on your WhatsApp status and your website's service pages. A testimonial about a bridal trial belongs on the bridal page, not buried in a feed.

Social media is the second home. One testimonial a week, posted as a simple Reel or a story with the client's own words over a real photo of the result, will outperform most of your polished content — because it is proof, not a promise. And your front desk should have them too: when a caller on WhatsApp asks "are you good with curly hair?" the fastest answer is not a paragraph about your training, it is a thirty-second clip of a curly-haired client saying she finally found her salon.

## One a week is enough — if you never stop

The mistake is treating testimonials as a project: a big push for a fortnight, then silence for six months. A salon that collects one genuine testimonial a week has more than fifty by the end of the year, covering every service and every worry a new client might have. That library becomes the answer to almost every enquiry: the price question, the trust question, the "is this salon for someone like me?" question.

Put one person in charge of it, even in a small salon. The owner's job is to ask at the mirror. One team member's job is to save the words, get the permission noted, and post one a week. Ten minutes a week, every week, beats any single campaign — and it costs nothing except the habit.

## The clients you already have are your best marketing

Every salon in the UAE has clients who already love it. The salons that grow fastest are not always the ones with the biggest ad budget. They are the ones that noticed what their happy clients were already saying, wrote it down, asked permission, and let those words do the selling. Your next ten clients are far more likely to believe a woman who sat in your chair than anything you say about yourself.

Start this week. At the next mirror reveal, when a client lights up, ask her if you can share her words. One yes is the beginning of a library — and a library of real voices is the most honest marketing a salon can own.
