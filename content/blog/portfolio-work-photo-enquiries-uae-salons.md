Every salon owner in the UAE knows this message. It arrives on WhatsApp late at night, often after a client has checked your Instagram and Google profile. "Can I see some of your work?" It looks like a casual question. In reality it is one of the warmest enquiries a salon ever receives — the person asking has almost decided. They are asking whether they can trust you with their hair, skin, and face on a wedding day.

That is why the reply matters. A slow answer, a random photo or a link to an unrelated feed can quietly end a nearly-made booking. A fast, honest, well-matched reply turns a browser into a booked chair.

## What the client is really asking

Behind the question there is usually one of three worries: quality — will the result look like the picture in my head? Fit — has this salon done this on someone like me? Safety — if it goes wrong, am I in good hands?

Your reply needs to answer all three. Sending your best blonde balayage photo to a client with dark, thick hair answers none of them — she will think "that is not me" and move on. The photo has to feel like her.

These enquiries cluster around the priciest, riskiest services: colour corrections, extensions, bridal work, microblading and keratin. A client will happily book a blow-dry without seeing a photo. She will rarely book a full head of colour without one.

## Build a small portfolio that answers fast

The salons that handle this well never send clients to scroll through a feed. They keep a small set of photos ready — usually no more than twenty — sorted by service: colour, bridal, nails, facials, men's cuts, extensions. Within each set, cover a range of hair lengths, skin tones and ages — in Dubai and Abu Dhabi, one afternoon can include a dozen nationalities.

Keep the set on the salon phone, not only on Instagram, so the person replying at 10 PM can send two or three photos in under a minute. Speed is part of the answer — a reply that arrives the next morning has already lost to the salon that replied in five.

Use real photos of real clients, taken in your salon in honest light. Filtered or over-edited images work against you — what clients trust is a photo taken in a real chair, by a real stylist, on a real person. One clear before-and-after pair beats ten glamour shots.

## Match the photo to the person asking

Before sending anything, ask one question: "What are you hoping for?" If she wants a copper balayage, send copper balayage on hair like hers. If she wants a bridal trial, send a bride, not a party look. Two well-matched photos beat ten random ones every time.

A short line of honesty earns more trust than any photo. If the result needs two sessions, say so. If her dark hair means the blonde she likes will take time, say so. Clients do not walk away from honesty, only from promises that sound too easy.

Add practical notes: how long it took, the price range, and whether a patch test or consultation is needed first. With those details, the client can decide without another round of messages — and her next message is usually "when can I come in?"

## What never to send

A few mistakes quietly damage a salon's reputation. The first is passing off another salon's work, or a photo from the internet, as your own — clients notice, and one discovery ends the trust completely. The second is showing a photo of a client who never agreed. Consent is why regulars keep letting you photograph their results: ask at the chair, note the answer, respect a no. The third is a photo that misleads — a filter that changes the colour, or a result from years ago by a stylist who has left. If the work is not yours, not recent or not honest, leave it out.

## The reply that books

A good portfolio reply has four parts, in one WhatsApp message. First, the photos — two or three, matched to her request. Second, one honest line: time, price range, any test needed. Third, a name: the stylist who did that work. Fourth, two real slots: "Sara has Thursday at 5 PM or Saturday at 11 AM — shall I hold one for you?"

That works because it removes every reason to delay: she can see the work, she knows the cost and time, she knows who will do it, and she has a concrete choice.

For high-value services, offer the consultation in the same message: "This colour starts with a free 15-minute consultation — I can book it with the appointment so you only come in once." One visit instead of two is reason enough to book now.

## When the work she wants is not in your book

Sooner or later someone will ask for a service you have never photographed or never offered. That is not a lost booking. Say plainly that you do not have a photo of that exact work, name the closest thing you have done, and offer a consultation where the stylist looks at her hair or skin and tells her honestly whether the result is achievable. Clients respect that far more than a salon that fakes it. And every one of those conversations is market research — if five people in a month ask for a service you do not offer, the demand is telling you something about your menu.

## Keep the portfolio fresh, and answer after hours

A portfolio goes stale fast. Photos from two years ago, by stylists who have moved on, misrepresent the salon today. Once a month, one person should spend twenty minutes reviewing the set: remove anything outdated, add the best recent work, and check every photo still has the client's permission on file.
