For years, the wedding beauty business in the UAE belonged almost entirely to the bride. Salons built bridal timelines and blocked out whole mornings for the wedding party — while the groom booked a haircut the day before and hoped for the best. That is changing fast. Across Dubai, Abu Dhabi and Sharjah, salons report a steady rise in grooms who want to look their best on the day and will pay for a proper plan rather than a last-minute trim.

The shift is not hard to explain. Weddings here are photographed and filmed from every angle, and the images live on Instagram and family group chats for years. Add a desert climate that punishes skin and hair, and walking into the biggest day of your life with a rushed cut and tired skin starts to feel like a gamble. Salons that noticed this early now sell groom prep as a named service, and it is becoming a reliable wedding-season earner.

## What a groom prep package actually includes

The best groom packages are not a bride's package with the word bride crossed out. Men book differently, and the services that sell reflect that. A typical package runs over three to four weeks: a skin consultation with two or three facials, a haircut and colour plan, beard shaping or a clean-shave routine, a brow tidy, a manicure and pedicure, and sometimes a back or shoulder treatment.

The first visit is a consultation, not a treatment. It asks about the wedding date, the venue, whether there will be outdoor photos, what the groom's skin is like in the UAE heat, and whether he has ever had a facial before. Many grooms have not. That first conversation sets the tone — it tells the groom what will happen, when, and what to expect, which matters more to a nervous first-timer than any discount.

## Timing is the whole product

Grooms who book early are easy to serve. Grooms who arrive four days before the wedding with a list of everything they want fixed are not, and the honest salon says so. The most successful packages are built around a simple timeline: six weeks out for the consultation and first facial, three weeks out for the haircut trial and beard plan, one week out for the final facial and grooming, and the day before for the final cut and clean-up.

Selling the timeline is part of the service. When a groom asks for a facial the day before his wedding, the answer is not a flat no — it is an explanation. A facial done too late can leave redness or a breakout that no photographer can fully hide. Offer a gentle hydrating treatment instead, and move the deeper work to the week before. Grooms respect honesty here: they are buying the confidence that they will look right in the photos.

## Pricing it without apology

Groom prep packages in UAE salons typically sit between AED 800 and AED 2,500, depending on the number of sessions and whether the package adds the best man or the groom's father. The mistake is to price it like a single service. A package works because it is sold as a plan: a set number of visits, a named stylist, a schedule and a clear finish line.

Two pricing habits work well. The first is a simple two-tier offer: a standard package and a premium one with an extra facial and a home-care kit. The second is to let the groom add a father or best man at a reduced rate, which turns one booking into two or three and fills quiet weekday slots. What does not work is discounting the package to close the sale — a salon that cuts its price to win a groom has taught him that the price was never real.

## Where groom prep fits in the diary

Groom prep is a diary-friendly service, and that is a big part of its appeal. Most groom visits happen on weekday evenings and Saturday mornings — times when salons are either busy with regulars or quiet enough to welcome extra work. A three-visit package spread over a month is far easier to schedule than a single long bridal block, and it brings a client into the salon three times instead of once.

The smart move is to assign one stylist to the whole package. Grooms, like brides, want a familiar face by the final visit. A groom who has seen the same stylist three times trusts the final cut, and that trust is what turns a one-off wedding client into a regular. Many salons find that grooms who start with a wedding package stay on for a monthly haircut and a quarterly facial.

## Marketing it without embarrassing anyone

Groom prep sells best when it is shown, not shouted about. The salons winning this work post a before-and-after reel of a groom's skin over three weeks, or a photo of a father and son getting ready together — a simple message that says "grooms get ready too." The tone matters: grooms in the UAE come from many cultures and comfort levels, and the message that works is practical, not flashy.

The other quiet channel is the bride. Bridal clients already trust their salon, and many are delighted to book their fiancé in. A salon that offers a groom add-on at the bridal trial — "shall we book him in for the same week?" — often fills the groom's diary without spending a dirham on advertising. The same applies to wedding planners and photographers, who meet grooms at exactly the moment they start caring about how they will look.

## Why this trend has staying power

Groom prep is not a gimmick. It sits at the intersection of three things that are not going away: weddings in the UAE are big, photographed occasions; men are spending more on grooming than ever before; and salons are looking for services that bring a client back more than once. A groom package delivers all three, using services most salons already offer. No new equipment, no new licence, no new hire — just a named package, a timeline and a team that knows how to talk a first-timer through it.

For a salon owner, the question is not whether grooms will keep booking. It is whether your salon is the one they book with.
