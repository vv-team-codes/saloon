Every UAE salon owner knows the pattern by heart. Tuesday at 11 in the morning, three chairs sit empty. Thursday at 7 in the evening, the phone is ringing, WhatsApp is buzzing, and you are turning away clients you would love to take. The busiest hours of your week are also the hours when you earn the least per chair, because every slot costs the same no matter when it happens.

Peak-hour pricing fixes that mismatch. It means charging a little more for the slots everyone wants — weekday evenings, the Thursday and Friday rush, the hours before a big event — while keeping quieter hours at your normal price. Done well, it earns you more from the same chairs and spreads demand into hours that currently sit empty.

## Why peak hours are worth more

Think about what a 7 PM slot really is. For a client, it is the only hour that fits around a job, school runs and traffic. For you, it is the scarcest thing you sell: there are only so many Thursday evenings in a month, and once they are gone, no amount of marketing brings them back. A 10 AM slot on a Tuesday is plentiful.

The maths is modest, which is exactly why it works. If your average bill is AED 180 and you add a 10 percent peak premium to roughly a third of your bookings, your monthly revenue rises by a few percent without a single extra client.

## Keep the premium small and specific

The premium should be small enough that no client has to think twice about it. Ten to fifteen percent is the range most salons land on. A haircut at AED 150 becomes AED 165 at peak; a blow-dry at AED 120 becomes AED 135. Round numbers help: your team can quote them without a calculator.

Be specific about which hours count. Peak is usually weekday evenings from around 5 PM to closing, plus Thursday and Friday evenings and the day before a public holiday. Say so plainly on your price list and in your WhatsApp replies. Vague peak pricing — a different price depending on who is asking — is what breeds resentment.

## Protect your regulars first

Regulars are the one group who must never feel punished by a change like this. They are the clients who kept you going through the quiet months, and they are the first to notice a price change. So give them a way through it.

The simplest approach is a grace period: tell regulars about the change a month before it starts, and let their existing standing appointments keep the old price for one more cycle. Another is a regulars' rate: clients who book the same slot every week or fortnight pay the standard price, while one-off peak bookings pay the premium. Any of these costs you almost nothing, and it turns a price rise into a reason to become a regular.

## Use the quiet hours as the other half of the deal

Peak pricing only works as a pair with something good at the other end. Every time you mention the peak premium, mention the quiet-hour price in the same breath. "Evenings are AED 165, weekday mornings are AED 150" is a choice, not a penalty. Some clients will pay the premium gladly. Others will happily move to a Tuesday morning — the empty chair fills, and the evening slot frees up for someone who will pay for it.

When a client asks for Thursday at 7 PM and you are full, offer the nearest peak slot at the peak price and the nearest quiet slot at the standard price, side by side. Let the client choose. Most of the time they will pick one of the two, and either way you have kept the booking.

## Test it on one service before you roll it out

Do not reprice your whole menu on a Monday morning. Pick one service where demand at peak is clearly stronger than supply — blow-dries and quick colour touch-ups are the usual candidates — and apply the premium to that service only, for four weeks. Watch three numbers: peak bookings for that service, how many clients move to quieter hours instead, and how many complain. If bookings hold steady and complaints stay near zero, roll it out to the rest of the menu. If one service drops sharply, the premium is too high for it.

The four-week test also gives your team time to practise the conversation. The words matter more than the number. "Our evening slots are in the highest demand, so they are priced a little higher — weekday mornings stay at the usual price" is honest and easy to say.

## Tell clients before they discover it

The fastest way to lose a client over peak pricing is for them to find out at checkout. Announce the change a month ahead: a short WhatsApp message, a small note at reception, and an updated price list that shows both prices clearly. Explain the reason in one line — evenings are the hours everyone wants, and the premium keeps those hours available — and point to the quiet-hour price in the same message.

After-hours enquiries deserve the same clarity. Many peak bookings start as a WhatsApp message sent at 10 PM, long after the salon has closed. If that message gets a reply the next morning with the peak price already stated, the client can decide with full information instead of arriving with a surprise. ## What to do with the extra revenue

A small premium only becomes real growth if you can see where the money goes. Put it to work on the thing that makes the next peak hour easier: an extra pair of hands on Thursday evenings, a better booking system, or a buffer that lets you hold a slot for a regular without losing money. Tell your team what the premium is funding, too. People defend a price they understand.

Peak-hour pricing is not about charging more for the same thing. It is about recognising that your 7 PM chair and your 11 AM chair are two different products, and pricing them like it.
