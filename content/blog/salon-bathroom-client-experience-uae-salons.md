Your client has had a brilliant balayage, a perfect blow-dry and a warm goodbye at the door. She walks out happy. Then, on the way home, she opens Google and leaves four stars instead of five. The reason she mentions in the review? The bathroom.

It sounds unfair, but that is how client experience works: people judge a business by its weakest point. A spotless salon with a tired, smelly or poorly stocked bathroom feels like a salon that cuts corners where nobody is watching. In the UAE, where alternatives sit one mall corridor away, clients notice fast.

The good news is that the bathroom is the cheapest room in your salon to fix. It takes no renovation and no big budget. It takes a routine, a few smart choices and someone who owns it.

## Why Clients Judge You by the Bathroom

A client sitting in your chair is being looked after. She sees the clean tools, the fresh towels and the stylist's care. The bathroom is the one place she visits alone, without your team watching — the one place where the real standard shows.

She notices the mirror, the tap, the smell, the bin and whether the door locks properly. A wobbly lock or an empty soap dispenser does not just annoy her — it quietly tells her that nobody checks the details here. And if nobody checks the details here, she wonders what else is being skipped.

## The Two-Minute Hourly Check

The single most effective change a salon can make costs nothing: a short bathroom check every hour, on the clock. Not when someone remembers. Not at the end of the day. Every hour, on the hour.

Give the check a named owner for each shift and keep a small checklist in the staff area. The check takes two minutes: flush and wipe, check the soap and hand towels, empty the bin if it is half full, check the floor, and spray the air. That is it. Two minutes, once an hour, and the room never gets the chance to look neglected.

The busiest time matters most. Between 5 pm and 9 pm, when the evening rush fills every chair, the bathroom gets its heaviest use — exactly when nobody has time to check it. Build the check into the diary: whoever is between clients at the top of the hour does the round. If everyone is busy, the receptionist does it. The job always has a name next to it.

## Stock It Like You Mean It

Nothing ruins the impression faster than an empty dispenser. Clients do not complain about it in person — they just remember it. So stock the room generously and check the levels, not just the appearance.

Keep a small cupboard or basket in the bathroom itself with spare hand towels, tissues, sanitary items, a spare roll and a bottle of surface spray. When the stock lives in the room, the hourly check takes seconds instead of a trip to the store room. Nobody tops up a dispenser if it means leaving the floor during a busy evening.

Choose quality where hands touch. A decent liquid soap, a proper hand cream and good hand towels signal care far more than an expensive scented candle. If you use paper towels, empty the bin often — an overflowing bin undoes everything else in seconds.

## Scent, Light and the Door That Locks

Three things decide how a bathroom feels within the first five seconds: how it smells, how it looks, and whether the door feels safe.

For scent, keep it simple and consistent. One mild air freshener or a reed diffuser, the same scent every week, is better than a different spray each day. Heavy perfume in a small room is worse than no scent at all, and in the UAE's climate a stuffy, airless bathroom feels ten times worse. If the room has a window or an extractor fan, use it.

For light, check the bulb. A flickering or yellowed light makes the whole room look tired, and it makes the mirror useless for the quick check every client does before leaving. A bright, clean white bulb and a mirror wiped free of water spots cost almost nothing and change the whole feel.

And the lock: test it yourself, today. A lock that sticks, rattles or does not catch is the fastest way to make a client uncomfortable. Fix it this week. Privacy is part of the service, and nowhere is that more true than behind a bathroom door.

## Make It Part of the Visit, Not an Afterthought

Stop thinking of the bathroom as a facility and start thinking of it as part of the visit. The same way you would never hand a client a stained towel, you should never send her to a room that feels forgotten.

Some salons in Dubai and Abu Dhabi already treat it this way. They put a small framed note on the wall with the salon's Wi-Fi password, a fresh flower or a small plant on the shelf, and a hand towel folded the way a hotel would fold it. None of this is expensive. All of it says the same thing: we thought about you, even in here.

Ask your team a simple question this week: when was the last time each of you actually used the client bathroom? If the answer is never, that is the problem. Walk in as a client would. Sit down. Look around. Try the lock. You will spot three things to fix in sixty seconds, and your clients have been spotting them for months.

## A Small Room With a Big Return

Nobody books a salon because of its bathroom. But plenty of clients quietly decide not to come back because of one. The reverse is just as true: a clean, fresh, well-stocked bathroom reinforces everything else you do well. It tells the client that the care she felt in the chair is real, not a performance.

Add it to your weekly routine. Once a week, the owner or manager walks the whole salon the way a first-time client would — including the bathroom. Fix what you see, restock what is low, and note what needs replacing. In a month, the smallest room in your salon will be working as hard as the biggest one.
