A client sits in your chair and says what every UAE facial therapist hears now: "My skin looks dull. I want a glow, but I don't want anything dramatic." A few years ago the answer was almost always a hydrating facial. Today, more salons have a second answer — a chemical peel.

Peels still carry an old reputation — many clients picture the harsh, flaking treatments of twenty years ago. The modern salon peel is different: a light, controlled exfoliation that takes thirty to forty-five minutes, and most clients go straight back to work. In the UAE, where the cooler months suit stronger skin treatments, peels are fast becoming a requested addition to the menu.

## Why peels are back

The first reason is results. UAE clients live with a mix that is hard on skin: strong sun, air conditioning and hard water. The result is often the same — dullness and uneven tone that a surface facial can only partly reach. A light peel works a little deeper, lifting away the dead skin that dulls everything. The glow after a first peel is visible before the client leaves — and visible results turn a one-off facial into a booked course.

The second reason is time. A light peel fits into a lunch break, with no long recovery and no need to plan around a social calendar. For the busy professionals who book after work, that matters: they want a treatment that shows on Friday night and does not interfere with Saturday.

## Why winter is peel season in the UAE

In the UAE, timing is a safety rule, not a marketing trick. After a peel, skin is more sensitive to the sun, and the cooler months from November to March are when clients can realistically follow aftercare: staying out of midday sun, wearing sunscreen, and avoiding the beach for a few days. In summer that advice is far harder to follow, and the pigmentation risk is genuinely higher.

Smart salons lean into this. November is the moment to introduce peels to regulars and book a short course of three sessions spaced a few weeks apart. A client who starts in November finishes before the heat returns, with a result worth talking about — and a reason to rebook.

## What a modern salon peel actually is

The word "chemical" does most of the damage to this treatment's reputation. A peel is a solution — usually fruit acids such as glycolic, lactic or mandelic acid — applied to the skin for a few minutes to loosen dead skin cells. The therapist neutralises it, and the skin renews itself over the following days. No machine, no needle, no cutting.

Start with the lightest options. A gentle lactic or mandelic peel suits most skin types, including the sensitive and deeper-toned skin common among UAE clients. Stronger peels belong in a clinic, not on a salon menu — and saying that boundary out loud is what makes a salon trustworthy.

Honesty also covers who should not have a peel: clients who are pregnant, who have an active skin infection, who recently had laser treatment, or who use strong prescription skin products. A short screening form before the first peel takes two minutes and protects everyone.

## Pricing it like a course, not a one-off

A single peel gives a glow. A course of three gives a result. Salons that price peels as a one-off often find clients try it once and never return; pricing it as a course keeps the client coming back and gives the treatment time to work.

A simple structure is a single-session price, and a three-session package at roughly two and a half times the single price. Sell the package at the consultation, once the client has seen what the first peel does, and book all three sessions into the diary at the start, spaced three to four weeks apart — so the course has a shape and the client has dates to keep.

Add-ons make the numbers work better: a hydrating facial a week later, and LED light therapy in the same visit — a small skin programme around the peel, and a reason for regulars to visit more often through the winter.

## The consultation that protects you

The consultation is the difference between a five-star review and a complaint. Ten minutes before the first session, covering four questions: the client's skin goal, her skin type and tone, the products she uses at home, and what she is doing in the sun over the next two weeks.

That last question matters most in the UAE. A client flying to the Maldives the day after her peel should not be having one today, and a client who cannot commit to sunscreen for a week should be offered a hydrating facial instead. Turning a client away from a treatment she is not ready for is the fastest way to earn her trust — she will come back, and bring her friends.

## Aftercare in the UAE sun

Aftercare is where salon peels succeed or fail — the UAE sun does not forgive carelessness. Every peel client should leave with three written instructions: sunscreen every morning for a week, no direct midday sun for a few days, and no scrubs, retinols or strong active products until the skin feels normal again.

A short WhatsApp message the next morning makes the advice stick: "Hope your skin feels good today — remember your sunscreen, and message us if anything feels unusual." ## How to add peels to your menu

Start small. Choose one gentle peel, train two therapists on it properly, and offer it to your existing facial regulars first — the clients who already trust you with their skin. Put it on the menu with a plain description and a price, not a dramatic promise: "A gentle exfoliating treatment for dull, tired skin. Thirty minutes. Visible glow, no downtime."

Track what happens for a month: who rebooks, which skin types respond best, and what questions come up. Then you will know whether peels deserve a bigger place on the menu, or a place in your winter bridal programme. The peel is not a miracle treatment, and salons should never sell it as one — but for clients who want visible results without drama, it is one of the most practical additions a UAE salon can make.
