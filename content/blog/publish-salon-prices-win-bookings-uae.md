Walk into any busy salon in Dubai or Abu Dhabi and you will see the same quiet standoff: a client who wants to know the price before she books, and a salon that would rather not say it until she is already in the chair. Most UAE salon owners keep their prices off the website, off Google and out of WhatsApp. Competitors might copy them. Price-shoppers might be scared off.

Those fears are real, but the cost of hiding your prices is higher than the cost of showing them. Every day, clients in the UAE check two or three salons and book with the one that answers their first question without making them ask: how much does it cost?

## The client who never messages

Here is the booking your salon loses without ever knowing about it: a woman in JLT finishes work at six and searches for a balayage price in Dubai. She finds one salon with a clear price range, a second that says "DM for prices", and yours, which says nothing at all. She messages the first salon and never messages you — not because your work is worse, but because you made her work harder to find out.

This is how most UAE salon clients actually choose. They compare before they contact. A haircut in a Dubai mall salon can cost anything from AED 80 to AED 400, and a client with a budget in mind needs to know whether she is in the right place before she spends twenty minutes in a WhatsApp conversation. When the price is invisible, the easiest assumption is that it is too expensive — and she moves on.

## What an honest price list actually looks like

Publishing your prices does not mean one fixed number for every service. Hair and skin do not work that way. A good salon price list uses honest ranges: "Balayage from AED 450", "Classic facial AED 250–350", "Gel manicure AED 140". The range tells the client the ballpark, and "from" is the honest truth — length, thickness and condition change the final figure.

Add one short line where it matters: what changes the price. "Long or thick hair may cost more — your stylist confirms the exact price at consultation." That single sentence does more for trust than any discount ever will.

Keep the list short enough to read in thirty seconds: ten to fifteen core services, grouped the way clients think. And once the list is published, it has to be the same everywhere: website, Google Business Profile, WhatsApp catalogue and the printed menu in the salon. Nothing damages trust faster than a client who was quoted AED 200 online and charged AED 320 at the till.

## It filters the wrong clients out — and the right ones in

The biggest fear owners have is that publishing prices will scare clients away. In practice, it scares away the clients you were never going to keep.

A client who books knowing the price has already accepted it. She does not haggle at the chair, and she does not leave a one-star review because the bill surprised her. The clients you lose are the ones who would have messaged "how much?", been told the price, and vanished anyway.

That time saving is real money. A front desk can spend an hour a day answering price questions from people who never book. When the price is already public, those messages shrink to the ones that matter: "Is Thursday at 7 available?" — a question from someone who is ready to book. And when the enquiry arrives at 11 PM, after the salon has closed, a 24/7 AI receptionist like ZYNA can answer it straight from your published price list, so the client gets her answer in seconds instead of waiting until morning.

## How to publish prices without starting a price war

Showing your prices does not mean competing on them. The salons that win on transparency are not the cheapest — they are the clearest. Three habits keep a published price list working for you.

First, show what the price includes. "Balayage from AED 450, including consultation, toner and a blow-dry" reads completely differently from a bare number. Clients compare value, not digits.

Second, never copy a competitor's prices or undercut them in public. Your price list should reflect your costs, your skill and your location, not the salon two doors down.

Third, treat the price list as a living document. Review it every month, and when a cost rises or a service takes longer than planned, update all four places the same day. A stale price list is worse than no price list at all.

## The search bonus nobody talks about

There is a second reward for publishing prices, and it costs nothing. Clients do not search Google for "best salon Dubai" as often as owners think. They search for "balayage price Dubai", "gel nails price Abu Dhabi", "hydrafacial cost Sharjah". Those are searches from people who have already decided what they want and are choosing where to get it.

If your salon's price page answers that search, Google will show it — and the client who lands on it is close to booking. Salons that hide their prices are invisible in exactly the searches that matter most.

This is also why the price page should live on your own website, not only on Instagram. A WhatsApp catalogue or an Instagram highlight disappears from search, while a page on your site keeps working for months.

## Start this week: the five-step price list

You do not need a designer or a marketing agency. One quiet afternoon is enough.

Write down your fifteen most-booked services and the price range for each, exactly as your team already quotes them. Put the list on your website, on your Google Business Profile, in your WhatsApp catalogue and on a printed card at reception. Train the whole team to quote the same numbers, so the price a client sees online is the price she hears on the phone. Then set a monthly reminder to review it, and update all four places together when anything changes.

The first week will feel strange. A few clients will still ask the price on WhatsApp. But the conversations that follow get shorter, warmer, and far more of them end with a time and a name. The salon that is honest about its prices is the one clients trust — and trust, in a city with a salon on every corner, is the marketing that actually books chairs.
