Every year, as early December approaches, the same pattern plays out in salons across the UAE. Flags appear in shop windows, group chats fill with party plans, and clients start thinking about how they want to look for the celebrations. It should be one of the best booking periods of the year. Too often, though, salons turn it into a discount war instead: 50 percent off everything, then a desperate last-minute offer that fills chairs for a weekend and empties the profit from the whole month.

There is a better way to market around National Day. Clients are not only looking for a bargain. They are looking for a reason to book, a service that feels special for the occasion, and a salon that makes the whole thing easy. A salon that plans around those three things can fill its diary, protect its prices, and come out of the season with new regulars rather than one-time discount hunters.

## Start early, not the week before

The biggest mistake in festive marketing is timing. If your National Day offer appears only a few days before the holiday, you are competing with every other salon at exactly the moment clients have already made their plans. The clients who book early are the ones who plan their hair, nails and skin around events, and they decide weeks in advance.

Start talking about National Day in the second half of November. That does not mean launching the offer immediately. It means planting the idea: a short message to regular clients that festive slots are opening, and a note in the salon that December appointments are already being booked. By the time you announce the actual offer, clients should already be thinking about when they want to come in, not whether they want to come in at all.

## Sell a themed experience, not a percentage off

A percentage discount is the laziest festive offer, and clients know it. It says nothing about your salon, it trains clients to wait for the next sale, and it is very hard to walk back in January when regular prices return. A themed service, by contrast, gives clients something they cannot get at any other time of year, which is exactly what makes it worth booking.

Think in terms of a small, time-limited menu rather than a sale across the board. A blow-dry and styling package designed for a National Day evening out. A manicure in the colours of the flag, offered as a set look with a name of its own. A short, refreshing facial for clients who want to look rested before a long weekend of gatherings. None of these need to be cheap. They need to feel considered, festive and finite: available for a short window, then gone.

Price them fairly, with the extra touches already included — a head massage with the blow-dry, or a hand treatment with the manicure. Clients compare a themed package with an ordinary appointment and see added value. Clients compare a 50 percent discount with your normal price and wonder whether they have been overcharged all along.

## Make the offer honest and easy to book

Festive offers fail most often on the details, not the idea. A client sees a beautiful post, messages to ask which days it applies to and how long it takes — and hears nothing back until the next morning, by which time she has booked elsewhere. In the UAE, where so many salon enquiries arrive on WhatsApp in the evening, after the salon has closed, that gap is where festive bookings are lost.

Every festive offer should answer four questions in the first message: what exactly is included, what it costs, which dates it runs, and how to book it. If the offer excludes certain services, or is only available on weekdays, say so plainly. Honesty at this stage does not lose bookings. It prevents the awkward conversation at the chair when a client discovers the deal was not quite what she thought.

Speed matters just as much as clarity. A campaign that drives enquiries at 10pm needs replies at 10pm, or at least a reply that confirms the booking straight away. ## Plan the diary for the festive rush

A successful campaign creates a diary problem, and that is a good problem only if you plan for it. National Day demand tends to cluster: the days just before the holiday fill first, evenings fill before mornings, and popular stylists fill before anyone else. Without a plan, the salon ends up overbooked, running late, and disappointing the very clients the campaign attracted.

Protect the diary with a few simple rules. Keep a realistic gap between appointments on festive days rather than stacking bookings back to back. Hold a small number of slots for regular clients who book late, instead of letting every chair go to first-time festive bookings. And tell clients honestly when a day is full rather than squeezing them in and running an hour behind. A client offered the next good slot will usually take it. A client kept waiting for an hour will not come back.

It also helps to spread demand deliberately. Offer the festive menu on quieter weekday mornings as well as peak evenings, and give clients a gentle reason to choose the quieter slot — an extra treatment step, or first choice of stylist. The goal is a full week, not a single frantic weekend.

## After National Day, turn one visit into a regular

The real return on festive marketing does not come from the holiday itself. It comes from what happens next. A salon that treats her as a future regular will not have to spend the same money attracting her again next year.

The follow-up should be simple and personal: a message the next day thanking her for coming in, a note about how the style or treatment will last, and a suggestion for the next visit timed to the service she had. No discount is needed. What the client remembers is being looked after, not being sold to. Festive marketing done well leaves a salon stronger than it started: a fuller diary, a team that coped with a busy period, and new clients who came in for a celebration and stayed because the experience was good.
