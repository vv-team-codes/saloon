In a city where every salon fights for attention on Instagram and TikTok, it is easy to assume print is dead. It is not. Walk through any villa community in Dubai or Abu Dhabi in the evening and you will see leaflets pinned to noticeboards, tucked under doors and stacked by the lift. Most are forgettable. A few are not — and those few quietly fill chairs.

A salon's best clients are usually its nearest ones. A flyer reaches a woman in the next street at home, at the moment she is thinking about her week, with no algorithm in between. Done well, a small leaflet drop is the cheapest new-client campaign a UAE salon can run.

## Why print still works in a WhatsApp-first city

Dubai is a WhatsApp-first city. Clients message before they call and book from their phones at midnight. A flyer fits that habit better than most digital ads, because a good flyer ends with one action: message us on WhatsApp. No website to navigate, no form to fill in, no account to create.

## Start with one neighbourhood, not the whole city

The biggest mistake salons make with leaflets is trying to cover everywhere at once. A thousand flyers spread thinly across five areas will bring almost nothing. The same thousand dropped carefully into one community will bring bookings.

Pick the streets and towers within a ten-minute drive of your salon, because those are the clients who will come back. If you are in a mall, pick the closest residential towers and villa communities. If you are on a high street, pick the buildings behind you.

Walk the area first and notice where people actually go: the supermarket entrance, the community pool, the nursery gate, the pharmacy, the gym. Notice which flyers are still on the noticeboards after a week — those are the boards worth using.

## What to put on the flyer — and what to leave off

A salon flyer has about three seconds to earn a second look: one clear photo, one clear message and one clear action. Everything else is clutter.

The photo should be real work from your own salon: a finished cut, a colour result, a neat manicure. Stock photos will be spotted instantly. Use one strong image, not a collage of six.

The message should name one thing: a signature blow-dry, a keratin treatment, a bridal package, a men's grooming offer. People remember one thing; they forget a menu.

The action should be a WhatsApp number or a QR code that opens a WhatsApp chat, plus the salon's name and area. Add the price or a price range for the featured service, because a flyer without a price asks the client to do the work of finding out. Leave off the rest: no long list of opening hours, no paragraph about your philosophy, no five social media handles.

## The offer that actually gets redeemed

A flyer needs a reason to act now. A vague "visit us soon" is not one. A specific, time-limited offer is.

The best leaflet offers are simple and honest: a first-visit price on one named service, a free add-on with a full colour, or a fixed package price that runs until a stated date. Keep the discount modest — a deep discount brings bargain hunters who never return. What you want is a fair reason for someone already curious to book this week instead of someday.

Put a short code word on the flyer, like "MARINA20", and ask clients to mention it when they message. Every booking that arrives with the code word is one you can trace back to the flyer.

## Where to drop — and where not to

A door drop works best in villa communities and low-rise buildings, where a leaflet under a door or in a letterbox is normal. In high-rise towers, the lift lobby and noticeboard matter more, because many towers restrict door-to-door distribution.

Always ask before you drop. Community management, building security and mall management all have rules about leaflets, and a salon that asks first gets welcomed back. Ask the supermarket, the nursery and the gym if you can leave a small stack — most will say yes, especially if you offer their members a small first-visit offer in return.

## Track it like a digital ad

Print only earns its place in your marketing budget if you measure it.

Count the flyers you print and distribute, and note the date, the area and the code word. Then watch the WhatsApp inbox for a fortnight and tally every enquiry that mentions the code word. At the end of two weeks you will know what the drop cost, how many enquiries it produced and how many became bookings.

If a drop brings nothing, do not just print more. Change one thing: the photo, the offer, the area or the drop point. Many salons find the second drop in the same area performs better than the first, because residents have now seen the name twice.

## The follow-up that turns a flyer into a regular

A flyer brings a first visit. What happens next decides whether it becomes a regular. When a flyer client messages, reply fast and personally — she has already chosen you over every other salon in the area. Confirm the service, the price and the time in one message, and ask one friendly question, such as whether she has had the treatment before.

After the visit, a short thank-you message the same evening does more than any loyalty card. And when the flyers start working, the enquiries often arrive late at night, after the salon has closed. ## A simple first campaign

Keep the first drop small: five hundred A5 flyers featuring one service, one photo, one price and one code word, in one community within ten minutes of your salon, over two evenings. Ask two nearby businesses to display a small stack, then watch the inbox for two weeks and count the code-word bookings.

If it works, repeat the same drop in the same area a month later, with a fresh photo and a new code word. Then try the next community. Slowly, street by street, you are building something no algorithm can take away: a salon the neighbours know by name, because it turned up in their hands.
