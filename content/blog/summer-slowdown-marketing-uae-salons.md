Every salon owner in the UAE knows the pattern. June arrives, the schools break up, and the diary starts to thin out. Regular clients fly home for the summer, and the chairs that were fully booked in May suddenly have gaps. It feels like a slump, but it is really a season — and seasons can be planned for.

The salons that survive summer best are not the ones that panic-discount in July. They are the ones that start marketing for summer in May, with a clear idea of who is still in town and what those people actually want. Here is how to think about it.

## Start with the clients who stay

Not everyone leaves. Dubai and Abu Dhabi are full of residents who stay through the summer — people without school-age children, professionals on short breaks rather than long ones, and newcomers who have only just arrived and have no trip planned. These are your summer market, and most salons barely speak to them.

Before the season starts, look at your own booking records. Who booked through last July and August, and which services did they choose? In many salons the answer is revealing: the summer regulars are often men booking grooming, women booking root touch-ups and blow-dries before staycation weekends, and clients who prefer weekday mornings when the salon is quiet and cool. Build your summer marketing around those real people, not around the clients who have already left.

A simple WhatsApp message to last summer's clients works better than a generic broadcast. Something honest and specific: "We are quieter in July, so we have opened extra morning slots and put together a few summer packages." It reminds them you exist, gives them a reason to book, and costs nothing to send.

## Build a staycation package, not a discount

The temptation in a quiet month is to cut prices across the board. Resist it. A 50 percent off poster trains clients to wait for the next sale, and it rarely brings back the money it gives away. What works better is a package — several services bundled into one visit at a fair combined price.

Think about what a staycation weekend actually looks like for your clients: a Friday morning haircut and blow-dry, a pedicure, and a quick facial before a hotel check-in or a family dinner. Put those together as a named summer package with a clear price and duration, and market it as a treat rather than a bargain. People buy experiences; they haggle over discounts.

Timing matters too. In summer, demand shifts. Evenings stay busy because nobody wants to be out in the afternoon heat, while late mornings and early afternoons go quiet. Price your packages to pull bookings into those empty hours — a weekday morning package, or a Sunday-to-Wednesday offer — instead of discounting the Saturday evening slots that would have filled anyway.

## Tourists and hotel guests are your summer market

While residents travel out, tourists travel in. Summer brings visitors escaping cooler climates, families on stopovers, and hotel guests with a free afternoon and a spa budget. Most of them will never find your salon unless you go to them.

The simplest route is the hotel next door. Walk in, introduce yourself to the concierge and the front desk team, and leave a small card or a one-page menu of your most tourist-friendly services: blow-dry, express manicure, beard trim, a 30-minute facial. Offer the hotel a small commission or a reciprocal arrangement, and make booking effortless — a WhatsApp number on the card, a reply within minutes, and a clear price before they arrive.

If your salon sits inside or near a mall, the same logic applies to footfall. Summer pushes everyone indoors, and malls get busier, not quieter. A small, well-placed offer at the mall entrance or a partnership with a neighbouring boutique can put your name in front of people who are already out spending.

## Use the quiet weeks to be seen

Summer is also the best time to fix the marketing you never have time for during the busy season. Your Google Business Profile, your Instagram grid, your service menu on WhatsApp — all of these decide whether a stranger books with you, and all of them can be improved in a quiet afternoon.

Take fresh photos of real results in good light. Update your service list with current prices so nobody has to ask. Post short videos of the work itself — a colour transformation, a precise fade, a calm facial room — and pin the post that shows your best result with its price and duration. A new resident searching "salon near me" in July is judging you on exactly these things.

This is also the moment to ask happy clients for a Google review. A short request at checkout — "Would you mind leaving us a quick review? It really helps" — takes ten seconds, and a handful of fresh summer reviews keeps your profile looking active while competitors go quiet.

## Book the autumn now

The smartest summer marketing does not end in August. Every summer client is a September regular waiting to happen, if you give them a reason to return.

When a new client books in July, offer them their next appointment before they leave the chair. Not a voucher, not a discount — a date. "Shall we put you in for the same time in four weeks?" Most people say yes, because deciding now is easier than remembering later.

Track the numbers as you go. How many summer packages sold? How many hotel referrals turned into bookings? Which WhatsApp message brought the most replies? Write it down. Next summer you will not be guessing — you will have a plan that already worked once.

## The quiet months reward the salons that keep showing up

Summer in the UAE is not a dead season; it is a different season. The clients change, the hours shift, and the marketing has to change with them. The salons that treat June, July and August as a chance to reach new people — residents who stay, tourists who arrive, mall shoppers escaping the heat — come out of summer with fuller diaries and new regulars.

Start early, market to the people who are actually here, and make it easy for them to say yes. The chairs will fill.
