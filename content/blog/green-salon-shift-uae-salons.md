A client in a Dubai salon recently asked a question her stylist had never heard before: where does all the water go? She was sitting at the backwash, watching the tap run, and she wanted to know whether the salon did anything about it. The stylist laughed it off. The client did not come back.

That small moment captures a shift quietly reshaping UAE salons. Sustainability has stopped being a talking point for a handful of eco-conscious boutiques and started becoming something ordinary clients notice. They ask what is in the products, what happens to the foils and the bottles, and whether the salon thinks about any of it at all. The salons answering well are finding that going greener does not mean going cheaper or plainer. Done properly, it cuts costs, earns loyalty and gives the salon a story no discount can match.

## Why the UAE is ready for greener salons

The UAE is a country that talks openly about water. Conservation campaigns and the national push toward sustainability are part of everyday life here. A salon that uses thousands of litres of water a week and throws away a bin full of plastic every day is no longer invisible — it looks out of step with the place it operates in.

Clients feel it too. Many UAE residents have lived in cities where recycling is normal and single-use plastic is frowned upon. When they sit in a salon chair in Dubai or Abu Dhabi, they expect the same standards they see in the malls, hotels and restaurants around them. A salon that meets that expectation earns a quiet kind of trust — and cutting water, power and disposables is margin, not charity.

## Water: the easiest win

Water is where most UAE salons can start, because the savings are immediate and measurable. A standard backwash tap can use ten litres a minute or more. Low-flow aerators and eco showerheads cut that by a third or more without the client noticing any difference in pressure — and in many cases the gentler flow is actually more comfortable.

The bigger change is habit. Rinsing a colour a minute longer than needed or leaving a tap running while mixing product adds up fast. The fix is one rule: the tap runs only while water is actually being used.

## The foil mountain — and what replaces it

Ask any colourist what a salon throws away most, and the answer is the same: foils, gloves, cotton pads, capes and applicators. A busy colour salon can use hundreds of foils a week, and every one of them goes in the bin.

Reusable foils are the most practical swap. Modern reusable alternatives wash cleanly, last for months and hold colour just as well as disposable foil. They cost more upfront and need a washing routine, but salons that have switched report that the investment pays for itself within a few months — and the bin at the end of the day is visibly lighter.

## Products: refill, not repurchase

The most visible green change a client will ever see is on the shelf. Refill stations for shampoo, conditioner and styling products are appearing in UAE salons, and they solve two problems at once: they cut plastic waste, and they bring clients back into the salon between appointments.

The model is simple. A client buys a product once, then returns with the same bottle and pays a little less for a refill. The salon keeps the margin, the client saves money, and the bottle stays out of the bin. It also creates a reason for a client to walk through the door on a quiet weekday, which is exactly when salons want footfall.

Clients also ask what is in the bottle — whether a formula is cruelty-free and the packaging recyclable. One honest range you can stand behind beats a full shelf you cannot explain.

## Energy and the quiet hours

A salon's electricity bill is dominated by air conditioning, lighting and hot water. In the UAE, cooling is the biggest single cost, and it is also the one with the simplest fixes. A door that stays open onto a hot mall corridor forces the air conditioning to work twice as hard. A thermostat set two degrees higher, with fans at the stations, keeps clients comfortable for far less.

LED lighting uses a fraction of the power of older fittings and flatters both the client and the colour work — green choices and good design usually point the same way.

## Telling clients without preaching

The mistake salons make with sustainability is turning it into a lecture. Nobody books a haircut to be told off about the planet. The salons doing this well treat it the way they treat everything else about the client experience: quietly, confidently, and only when asked.

A small sign at reception — "we refill, we reuse, we recycle" — is enough, plus a refill reminder when a product runs low. The client who cares will notice and stay loyal because of it.

What never works is the green claim the salon cannot back up. If the products are not cruelty-free, do not say they are. If the foils still go in the bin, do not claim zero waste. UAE clients are well-travelled and well-informed, and a claim that falls apart on a second look costs more trust than saying nothing at all.

## Starting small, starting this month

A salon does not need a grand relaunch to join this shift. The practical starting point is a one-month audit: weigh the bin for a week, read the water and electricity meters, and count how many disposable items the salon uses in a day. The numbers will point to the first change, and it is usually the cheapest one — aerators on the taps, reusable foils, or a refill station for the best-selling shampoo.

Then tell the team and the clients. A salon that honestly uses less water and throws away less plastic than last year has a story worth sharing — and a true story is an advantage no competitor can copy overnight.
