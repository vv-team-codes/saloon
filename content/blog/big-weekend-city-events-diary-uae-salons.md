Dubai does not have quiet weekends the way other cities do. One week it is a concert at the Coca-Cola Arena, the next a trade exhibition at the World Trade Centre, then a New Year's Eve countdown or a festival weekend that fills the hotels. For a salon owner, each of those weekends shows up the same way: the phone starts buzzing on Wednesday, every regular suddenly wants Friday, and by Thursday evening the diary is full of strangers while loyal clients ask why they cannot get in.

## Why big weekends hit the diary differently

A normal Saturday builds gradually: regulars book during the week, a few walk-ins drift in from the mall, and the evening rush peaks around 6 or 7 pm. A big-event weekend compresses all of that into a shorter window, and the clients are different. Event-goers book late and want everything at once: hair, nails, makeup, a blow-dry that lasts the night.

## Watch the city's calendar, not just your own

The simplest habit is also the one most owners skip: keep the city's event calendar next to your diary. Once a month, spend twenty minutes looking at what is coming: the big concert announcements, the exhibition season at DWTC, GITEX week, the shopping festival, New Year's Eve, the major sporting fixtures. Mark the weekends that will matter on a wall calendar in the salon.

## Open the diary earlier for event weeks

For a normal week, opening bookings two or three weeks ahead is plenty. For a big-event week, open earlier, and tell people you have done it. A short WhatsApp message to your regulars two weeks before a big weekend does more than any advertisement: "Concert weekend is coming, and Friday fills fast. If you want your usual slot, book it now."

This is the single most valuable move. Your regulars get their chairs before the visitors arrive, and you go into the week knowing how much of the diary is already spoken for.

## Protect your regulars first

Here is the rule that matters most on a big weekend: your regulars booked first, and they should be served first. The pressure of a busy weekend pushes owners the other way. A visitor offers to pay for three services at once, and suddenly a regular's standing Friday appointment is being "moved" to make room.

Do not do it. A visitor who books once for a concert is worth one visit. A regular who has come every three weeks for two years is worth dozens. Regulars notice when they are pushed aside. They may not complain. They simply book somewhere else next time.

Hold a few prime slots for regulars until the Wednesday before the event weekend, then release the rest. When a new client asks for a regular's slot, offer the nearest real alternative: a different stylist, a different time, or a shorter service that fits. A good alternative keeps the booking. A flat no loses it.

## Package the spike honestly

A big weekend is a chance to earn more, but the way you earn it matters. Putting your prices up for one weekend and dropping them again on Monday is the fastest way to damage trust. The better approach is packaging, not price rises.

An "event-ready" package works because it is honest about what it is: a blow-dry, a manicure and a makeup touch-up, booked as one appointment, at a fair combined price, in a single time block. Clients love it because it solves their whole evening in one booking. You love it because it fills a chair for two hours with one client instead of three separate bookings that each carry a no-show risk.

Mall salons have an extra advantage: footfall surges during big event weeks, so a specific sign or a Thursday WhatsApp status, naming the service, the price and the time, can pull in visitors who are already in the building.

## The day itself: running a diary built for a rush

Start the day with a ten-minute huddle. Everyone knows who is coming, which appointments are long, where the gaps are, and who is on standby to take a walk-in. Confirm the afternoon's big bookings by WhatsApp in the morning, because an unconfirmed 5 pm blow-dry that never shows up is a chair you cannot refill at 5 pm.

## After the event: turning one-time visitors into regulars

The weekend ends, the visitors fly home, and most salons move on. That is a wasted opportunity. Every new client who sat in your chair is someone who already trusts you with their hair or their nails. A short WhatsApp message on Sunday or Monday, thanking them and offering a simple next step, is often all it takes.

Keep it personal and specific. Mention the service they had, not a generic offer. "Hope the concert was brilliant. Your blow-dry should hold for a couple of days, and if you would like the same again before your next night out, here is the booking link." A small number will come back, and over a year of big weekends, that small number becomes a real part of your client base.

Big weekends in Dubai are not going away. The owners who do well from them are not the ones with the biggest salons. They are the ones who saw the weekend coming, protected their regulars, packaged the demand honestly, and ran a tight diary on the day.
