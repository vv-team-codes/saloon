It is one of the most common messages a UAE salon receives on WhatsApp: "What products do you use?" Sometimes it carries a brand name. Sometimes it is a new client's very first message. And too often, it gets a reply that ends the conversation instead of starting it.

A one-word answer, a photo of a shelf, or a long list of brand names rarely books anyone. She was not asking for a stock list. She was asking whether she can trust your salon with her hair, her skin, and her money. Answer the question behind the question, and a product enquiry becomes one of the easiest bookings you will make.

## Why clients ask about your products

There are four reasons behind the question, and each one needs a slightly different answer.

First, quality. A client who paid for a bad colour or an irritating facial wants reassurance before she books again. The brand name is her shortcut for asking whether your work will be any better.

Second, safety. Clients with sensitive skin, allergies, or a scalp that reacts badly to certain formulas want to know what will actually touch their hair and skin. The product question is often a health question in disguise.

Third, value. A client comparing three salons in the same mall or neighbourhood uses products to judge whether your price is fair. She is really asking what she gets for the money.

Fourth, loyalty to a brand. Some clients have used the same colour range or treatment line for years, and they want to know whether you stock it before they change salons. If you do not, that is not the end of the conversation — but only if you answer well.

## The answer that books: name it, explain it, tie it to them

A good reply does three things. Name the product. Explain, in plain words, why you chose it. Then connect it to the client's hair or skin.

"Yes, we use Wella colour for blonding — it lifts evenly in this humidity and keeps the tone from going brassy. For your fine, colour-treated hair, we would use the gentler developer and finish with a bond treatment."

That reply works because it is specific, it shows expertise, and it moves the conversation toward the person asking. Compare it with "We use professional products" — which tells the client nothing — or a photo of ten bottles, which asks her to do the work herself.

## "Do you use this brand?" — when the answer is no

Sooner or later, a client will ask for a brand you do not stock. This is where most salons lose the booking — with a flat no.

Never end on the no. Acknowledge the brand, explain what you use instead, and say why the alternative gives her the same result.

If the brand matters because of an allergy or a reaction she has had before, take it seriously. Ask what happened, note it on her record, and offer a patch test or a consultation before any service. A client who feels heard on a safety concern becomes one of your most loyal regulars.

## When the question is really about allergies

Some product enquiries are allergy questions wearing a different coat. "Is your shampoo sulphate-free?" "Is the henna pure?" These clients are not shopping around. They are checking whether it is safe to sit in your chair.

Answer these messages first, and answer them precisely. Vague reassurance — "don't worry, our products are safe" — is the worst reply you can give. Name the product, name what it does or does not contain, and offer a patch test or an ingredient list. If you are not sure, say you will check with the stylist and come back within the hour — and then actually do it.

Keep a short note in your client records for anyone who mentions an allergy or sensitivity. Next time she messages, your reply can start with what you already know about her — the kind of detail that keeps clients coming back.

## What never to say

A few habits quietly kill product enquiries. Listing every brand you stock in one long message reads like a warehouse inventory, not a recommendation. Bad-mouthing another brand makes you sound defensive, and the client may love the brand you just dismissed. And never claim a product does something it does not — if a treatment cannot repair split ends in one session, say so. Clients in the UAE talk to each other, and one overpromise travels further than ten good reviews.

## A reply you can use today

Keep one saved reply for product enquiries and teach the whole team to use it. Something like this:

"Great question! For colour we use [brand] — it gives even results in the UAE humidity and is gentle on treated hair. For your hair, the colourist would [one specific thing]. Would you like a free 10-minute consultation this week to confirm the shade and price? We have Thursday at 5:30 or Saturday at 11."

Notice what the message does. It answers the question, shows knowledge of local conditions, makes the advice personal, and ends with two real slots. The client does not have to think about what to ask next. She only has to pick a time.

## Keep a product note on every enquiry

Product enquiries are also free market research. If five clients in a week ask about a particular keratin range or a wudu-friendly polish, the market is telling you what to add to your menu. Log the requests, and once a month decide whether one is worth trialling.

The same note helps with retention. A client who asked about a brand, booked, and came back should be greeted with that detail remembered: "We kept the same colour formula from your last visit." Small sentences like that make a client feel like a regular, not a walk-in.

Answering product questions well is not about stocking the most expensive brands. It is about showing the client, in one short message, that you know your products and what to do next.
