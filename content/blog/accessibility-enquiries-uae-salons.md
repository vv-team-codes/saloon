Most salon enquiries are about price, availability or a hairstyle from a photo. Every so often, a message arrives that is different: "Can I get in with a wheelchair?" or "My mother uses a walker — is your salon easy for her?" The person writing has probably already been turned away, politely or otherwise, by other businesses. How you answer in the next few minutes decides whether they ever come through your door.

## Why this enquiry deserves a better answer than "yes, sure"

The temptation is to reply quickly with a reassuring "Yes, we're accessible!" and move on. The problem is that accessibility is not one thing. A salon can have a step-free entrance and still be impossible for a wheelchair user if the washbasin area is too narrow, the chairs are fixed, or the only accessible toilet is two floors down. A client who arrives on the strength of a breezy "yes" and then cannot get her hair washed will not come back — and she will tell people why.

## Answer the question they actually asked

A good reply starts by finding out what the client needs, rather than guessing. "Wheelchair" can mean a manual chair, a powered chair, a mobility scooter or a walker, and each one changes the answer. Two short questions are enough: what kind of mobility aid do they use, and which service are they hoping to book? With those two facts, you can give a useful answer instead of a generic one.

Then describe the journey through your salon, step by step, in plain words. Is there a step at the entrance, or a ramp? Is there a lift, and does it reach your floor? How wide are the doorways between reception, the styling chairs and the washbasins? Can a wheelchair fit beside a styling chair, or would the client need to transfer? Is there an accessible toilet in the building? These are the facts the client needs to plan her visit, and listing them shows you have actually thought about them.

## Prepare before you promise

The worst outcome is a confident booking followed by an awkward arrival. Before you confirm a slot, check the practical details. Which chair will the client use, and is there space to position a wheelchair next to it? Can your team help with a transfer safely, or should the client bring someone to assist? Does the washbasin recline, and can it be reached without a step up? Is there somewhere to store a wheelchair during the service?

If the answer to any of these is no, say so now, and offer the workaround. "Our washbasins are on a raised platform, so we wash hair at the chair with a portable basin instead" is a perfectly good answer. "Our accessible toilet is on the ground floor, two minutes away by lift" is honest and useful. What clients cannot forgive is discovering the problem at the door.

## Make the booking itself easy

Accessibility enquiries often come with a second, unspoken question: will this be a hassle? Answer that by making the booking easy. Offer a slightly longer slot without charging extra for the time, because transfers and positioning take a few minutes. Suggest a quieter time of day if the client would prefer it — weekday mornings in most UAE salons are calmer than the Saturday rush — but never insist on it. The client chooses her time; your job is to make whichever time she picks work.

Confirm the booking in writing, with the practical details included: the entrance to use, where to park, who to ask for on arrival, and your direct number in case anything changes on the day. That message removes the anxiety of the unknown, and it shows the client that her visit is already being looked after.

## When the honest answer is "not yet"

Some salons genuinely cannot accommodate every mobility need. A first-floor shop with no lift, a washbasin area that cannot be widened, a building with no accessible toilet — these are real constraints, especially in older buildings. If that is your situation, do not bluff. A dishonest yes is worse than an honest no.

What you can do is be specific about what is possible. Perhaps a wheelchair user can be served at the chair for a cut and blow-dry, even if the washbasin is out of reach. Perhaps you can offer a home visit for the services that need the basin. Perhaps you can recommend a nearby salon you trust that is fully accessible, and mean it. A client who is treated with respect when you cannot help her will remember your salon fondly — and may send friends, family or colleagues who can use it.

## The loyal client you win by getting this right

Clients with mobility needs are among the most loyal clients a salon can win, precisely because so few businesses serve them well. A wheelchair user who finds a salon where she is welcomed, prepared for and treated normally does not shop around. She rebooks, she brings family, and she recommends you to a community that talks to each other. In a city as connected as Dubai, that recommendation is worth more than any discount.

Accessibility needs are also far more common than most owners assume. A client recovering from surgery, an elderly mother who uses a walker, a regular whose knee has given way — mobility needs arrive gradually, in ordinary clients you already know. A salon that has thought about access keeps those clients. A salon that has not loses them quietly, one cancelled booking at a time.

So write down your salon's real accessibility facts — entrance, lift, doorways, chairs, washbasins, toilet, parking — and keep them where anyone answering messages can find them. Add them to your saved replies, so the next enquiry gets the same careful answer even at 11 PM on a Friday.
