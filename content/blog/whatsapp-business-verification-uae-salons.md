A new client finds your salon on Google Maps or Instagram, likes what she sees, and taps the WhatsApp button. In the next five seconds she makes a decision most salon owners never think about: is this a real business, or just a number?

She looks at the name at the top of the chat, the profile photo, the description, the opening hours and whether a small green tick sits beside the name. If the profile looks half-finished or anonymous, she hesitates. If it looks like a proper business, she types her question.

## Why the green tick matters in the UAE

WhatsApp is the default way clients contact salons here. It is how regulars rebook, brides ask about trials, and new residents in Dubai Marina or JVC find a salon for the first time.

Verification, shown as a green tick next to your business name, tells a stranger that Meta has confirmed this number belongs to a real, registered business. It does something quieter and more useful: it removes doubt. A client about to pay a deposit for a bridal package wants to know she is talking to a salon, not a stranger with a phone. The tick answers that question instantly. It matters even more for salons in malls and on high streets, where a client may be choosing between three salons within a few hundred metres. The one whose profile looks complete and verified feels like the safer choice — and the safer choice gets the message first.

## What verification actually is (and is not)

WhatsApp Business, the free app most salons use, is not the same as verification. The app gives you business tools: a profile, quick replies, labels, away messages and a catalogue. Verification is a separate step, applied for through Meta, and granted to businesses that prove their identity with official documents such as a trade licence.

A verified account is not a guarantee of quality. It is a guarantee of identity. It says this business exists, it is registered, and this number is genuinely its number. That is exactly the reassurance a first-time client wants before she books a keratin treatment or a bridal package.

The process is a paperwork exercise, not a technical one. You submit your trade licence details, confirm the business name matches the licence, and wait for Meta's review. Any legitimate salon with a valid UAE trade licence can apply. If a first application is refused, the usual reason is a mismatch between the name on the licence and the name on the WhatsApp account — fix the name, and reapply.

## A complete profile does half the work

The tick gets attention, but the profile does the persuading. Many UAE salons run WhatsApp Business yet leave the profile almost empty: a logo, no description, no hours, no address, no services. That is a missed booking every single day.

Start with the business description. You have limited space, so use it for what a new client actually needs: what you do, who you serve and where you are. "Ladies salon in Jumeirah — hair, colour, nails and facials. Open daily 10am to 9pm."

Add your real opening hours, and keep them honest. If your salon closes at 9pm, say so — and use an away message after hours rather than leaving clients wondering whether anyone will reply. Add your address with the building name and a nearby landmark. A client in a mall in Deira or a villa community in Arabian Ranches navigates by landmarks, and a clear address turns a "where are you exactly?" message into a booking.

Then fill in your catalogue with honest price ranges. When a client can see your balayage price before she types, the conversation starts at the booking stage instead of the price stage.

## Using the tick in your everyday replies

Verification only helps if clients can see it. Put your number — as a tap-to-chat link — on your Instagram bio, your Google Business Profile and your website, so every place a client discovers you leads to the same verified chat.

Train your team to reply in the same voice the profile promises: a short, warm, complete answer — the service, the price range, two real time slots — keeps the client moving toward a booking.

The tick also helps after hours. Clients message salons late at night, often after 10pm, when they are planning the next day. A verified profile with an away message that says when you will reply keeps that client warm overnight. ## The mistakes that waste it

The most common mistake is inconsistency. A salon applies for verification with one name, then uses a different name on Instagram and a third on its shop sign. Keep the business name identical everywhere: licence, WhatsApp, Instagram, Google and the sign above the door.

The second mistake is treating the profile as a one-time job. Menus change, prices change, opening hours change during Ramadan or the summer, and a profile that still shows last year's prices quietly costs bookings. Ask one person to check it on the first of every month — five minutes is enough.

The third mistake is the empty catalogue. A verified tick beside a profile with no services listed tells a client you are real but gives her nothing to book. Fill in your ten most-requested services with clear prices and realistic durations, and update them when your menu changes.

## A one-week plan to get it done

Day one, gather your trade licence and check your WhatsApp business name matches it exactly. Day two, rewrite your description, hours and address, and add your catalogue with prices. Day three, submit the verification application and update the WhatsApp link on your Instagram bio and Google Business Profile. Days four to seven, answer every new enquiry with the same standard: service, price range, two real slots, and a friendly close.

You will know it worked when you see fewer "is this a real salon?" questions and more clients who arrive already decided. The green tick is not magic, and it will not fix a slow reply or a messy diary. But in a city where the first contact almost always happens on WhatsApp, it is the cheapest trust signal a salon can buy — and the profile behind it is the cheapest salesperson you will ever hire.
