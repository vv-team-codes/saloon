Walk into a salon in Dubai or Abu Dhabi this month and ask what is new, and there is a good chance the answer involves a mask that fizzes. Oxygen facials — often sold as bubble facials — have become one of the most requested glow treatments in UAE salons. The treatment looks theatrical on camera, feels gentle on the skin, and fills the gap between a basic facial and a device-heavy treatment.

## What an oxygen facial actually is

An oxygen facial has two parts. First, a stream of pressurised oxygen pushes a fine mist of serums — usually hyaluronic acid, vitamins and antioxidants — onto the skin. Second, many salons add the part clients film for Instagram: a gel mask that reacts on the skin and forms a layer of tiny bubbles, which gives the treatment its bubble-facial nickname.

The appeal is simple: no downtime, no peeling and no redness to hide, so a client can walk out and go straight to dinner or a wedding. The glow is immediate — skin looks plumper and fresher within the hour — even though the effect lasts a few days rather than weeks.

That honesty matters: an oxygen facial is not a replacement for a chemical peel, microneedling or a laser treatment, and salons that sell it as one end up with disappointed clients. It is a glow treatment, and it should be sold as one.

## Why the UAE is the perfect market for it

Three local factors explain why this treatment is taking off here. The first is the climate: air conditioning, desert air and long days outdoors leave skin feeling dehydrated, and a treatment promising instant hydration in under an hour speaks directly to that feeling.

The second is the event calendar. Dubai and Abu Dhabi run on weddings, brunches and corporate dinners, and clients want to look fresh the same evening. A facial with no recovery time fits neatly into a day that already has a hair appointment in it.

The third is the social media effect. The bubbling mask is made to be filmed: clients post it, tag the salon and do the marketing for you.

## Who it suits — and who it does not

The best candidates are clients with dull, tired or dehydrated skin, and anyone preparing for an event in the next 24 to 72 hours. It also works well as a first facial for a nervous new client, because nothing about it is invasive and there is nothing to recover from.

It is less suitable for clients with active breakouts, open skin or a known sensitivity to a serum ingredient. A short consultation before the first session — five honest questions about skin, allergies and expectations — avoids nearly every complaint. If a client hopes it will fix pigmentation or deep lines, say so kindly and offer the treatment that actually addresses it. That conversation protects your reputation far more than any sale.

## How to price it without undercutting yourself

The most common mistake is pricing a new treatment like a basic facial. An oxygen facial uses a machine, serums and a specialist mask, and takes 45 to 60 minutes of a therapist's time. Price it as a premium glow treatment, not an entry-level facial.

A practical structure is three tiers: a 30-minute express version for a quick pre-event glow, a full 60-minute version with a cleanse, the oxygen infusion and the bubble mask, and a course of four sessions across a month for clients who want the glow to last through wedding season. The course is where the real revenue sits, turning a one-off novelty into a habit.

Resist the temptation to launch with a deep discount: a treatment that starts at half price trains clients to wait for the next offer. A better launch is a free upgrade from a standard facial for your regulars in the first fortnight, so clients try it at full value and come back because they liked it.

## Fitting it into a busy diary

The oxygen facial is kind to a diary: under an hour, no recovery room, one treatment bed, so it slots neatly into the gaps between longer appointments. It works best in two windows — late afternoon, when clients are preparing for the evening, and Saturday mornings, when the weekend's events are still ahead.

It also pairs well with other services: a client in for colour can add a 30-minute express oxygen facial while the colour processes, and a bride's trial can end with one so she leaves glowing. These small combinations lift the average bill without adding a single chair.

## Training and honest marketing

The treatment is easy to learn, but it still needs training: how to hold the oxygen wand, how much serum to use, and when to stop if the skin reacts. A one-day supplier training session plus a week of practice on staff is usually enough. Keep a simple record of what was used on each client, so the next visit starts from knowledge, not guesswork.

When you market it, show the real thing: your own photos and short clips of the bubbling mask on a real client, with her permission, and a plain statement of what it does — instant glow and hydration, no downtime, best within a couple of days of an event. Clients in the UAE are well informed and quick to spot overpromising.

## Is it worth adding to your menu?

An oxygen facial will not transform your salon on its own. What it does is give you a photogenic, event-friendly treatment between your basic facial and your device treatments, attract curious first-time clients, and give regulars a reason to book an extra visit before a big night out. For salons with a steady facial clientele, it is one of the lowest-risk additions you can make.

Start small: train one therapist, offer it for a month, and track how many clients book it, how many rebook, and how many came because they saw it on someone else's phone. If the numbers hold, it earns a permanent place on the menu.

Like any treatment, its success depends on the experience around it — the consultation, the timing, the follow-up. A client who books a glow facial at 6 pm for an 8 pm dinner needs her confirmation, reminder and aftercare note to arrive on time.
