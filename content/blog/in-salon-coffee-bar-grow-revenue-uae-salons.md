Picture a client at 7 pm on a Thursday, colour developing, forty minutes to wait — probably scrolling her phone with a paper cup of water beside her. Two doors down, a client in the same position is sipping a proper flat white, has ordered a second for her friend, and is telling her she should book in too. The difference is a small counter, a decent machine and two square metres of floor — one of the cheapest growth moves a UAE salon can make.

A coffee bar is not about becoming a café. It is about earning from time your clients already spend with you, and turning the people waiting in your salon into future clients.

## Why it works so well in the UAE

Dubai and Abu Dhabi salons have three advantages. First, long services: colour, keratin and bridal prep keep clients in the chair for two or three hours, with waiting built in. A drink turns that dead time into a small pleasure, and small pleasures are what clients remember.

Second, companions. In the UAE, clients routinely arrive with someone — a friend who drove, a husband waiting, a mother along. With a coffee bar and a comfortable corner, they leave having experienced your salon. Many new clients start exactly like this: they came in as someone's plus-one, liked the place, and booked their own appointment the following week.

Third, the climate. For five months of the year, stepping outside means stepping into serious heat, so a cold drink in a cool, calm salon is genuinely appealing — an iced coffee in July is an easier sell than any service on your menu. Mall locations get this doubled: shoppers drifting past a salon that smells of fresh coffee stop and look in.

## What it actually earns

The direct money is modest but real: twenty drinks a day at around AED 15 is AED 300 a day — over AED 8,000 a month — from a machine that cost a few thousand dirhams. The indirect money is bigger. Clients who linger order the add-on they had been undecided about: the blow-dry, the brow tidy, the treatment while the colour sets. A relaxed client says yes more easily than a bored one.

Then there is the companion effect. If one companion a week books a service because they enjoyed waiting in your salon, that is fifty new clients a year, each worth hundreds of dirhams over their lifetime with you. Clients who enjoyed the whole visit also come back sooner and recommend you more warmly. None of this shows up as a line called "coffee" in your accounts, which is why owners underestimate it.

## Start small: the low-cost setup

You do not need a kitchen, a barista or a refit. Start with a bean-to-cup machine on a small counter near reception, a mini fridge, and a menu of five or six items: espresso, flat white, cappuccino, karak, iced coffee and one fresh juice. Any team member can make a good drink in a minute, with no training and no extra hire.

Keep the counter visible but out of the working area — clients should see it from the waiting seats, but nobody carrying a hot cup should walk through a stylist's workspace. Two stools beside it is enough, and the setup fits space most salons already waste: the dead corner by the entrance, or the gap beside the retail shelf.

Price drinks honestly: AED 12 to 18 covers good beans and milk with a healthy margin. Some salons include one drink free with any service over ninety minutes; the second drink — for the companion, or the refill — is where the money is.

## The licence and hygiene basics

Before you buy anything, check the paperwork: serving food and drink is a separate activity from running a salon, and the rules depend on your licence and location — mainland, free zone or mall. Ask your licensing authority or PRO whether a small coffee service needs an added activity or a food-handling permit, and check your lease if you are in a mall. Hygiene is non-negotiable: fresh milk stored properly, a machine cleaned daily, cups washed properly, no food left out in a warm room. Clients notice a dirty coffee corner instantly. Put one team member in charge of the morning clean and the evening shutdown.

## Make it part of the service

Offer a drink at the start of every long appointment, not when the client asks. Name one drink after your signature service, and give regulars a simple loyalty touch: every fifth coffee free.

Launch it on a quiet Tuesday, not a frantic Saturday: the bar is busiest at the 6 to 9 pm peak, but the setup is easiest in the quiet weekday mornings. And when clients message after hours asking whether you have a waiting area, or whether their husband can wait with a coffee while they have their colour done, those questions deserve a fast answer — a 24/7 AI receptionist for salons like ZYNA can answer them the moment they arrive, even at midnight, so the client books instead of moving on.

## The mistakes to avoid

Do not let the coffee bar become the business. If the smell overpowers the salon, if the machine noise interrupts consultations, or if a team member is making drinks when they should be with a client, you have gone too far. The bar serves the salon, not the other way round.

Do not over-invest at the start: a AED 20,000 espresso setup before you know whether your clients even drink coffee is a gamble. Start with a good bean-to-cup machine, prove the demand over a month, then upgrade if the numbers justify it. And do not price drinks like a hotel lobby — a AED 28 coffee in a salon feels like a surcharge, not a treat.

## How to know if it is working

Give it thirty days and track four numbers: drinks sold per day, the average spend of clients who had a drink, companions who booked a service within a month, and clients who mention the coffee bar in a review. If drinks cover the cost of beans, milk and cups — and a few companions convert — the bar has paid for itself.

A salon grows by finding revenue in the hours and spaces it already owns. The coffee corner does exactly that: it monetises waiting time, warms up companions, and gives clients one more reason to choose your chair.
