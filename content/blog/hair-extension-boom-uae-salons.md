Walk into any busy salon in Dubai or Abu Dhabi this wedding season and you will hear the same request at the consultation chair: "Can you make my hair longer — and thicker — before Saturday?" Hair extensions have moved from a niche bridal add-on to one of the most requested services on UAE salon menus. For salon owners, that shift is a genuine opportunity, but only if it is handled with honest advice, realistic pricing and the right diary planning.

## Why extensions are booming right now

Several forces are pulling in the same direction. First, the occasion calendar. Winter wedding season, engagement parties and festive events mean clients want dramatic hair for a single evening, and extensions are the fastest way to get there. Second, everyday life in the UAE is hard on hair. Heat, humidity, hard water and frequent heat styling leave many women with hair that feels thinner than it used to, even when it is healthy.

Then there is what clients see on their phones. Before-and-after reels of volume transformations travel fast, and clients arrive with a screenshot and a clear picture of the result they want. The demand is already there. The work is turning a screenshot into a service the client still loves six weeks later.

## Which methods suit the UAE climate and lifestyle?

Not every extension method suits every client, and a salon that offers one method for everyone will eventually disappoint someone. Tape-in extensions are the most popular starting point in the UAE. They sit flat, suit fine hair, and can be fitted in about an hour, which makes them practical for clients who want volume without losing a whole afternoon. They need repositioning every six to eight weeks — a rhythm to explain at the first appointment, not later.

Keratin-bond extensions suit clients who want the most natural movement and are happy to invest more time and money. Weft extensions work well for thicker hair that wants serious volume, while clip-in sets are the honest answer for someone who only wants length for one event and does not want a commitment.

The method matters less than the match. A good consultation asks about swimming, gym routines, how often the client washes her hair, and whether she ties it up for work. A client who swims three times a week needs different advice from one who only wears her hair down at the weekend.

## The consultation is where the money is made — or lost

Extensions are one of the few salon services where an honest "no" is more valuable than a quick "yes". Hair that is already fragile from over-bleaching may not hold extensions well. A colour match that is one shade off will be visible in daylight, even if it looks fine under salon lighting. A client who expects waist-length hair from a shoulder-length base in one visit needs a realistic conversation about what two or three sessions can achieve.

Build the consultation into the service, not around it. Fifteen minutes with the hair in natural light, a discussion of the client's routine, and a clear explanation of what maintenance will cost over three months. Clients who understand the full picture rarely haggle, because they can see exactly what they are paying for.

## Pricing extensions honestly

Extension pricing confuses clients because it has two parts: the hair itself and the work of fitting it. Be transparent about both. Quote the hair and the application separately, and always quote the maintenance visit at the same time. A client who hears "AED 1,200 for the hair, AED 400 to fit, and AED 250 every six weeks to move them up" can make a real decision. A client who hears only the first number feels misled later.

Resist the temptation to compete on the cheapest quote. Cheap hair tangles, sheds and fades, and the client blames the salon, not the supplier. Mid-range to premium hair that lasts through two or three refits is better value for the client and better for your reputation. Some salons offer a starter package — a shorter set at a lower price — so new clients can try extensions before committing to a full head.

## Fitting extensions into a busy diary

Extensions change the shape of your diary. A full tape-in fitting needs a solid block of time, and maintenance visits need to be easy to book or clients will stretch the gap and damage their own hair. Many salons reserve specific extension slots on weekday mornings, when chairs are quieter, and keep Saturday evenings for the quick services that fill the peak hours.

Training matters too. Extensions should not be a service every stylist offers from day one. One or two trained specialists, with a clear standard for consultation, fitting and aftercare, will deliver more consistent results than a whole team learning on clients.

## Marketing extensions without filters

Extension marketing works best when it is honest. Real before-and-after photos in daylight, shot on a phone, convert better than polished studio images because clients trust what they can imagine on themselves. Show the consultation, the colour match and the finished result — not just the finished result. Short videos of the transformation, with the time and the price range in the caption, answer the two questions every enquiry asks.

Aftercare content is marketing too. A simple reel showing how to brush extensions, or how to tie hair up for the gym, keeps your salon in front of clients between visits and brings them back for maintenance instead of drifting to a cheaper competitor. Extension clients are also your best referral source: a visible transformation gets noticed.

The extension boom is not a passing trend. Clients in the UAE want length, volume and versatility, and they want it from a salon they trust. Salons that consult honestly, price transparently and plan the diary around the service will turn a trend into a steady, high-value part of the business. The ones that rush it will learn the expensive way that one disappointed extension client tells more people than ten happy ones.
