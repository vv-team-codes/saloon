Every UAE salon closes sometimes. A week for Eid. A fortnight while the owner takes a holiday. Ten days for a refit, a deep clean and new chairs. The closure itself is usually planned well. The reopening is where the money is lost.

What happens on the first morning back decides the next month. Open the diary too wide and the team drowns by Wednesday, and the salon looks chaotic just when it should look its best. Open it too cautiously and a quiet first week turns into a quiet second week, because clients who tried to book while you were closed have already gone elsewhere.

The salons that reopen well treat the first week back as a project of its own, planned before the doors shut.

## Start the reopening before you close

The most useful reopening work happens on the last day before the break: spend twenty minutes on the diary and make three lists.

First, the clients who were due during the closure — the regulars whose usual four-week colour, six-week cut or monthly facial falls inside the closed dates. Second, the clients who already have bookings in the first week back. Third, the enquiries that arrived while you were closed, in the order they arrived.

Send the reopening message before you close, not after. One WhatsApp message to the first list, sent on the last working day: the date you reopen, the first three days you are taking bookings, and a direct question — "Shall I hold your usual slot?" Clients who reply yes go straight into the diary, so the first week is already partly booked when the doors open.

## Work the backlog in order, not in a panic

When the salon reopens, the phone and WhatsApp will be busy. The instinct is to answer everything at once and fill every gap as fast as possible. That is how the diary ends up full of the wrong appointments — long services stacked at the same hour and one stylist triple-booked.

Triage the backlog in this order. First, the regulars whose appointments were cancelled by the closure itself. They lost their slot through no fault of theirs, and they should be offered the first pick of the reopened diary, with their usual stylist where possible. Second, the regulars who message in the first two days back. Third, new enquiries and walk-ins.

It helps to give the backlog a shape. Open the first two days back with the diary your regulars know — their usual slots, their usual stylists, normal hours — and keep two or three short slots open each day for walk-ins.

## Don't let the first week be a crush

The temptation after a closure is to book every chair from the first hour, to make up for the lost days. Resist it. A team returning from a break needs a day to find its rhythm — the new stock put away, the chairs checked, the first colour mixed without rushing. A diary that is full by 9 AM on day one leaves no room for the small things that go wrong in a reopened salon: a delivery that arrives late, a client who arrives twenty minutes early, a service that runs over.

A good rule is to book the first day back to about three quarters full, the second day a little fuller, and to reach a normal full diary only by day three or four. The lost revenue on day one is small; the cost of a chaotic first week is much bigger.

The same applies to hours. If the salon normally opens at 10, do not open at 8 on the first day back to catch up. The team will be tired by midday and the evening diary will suffer. Keep normal hours for the first week and let the diary fill at its own pace.

## Tell clients what changed, once

A reopening is also the moment clients notice what is different. New chairs, a new price list, a stylist who has left, a service that has been added. Tell them once, clearly, in the reopening message and again at the chair — and then stop.

If prices have changed while the salon was closed, say so in the reopening message, not at the till. A client who discovers a new price at checkout feels tricked, and the first week back is the worst possible time for that feeling. If a popular stylist has left during the break, tell her regulars before they arrive, and name the stylist who will look after them instead.

## Use the quiet hours, don't fear them

There will be quiet hours in the first week back. A Tuesday at 11, a Wednesday afternoon that never filled. Do not fill them with discounts. Use them for the work that a closed salon creates: the stock count, the client notes that need updating, the diary audit for the month ahead. A quiet hour spent putting the salon in order pays for itself by Friday, when the diary is full again.

And if a quiet hour does need filling, offer it to one person — a regular who has been waiting, or a client whose appointment was cancelled by the closure — with a personal message, not a broadcast. The client who gets a personal "we have a slot on Wednesday at 11 if it helps" feels looked after. The client who gets a group message about empty chairs feels like a number.

## The week after matters more

The real test of a reopening is not the first week but the second. A salon that fills week one with every regular it could reach and then has nothing for week two has simply moved the quiet week, not solved it.

While the first week is running, keep booking forward. Every client who sits in the chair in week one should leave with her next appointment booked — the usual interval, the usual stylist, written down before she reaches the till. That one habit turns a reopened salon back into a salon with a future, instead of a salon that is always chasing the week it just lost.

Close with a plan, reopen in order, and protect the team from a crush on day one. The clients looked after during the closure will fill the diary for the rest of the month.
