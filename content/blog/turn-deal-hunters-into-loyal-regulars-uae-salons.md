Every salon owner in the UAE knows this client. She arrives the week a voucher lands, books the full package at the offer price, then disappears until the next offer — same smile, same package, same discount. She is never rude and she always pays, but she has never booked at full price, and the peak hours she takes may be costing you more than she earns you.

This is the deal-hunter, and in a market as offer-heavy as the UAE, most salons have more of them than they realise. Vouchers, group-buy deals, National Day offers, White Friday bundles — the offers work, and that is exactly the problem. They fill chairs today and quietly train clients to believe your real price is the discounted one. The question is not how to stop offering deals. It is how to turn the clients who arrive through a deal into clients who stay for the salon.

## First, know who you are dealing with

Not every client who uses an offer is a deal-hunter. A regular who books her usual colour and happens to use a birthday voucher is still a regular. A new client who tries you once on a deal and returns at full price was a smart acquisition. The deal-hunter is different, and the pattern is easy to spot once you look for it.

Check your last six months of bookings. The deal-hunter has visited three or more times and has never paid full price. She books the most discounted service on the menu, often in peak evening or Saturday hours. She never rebooks at the chair, ignores follow-ups unless they contain another offer, and rarely asks for a stylist by name — because the stylist is not the point. The deal is.

In a city where every salon runs a promotion, clients have learned to shop this way. Give them a reason to stay that is worth more than the next discount.

## Stop discounting the thing she already likes

The most common mistake is sending a deal-hunter a bigger offer to bring her back. It works once, and it makes the problem worse every time, because each new discount confirms her belief that waiting pays. The goal is the opposite — to move her from price to value.

Never discount the service she already loves. If she came for a discounted balayage, it should never be cheaper next time. Offer something that costs you little and feels personal instead: a complimentary deep-conditioning treatment with her next full-price colour, a priority window for the Saturday slot she always wants, or a free fifteen-minute consultation to plan her hair for the wedding season. These are rewards for booking, not discounts on the service. A reward says you are valued; a discount says the service is worth less.

## Make the second visit about the person, not the price

The moment that decides it is not the offer visit — it is the second one. If she returns at full price once, the pattern breaks, and the next booking is far easier. Everything you do should aim at making that second visit happen, and making it feel different from the first.

This is where personal details matter. Remember what she mentioned: the wedding she was preparing for, the hair she wants by December, the frizz she fights every summer. When you follow up, do not send a voucher. Send a message that shows you were listening. "Your colour should be settling in nicely now — would you like me to hold your usual Thursday slot for a gloss refresh?" That message shows you remember her, gives her a reason to return, and puts the next appointment — not the next offer — first.

## Give her a path, not a pressure sale

Deal-hunters are allergic to pressure. What works instead is a clear, honest path. Tell her what her hair or skin needs over the next three months and what it will cost, in plain numbers. A simple plan — colour now, gloss in six weeks, a trim in between — turns a one-off bargain into a routine.

Some salons formalise this as a "first three visits" journey: visit one is the offer, visit two adds a complimentary treatment, visit three earns a regulars-only perk — priority booking or a preferred stylist guarantee. Every step rewards coming back, not waiting for a cheaper price. By the third visit she has a stylist she trusts and a routine that fits her calendar — a reason to stay no voucher can match.

## Protect your best hours

Saturday afternoons and the 5 pm to 9 pm weekday window are the hardest slots to fill at full price, so they are the worst hours to give away at a discount. Many UAE salons now run offers only in quiet hours — weekday mornings and early afternoons — and keep peak hours for regulars.

It also changes the experience she gets. A quiet Tuesday morning means a calmer visit, a longer consultation, and a stylist with time to talk — exactly what turns a bargain-hunter into a regular. A frantic Saturday evening gives her the service, but never the relationship.

## Measure what matters

Once a quarter, pull a simple list: clients who have visited three or more times in the last six months, and what share of those visits were discounted. If a fifth of your regular bookers have never paid full price, you have a segment worth working on. Track one number: how many make a full-price second visit within ninety days of their first offer visit. That tells you whether your offers are building a business or just renting chairs.

Target one in four of your offer-only clients for a full-price second visit over the next three months. Start with the twenty most recent deal-hunters: send each a personal follow-up mentioning something specific from their visit, and offer a complimentary add-on — not a discount — on their next full-price booking.

The deal-hunter is not a problem client. She is a client who has not yet been given a better reason to come back than a lower price. Give her one — a stylist who remembers her, a plan for her hair, a slot she can count on — and the next time she messages, it will be to ask when you can fit her in.
