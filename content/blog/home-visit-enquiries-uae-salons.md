"Can you come to my home?" It usually arrives on WhatsApp late in the evening, from a client you have never met. She wants a blow-dry before a dinner, makeup for a family gathering, or a manicure she cannot fit into a salon visit. In the UAE, the question comes up more than in most places: villas sit far from the nearest salon, new mothers cannot leave the house easily, elderly clients find the trip tiring, and hotel guests are in Dubai for three days.

How you answer decides whether you gain a booking, a regular, or nothing at all. Many salons lose it in one of two ways: an instant "no, we don't do that", which ends the conversation before it starts, or a vague "maybe, let me check", which turns into a long negotiation and then silence. There is a better middle: a clear answer, three quick questions, and an honest price, all in the first reply.

## Why clients ask for a home visit

Not everyone who wants a home visit wants the same thing. A new mother wants the service without arranging childcare. An elderly client wants it without the journey. A bride getting ready in a hotel room wants the stylist where the morning is happening. For some, a home visit is a preference. For others, it is the only way they can have the service at all.

That distinction matters, because the client who cannot travel is a different conversation from the client who simply prefers her own sofa. Both deserve a real answer, not a reflex no.

## Decide your answer before the message arrives

The worst home-visit replies are invented on the spot, while the client waits. Decide as a team, in advance: do you offer home visits, for which services, in which areas, and at what price?

Write the decision down: the services, the areas you will travel to, the hours you will offer them, the minimum spend, and the call-out fee. If you do not offer home visits at all, decide that too, and decide what you offer instead. A firm "no, but here is what we can do" beats a hesitant "let me check and get back to you" every time, because a client waiting for an answer is already messaging the next salon.

## The reply that keeps the booking

A good first reply does three things: it answers the question, it asks for what you need, and it moves towards a time. If you offer home visits, say so warmly, then ask three questions: which service, which area, and when. "We do offer home visits for blow-dries, makeup and manicures. Which service did you have in mind, and which day works for you?" That is enough.

If you do not offer home visits, say so kindly and offer the real alternative in the same message. "We don't offer home visits, but I can book you in the salon tomorrow at 11am with Sara, and we will have you out in 45 minutes." Keep the reply short, because every extra question is another reason to go quiet. And reply fast: a home-visit enquiry sent at 9pm is usually decided by 10pm, and the salon that answers first usually wins it.

## Price the visit honestly

The common mistake is quoting the salon price and discovering the travel later. A home visit costs more than a salon visit: travel time in both directions, fuel and parking, the kit carried to the door, and the chair left empty while the stylist is on the road. Pretending otherwise is how a home visit quietly loses money.

Use a simple structure the client can understand: salon price, plus a call-out fee, plus a minimum spend. "A blow-dry at home is 180, which includes the visit fee, and our minimum home booking is 150." State it before you confirm the slot, not after. Clients who balk at the fee were never going to pay it, and finding out now saves a no-show later.

## When the answer is no

Most salons cannot send a stylist everywhere, and some cannot send one anywhere. That is fine, as long as the no is handled well. Thank the client, name the reason once, and offer the real alternative. "We keep our stylists in the salon during the evening rush so booked clients are not kept waiting. But I can book you tomorrow at 11am, and we will have you out in 45 minutes." Never write "we don't do home service" and stop there. The client asked because she wants your work, and the salon visit is the same work in a better chair, with better light and every tool within reach.

If she genuinely cannot travel, a trusted referral to a home-service specialist is better than a reluctant yes you will regret. A graceful no keeps the door open. A flat no closes it.

## Safety and kit

If you do offer home visits, a few rules protect your team. Never send a stylist to a first-time address without a confirmed booking, a deposit, and a shared live location. Keep a record of every home visit: client name, number, address, service, time, and who went. Deposits matter more at home than in the salon, because a no-show at a villa costs the travel as well as the empty chair.

Pack a kit checklist so nothing is forgotten. Check what your trade licence covers before you advertise home service, and keep home visits out of the peak diary. A stylist stuck in traffic at 6pm is a stylist missing three evening clients. Home visits belong in the quiet hours, where an empty chair costs you nothing.

## One home visit can become a regular

Treat the first home visit as the start of a relationship, not a one-off job. Leave with the next booking already made: "Same time next week?" Note what she liked, the products used, the parking note, and the gate code. A short WhatsApp message the same evening, "Hope the blow-dry held up for dinner", does more than any discount ever will.

Some home clients eventually come to the salon once they trust you. Some stay home clients for years and become your steadiest income.
