It usually arrives as a short WhatsApp message, sent late at night or during a lunch break: "What's your cancellation policy?" Sometimes it comes with a deposit question attached — "Do I have to pay in advance?" — and sometimes it is the only message a new client sends before deciding whether to book at all.

Salon owners in the UAE often read this as a warning sign. In practice, it is usually the opposite. The clients who ask about your policy in advance are the careful ones. They have been burned before: a deposit lost at another salon, or a booking they could not move when plans changed. They are not planning to cancel. They are checking whether your salon is fair.

## The question behind the question

Nobody asks about a cancellation policy because they enjoy reading terms and conditions. They ask because they want to know three things: will I lose my money, will this be awkward, and will you treat me reasonably if life gets in the way?

In the UAE, that last worry is real. Plans change fast here. A client books a colour appointment on Monday and by Wednesday her husband's work trip has moved, the school has announced a parents' meeting, or a flight has been rescheduled. Treat the policy question as a buying signal, not an objection. The client is already imagining herself in your chair.

## Say the policy in one clear message

The biggest mistake salons make is answering with a wall of rules. A long message listing every condition, exception and deadline reads like a contract, and contracts make people nervous. The second mistake is the opposite: a vague "don't worry, it's fine" that tells the client nothing and leaves the front desk with nothing to stand behind when a dispute actually happens.

The right answer fits in one short message, in plain language: "No problem at all — you can reschedule free of charge up to 24 hours before your appointment. We take a small deposit of AED 50 to hold longer services, and it moves with you if you reschedule in time."

That single message answers the question directly, gives a clear deadline, explains the deposit, and tells the client that rescheduling is easy.

## Explain the deposit as protection, not a penalty

Deposits are where most policy conversations go wrong. Clients hear "deposit" and think "money I might lose." Salon owners say "deposit" and mean "protection against an empty chair." Both are right, which is why the wording matters.

Frame the deposit around what it does for the client. It holds her slot. It guarantees her stylist. "We take a small deposit to reserve your slot and your stylist, so your appointment is guaranteed" lands very differently from "we require a deposit to secure bookings."

Be specific about the amount and what happens to it. A deposit of AED 50 to AED 100 for longer services is easy for most UAE clients to accept when they know it comes off the final bill and moves with them if they reschedule in time. What clients resist is a vague, non-refundable fee with no clear rules. A transferable deposit serves you better: a client who reschedules keeps her money with you, while a client who loses a deposit often loses the salon too.

## Answer "what if I cancel?" — and the pushback

Some clients push further: "But what if something comes up on the day?" Have an honest answer ready, and make it the same answer every member of your team gives: "If something comes up, just message us as early as you can. If we can fill the slot, we will move your deposit to your next visit. We only keep it when a chair sits empty with no notice."

That answer is fair, easy to remember, and gives your team a clear rule to follow. It also asks the client to message you early — exactly the behaviour that protects your diary.

Occasionally a client pushes back on the policy itself: "Other salons don't charge a deposit." The worst response is to argue, or to drop the deposit on the spot, because both tell her the policy was never really a policy. A calm reply works better: "I completely understand — some salons work differently. We keep deposits small and they always come off your bill, because it lets us guarantee your slot and your stylist. Would you like me to hold the 6 pm slot for you?"

## Keep it consistent across the team

A policy only works if everyone in the salon explains it the same way. In many UAE salons, enquiries are answered by different people across the day — the receptionist in the morning, a stylist between clients, the owner in the evening — and the policy changes with each of them. One person says 24 hours, another says 48, a third waives the deposit entirely because she is busy and wants the conversation over.

Write the policy down in three sentences and save it as a quick reply on your salon's WhatsApp. Every team member uses the same wording, every time. It takes ten minutes to set up and it ends the most common source of front-desk disputes: the client who was told one thing by one person and another thing by someone else.

## The close: from policy to booking

The goal of a policy conversation is not to win an argument about terms. It is to get the client into your chair. So every policy answer should end with a booking step, not with the policy itself.

After you have answered the question, offer the next move: "Shall I book you in for Thursday at 6 pm with Sara? I can send you the deposit link now and it will be confirmed straight away." A client who has just been reassured is ready to say yes. A client left with a policy and no next step will think about it — and usually book somewhere else, or not at all.

Handled well, the cancellation question becomes one of your best conversion moments. The client arrived cautious and leaves feeling looked after, knowing the rules are fair and that your salon will treat her reasonably if her plans change.
