Your next client is scrolling Instagram at 9 pm when a photo of a glossy balayage stops her thumb. She taps — and instead of a website she has to navigate, a WhatsApp chat opens with your salon, the service already named: "Hi! I'd like to book this balayage — when are you free this week?"

That tap is the whole idea. The ad runs on Facebook or Instagram, and the call to action opens a WhatsApp conversation instead of a website — removing the step where most salon advertising quietly fails: the landing page.

## Why the tap beats the website

When an ad links to a website, the client taps, waits for the page to load, hunts for prices, fills in a form, and waits for a callback. Every step loses people.

A click-to-WhatsApp ad skips all of that: no form, no account, no password reset. She types one message and she is talking to your salon. For impulse bookings — a blow-dry before dinner, a manicure before the weekend — that low friction turns a view into a booking.

## Start with one service, not your whole menu

The most common mistake is promoting everything at once. An ad that lists hair, nails, facials, waxing and bridal gives the client nothing specific to want, and gives you no way to know what worked.

Pick one service with a clear result and a clear price range — a balayage with a starting price, a gel manicure with a photo of real work, a 30-minute express facial. Run the ad for that service only, with one strong photo or short video of the actual result.

One service also makes the conversation easy. When the client taps, the pre-filled message can name the service: "Hi, I'm interested in the gel manicure — how much and when can I come?" Your team knows exactly what to answer. Start with the service that already sells well and photographs well.

## The opening message does half the selling

When a client taps the ad, WhatsApp opens with a pre-written message she can send as it is. A blank chat box makes people hesitate; a ready message makes starting easy.

Write it as the client would write it: "Hi! I saw your ad for the balayage — could I book this week?" That tells your team what she wants; a vague "please share details" starts a slow back-and-forth that often dies.

On your side, prepare the first reply in advance. It should answer the three questions every ad clicker has: how much, when can I come, and how long does it take. "Hi, thanks for messaging! Balayage starts at AED 450 and takes about three hours. We have Thursday at 5 pm or Saturday at 11 am — would either work for you?"

## Set the budget like a test, not a gamble

You do not need a big budget to learn whether these ads work. Start small — enough to reach a few thousand people in your area over a week — and watch cost per booked appointment, not cost per click.

Clicks are cheap and misleading: a hundred taps that produce two bookings cost more than twenty that produce five. Tag each WhatsApp booking to its source, and at week's end divide what you spent by the appointments that actually sat in the chair. If a AED 300 week brought six bookings, each cost AED 50 to win — and if those clients spent an average of AED 250, the ad paid for itself several times over.

Targeting matters more than budget. Limit the ad to the area your clients travel from — your neighbourhood and the mall catchment if you are in one. One day of data tells you nothing; seven days shows whether the service, the photo, and the price are pulling their weight.

## Answer fast, or the ad spend is wasted

Here is the uncomfortable truth: these ads make your reply speed part of your advertising. A client who taps at 10 pm and hears nothing until noon has usually booked elsewhere. The money you spent to win that tap is gone.

This is where most salons quietly lose: the ad runs around the clock, but the team answers only during opening hours. If your salon closes at 9 pm, the evening scroll arrives when nobody is watching the phone.

You need a plan for after-hours replies: a team member checking messages in the evening, or an automated greeting that answers the three basic questions and offers two real slots for the next day. It does not need to be clever — just useful: the price range, the duration, and two available times. ## The follow-up that turns one visit into a regular

A click-to-WhatsApp ad wins a first visit; the follow-up wins the second. The day after the appointment, send a short personal message: how did she like the result, and would she like the same slot next month?

Keep a simple record of which clients came from ads and what they booked. After a month, the pattern shows which service brings the best clients and which price converts.

One caution: do not message everyone who ever tapped an ad with weekly offers. A tap is permission to answer, not permission to broadcast. A stream of promotions is how a salon gets muted — and a muted number is a lost client.

## A simple first-week plan

On day one, choose your service, your best photo, and your starting price. Write the pre-filled message and the first reply, and agree who answers the chats. On day two, launch with a small daily budget and a tight local radius. Then answer every message within minutes, log every booking, and resist changing the photo or price after two days.

At the end of the week, look at the cost per booked appointment. If it works, raise the budget a little. If it does not, change one thing — the photo, the price, or the opening reply — and test again. WhatsApp gives you the answers faster than any other channel.

Your clients are already on WhatsApp, already scrolling, and already choosing salons by message. A click-to-WhatsApp ad meets them there and removes the steps between seeing your work and sitting in your chair.
