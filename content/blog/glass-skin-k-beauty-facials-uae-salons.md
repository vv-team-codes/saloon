If you run a salon in Dubai or Abu Dhabi, you have probably heard it by now. A client settles into the chair, opens her phone, and shows you a photo of skin that looks almost lit from within — smooth, dewy, poreless, with a soft shine that seems to come from underneath rather than from highlighter. "Can you do this?" she asks. She is asking for glass skin.

## What "glass skin" actually means

Glass skin is not a single product or machine. It is a result: skin that is deeply hydrated, gently exfoliated, and even in tone, so light bounces off it cleanly. The Korean approach is layered and patient. Rather than one aggressive treatment, it stacks several gentle steps — double cleansing, mild exfoliation, hydrating essences, sheet masks, and light serums — so moisture builds up in the skin instead of sitting on top of it.

Clients often think glass skin is a finish you apply, like a coat of polish. In reality it is a condition you build, and a salon that explains this in the first consultation gains a client far more likely to book the second and third session, where the visible change actually happens.

## Why the trend fits the UAE so well

The UAE climate is part of the reason glass skin has taken hold here. Between the dry desert air, the air conditioning that runs most of the year, and the sun, many clients live with skin that is permanently a little dehydrated, and foundation can look dry by mid-afternoon.

There is also a cultural fit. Korean skincare already has a strong following among the Emirates' expat communities and Emirati clients alike, and Korean beauty counters in Dubai's malls have done the groundwork: clients already know the language of essences, ampoules, and sheet masks. When a salon offers a glass-skin facial, it is offering a professional version of something the client already believes in.

The timing helps too. With December party season and the winter wedding calendar filling diaries, clients want a treatment that makes skin look its best for photographs and events — not a week later, but now.

## What a glass-skin facial involves

A typical glass-skin facial runs 60 to 75 minutes. It starts with a double cleanse — an oil-based cleanser followed by a gentle foam — to remove makeup, sunscreen, and the day's dust. Then comes a mild exfoliation, often an enzyme peel or a light lactic acid step, to smooth the surface without the redness of a stronger peel.

The middle of the treatment is where the hydration happens. A hydrating essence or ampoule is pressed into the skin, often with a cool facial device or a gentle massage. Many salons add a sheet mask, sometimes with a hydrating serum underneath, and finish with a light moisturiser and SPF.

## Honest expectations: what to tell clients

This is where salons earn trust — or lose it. One glass-skin facial will leave skin looking plumper and more luminous for a day or two. It will not permanently change skin texture, erase pigmentation, or replace a skincare routine. Clients with acne, rosacea, or very sensitive skin need a conversation first, and sometimes a patch test, because layering active products can irritate skin that is already reactive.

The honest pitch is this: a glass-skin facial is the start of a skin-health habit, not a one-off fix. Most salons recommend a course of three or four sessions, spaced two weeks apart, followed by a monthly maintenance facial.

## Pricing it so it pays

Glass-skin facials in the UAE typically sit in the mid-range of a salon's facial menu — above a basic express facial, below a high-tech device treatment. A common range is AED 250 to AED 450 for a single 60-minute session, with a course of four priced at a modest reduction, usually 10 to 15 percent. The product cost per treatment is low compared with machine-led facials, and the diary cost is modest: a little over an hour, with no downtime.

The smart move is to price the course, not just the session. A client who buys a course of four is four booked chairs, four chances to recommend home care, and four opportunities to build a habit that brings her back every month.

## Fitting it into the diary

A glass-skin facial is a flexible diary filler. It suits the quieter weekday hours, where a 60-minute treatment can be offered at a gentle off-peak rate, and it suits the peak evening hours too, because clients booking before a dinner or a weekend event want exactly this kind of glow. It also pairs well with other services: a client having a blow-dry or a manicure can add a glass-skin facial in the same visit, turning one chair into a longer, higher-value booking.

For salons with limited treatment rooms, the facial is forgiving. It needs a comfortable bed, good light, and a calm corner — not a dedicated machine room.

## Marketing it without filters

The temptation with any glow treatment is to post heavily filtered photos. Resist it. Clients in the UAE are increasingly sceptical of filtered results, and a filtered before-and-after is the fastest way to lose a booking when the real result does not match. Use real photos, in daylight, with the client's written permission.

Short videos work well: a 20-second clip of the sheet-mask step, a close-up of skin after the final essence, a therapist explaining one step in plain language. Post these on Instagram and WhatsApp Status, and make sure every post ends with a booking path — a WhatsApp link or a booking button — because interest fades fast.

## How to start, in one week

Adding a glass-skin facial does not need a grand launch. Pick one therapist to train first, agree the protocol in writing, and run the treatment on three or four regular clients at an introductory price in exchange for honest feedback and a photo. Watch what they say: if they rebook, you have a service. If they ask when they can come back, you have a signature.

Then put it on the menu properly — a clear name, a plain description, a price, and a duration — and tell your existing clients about it in a short WhatsApp message. No discount spiral, no hype. Just a good treatment, explained honestly, offered at the right moment.
