It is 6pm on a Thursday in Dubai. The evening diary is nearly full, three clients are mid-conversation about Saturday slots, and your phone shows one grey tick after another. Messages are not sending. WhatsApp is down.

For most UAE salons, WhatsApp is the booking desk, the receptionist and the diary's front door. When it stops working, the damage is the hour of silence that follows, while clients assume you are ignoring them and book somewhere else.

A salon with a plan barely notices the outage. Here is how to be the first kind.

## First, check it is not just you

Before you do anything else, confirm the problem is real. Ask a colleague to send a message, or try from a different phone. Check your Wi-Fi by opening another app; if the salon Wi-Fi is the problem, switch to mobile data and test again.

If WhatsApp is genuinely down, stop resending the same message — duplicates will flood the client's phone when the service returns. One calm message, sent once, is enough.

## Switch clients to a channel that still works

The first job is to give clients somewhere else to reach you. In the UAE, that usually means the phone itself. Calls and SMS still work when WhatsApp does not, and clients understand a short text that says the app is down and the salon is still open.

Send a brief SMS to anyone with a booking in the next 48 hours, starting with the ones you were mid-conversation with: WhatsApp is down, here is the number to call or text, your booking stands. Three sentences are enough. They need to know their appointment is safe and how to reach you.

Instagram direct messages are your second backup, because many UAE salon clients already follow you there. Post a story saying WhatsApp is down, bookings are still being taken by phone, and the salon number.

Everyone on the team should know which channel is the main backup. If the front desk answers calls while a stylist answers Instagram, two clients can be promised the same chair. Pick one person to own the backup channel for the shift.

## Protect the diary before anything else

The real risk of an outage is not a missed message. It is a lost booking. When WhatsApp stops, your team can no longer check the shared diary from their phones, and a stylist may promise a slot that is already taken.

Print the day's diary every morning, or keep a paper copy at the front desk updated at the start of each shift. When the app goes down, the paper diary becomes the single source of truth. No booking is made unless it is written on that page.

For the rest of the evening, take bookings in pencil: name, service, stylist and phone number, and tell the client you will confirm on WhatsApp once it is back. When the app returns, the first job is to transfer every pencil note into the diary and the client's chat.

## What to say, and what never to say

Clients are forgiving about an outage they understand. They are not forgiving about silence, or about a message that sounds like an excuse. The best message is honest, short and useful: WhatsApp is down, here is how to reach us, your booking is safe.

Never blame the client, never promise a reply "in five minutes" when you do not know when the service will return, and never use the outage to push a promotion. A client who cannot reach you is worried, not shopping.

If a client is upset, acknowledge it once and then solve the problem. Most complaints disappear the moment the client has a confirmed booking in hand.

## When WhatsApp comes back

The recovery matters as much as the outage. The moment messages start sending again, do not simply carry on as normal. Work through the chats in order: first the people with bookings in the next 48 hours, then the people who were mid-conversation, then everyone else.

Every reply should do three things: confirm the booking or the answer, apologise briefly for the delay, and give the client nothing else to do. "Your Saturday 11am balayage with Sara is confirmed — sorry for the delay while WhatsApp was down. See you then."

Then post one story saying WhatsApp is back, and check the diary against the paper copy. If a pencil booking conflicts with something taken during the outage, call the client yourself and fix it before they arrive.

## The 30-minute plan that makes the next outage boring

Outages are rare, but they are certain. A short preparation session, done once, turns the next one from a crisis into a quiet half hour.

Keep a second way to reach every regular. A phone number is enough — it lets you send an SMS when WhatsApp fails. If your client records only hold a WhatsApp number, you have no backup at all.

Print the diary every morning and keep it at the front desk. It is the only backup that works when every screen in the salon is useless.

Write a one-page outage script and pin it near the front desk: who owns the backup channel, what the SMS says, and the order in which chats get answered when the app returns. A script means nobody has to think clearly on a stressful evening.

Back up WhatsApp itself. Turn on chat backup in the app settings, and check once a month that it is actually running. It will not help during an outage, but it saves your client history if a phone is lost or replaced.

Decide in advance what your backup channel is. If the answer is "call us", put the salon number on your Instagram bio, your Google listing and your WhatsApp business profile, so a client who cannot message you can find it in seconds.

A salon that keeps taking bookings while WhatsApp is down earns something no promotion can buy: clients who trust the salon will look after them even when things go wrong. That trust turns a one-time visitor into a regular, and a regular into your best advert.
