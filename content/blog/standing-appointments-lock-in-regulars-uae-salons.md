Every UAE salon owner knows the client. She comes every Thursday at 6 pm for a blow-dry, or every second Saturday for colour. She never books far ahead, because she never needs to — until one week she messages at 5 pm and the diary is full. She tries another salon that evening, likes it well enough, and quietly never comes back. Nobody did anything wrong. There was simply no slot held for her, and in a market as competitive as Dubai's, that is all it takes.

Standing appointments fix exactly this. A standing appointment is a recurring slot reserved for one client: the same service, the same stylist, the same day and time, every week or every fortnight, until the client says otherwise. It is one of the simplest retention tools a salon can run, and it works because it removes the two things that slowly push regulars away: friction and uncertainty.

## Why regulars drift without one

Most salon clients do not leave after a bad experience. They leave after a series of small inconveniences. The slot they want is taken. Their stylist is on leave and nobody tells them in advance. They forget to book, remember on the day, and the diary is full. Each time, the salon asks the client to do the work of staying loyal: remember, message, negotiate, wait for a reply.

A standing appointment flips that around. The salon does the remembering. The client simply shows up. For the salon, it turns a variable stream of bookings into a predictable spine of revenue, especially in the busy evening hours between 5 and 9 pm when the diary fills first and the walk-in pressure is highest.

## Who to offer it to first

Start small. Offer standing appointments to your twenty most reliable clients: the ones who already come every week or fortnight, who rarely cancel, and who ask for the same stylist. These are the clients who will say yes immediately, and their slots become the anchor of the diary.

Do not offer it to everyone at once. A standing slot is a promise. Begin with your regulars, prove the routine works for a month, then extend it to clients who have visited three or four times and show the same pattern. The offer itself is a compliment — it tells a client you have noticed her loyalty and want to protect it.

## How to set it up without breaking the diary

A standing appointment only works if the diary can hold it honestly. Book the slot as a recurring entry, not as a series of individual bookings made week by week. That way it survives staff changes, holiday rosters and the Monday-morning rush to fill the week.

Protect the peak hours first. Standing slots should sit in the times clients actually want — Thursday and Friday evenings, Saturday mornings — because that is where the competition for chairs is fiercest. Fill the quiet weekday hours with one-off bookings, promotions and new-client consultations, and let the standing slots anchor the hours that already sell themselves.

Build in a simple rule for changes. A client can move a standing slot once without losing it; if she moves it twice in a row, the slot opens up and she rebooks like anyone else. The point is reliability, not punishment, so explain the rule warmly when you offer the slot: "This time is yours every week. If you need to move it, just tell us by the day before and we will find you another."

## Handling holidays, leave and the quiet months

Standing appointments need a plan for the weeks when life gets in the way. Ramadan, school holidays and the summer months when many UAE residents travel will break the pattern, and that is fine — as long as the salon manages the break instead of letting it happen silently.

When a regular tells you she is travelling, note the return date and offer to hold the slot until she is back, or to restart it on her first week home. When her stylist is on annual leave, message her a week ahead with a named alternative and the option to wait. The message matters more than the solution: clients forgive a changed stylist, but they do not forgive being told at the door.

A short WhatsApp message the day before each standing appointment is enough to keep the rhythm. It is not a reminder so much as a confirmation of something already agreed — "See you tomorrow at 6, same as always" — and it gives the client an easy chance to move the slot before the day fills up. ## The quiet commercial upside

Standing appointments do more than keep clients. They make the business easier to run. When a third of the week's diary is already filled by standing slots, the team knows its baseline revenue before Monday begins. Stylists know their regulars' preferences without checking notes every time. Product ordering gets simpler, because the same services repeat on the same days.

They also create natural moments for honest growth. A client with a standing fortnightly blow-dry is the easiest person in the world to offer a treatment add-on to, because the trust is already there. A client whose standing slot is Thursday at 6 pm will notice if you ever need to move it — and that is precisely the point. She notices because the slot matters to her. That is loyalty you can measure in a diary, not just in a survey.

## A 30-day way to start

Week one, list your twenty most regular clients and note their usual day, time and stylist. Week two, offer the standing slot to each of them in person or by WhatsApp, one at a time, with the simple change rule explained. Week three, move their bookings into recurring diary entries and set up the day-before confirmation message. Week four, count how many accepted, how many showed up, and how many evening slots are now filled before the week even starts.

If half of them accept, you have ten clients who will never again need to remember to book — and ten chairs that are already earning before the week begins. That is what retention looks like when it is built into the diary instead of bolted on afterwards.
