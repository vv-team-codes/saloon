A client finishes her third facial in two months and asks, almost as an afterthought, "Do you have a package for this?" That question is worth thousands of dirhams over the next year — yet in many UAE salons it gets a tired answer: a price list PDF dropped into the chat, then silence. She meant to think about it. She never did.

Packages are not sold by a price list. They are sold in a conversation, and in the UAE that conversation almost always happens on WhatsApp. A salon that sells a package properly in chat turns a one-off facial or laser session into six booked visits, paid for in advance.

## Why packages sell better in a chat than on a price list

A price list answers one question: how much? A package sale must answer four: what am I getting, why is it better than paying session by session, how do I pay, and when do I come in? A PDF cannot ask which of those worries her most. A chat can.

WhatsApp also fits how UAE clients decide. Many message in the evening, after work, when the salon phone is unanswered. They compare options on their phone, show a friend the screenshots, and reply the next morning. An offer that lives only on a printed menu misses all of that. 
## Pick three packages, not thirteen

The first mistake is putting every service into a package. A page of bundles confuses the client and exhausts the team. Pick three that already sell well singly and clients naturally repeat: a facial course, a laser course, and one hair or nail ritual. Give each a plain name and one honest price.

Six sessions is the sweet spot: long enough to feel like a commitment, short enough to picture the end of it. State the per-session price beside the package price so the saving is obvious, and state the validity period in the same message — nine months for a six-session facial course is fair in Dubai, where summer travel interrupts routines. 
## The five-message package conversation

First, the recommendation. After a session, or when a client asks about a price, name the package that fits her: "For the glow you want before your sister's wedding, the six-session hydra facial course is the one I'd suggest." 
Second, the contents. One message, plain language: six sessions, what each includes, spacing, and total price. No jargon, no asterisks.

Third, the honest comparison. Tell her what she saves against six single sessions, and what she does not get — it is not a licence to demand the senior stylist every time or swap sessions for other services. Clients respect limits stated upfront.

Fourth, the payment. This is where most package sales die. Many clients love the idea and stall at a lump sum of several thousand dirhams. Offer to split it: half now, half at the third session, or three monthly instalments, with the dates written in the same chat so there is no awkward chasing later. 
Fifth, the booking. Do not end with "let me know". End with two concrete dates and ask her to pick one. 
## Book the next sessions before she leaves

The salons that get the most from packages do not sell six sessions and hope the client rebooks. They book the next two or three before she leaves the chair, and send the rest on WhatsApp the same evening. In Dubai this matters more than anywhere: a client who travels for six weeks in summer will drift away if her next visit is undefined.

Space facials three to four weeks apart and laser four to six, and leave the last session unbooked so she can place it once her travel dates are set. Confirm each next date, with a reminder the evening before. 
## The reminder that protects the package

Packages quietly fail in one way: the client uses three sessions, life gets busy, and the rest are forgotten until the package is about to expire. A short WhatsApp check-in at the halfway point prevents that. Not a sales message — a service message: "You have three sessions left on your facial course, running until March. Shall I hold your usual Thursday evening slot next week?"

It reminds her of value she has already paid for, gives her a specific next step, and shows the salon is keeping track so she does not have to. Send it once, and if she does not reply, one gentle follow-up a fortnight later. After that, leave her alone. Chasing a client about sessions she has already paid for feels like pressure, and pressure turns a loyal client into a lost one.

## What never to do in a package chat

Never use a package to rescue a quiet week by blasting it to your whole contact list. An offer sent to someone who had one haircut eight months ago is not marketing; it is spam, and the fastest way to get your number reported. Packages are offered one-to-one, to clients who already buy the service, when they are thinking about it.

Never invent urgency. "Only two packages left at this price" is not true in a salon, and Dubai clients have heard it too many times to believe it. If a price is genuinely changing — say a supplier increase in January — say so plainly, with the date. 
And never let a package enquiry sit unanswered overnight. A client asking at 10 PM is ready to buy; by 10 AM she has often talked herself out of it. ## A simple first-week plan

You do not need new software to start. Write one short message for each of your three packages: what it includes, the price, the saving, and the instalment option. Save them as quick replies so the whole team sends the same honest version. Then, for one week, offer the matching package to every client booking a repeatable service for the second time. Track two numbers only: offers made, and packages sold.

A package is not a discount trick. It is a promise: she commits to coming back, and the salon commits to looking after her across six visits — one of the steadiest sources of income a UAE salon can build.
