December in a UAE salon is a wonderful problem. The diary is full from the first week, party-season bookings stack up through the evenings, gift vouchers fly off the shelf, and the till looks healthier than it has all year. Then January arrives. The phones go quiet, the chairs sit empty on weekday mornings, and the same rent, salaries and supplier bills land on the desk as if nothing changed.

This is the rhythm every salon owner in Dubai and Abu Dhabi knows: a few months of plenty, then a few months of scraping by. The salons that survive it are rarely the ones that earned the most in December. They are the ones that kept some of December's money for January.

## The quiet months are not a surprise

A salon's year in the UAE has a shape you can almost set your watch by. December brings party season and the year-end rush. The weeks before Eid, and the month of Ramadan, bring another wave of demand. Summer is slow as families travel, January is the quietest month of all, and the weeks after Eid can dip sharply once the celebrations end.

None of this is new information. Every owner who has run a salon for two years can sketch this calendar from memory. Yet many still run the business as if the busy months will last forever, spending peak-season takings on new equipment and extra stock, and then discovering in January that the money for rent is already gone. The fix is a habit: put money aside while it is coming in, so the quiet months are paid for in advance.

## Decide the cushion before the rush starts

A cash cushion is simply money set aside that you do not spend, held for the months when takings fall. A useful target is one full month of fixed costs — rent, salaries, utilities and licences — sitting untouched in a separate account.

Add up your monthly fixed costs, the bills that arrive whether you have one client or a hundred. If that total is AED 40,000, your cushion target is AED 40,000. That figure becomes the goal for the busy season, not an afterthought for whatever is left at the end of the month — because whatever is left at the end of the month is usually nothing. Set the target now, before the December rush peaks, and keep it where you can see it every day.

## Skim a fixed share off every busy week

The owners who actually build a cushion do it the same way: a fixed percentage of every week's takings moves into a separate account the moment it arrives, before anything else is paid.

Ten per cent is a good starting point. On a week where the salon takes AED 25,000, AED 2,500 goes straight into the cushion account. It is not a reward for a good week and it is not spare money — it is a bill, paid to your future self, and it comes first.

Keeping it in a separate account matters more than it sounds. Money sitting in the main business account gets spent — on an extra delivery of colour, a new trolley, a last-minute decoration order. Money in a different account, one you do not check daily and do not carry a card for, stays put. Most UAE banks let you open a second business savings account in minutes.

Some owners skim more during the biggest weeks — fifteen or twenty per cent during the December party rush and the pre-Eid fortnight builds the cushion fast. The rule stays the same either way: the percentage is fixed in advance, and it moves the same day the money arrives.

## Protect the cushion from the usual leaks

A cushion only works if it survives until the quiet months. Three leaks drain it early, and all three are avoidable.

The first is deposits. When a client pays a deposit for a December appointment, that money is not income yet — it is a promise, and it may need to be returned. Keep deposits in the main account and never count them as takings when you work out your weekly skim.

The second is the equipment trap. December is when suppliers run their best offers, so it is tempting to buy the new chair or the new steamer with peak-season money. If the purchase genuinely earns its keep, buy it — but pay for it from a planned equipment budget, not from the cushion. A cushion spent on equipment is gone when January's rent arrives.

The third is the owner's own pay. Many owners take whatever is left at the end of a busy month as salary, which means they take too much in December and nothing in January. Set a fixed monthly salary for yourself — the same amount every month, good or bad — and let the cushion do the smoothing.

## Use the cushion on purpose, not in a panic

When the quiet month arrives, the cushion has a job: it pays the fixed costs so the salon stays calm, the team stays paid on time, and nobody has to make a desperate decision.

That is the moment to spend it deliberately. Keep the salon running at full quality through the quiet weeks. Clients notice when a salon tightens up in January — fewer staff on the floor, shorter hours, tired-looking shelves — and the ones who drift away in the slow months rarely come back. A salon that stays generous and well-staffed through a quiet month keeps its regulars, and its regulars are the reason the next busy season is busy.

## Start with this December

You do not need new software, a new accountant or a new booking system to do this. You need a number, a percentage and a separate account. Work out one month of fixed costs tonight. Set the skim at ten per cent. Move the first transfer after your next busy weekend.

By the time January arrives, you will know something most salon owners do not: exactly how much of the quiet month is already paid for. That is the difference between a salon that dreads the slow season and one that plans for it — and planning for it is how a salon grows, year after year, in the UAE.
