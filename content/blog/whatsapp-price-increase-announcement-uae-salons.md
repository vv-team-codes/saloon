Every salon owner in the UAE reaches the same moment eventually. Rents climb, product costs rise, and the team deserves a better wage — yet the price list has stayed the same for two years. You know the increase is fair. The hard part is telling your clients, and in a WhatsApp-first market like the UAE, that conversation happens in a chat window, not across the reception desk.

A price rise announced badly costs you regulars. Announced well, most clients accept it in a single message and book their next visit on the spot. The difference is timing, wording and honesty.

## Tell them before they find out at the till

The single biggest mistake is letting a client discover the new price when she is paying. Nobody likes a surprise at checkout, and in a busy Dubai or Abu Dhabi salon the awkwardness is worse — she is standing at the desk, her hair is done, and she feels cornered. That is how a loyal regular decides to try the salon down the road.

Send the message before the new prices start, ideally two to three weeks ahead. That gives clients time to absorb it, ask a question, and book one last visit at the old price.

Use the same lead time for everyone. Regulars should never hear about the increase from another client, or from a WhatsApp Status, before they get their own message.

## One message, three parts

A good price-increase message is short. Clients read WhatsApp in seconds, between meetings or in a taxi, so it needs to work on one screen. Build it from three parts: the reason, the change, and the reassurance.

Start with the reason, in plain language. Not a lecture about the economy — one honest sentence. "Over the past year our product and rent costs have risen sharply, and we want to keep the quality of your service exactly as it is."

Then state the change clearly. Give the new price for the services they actually book. "From 1 December, a cut and blow-dry will be AED 180, and balayage starts from AED 650." Vague phrases like "prices will be adjusted" invite suspicion. A number is honest, and honesty is what keeps trust.

Finish with reassurance: the same stylist, the same products, the same care. If you are holding the price on one popular service for regulars, say so.

## Personalise it for your regulars

A broadcast-style message sent to every client feels like a bill. Your regulars deserve better, and WhatsApp makes personalisation easy. Start with the client's name and the service she actually books. "Hi Aisha, thank you for trusting us with your colour for the past two years" lands very differently from "Dear valued customer."

For your most loyal clients, consider a voice note instead of text. A thirty-second voice message from the owner turns a business announcement into a personal conversation — the closest thing to telling her face to face.

If a client has been with you since you opened, offer a grace period. "Your current price is held for you until the end of January" costs you very little and tells her, unmistakably, that loyalty goes both ways.

## Expect replies — and answer them well

Some clients will reply straight away. Most will say something kind. A few will push back, and how you handle those replies decides whether the price rise costs you anyone at all.

The most common pushback is a version of "but I have been coming for years." That is not a negotiation; it is a client asking to be valued. Reply with warmth and a fact: "Thank you for being with us all this time — that is exactly why we wanted to tell you personally first. Your cut with Sara stays the same service, and we have held the blow-dry price for our regulars." Do not apologise for the increase, and do not offer a discount in the same breath. An apology suggests you did something wrong, and a discount teaches clients that complaining works.

Reply fast. A price message sent on a Sunday evening will get replies on Sunday evening. ## Use the announcement to fill the diary

A price-rise message is also a booking opportunity. End it with a simple invitation: "If you would like one more visit at your current price, we have slots on Thursday and Saturday this week." Clients who were drifting suddenly have a reason to book now, and that one line can fill quiet weekday chairs.

You can also offer regulars the chance to pre-book their next three visits at the current price. This is not a discount — it is a reward for committing, and it locks in three more visits from clients you most want to keep.

Timing matters too. Send the announcement in the early evening, when clients are scrolling after work — not on a Friday afternoon, when it will sit unread all weekend.

## What not to do

Do not hide the increase in a long message about other things. Clients will find it, and the feeling of being tricked is worse than the price itself. Do not blame your landlord, your supplier or the economy in a long complaint — one sentence of reason is enough. And do not raise prices and change something else at the same time, like removing a service or cutting opening hours. One change at a time lets clients adjust; two at once feels like a different salon.

Most importantly, do not skip the announcement altogether and hope nobody notices. They will notice, and the client who notices at the till tells her friends. The client who gets a warm, honest WhatsApp message tells her friends too — but a very different story.

## The message that keeps them

A price increase is a test of the relationship you have built with your clients, and in the UAE that relationship lives on WhatsApp. A short, honest, personal message — sent early, with real numbers and a real reason — tells your clients that you respect them enough to be straight with them. Most will accept it, and the ones who book one last visit at the old price have just told you they are staying.
