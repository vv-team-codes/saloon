Every salon owner in the UAE knows the pattern. For most of the year, the diary fills in a familiar rhythm: weekday afternoons are quiet, evenings are busy, and weekends are a scramble. Then Ramadan arrives, and the rhythm changes completely. Days slow down. Evenings come alive. And in the final week before Eid, the salon becomes the busiest place in the neighbourhood.

It is the biggest beauty rush of the year in the UAE, and it rewards the salons that planned for it months ahead.

## Why Ramadan changes the salon calendar

Ramadan reshapes daily life across the Emirates. Working hours shorten, families gather after iftar, and social evenings stretch late into the night. For salons, that means the traditional 10am to 7pm day stops matching when clients are actually free.

The busiest hours shift to after iftar, often from 8pm onwards, and the last few nights before Eid can be the single most profitable stretch of the year. Hair colour, blowouts, henna, facials, manicures, threading — everything a client wants to look her best for Eid gets booked in a compressed window, and many clients book two or three services in one visit.

Salons that wait until Ramadan starts to think about this are already behind.

## Start with the diary, not the decorations

The most useful Ramadan planning starts three to four months ahead, with the diary itself. Look at last year's bookings if you have them. Which nights filled first? Which services spiked in the final week? How many clients asked for late slots you could not offer?

Use those answers to shape this year's plan. If your last slot was 8pm and clients kept asking for 10pm, add a late shift in the final ten days. If colour appointments clogged the diary while quick services waited, block specific chairs for express work so the diary does not jam.

Many salons also open bookings for the final week much earlier than usual — sometimes six weeks ahead — and take a deposit for Eid-week appointments. Clients understand this. A deposit protects the chair, and it tells the client the slot is genuinely held for her.

## Staffing is the real bottleneck

The diary is only as good as the team working it. Ramadan hours are long and late, and tired stylists make mistakes, get sick, or quietly burn out before Eid even arrives.

Plan the rota as carefully as the diary. Stagger start times so the salon is fully staffed for the evening peak without everyone working a fourteen-hour day. Give your strongest stylists the busiest nights, and protect at least one rest day a week for each person, even in the final week.

Talk to the team early about Ramadan expectations: later finishes, shorter breaks during peak hours, and how overtime will be handled. If you need extra hands, hire them now, not in the last ten days — a freelancer who has never worked your chairs will slow the team down precisely when speed matters most.

## Packages that fit the season

Ramadan is also a packaging opportunity, because clients think in complete looks rather than single services. A pre-Eid package that bundles hair, nails and a facial into one visit at a clear price is easier to sell than three separate bookings, and it fills more chair time per client.

Keep packages simple. Three options are plenty: a quick refresh, a full Eid-ready package, and a premium option with a senior stylist. Price them honestly and state what is included and how long the visit takes. Clients booking at 10pm want certainty, not surprises.

## Tell clients before they ask

The salons that win Ramadan are the ones that communicate first. Announce your Ramadan hours, your Eid-week booking window and your deposit policy in one clear message, well before the month starts. Put it on WhatsApp, on your Google Business Profile and in the salon itself.

Then repeat it — once when bookings open, once at the start of Ramadan, and once more in the final week. Clients do not mind repetition when the message helps them plan.

This is also the time to confirm every Eid-week appointment personally. A short WhatsApp message two days before, naming the service, the stylist and the time, cuts no-shows dramatically in a week when every empty chair is lost money.

## After-hours enquiries will spike

One pattern surprises many owners the first time: enquiries do not stop when the salon closes. Clients message at midnight, during suhoor hours and on their way home from evening gatherings. They ask about availability for the next day, about prices for Eid packages and whether a slot is still open.

A salon that answers those messages quickly wins bookings a slower competitor never sees. The ones that wait until morning often find the client has already booked elsewhere. ## Plan the week after Eid too

Here is the part most salons forget: the rush ends, and the diary goes quiet. The week after Eid is often one of the slowest of the year. Clients have had their big treatments, spent their budgets and are settling back into normal routines.

Smart owners plan for the dip before it arrives. Book the next visit while the client is still in the chair during Ramadan — a colour refresh or a facial three weeks later, offered while she is happy with the result.

## A simple timeline to start now

If you take nothing else from this, take the timeline. Three months out, review last year's numbers and set your Ramadan hours. Two months out, confirm the rota, hire any extra hands and design your packages. Six weeks out, open Eid-week bookings with deposits and announce the plan to clients. Two weeks out, confirm every appointment personally. And the week after Eid, follow up and rebook.

Ramadan rewards preparation more than almost any other moment in the UAE salon calendar. Start now, while there is still time to plan calmly, and the year's biggest beauty rush can become your best month — not your most stressful one.
