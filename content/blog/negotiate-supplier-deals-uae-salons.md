Walk into almost any salon in Dubai or Abu Dhabi and you will find the same quiet problem. The colour tubes, the shampoos, the styling products, the disposables — they arrive every week or two, the invoice is paid without a second thought, and nobody quite remembers when prices last went up. Product cost is one of the biggest running expenses in a salon, yet it is the one most owners negotiate the least. Rent gets a hard conversation once a year. Suppliers get an email that says "please send the usual."

That habit is expensive. In a market where a busy salon can spend thousands of dirhams a month on professional products, a ten percent improvement on your supply bill is often worth more than a month of marketing. And unlike raising prices or cutting services, it costs your clients nothing.

## Start by knowing what you actually buy

You cannot negotiate what you have not measured. Pull three months of invoices and write down what you spend per month, which five products account for most of it, and how often each is ordered. Most owners find that a small handful of products — usually colour, developer, shampoo and one or two treatment lines — make up the majority of the bill, and that a few items on the order barely get used and sit expiring on a shelf.

That list is your negotiating position. A supplier will take you far more seriously when you can say "we spend AED 4,200 a month with you, and colour alone is AED 2,600 of it" than when you ask for a general discount on everything. It also tells you where to focus: a five percent saving on your biggest line beats twenty percent on a product you order once a quarter.

## Negotiate on more than price

Many owners ask only for a lower price per unit — but price is only one lever, and it is the one suppliers are most trained to resist. Several others can be worth just as much.

Credit terms are the first. If you currently pay on delivery or within seven days, moving to thirty-day terms frees up cash that stays in your business for a whole extra month.

Delivery is the second. In the UAE, a supplier who delivers late can ruin a day: a colour appointment booked for 6 PM with no developer in the cupboard is a lost client and a bad review. Ask for a guaranteed delivery window and a named contact for urgent orders. A supplier who delivers within 24 hours is worth more than a slightly cheaper one who takes four days.

The third lever is free goods and training. Distributors regularly have room to offer one free item for every ten purchased, or a free training session for your team on a new line. Training is especially valuable: a team that knows how to use a product properly wastes less of it, which cuts your cost per service without any price change at all.

## Use volume — honestly

Suppliers give better terms to salons that order predictably. If you order colour every week in small, urgent batches, you are the most expensive kind of customer to serve. Consolidate into one larger order every two weeks and tell the supplier your expected monthly volume in writing.

That does not mean over-ordering to chase a discount. Colour and treatment products have shelf lives, and a storeroom full of expiring stock is not a saving. Ask your supplier to show you their tier thresholds — many owners never ask, and never discover they are a few hundred dirhams a month away from a better rate.

If your volumes are genuinely small, consider joining forces. Two or three independent salons in the same area ordering the same core products through one account can unlock distributor pricing none of them could reach alone.

## The conversation itself

The best supplier conversations happen before you need something, not after a price rise lands in your inbox. Pick a quiet weekday morning, not a frantic Saturday, and frame the talk as a partnership discussion, not a complaint. Open with what you value: the product quality, the delivery reliability, the relationship. Then lay out your numbers and ask a direct question — "What can we do together to bring my monthly bill down by ten percent?"

Give the supplier something to work with: a six-month commitment on your core lines, or payment by standing order instead of chased invoices. Suppliers value certainty, and certainty is the currency you trade for better terms.

And do not be afraid of a polite no. If your current supplier cannot move on price, ask about credit terms, delivery, training or free goods instead. If they cannot move on anything, get a quote from a second distributor for the same lines. You do not have to switch to benefit from a second quote. Simply knowing the market price changes the next conversation.

## Small habits that protect the saving

A negotiated deal only pays off if the savings survive day-to-day habits. Three small routines protect it. First, put one person in charge of ordering, and make them check stock before every order — duplicate orders are the most common leak in a salon. Second, track product usage per service: if one stylist uses noticeably more colour than another, that is a training conversation, not a supplier problem. Third, review your supplier terms every six months — prices drift, and a deal that was good in January may not be good in July.
