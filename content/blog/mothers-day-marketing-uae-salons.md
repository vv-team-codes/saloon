Every salon owner knows the big days: Eid, National Day, the December party rush. But one date is often under-planned, and it quietly brings some of the easiest bookings of the year. Mother's Day, on 21 March, is one of the biggest gifting days in the country. Sons and daughters who would never book a facial for themselves will happily buy one for their mum. The demand is real and short-lived: salons that plan for it fill chairs for a week, while the rest turn gift buyers away.

Treat it as a campaign, not a day: buying starts two to three weeks earlier, peaks in the final 48 hours, and redemptions stretch for weeks afterwards. Plan for all three phases and the day pays you three times.

## Start early, because the diary fills before the day does

Most Mother's Day bookings are not made on Mother's Day. The daughter planning a blow-dry and facial for Saturday 21 March starts looking the week before; the son buying a voucher starts earlier still. If your packages only appear a few days beforehand, the organised buyers have already gone elsewhere.

Put the plan together four weeks out: two or three packages, not ten. A 60-minute treat for the son buying a gift, a longer morning for a mother-daughter pair, and one premium option. Name them clearly, price them clearly, and list them on your WhatsApp catalogue, Google Business Profile and Instagram highlights at the same time, so your team can answer "what do you have for Mother's Day?" in one message.

Block the diary honestly. Mother's Day falls on a Saturday in 2027, already one of the busiest days in a UAE salon. Hold back a few Saturday slots for mother-daughter pairs and steer single redemptions towards quieter days. A voucher bought on Mother's Day and redeemed on a quiet Tuesday fills a chair that would otherwise sit empty.

## Sell the gift, not the treatment

A Mother's Day buyer is not shopping for a facial. She is shopping for a feeling — I thought of you, I want you to be looked after. Sell that feeling; the treatment is just how you deliver it.

That is why gift vouchers are the engine of this campaign. A voucher is the perfect last-minute gift, it costs you nothing until it is redeemed, and every voucher sold is a future booking already paid for. Make buying one effortless. A client should be able to message you on WhatsApp, pick a package, pay, and receive a digital voucher within minutes — no phone call, no visit, no form. The buyer messaging at 11pm will not wait until morning.

Mother-daughter packages deserve their own push, because they bring two clients through the door at once. Market them as shared time, not two treatments side by side. Two chairs together, tea served at the same time, a photo at the end if they want one. Price the pair as one package, and say so plainly. Many daughters then come back alone — the pair introduces your salon to someone new.

Do not discount the packages into the ground. Mother's Day buyers are not bargain hunting; they want something lovely. A small gift with the booking — a hand cream, a candle, a handwritten card — feels more generous than ten percent off and costs you far less. Keep prices steady and let the occasion do the work.

## The last 48 hours are won on WhatsApp

The final two days before Mother's Day are the busiest for enquiries and the hardest to staff. Messages arrive late at night, early in the morning, and in the middle of the Saturday rush. "Do you have anything for tomorrow?" "Can I buy a voucher for my mum?" "Is there a slot for two at 4pm?" Each one is a sale, and each one goes cold if the reply takes hours.

The salons that do well have a system: saved replies for the three packages, a pinned message with the voucher link, and one person per shift whose job is answering enquiries first. And a 24/7 AI receptionist like ZYNA answers the messages that arrive after closing time, so the midnight buyer still gets a helpful reply and a booking link instead of silence until morning.

Speed matters more than polish in those final hours. A short reply with a real slot beats a beautiful reply that arrives too late. If Saturday is full, say so in the same message and offer Sunday or a voucher for the following week.

## The week after matters as much as the day

Most salons miss this: the vouchers sold around Mother's Day are redeemed over the next four to six weeks. That is a second campaign hiding inside the first, and it is where the real profit sits.

When a voucher is redeemed, treat the visit like a first visit, not a freebie — the mum who received the facial has likely never been to your salon. She is judging everything: the welcome, the consultation, the way the stylist listens. If she leaves happy, she becomes a regular. Treat her like a second-class client and she never comes back — and the gift giver hears about it.

Rebook her before she leaves, with two specific dates. A simple WhatsApp message the next day turns a one-off gift into a relationship. Track how many vouchers turn into second bookings — a far better measure of success than how many vouchers you sold.

## A simple four-week plan

Four weeks out, decide on your three packages, price them, and list them on WhatsApp, Google and Instagram. Three weeks out, announce them: one good post, one WhatsApp broadcast, and a mention to every client in the chair. Two weeks out, push vouchers — "Buy the gift now, she can enjoy it any time" — and remind clients in the salon, because the daughter in your chair is your most likely gift buyer.

In the final week, switch to urgency: daily reminders of remaining Saturday slots and a clear last-order time for vouchers. On the day, post a short thank-you to all the mums. The week after, follow up every redemption with a rebooking offer.

Mother's Day alone will not make your year. But a salon that runs this campaign well gains a week of full chairs, pre-paid vouchers filling quiet days, and new clients who arrived as a gift and stayed as regulars. That is a good return from a single date on the calendar.
