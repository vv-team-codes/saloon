Walk into any busy salon in Dubai or Abu Dhabi on a Thursday evening and you will hear it. The hair chairs are full, the nail stations are booked out, and the client in the chair is asking a question that has nothing to do with hair: do you do facials here? Can I book a massage after my colour? That question is a growth opportunity sitting in your own diary.

Adding spa services does not have to mean a second lease, a full refit or a six-figure investment. Done carefully, it means using the space, the team and the client base you already have to sell a second kind of visit to the same person.

## Start with the licence, not the treatment menu

In the UAE, the first question is never which facial to offer. It is whether your trade licence covers the activity. A licence that covers ladies' hairdressing does not automatically cover massage, facials or body treatments, and adding an activity after the fact can mean a licence amendment or a different activity code entirely.

Check with your licensing authority before you buy a single product. In Dubai that usually means the Department of Economy and Tourism or your free zone authority; in Abu Dhabi, the Department of Economic Development. The amendment is often straightforward, but it takes time, and discovering the gap after you have already started selling spa services is the expensive way to learn it.

## Pick two services, not ten

The biggest mistake owners make is launching a full spa menu on day one. Ten new treatments means ten sets of products, ten training sessions and a team that is nervous about all of them. They need two or three services done well.

Look at what your clients already ask for. In most UAE salons, the two safest starting points are a signature facial and a relaxing massage. Facials pair naturally with the skincare conversations your stylists already have, and they use the quiet weekday hours when hair chairs sit empty. Massage brings in a different kind of client, often in the evening, and fills the treatment room when hair demand softens.

## Use the space you already have

A full refit is rarely necessary. Most salons already have one underused corner: the back room used for storage, the second wash area, the consultation nook that sits empty most of the day. A treatment bed, a trolley, soft lighting and a curtain or partition can turn that corner into a working treatment room for a fraction of what a refit would cost.

Think about the flow of the day. Hair colour processing takes thirty to forty-five minutes, and during that time the client is sitting with foils in, doing nothing. That is exactly the window in which a hand massage or a quick express facial can happen. The treatment room earns money from time that was already booked, and the client leaves feeling she had a far richer visit than a colour alone.

## Train one person properly, then the next

Spa services live or die on the skill of the person delivering them. A facial given by someone who has had a single afternoon of training will not bring a client back, and a bad first massage can put a client off spa services at your salon for good.

Send one team member for proper certification first, and let her become the spa lead. She learns the treatments, the products, the consultation questions and the hygiene routines, and then she trains the next person under her supervision. This is slower than training everyone at once, but it protects the quality your regulars expect, and it gives you one person who owns the new service line.

## Price it like a new business, not an add-on

New spa services are often priced too low because owners treat them as extras to the hair business. A facial that takes an hour, uses professional products and occupies a treatment room is not an add-on. It is a service with its own costs, and it needs its own price.

Work out the real cost per treatment: products, laundry, the therapist's time, the room, and the quiet hour it occupies. Then look at what comparable salons in your area charge, and price in the same range. Clients in the UAE are used to paying for quality spa experiences, and a price far below the market does not read as a bargain. It reads as a warning.

## Tell your existing clients first

You do not need an advertising campaign to launch spa services. You already have the best audience a new service could ask for: the clients sitting in your chairs every week. They trust you, they already come to you, and many of them are already buying spa services somewhere else.

Start simply. Tell every client at the chair that the new service is coming, and offer a small number of opening slots at a fair introductory price. Put a simple sign at reception and a line on your WhatsApp status. When a client asks about a facial, answer with a specific time and a specific price, not a vague promise to check.

This is also where after-hours enquiries matter. Clients often message late at night, after they have seen a friend's post or finished a long day, and they ask about the new service when nobody is at the desk. ## Measure it for ninety days

Give the new service line ninety days before you judge it. Track four numbers each week: how many spa appointments were booked, how many of those clients were already your clients, the average spa bill, and how many spa clients rebooked within six weeks. Those four numbers tell you whether the expansion is working, and they tell you faster than any feeling about it will.

Adding spa services to a UAE salon is not a leap into a different business. It is the same business, serving the same clients, with a wider set of reasons for them to come back. Start with the licence, pick two services, use the space you have, train one person well, price it honestly and tell the clients you already have. The refit can wait. The revenue does not have to.
