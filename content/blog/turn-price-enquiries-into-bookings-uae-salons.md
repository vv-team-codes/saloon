Ask any salon owner in Dubai or Abu Dhabi what message lands in their inbox most often, and the answer is almost always the same: "How much for balayage?" "Price for a bridal package?" It arrives on WhatsApp, on Instagram, and sometimes as a missed call followed by a short text. How you answer it decides whether that person books, keeps shopping around, or disappears completely.

Most salons lose price enquiries in one of two ways. They reply with a bare number and nothing else, which invites an instant comparison with the cheaper salon down the road, or they reply too slowly, by which time the client has already messaged three competitors.

## Why "how much?" is rarely just about price

When a client asks about price before anything else, they are usually trying to reduce risk. They do not know your salon yet, how long the service takes, or whether the result will suit them. A number feels like a safe way to decide without committing to a conversation.

That is why a one-number reply so often fails. "AED 350" tells a stranger nothing about what she actually gets: whether that includes a consultation, a toner, or a blow-dry, or whether the stylist is senior or junior. All she can do is compare AED 350 with AED 280 somewhere else, and cheaper usually wins that comparison even when it should not.

A better reply treats the price question as the start of a conversation, not the end of one. Answer the number honestly, then add the context that makes it make sense: what is included, how long it takes, and what happens next. That shift changes the decision from "who is cheapest?" to "who do I trust?"

## Answer the number, then add the value in one breath

The worst thing you can do with a price enquiry is dodge it. Clients in the UAE are busy, messaging between meetings or while waiting for a delivery, and a vague reply like "prices vary, please visit us" feels like a dead end. Answer the price clearly, then give two or three concrete details that justify it.

Instead of "Balayage AED 450", try: "Balayage starts at AED 450, including a consultation, toner, and blow-dry, and it usually takes about two and a half hours with a senior colourist. Would you like me to check Thursday or Friday evening?" The price is the same, but the client now understands the service and has a clear next step.

## Ask one good question before you quote

Not every price question can be answered with a single figure, and pretending otherwise creates problems later. A colour service depends on hair length, previous colour, and the result the client wants. Quoting blind can mean an awkward correction at the chair.

Train whoever handles enquiries to ask one short, helpful question before quoting when the service genuinely varies. "Is your hair currently coloured, or natural?" is enough. The question shows expertise, protects you from misquoting, and keeps the conversation moving. Clients rarely mind one question. What they mind is being passed between staff or asked the same thing twice.

## Speed matters more than perfection

In a city where clients message at lunch, during the evening commute, and late at night, the salon that replies first often wins the booking. A client who messages five salons on a Tuesday evening is usually ready to book one of them that same night, so a reply that arrives on Wednesday morning may already be too late.

Peak enquiry hours in the UAE are easy to predict: lunch breaks, the early evening after work, and weekend mornings when people plan the week ahead. Yet these are also the hours when your front desk is busiest with clients already in the salon, and that mismatch is where most enquiries quietly die.

The fix does not have to be complicated. Set a simple rule: every enquiry gets a first reply within a few minutes during opening hours, even if it is just an acknowledgement with the price and a promise of a time check. Outside opening hours, an automatic reply giving the starting price range and opening hours keeps the conversation alive until morning.

## Do not discount by default

When a price enquiry stalls, the temptation is to offer a discount to save it. A cut sometimes works in the moment, but as a habit it trains clients to haggle and squeezes your margins. Before discounting, try a different lever: availability, convenience, or a small included extra that costs you little but feels valuable to the client.

Offering the last evening slot before the weekend, a complimentary consultation, or a blow-dry included with a colour service often moves a hesitant client further than a price cut, and it protects your positioning. A salon known for thoughtful service and clear pricing builds a steadier client base than one known for always being negotiable.

## End every price reply with a booking step

End each price reply with one specific option, not an open question. "Would Thursday at 6 pm or Friday at 5 pm work for you?" is far stronger than "Let us know when you would like to come." Give two times and confirm what the client needs to do to hold the slot. If a deposit is required for longer services, explain it in the same message so there is no surprise later.

Then follow up once if there is no reply. A short, friendly message the next day — "Just checking, did you want me to hold Thursday at 6 pm for your balayage?" — recovers a surprising number of bookings. One follow-up is helpful; three becomes pressure.

Good enquiry handling should not depend on one talented receptionist remembering everything. Keep a short, up-to-date price guide your whole team can use, and make sure the version on WhatsApp, Instagram, and your website all match.
