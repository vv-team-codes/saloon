A client has picked her service, checked the price, and is nearly ready to book. Then comes the question that stalls the whole conversation: "Do you take Tabby?" Or "Can I pay by card?" Or "Can I split it with my friend?" How a salon answers that question decides whether the enquiry becomes a booking or quietly disappears.

Payment questions are not small talk. In the UAE, a client may be budgeting a big colour appointment across two paydays, sharing a bridal package with her sister, or simply checking she will not have to carry cash through a mall. If the answer is slow, vague or embarrassed, she assumes the worst and books somewhere that sounds certain. The good news is that payment enquiries are among the easiest to handle well, because the answers are fixed. The salon just needs to decide them once and write them down.

The reply that works is short, specific and warm. Name the methods you accept, name the one you do not, and attach the answer to a booking step. "Yes, we accept cards, Apple Pay and Tabby on services over AED 500. We take a AED 100 deposit to hold the chair, and the balance is due on the day. Shall I hold Thursday at 6pm for you?" That one message answers the question, removes the awkwardness and moves the conversation forward. Compare it with "Yes we take card" and silence. The first version books. The second leaves her to work out the rest alone.

## Decide your payment rules before the question arrives

A salon that invents its payment policy in the middle of a WhatsApp chat will sound uncertain every time. Sit down once, with the owner and the front desk, and settle the questions clients actually ask. Which cards and wallets do you accept? Do you take cash? Is there a minimum spend for Tabby or another buy-now-pay-later option? Do you split one bill across two cards? Do you hold a slot without a deposit, and if not, how much is the deposit and how is it paid?

Write the answers in one page and keep it where whoever replies to messages can see it. The team should never have to guess, and the client should never get two different answers from two different staff members. If a method is not available, say so plainly and offer the nearest alternative. "We do not take Tabby yet, but we do accept all major cards and Apple Pay, and you can split the bill across two cards" keeps the booking alive. "No" on its own ends it.

Be honest about the limits of instalment options too. A buy-now-pay-later plan spreads the cost, it does not make the service cheaper, and some clients do not realise that until the first payment is due. A salon that explains this kindly at booking earns more trust than one that lets a client discover it later and blame the salon.

## The deposit conversation is a payment conversation

Deposits deserve their own clear script, because they are where most payment enquiries turn awkward. A client who has never paid a deposit to a salon before may read it as a sign the salon does not trust her. Frame it the other way round: the deposit protects her appointment. "We ask for AED 100 to hold the chair, because the colour appointment blocks three hours and we turn other clients away for that slot. It comes off your final bill, and if you need to move it, just tell us 24 hours ahead and we will move the deposit with you."

Send the payment link or the account details in the same message as the deposit request, not in a follow-up an hour later. Every extra step is a place where the client can drift away. And when the deposit arrives, confirm it immediately: "Received, thank you. Your Thursday 6pm with Sara is confirmed." That confirmation is what turns a payment into a commitment she will keep.

## Splitting bills, sharing packages and group bookings

Group bookings create the messiest payment questions. Three friends booking together, a bride and her mother sharing a package, a client who wants to pay for her sister's blow-dry as a gift. Decide the rules in advance. Can one person pay for the whole group, or does each person pay for her own service? Can a package be shared across two people? Who pays the deposit when the booking is made by one person for four?

The cleanest approach is to name one payer at booking, take one deposit from that person, and settle individual balances at the salon on the day. It keeps the WhatsApp thread simple and avoids the salon becoming a referee between friends. If a client wants to gift a service, sell it as a voucher with a clear value and expiry, so the recipient arrives knowing exactly what is covered and no one has to discuss money at the chair.

## Put the answers where clients can find them

Many payment enquiries never need to happen. Add a short payment note to the WhatsApp service menu, the booking confirmation message and the salon's Instagram highlights: cards, wallets, instalment options, deposit amount, and the fact that the deposit comes off the final bill. Clients who can see the answers before they ask will ask fewer questions and book faster.

Review the enquiries once a month and look for the question that keeps appearing. If five clients in a week ask whether you take a particular wallet, that is a signal worth acting on, either by adding the method or by making the answer easier to find. The salons that handle payment questions best are not the ones with the most payment options. They are the ones whose answers are decided, written down and delivered in the same message as the booking.
