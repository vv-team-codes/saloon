A client walks into a Dubai salon at 7 pm, straight from the gym. She is not here for a blow-dry. She wants to wake up tomorrow with her brows already done — no pencil, no smudging through a spin class or a swim. This is the promise driving one of the fastest-growing services in UAE salons: semi-permanent makeup.

Microblading, powder brows, lip blush and eyeliner enhancement have moved from niche add-on to headline service — and the question for salons is whether they are ready to offer it properly.

## What semi-permanent makeup actually is

Semi-permanent makeup, often called PMU, places pigment into the upper layers of the skin with a fine needle or blade. It is not a tattoo. The pigment sits shallower, fades gradually, and typically lasts 12 to 24 months depending on skin type, sun exposure and aftercare.

The most requested services are microblading for hair-stroke brows, powder brows for a softer filled look, lip blush for colour and definition, and lash-line enhancement for eyes that look defined without eyeliner. Each one is a skilled, detailed treatment. A full brow treatment in Dubai commonly starts around AED 900 and can pass AED 2,000 in established salons, with a top-up session included.

## Why the UAE is a natural fit for PMU

Few markets suit this trend as well as the UAE. The climate does half the selling for you. Humidity, heat, swimming pools and gym sessions melt conventional makeup before lunch. Clients who spend their days between air-conditioned offices and outdoor heat want a look that stays put.

Lifestyle plays a part too. Many UAE clients are busy professionals and mothers who want to look polished with minimal morning effort. A lip blush that survives a coffee, a workout and a school run is not a luxury for them — it is a practical answer to a real daily problem.

## The services clients ask for most

Brow work leads the list. Microblading suits clients who want individual hair strokes and already have some brow hair. Powder brows suit those who want a fuller, makeup-like finish, and they tend to hold better on oily skin — worth remembering in a humid climate.

Lip blush is the quiet riser. Clients ask for it to even out lip tone, add a soft tint and define the border without looking like they are wearing lipstick. It photographs beautifully, which matters in a city where clients share everything.


## What it takes to offer it well

This is where salons should be honest with themselves. Semi-permanent makeup is not a service you add after a weekend workshop. It demands proper training, practice on models, and a clear understanding of skin, pigment and healing.

Consultation is the heart of the service. A good artist spends as long talking as treating: mapping the brow shape with the client, agreeing on colour, explaining that brows heal darker before they settle, and checking medical history and allergies. Some clients should not be treated, and saying so builds more trust than saying yes to everyone.

Hygiene is non-negotiable: single-use needles, sterile equipment and a treatment room that looks clinical, not improvised. Licensing needs checking too — requirements vary by emirate and free zone, so confirm what your salon licence covers before advertising the service.

## Pricing it honestly

PMU pricing tempts salons in two wrong directions: too cheap, which signals inexperience, or inflated without the results to justify it. The honest middle is to price for the skill, the time and the follow-up.

A typical treatment takes two to three hours including consultation and mapping, plus a top-up appointment four to six weeks later. Price the package as one service — treatment plus top-up — and say so clearly. Clients accept a four-figure price when they understand what is included and see real healed results, not filtered photos taken minutes after treatment.

## Fitting it into the diary

PMU does not slot into a diary like a blow-dry. A single brow appointment can occupy a chair for three hours, which makes it a poor fit for the peak evening rush when quick services earn more per hour. Smart salons schedule PMU in quieter morning and early-afternoon slots, and keep evenings for high-turnover treatments.


Because the service generates many questions, enquiries often arrive late at night — clients research after the children are asleep and message at 10 or 11 pm. ## Marketing it without filters

The most effective PMU marketing is healed, honest work. Before-and-after photos taken four weeks after treatment, in natural light, with no filter, tell a client more than any studio shot. Video of the consultation and mapping process also helps, because the biggest fear new clients have is ending up with brows they did not ask for.

Content that answers real questions works best: how long it lasts, whether it hurts, what the healing week looks like, and who should not have it done. A salon that answers these openly becomes the trusted choice, not just the cheapest one.

## The risks of rushing in

The boom brings a warning with it. Badly done PMU is visible, hard to fix and damaging to a salon's name.

Start small. Send one artist for proper training, build a portfolio on models, and launch quietly to existing clients who already trust you. Let the results — and the rebookings — do the growing.

## A trend with staying power

Semi-permanent makeup is not a passing fad in the UAE. It answers a real need — looking polished in a hot climate with a busy life — and it creates the kind of long-term client relationship salons dream of: a treatment that brings people back every year, with top-ups, referrals and trust built in.
