A message arrives on WhatsApp late in the evening: "I'm losing a lot of hair. Can you help?" It is one of the most sensitive enquiries a UAE salon will ever receive. The person writing it is usually worried, sometimes embarrassed, and often has already tried three shampoos and a supplement she saw on Instagram. How your salon answers in the next few minutes decides whether she books a consultation or quietly disappears.

Hair loss is common in the UAE, driven by hard water, air conditioning, humidity and stress. The salons that win these bookings are not the ones with the boldest claims — they are the ones that sound calm, specific and honest.

## Why this enquiry is different

Most salon enquiries are about price, time or availability. A hair loss enquiry is about identity, and the client reads your reply for tone as much as for information. A breezy "book now" feels dismissive; a medical-sounding explanation feels alarming. What works is a reply that takes the worry seriously, sets honest boundaries and offers a clear next step.

## The first reply: four parts that build trust

A good first reply has four parts and fits in one WhatsApp message. First, acknowledge the worry without drama: "Thank you for trusting us with this — many of our Dubai clients ask about shedding." Second, be honest about limits: "We are stylists and scalp-care specialists, not doctors, so we never diagnose the cause." Third, name the next step: a scalp consultation, with a named stylist, a duration and a price. Fourth, ask one or two useful questions that help you prepare.

That structure reassures the client, protects your salon from ever overpromising, and moves the conversation toward a booking. A reply that only says "Come in and we will see" does none of that, and the client usually goes quiet.

## Five questions before you book anything

Before confirming, you need enough to match the client with the right stylist and enough time. Five questions cover it: when did the shedding start, and has it changed recently? Is the hair coming out all over, or thinning in one area? Have you had any big changes — illness, new medication, a move to Dubai, a stressful few months? What are you using on your hair now? And have you seen a doctor or had blood tests for it?

Asked early, these questions show expertise and help the consultation start well. Keep them in the chat, not in a form link — a form feels like admin, while a chat feels like care.

## What never to promise

Never promise regrowth, never name a number of sessions after which hair "will come back", and never suggest a treatment will fix a medical cause. Phrases like "guaranteed results" and "stops hair fall in four weeks" are not just dishonest — they are the reason a disappointed client leaves a one-star review that mentions your salon by name.

What you can promise is honest: a careful scalp assessment, a clear explanation of what you see, a realistic plan, and a recommendation to see a doctor if needed. Clients are relieved to hear it — it tells them you will not sell them a package and hope for the best.

## Designing the consultation itself

A hair loss consultation should feel different from a normal appointment. Give it a name on the diary — "scalp consultation" — a set duration of 30 to 45 minutes, and a named senior stylist. Price it honestly: many UAE salons charge a small fee redeemable against a treatment, which filters out curiosity bookings while keeping the door open for serious clients.

During the consultation, look before you recommend: check the scalp under good light, ask about the client's routine, and talk through what you see in plain words. Then give a three-part plan — what the salon will do, what the client does at home, and a review date eight weeks out. A review date turns a single visit into a relationship.

## The UAE angle: water, weather and timing

Dubai clients often blame the water first. Hard water and heavy air conditioning do make hair dry and brittle, but rarely explain sudden shedding alone. A good reply acknowledges the theory without agreeing to it: "The water here is hard on hair, and it can make shedding look worse, but sudden shedding usually has more than one cause. That is exactly what the consultation helps us work out."

Many salons see a wave of these enquiries in September and October, after the summer heat, and again after Ramadan. And because they often arrive late at night, when worry peaks, a fast reply matters more than a perfect one. ## When to refer, and how to say it

Some clients need a doctor, not a stylist: patchy loss, a sore or flaky scalp, sudden shedding after illness, or loss continuing for many months. Saying so does not lose the booking — it usually saves it. "What you describe sounds like something a doctor should look at first, and once you have that answer we can plan the salon care around it" is a sentence clients remember, and it is often the reason they return afterwards.

## The follow-up that turns one visit into a regular

After the consultation, the follow-up matters as much as the visit. A short WhatsApp message the next evening — "It was good to meet you today. Here is the plan we discussed, and your next appointment is on Thursday at 6pm" — shows the client she was heard. A second check-in after a few weeks, asking how the scalp feels rather than selling the next treatment, keeps the relationship warm without pressure.

Keep notes on what you observed, recommended and when you will review. When she returns in eight weeks and you remember the details without asking her to repeat them, a worried enquirer becomes a loyal regular — and tells her friends your salon took her seriously.

Hair loss enquiries will keep arriving late at night, full of worry. Answer them with a fast, warm first reply, honest boundaries, a real consultation and a plan with a review date — that is how a difficult message becomes a booked chair, and a client who stays.
