Mention the word "perm" to a client over forty and watch her face: tight, frizzy curls, the smell of perm solution filling the salon, hair that felt like straw for months. That is the perm she remembers — and why so many UAE salon owners still flinch at offering one.

The perm clients are asking about in 2026 is not that perm. It is softer, looser and far kinder to the hair, creating a lived-in wave that looks like it happened on a beach holiday rather than in a salon chair. In a market where clients want low-maintenance style that survives Dubai humidity, it is becoming one of the most requested services.

## Why this perm is different

The old perm used small rods, strong solution and one setting for everyone, and the result was uniform, tight and hard to grow out. Modern perming works the other way round: larger rods, gentler formulas and techniques chosen for the client's hair.

A digital perm, the version most UAE salons add first, uses heated rods and a gentler solution to create soft waves that hold when dry and relax when wet. A root perm lifts only the roots, giving fine hair volume. A spot perm adds a bend exactly where it is needed — a flick at the ends.

That difference matters for how you sell it. Clients who would never book "a perm" will happily book "soft waves that last three months."

## Why the UAE is the perfect place for the comeback

First, humidity. From May to October, a blow-dry can fall flat before a client reaches her car. A perm works with the moisture in the air instead of fighting it, so the wave looks better on a humid evening than it did leaving the salon.

Second, the low-maintenance lifestyle clients here want. Many work long hours and travel often, and would rather spend their mornings doing anything other than styling their hair. A perm that lets them wash, scrunch and go beats a cut that needs a blow-dry every morning — the same logic behind the express beauty boom and lash lifts.

Third, the sheer variety of hair in the UAE. Your chairs see straight, wavy, thick, fine, colour-treated and heat-damaged hair from every background, and a modern perm is a set of techniques a good stylist can adapt to most of them. The salons booking out perms fastest treat it as a consultation-led service.

## What it looks like on the menu

Do not bury the perm in a long list of chemical services. Give it a name clients understand: "Soft Wave Perm," "Root Volume Perm" and "Beach Wave Perm" all describe the result, not the chemistry.

Photography matters more here than for almost any other service: a perm is hard to describe and easy to show. Real before-and-after photos of your own clients — same lighting, same angle, hair worn down — will do more than any description. Take the photos every time, with permission.

## The consultation that makes or breaks it

A perm is one of the few services where a rushed consultation can damage hair. Start with the hair's history: colour, bleach, keratin and previous perms all change what the hair can handle. Be honest when the answer is not yet — a client told to wait six weeks trusts you far more than one given a perm her hair could not take.

Then talk about the wave itself, with photos rather than words — "loose" means something different to every client, and a picture closes that gap in seconds. Agree on the size of the wave and how it will look when she styles it herself at home.

Finally, set the time expectation: a modern perm takes two to three hours. Clients who know that in advance arrive relaxed; clients who expected a quick appointment spend the whole service checking their watch. Put the duration on the booking confirmation.

## Pricing it honestly, and fitting it into the diary

Price the perm as a premium, time-heavy service, not a cheap add-on. A soft-wave perm sits well above a cut and colour, and clients accept that when they understand what they are buying: three months of styling they do not have to do. Break the price down — the service, the bond-protecting treatment, the aftercare products. Clients rarely object to a price they understand.

Do not discount the first perm to fill the diary — a client who books on a discount expects it next time, and the service takes too long to give away. Offer a consultation, a patch test and a clear price instead.

A perm takes a chair for two to three hours, so treat it as a daytime service. Book perms into the quieter late-morning and early-afternoon hours, when chairs would otherwise sit empty, and protect the 5 pm to 8 pm rush for quick services. Stagger bookings — one at 11 am, another at 1 pm — so two long services never collide at the rinse basin.

## Aftercare that keeps the curls — and the client

A perm lives or dies on the first 48 hours: no washing, no tight tying, no tucking behind the ears. Send those three rules by WhatsApp after the appointment — a short message will be followed far more reliably than a verbal warning at the till.

Recommend a sulphate-free shampoo and a leave-in conditioner, and show her how to scrunch the wave with her hands instead of brushing it out — a brush is the fastest way to turn soft waves into frizz.

Book the follow-up before she leaves: a quick check-in at four weeks, when the wave has settled, is the perfect moment to trim the shape and rebook. Clients who return become perm regulars — and once someone finds a stylist who understands her waves, she does not shop around.

## Starting small

The perm's comeback is not a fad. It is a practical answer to a real UAE problem: hair that has to look good in humidity, with minimal daily effort. Salons that offer it well are finding it brings in the client every salon wants: one who books ahead, follows advice and comes back every few months.
