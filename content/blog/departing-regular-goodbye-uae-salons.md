Dubai is a city of arrivals and departures. A client who has sat in your chair every three weeks for four years can walk in one Thursday evening and say, almost in passing, "This is my last colour before we move back home."

Most salons handle that moment like any other booking: note it, do the service, take payment, say goodbye at the door. But that last visit is not an ordinary appointment. It is the final impression a loyal client carries of your salon, and that impression travels further than any advert.

## The last visit is not just another appointment

A client who leaves Dubai feeling loved by her salon does three things. She tells her friends back home about you, and some of them will one day move to Dubai themselves. And if she ever returns to the UAE, even for a holiday, your salon is the first place she books.

## Start with the question nobody asks

When a client mentions she is leaving, ask one simple question: "When is your last day in Dubai?" A client leaving in three weeks has time for a proper final appointment.

Write the date in her client notes the same day, and tell the front desk. Too often the news stays with one stylist and the rest of the team finds out after she has gone. When the whole team knows, the receptionist greets her by name and her first stylist gets a chance to say hello. Also ask where she is going — that answer becomes the most generous thing your salon can do for her.

## Make the final service feel special, not sad

Treat the last appointment like a client's birthday visit: a little more time, a little more care, and one small touch she was not expecting.

Then add one small ritual: the head massage she always loved, done properly; her favourite drink waiting at the chair; the stylist she first came to popping over to say hello. The gesture matters less than the message: we remember you, and this visit matters to us. Keep it warm and light — she came for a service, not a leaving party.

## The practical part: what she needs to take with her

A client leaving Dubai has practical needs your salon is perfectly placed to meet. First, her hair and skin routine is about to change. The climate she is moving to may be colder, drier or harder on her hair than Dubai's humidity and desalinated water. A five-minute conversation about what will change, and which products to take with her, is genuinely helpful — offer the shampoo or treatment she already uses in a travel-friendly size.

Second, write down her colour formula. A small card with her exact shade, developer, toner and notes about her hair gives her next stylist a real head start and saves her from a disappointing first appointment somewhere new.

Third, take a proper photo of the finished result — not a quick snap for your social media, but a good photo she can show her next stylist. Send it on WhatsApp before she leaves, with a short note about what was done.

## The goodbye gift that is not a discount

Many salons reach for a discount when a client leaves. Resist it. A discount on a final visit feels transactional. A small, personal gift works far better: a travel-size version of the product she loves, a handwritten card from the team, or a small sample set for the flight. The gift should say "we will miss you", not "please come back". Give it at the end of the appointment, not the start.

## Help her find her next salon — yes, really

When a loyal client leaves Dubai, offer to help her find a good salon where she is going. You probably know someone: a former stylist who moved to London, a colleague who trained in Manila, a friend who runs a salon in Toronto. Even a short list of two or three names, with a note on what each one is good at, is an extraordinary kindness.

Why help a client you have already lost? Because generosity is the most memorable thing a business can do. She remembers the salon that helped her land safely in a new city, and she tells the story. In Dubai, new arrivals constantly ask departing friends for a salon recommendation — and the salon that treats leavers well is the one they name.

## Keep the door open after she leaves

The goodbye does not end at the door. A short WhatsApp message the next day — "Safe travels, it was lovely seeing you yesterday" — takes ten seconds and is remembered for years. If she replies from her new city, answer. She is no longer a paying client, but she is still part of your salon's story. ## A simple goodbye routine your team can follow

None of this works if it depends on one thoughtful stylist. Write it down as a routine the whole team knows: note the leaving date and destination in her client record the same day; tell the front desk and the stylist who knows her best; offer a slightly longer final slot with one small unhurried ritual; prepare her colour formula card, a good photo of the result and any products she needs; give a small personal gift at the end, not a discount; offer to help her find a salon in her new city, and follow through; and send a short WhatsApp message the day after she leaves.

Dubai will always be a city of goodbyes. The salons that thrive here treat every departure as a chance to prove what kind of business they are — one that values people even when there is nothing left to sell them. Do that well, and your departing clients become your best advertisement in every city they land in.
