Most UAE salon marketing is aimed at one person: a woman in her twenties, scrolling Instagram at midnight, hunting for the next balayage trend. She is a real client, and she matters. But she is often not the most valuable one.

Look at your own diary. The clients who book every three weeks without being chased, who arrive on time, who pay the full price without haggling, who bring their sister and then their neighbour — many of them are over fifty. Long-term residents, empty nesters and retirees who have lived in Dubai for decades: steady, good spenders, loyal for years. Yet almost no salon markets to them on purpose. That is a gap your salon can fill, and it costs far less than another round of ads aimed at everyone.

## They find salons differently — so market differently

A client over fifty rarely discovers a salon from a Reel. She asks a friend at the gym, reads Google reviews properly — all of them, including your replies — or walks past your door on her way to the supermarket. She may message on WhatsApp, but she will often just call, and she will judge you by whether someone picks up.

So the first marketing move is unglamorous: answer the phone, reply to WhatsApp quickly and in plain language, and keep your Google Business Profile honest — real photos, real prices, real opening hours, and replies to every review. Ask your happy older clients for a review while they are still in the chair, and ask in person, not by automated message. One warm review from a sixty-year-old regular beats a hundred likes on a transformation video.

## Name the services they actually want

Open most salon Instagram pages and you would think nobody over forty ever gets a haircut. The services older clients book most — grey blending, root touch-ups, scalp care, gentle colour for thinning hair, classic cuts, manicures and pedicures — barely appear.

Say them out loud. A simple post that says "grey blending that grows out softly, no harsh line, from AED 180" will reach the exact person who has been searching for it for months. Thinning hair needs a consultation, not a promise. Honesty in your marketing earns a trust no discount can buy.

## Market the comfort, not just the result

For a younger client, the draw is the look. For a client over fifty, the draw is often the visit itself — and that is something you can advertise honestly. Comfortable chairs with arms. Good lighting at the mirror. An unhurried appointment that is never squeezed between two others. A stylist who faces the client when speaking, music at conversation level.

Put that in your marketing, in plain words: "quiet weekday mornings, unhurried appointments, comfortable chairs, tea on arrival." It sounds small. It is why she chooses you — and tells her friends.

## The quiet morning is your best marketing tool

Weekday mid-mornings are the hardest hours to fill in most UAE salons — and the hours older clients prefer. Match the two.

Build a simple weekday-morning ritual and market it as one thing, not a discount: a calm slot, a named stylist, tea or coffee on arrival, no rushing, and a standing appointment at the same time each visit. Give it a name your team can say out loud — "Tuesday mornings with Sara" beats "10% off before noon" every time, because it sells belonging, not cheapness. Your regulars will do the marketing for you.

## Price honesty wins this client

Older clients have long memories for prices. They notice when the bill is higher than the quote, and they notice when a salon keeps its word. Publish clear prices for the services they book most, and train the team to confirm the total before starting, including any add-on. A short, up-to-date price list on WhatsApp or your website removes the one fear that stops many from trying a new salon: an awkward surprise at the till. A simple card stamped at the till, earning a complimentary blow-dry after six visits, beats any points app that needs downloading and a password.

## Remember them — that is the marketing

The most powerful marketing to a client over fifty is not an advert at all. It is remembering. Remember the usual cut, her tea, the quiet chair by the window. Write it down, share it with the team, and use it. A personal WhatsApp message the day before — "Same time Thursday? Sara has your chair ready" — is worth more than any broadcast.

And when she is happy, ask her to tell someone. Not with a referral code or a voucher, but with a sentence: "If you know someone who would like the same kind of visit, we'd love to meet them." Older clients do not share links; they vouch for you, in person, to people who trust them.

## Where to reach them

You will not find this audience by spending more on the same channels. A small, steady presence in the places they already look works better: local community and residents' groups, the noticeboard at the community centre, a short listing in a neighbourhood newsletter, and Google Maps, where nearby "salon near me" searches bring in walk-ins and calls.

Many enquiries from older clients arrive in the evening, after dinner. If those messages sit unanswered until morning, the moment passes. A 24/7 AI receptionist like ZYNA answers WhatsApp enquiries and books the appointment whenever the message arrives, so the client who messages at 9 pm still wakes up to a confirmed slot.

## What not to do

Do not use youth-only imagery in every advert and then wonder why older clients assume the salon is not for them. Do not push an app download, a booking portal or a loyalty scheme that needs a login. Do not discount aggressively to win them — a deep discount tells a careful client that the normal price was inflated. And do not assume they are not on WhatsApp: for many of them it is the main channel.

The clients most salons overlook are the ones most salons would love to keep. They book regularly, they pay fairly, they bring their friends, and they stay. It needs clear prices, honest services, a comfortable visit, and the habit of remembering who they are. Start with the quiet morning, the honest price list, and one genuine review from a client who has been coming for years.
