About two weeks before Eid, every salon owner's phone starts buzzing non-stop. Regulars who usually book a week ahead suddenly want tomorrow. Then, the morning after Eid, the diary goes quiet while the rent and payroll carry on as before.

The Eid rush is the biggest revenue window in a UAE salon's year, and it comes twice. Owners who plan only for the busy fortnight end up exhausted, then broke in the quiet week. The ones who do well treat Eid as a cycle: build-up, rush, and recovery.

## The rush starts earlier than you think

Most owners expect the rush to start in the final week. In practice, it starts ten to fourteen days before Eid, with the planners — clients who book hair, colour, facial and nails early, because they know the last week will be impossible. If your diary is still wide open twelve days out, your regulars have probably booked elsewhere.

Open Eid bookings four weeks in advance and tell people about it. A short WhatsApp broadcast to your regulars — "Eid bookings are now open, and the last three days always fill first" — fills the early slots with your best clients and creates urgency for everyone else. Do not wait for clients to ask.

## Build the Eid diary in three layers

Smart owners build the fortnight in three layers. The first is the core: regulars who pre-booked, bridal parties, and full packages — in first, at their usual stylists, at full price, with a deposit.

The second layer is henna and party prep. Henna bookings cluster in the final two or three days, and they are short, high-turnover services. Block them together in the late afternoon and evening, with a dedicated henna artist if you can.

The third layer is the release valve: a handful of slots held back until the final forty-eight hours, for the loyal client whose plans changed and the walk-in who becomes a regular. When every slot is sold a week early, you have no room to say yes — and the clients you turn away in the last two days are the ones who remember it.

## Price the rush honestly

Eid is not the moment to double your prices. Clients notice, and a salon that charges holiday prices finds its regulars thinner by January. What works instead is packaging. A "ready for Eid" package — cut, colour refresh, facial and nails, booked as one visit — is easier to sell than four separate services and worth more per chair hour.

Deposits matter more during Eid than at any other time of year. A no-show in the final three days costs you a slot that ten other clients wanted. Ask for a deposit on every booking in the last week and keep the rule the same for everyone.

## Protect your team or the rush breaks them

A salon runs on its stylists, and the Eid fortnight asks more of them than any other two weeks of the year. Twelve-hour days, back-to-back clients and no lunch break is how you lose a good stylist in January — or get sloppy work and unhappy clients in the final days.

Plan the rota before the rush, not during it. Stagger start times so the salon is covered from the quiet morning through the peak evening hours, and build a real break into every shift. A stylist who has eaten and rested does better work at 8 pm than one who has been on her feet since 9 am. Pay overtime properly and say so in advance — it costs less than replacing a stylist who quits.

If you need extra hands, arrange them early. A freelance stylist booked three weeks out is a solution; the same person found in a panic on the last weekend is a gamble. Give the extra hands the simple jobs — blow-dries, manicures, henna — so your senior team stays on the complex work.

## The last forty-eight hours

The final two days have their own rhythm. Henna night is the peak: clients arrive in groups and expect the salon to feel festive. A small touch — dates and Arabic coffee, music, a tidy waiting area — turns a crowded evening into an experience clients photograph and share.

This is also when the enquiry flood is at its worst. Messages arrive at midnight and at 6 am, mostly the same questions: are you open, what time, and how much. Nobody on your team has time to answer them one by one while the chairs are full. ## The week after Eid is the real test

Here is the part most owners get wrong. The rush ends, the salon goes quiet, and the owner finally breathes out — then realises there is almost nothing in the diary for the next ten days. Clients have spent their beauty budget, many are travelling, and the phone barely moves.

The fix happens before Eid, not after it. When a client is sitting in your chair during the rush, book her next visit before she leaves. Not a vague "see you soon" — a real date, two or three weeks out, confirmed by WhatsApp before she walks out. Clients who leave with a next appointment booked come back. Clients who leave with nothing booked drift, and by the time they think of you again, they have tried somewhere else.

Use the quiet week deliberately. It is the best week of the year for a deep clean, a stocktake, and the maintenance you kept postponing. It is also the right week for a gentle offer to your regulars — a weekday facial, a quiet-morning blow-dry. And look at the numbers: which services earned the most, which slots went unfilled, and what to do differently next Eid. Write it down while it is fresh.

## A simple Eid plan you can start this week

Four weeks out, open Eid bookings and message your regulars. Two weeks out, confirm the rota and lock in your henna artist. One week out, take deposits on every remaining booking and prepare your waitlist. The day after Eid, send a short thank-you to every client who booked.

The owners who come out of Eid stronger are the ones who planned the whole cycle and made sure the week after Eid had bookings in it before the rush began.
