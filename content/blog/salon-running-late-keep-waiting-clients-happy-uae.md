It is 7:15 on a Thursday evening in a Dubai salon, and the diary has slipped. The colour client in chair three needed an extra round of toner, and the 6:30 blow-dry arrived ten minutes late. Now the 7:00 client is sitting by the door checking her phone. Nobody has done anything wrong. But the next twenty minutes will decide whether she comes back.

Every salon runs late sometimes. In the UAE it happens more than owners admit, because the busiest hours are compressed into a short evening window. Clients finish work, fight the traffic, and arrive between six and nine expecting a smooth visit. When one appointment overruns, the delay ripples through the whole evening. The salons that keep their clients are not the ones that never run late — they are the ones that handle the wait well.

## Say something in the first minute

The single biggest mistake is silence. A client who is greeted, told honestly what is happening and given a real time will wait far more patiently than one left to guess. The moment you know, walk over, use her name, and say it plainly: the stylist is finishing the client before her, it will be about fifteen minutes, and you are sorry to keep her waiting.

Do not give a time you cannot keep. "Five minutes" said three times is worse than "twenty minutes" said once. Clients forgive a warned-about delay; they do not forgive being misled. If the wait grows, go back and update her before she has to ask.

## Never blame the client before her

It is tempting to explain the delay by pointing at the previous appointment. Resist it. The woman waiting does not want to hear that the client in the chair was late or asked for extra work. She hears only one thing: when her turn comes, she will be the one rushed. Keep it simple and take ownership. "We are running about fifteen minutes behind this evening" is enough. It is honest, and it leaves her confident her own appointment will get its full time.

## Make the wait part of the visit

A waiting client who is comfortable is still having a good experience. Offer her a drink without being asked — karak, Arabic coffee, iced water with mint. Show her to the best seat in the waiting area, not the stool by the door. Offer the Wi-Fi password and a charger.

Then give the wait a purpose. A hand massage while she waits costs five minutes of a junior team member's time and changes the whole feeling of the evening. So does a quick look at the colour chart for her next visit. The goal is not to distract her from the delay. It is to make the delay feel like the salon's time being spent on her, not her time being wasted.

## Keep her updated while she waits

One update is not enough if the wait stretches. Every ten minutes, someone should check in — not with an apology on repeat, but with something useful. "Your stylist is rinsing now, so about five more minutes. Can I get you anything while you wait?" That gives a real update and a small choice, which restores a feeling of control.

If the delay passes thirty minutes, stop managing the wait and start offering options. Can she come back in an hour? Would she prefer the first slot tomorrow morning? Is another stylist free now? Some clients will happily wait. Others have a dinner reservation or a child to collect, and for them the kindest thing is a genuine alternative.

## Brief the stylist before she sits down

The worst version of a late appointment is the client finally reaching the chair and having to start from the beginning. She has already waited; she should not have to repeat her consultation or re-explain the colour she wants.

Before she moves, take thirty seconds to brief the stylist: who she is, what she booked, anything she mentioned while waiting. When the stylist greets her by name, already knowing what she wants, the client feels remembered rather than processed.

## Do not rush the service to catch up

A late start tempts every stylist to hurry: a blow-dry in twenty minutes instead of thirty, a colour rinsed a few minutes early, a finish skipped because the next client is waiting. Clients notice the rushed section and the mirror check that never happened. One rushed service undoes all the good work of the waiting area.

Protect the full service time, even if the diary slips further. It is better for two clients to wait ten minutes each than for one to leave unhappy with her service. If the evening is genuinely unrecoverable, call the last client of the day before she leaves home and offer her a real choice.

## A small gesture beats a big discount

When the visit is over, a genuine apology from the stylist, priority on the next booking, or a small complimentary add-on next time, such as a deep conditioning treatment or a brow tidy, tells the client the delay mattered. Do not reach for a blanket discount — discounts train clients to expect compensation for every imperfection.

Then follow up. A short WhatsApp message the next day — thanking her for her patience and offering first pick of times for her next visit — turns a delayed evening into a story of good service. Most clients never receive that message. The ones who do remember it.

## Build a diary that slips less often

The best recovery is the one you rarely need. If colour appointments overrun most evenings, the booked duration is wrong, not the stylist. If one late client knocks the diary sideways every evening, add a short buffer before peak hours. Ten minutes reviewing the pattern once a week saves the team an hour of apologies the next.

Clients in the UAE have plenty of salons to choose from. They stay with the one that treats their time as valuable, especially when things do not go to plan. A salon that handles a delay well does not lose a client — often it keeps her for years, because she has seen how the salon behaves when things go wrong, and that is when trust is really built.
