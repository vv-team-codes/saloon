Every day, your clients watch you reach for a bottle, work it through their hair or onto their skin, and ask the same question: "What is that? Can I buy it?" Most UAE salon owners answer by selling them someone else's brand. You earn a small margin, the brand earns the loyalty, and the client remembers the label — not your salon.

There is another way. An own-label product line puts your salon's name on the bottle. The client takes home a piece of your expertise, uses it every morning, and comes back to you when it runs out.

## Why your own label beats another brand on the shelf

Selling a well-known brand is easy, and that is exactly the problem. Any salon in your mall or on your street can stock the same bottle. The client can compare prices online, buy it cheaper elsewhere, and forget where she first tried it.

Your own label changes the maths. Nobody else sells it, so nobody undercuts you. The margin is yours: where a branded product might leave you 30 to 40 percent, an own-label product typically leaves 60 percent or more once the line is running. When a client uses your argan oil or your scalp serum at home, your salon stops being a place she visits every few weeks and becomes part of her daily routine.

## Start with one product, not a range

Start with one product — the one your clients already ask about most. For most UAE salons that is a hair oil, a leave-in treatment, or a scalp serum. The heat, humidity, hard water and daily styling here create the same problems for almost every client, so a product that solves one of them has a ready audience.

Pick the product you already recommend five times a week. You already know what good looks like, and that knowledge is your quality control.

## Finding a manufacturer and getting the formula right

You do not need a factory. Private-label cosmetic manufacturers will produce a product to your specification in small runs — often from as few as 100 to 500 units for a first order.

Talk to at least three manufacturers before you commit. Ask for samples, and test them the way your clients will: on real hair, in Dubai humidity, over a full week — not just once in the salon.

Be honest about what the product is. If it is a simple, effective hair oil with three good ingredients, say so. UAE clients are increasingly reading labels and asking questions, and a short, honest ingredient list builds more trust than a long list of exotic claims.

## The money: what it really costs

A first own-label product is a modest investment, but it is still real money, so budget it properly. A typical first run of 200 to 300 units of a hair oil or serum, including bottles, labels and basic packaging, usually costs between AED 4,000 and AED 10,000 depending on the formula and the packaging you choose. Spend on the product inside, not the box outside.

Price it by working backwards from what the client will pay. If a good imported hair oil sells in Dubai for AED 120, your own-label version at AED 90 to AED 110 feels fair and still leaves you a healthy margin. Do not price it as a bargain product: a cheap price tells the client it is a cheap product.

Track the numbers from day one. How many bottles did you sell in the first month? How many buyers came back for a second? A product that sells 20 bottles a month at AED 60 profit is AED 1,200 a month from one small shelf.

## Labelling, registration and the rules in the UAE

This is the part owners most often skip, and it matters. Cosmetic products sold in the UAE must be registered and properly labelled, and your salon's trade licence must cover the activity of selling them. The exact requirements depend on your emirate and your licence type, so check with your free zone authority, DED office, or a business setup adviser before you order your first run.

At minimum, your label needs the product name, your brand name, ingredients, how to use it, the net quantity, a batch number and an expiry date. Keep the manufacturer's certificates and test reports on file. Doing this properly is what separates a real brand from a bottle with a sticker.

## Selling it without hard-selling it

An own-label product sells itself if the client experiences it first. Use it in the service: the oil goes on during the blow-dry, the serum finishes the treatment. When the client asks what it is — and she will — that is the moment to offer it. No pitch, no pressure, just a simple line: "That's our own oil. You can take a bottle home if you'd like."

Put one bottle on the mirror shelf at each station, not twenty on a display at the door. Have your stylists write the product name on the client's aftercare note so she remembers it at home.

WhatsApp does the follow-up work for you. A short message a few days after the visit — "Hope the oil is working well for your hair" — reminds the client the product exists and makes reordering as easy as replying to a message. ## From one bottle to a small range

Give your first product three months. If it sells steadily and clients ask for more, add a second product that pairs with it — a shampoo to go with the oil, a mask to go with the serum. Two or three products that work together are a range; ten random products are a cluttered shelf.

Let your clients guide the range. Keep a simple note of every product request that comes in on WhatsApp or at the chair. When the same request appears ten times, that is your next product.

An own-label line will not transform your salon overnight. But it is one of the few growth moves that pays you three times: a better margin on every sale, a client who thinks of your salon every morning, and a brand that grows in value along with your business. Start with one bottle, get it right, and let your clients ask for the next one.
