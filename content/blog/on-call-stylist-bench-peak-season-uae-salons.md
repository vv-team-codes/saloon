Every UAE salon owner knows the feeling. It is 7 PM on a Thursday in December, the diary is full until closing, and the phone keeps ringing. A regular wants a blowout before a party. You have the chairs and the demand, but not the hands — so you turn her away and watch the revenue walk out of the door.

The answer is not always another full-time hire — one costs a salary in quiet January as well as in busy December. The answer is a bench: a small group of trusted part-time stylists you can call on when the diary overflows. Built well, it turns your busiest weeks into your most profitable ones.

## Who belongs on your bench

A good bench is small — four to six people is enough for most salons. Three kinds of people work best.

First, experienced freelancers who already work across the city. Second, part-timers who want two or three shifts a week. Third, your own former team members. A former team member who left on good terms already knows your clients and standards.

Every person on the bench should have worked a real shift in your salon before the peak arrives.

## Find them before you need them

Peak demand in the UAE is predictable — Ramadan nights, December party season, Eid week — and the single biggest mistake owners make is starting the search when the diary is already full. A bench is built in the quiet weeks, so start six to eight weeks before your next peak.

Ask your team first. Good stylists know other good stylists, and a personal recommendation beats a cold application every time. Talk to beauty schools in your area; their recent graduates are often looking for experience.

Be honest about what you are offering. A bench role is not a full-time job, and pretending otherwise wastes everyone's time. Say plainly that it is paid per shift and that the stylists who prove themselves get called first.

## The trial shift that tells you everything

Never put a bench stylist on a paying client without a trial shift. Book her for a real busy shift — a Saturday afternoon, not a quiet Tuesday morning — and watch four things: speed, consultation, cleanliness, and calm under pressure.

Can she finish a blowout inside your normal service time? Does she ask the client questions before she starts, reset her station between clients without being told, and stay calm when the diary slips by twenty minutes?

After the shift, ask the clients. A quick "how was your stylist today?" at checkout tells you more than any CV. Keep a short note on each bench stylist so you can match the right person to the right client at short notice.

## Pay that keeps them answering the phone

A bench only works if people say yes when you call, and the most common reason they stop is pay that does not respect their time — a low day rate, or a shift cancelled at the last minute with nothing to show for it.

Agree the terms in writing before the first shift. A clear day rate is the simplest arrangement for blowouts, styling and nails; for colour and long services, commission works better with a minimum guaranteed amount for the shift. Either way, add a peak premium: a little extra for Saturday evenings, Ramadan nights and Eid week.

Always pay a call-out fee if you cancel with less than a day's notice — otherwise they will stop holding space for you.

## Scheduling the bench without chaos

Keep one person in charge of the bench — usually the manager or the senior receptionist — and a shared list the whole team can see, showing who is on it, what each person does best, and when each is available.

Use a call order, not a group message to everyone at once. Call your first choice, give her an hour to answer, and then move to the next name.

Set your notice windows in advance: a Saturday evening shift confirmed by Thursday, a Ramadan late-night shift two days ahead. When a shift is confirmed, send her the client's name, service, time and notes.

## Keeping the bench warm in quiet months

A bench goes cold if you only call in December and Ramadan. People drift away and forget your salon exists. Keeping it warm costs very little.

Offer one shift a month in quieter times and invite bench stylists to team training sessions. And pay on time, every time — a part-timer will not chase you for money when other salons pay on the day.

If a quiet month is coming, say so — a short message keeps the relationship alive; silence does the opposite.

## The one mistake that kills a bench

The fastest way to lose a good bench stylist is to treat her like a stranger. Clients notice when a stylist has no idea who they are or what they had last time — and that is yours to fix, not hers.

Before a bench shift, share the same client notes your regular team reads: last formula, preferred length, anything that matters. Introduce her by name and with confidence — "Sara will look after you today, she is one of our regular team" — and a substitute becomes a trusted stylist, often a future full-time hire.

## Your 30-day bench plan

Week one: list the three busiest weeks of your next peak and count the extra hands each needs. Week two: ask your team, suppliers and contacts for names, and invite two or three people for trial shifts. Week three: run the trials on real busy days and keep notes. Week four: agree pay in writing, share the call order, and confirm the first shifts.

A bench will not fix a salon that is understaffed all year round. But for one that runs well in normal weeks and overflows in busy ones, it is the cheapest growth you will buy.
