Every evening, salons across Dubai and Abu Dhabi lock their doors and leave a fully equipped space sitting empty for the next ten hours. The chairs and mirrors are there. The lighting is flattering, the air conditioning is already paid for, and the rent is due whether the room is used or not. For a growing number of UAE salon owners, that empty evening is starting to look like an income stream they already own.

Renting your salon space after hours is not the same as renting out a chair. Chair rental brings a stylist in during working hours. Space rental hands the whole room to someone else when your team has gone home — a photographer shooting a bridal portfolio, a henna artist running a workshop, a makeup trainer holding a masterclass, or a small brand filming a product video. They need a good-looking space for a few hours. You have one sitting unused.

## Who actually rents a salon after hours?

Freelance photographers love salons because the lighting is designed to flatter faces, and the mirrors and styling stations photograph beautifully without any set-building. Henna artists and makeup trainers need a presentable space for small groups of five to ten people, and a salon already has the seating, mirrors and lighting they would otherwise have to hire. Small beauty brands filming for Instagram or TikTok want a setting that reads as professional without paying for a studio.

The clients you want treat your salon like a venue, not a favour. They arrive on time, bring their own consumables, clean up after themselves and pay without being chased.

## Price it like a service, not a favour

The most common mistake is pricing space rental like a casual favour — a round number mentioned in a WhatsApp chat, with no deposit and no written terms. That is how you end up with a cancelled booking at 8pm and a salon sitting empty anyway. Treat the rental like any other service on your menu, with a clear price, a clear duration and a clear booking process.

Start with an hourly rate and a two-hour minimum. A compact salon in a community location might start around AED 150 to 250 per hour; a larger, well-finished salon in a mall or on a high street can reasonably ask AED 300 to 500. An evening block — say 7pm to 11pm — often sells more easily than an hourly rate, because it gives the renter certainty and gives you a bigger single payment. Whatever you charge, take a deposit of at least 50 percent at booking, with the balance due before the renter gets the keys or the access code. A deposit quietly filters out the people who were never serious. Say in writing what is included — chairs, mirrors, lighting and air conditioning usually are; your colour stock, tools and product shelves are not.

## Set rules that protect your salon

A few simple rules, shared before every booking, prevent almost every problem. First, access: who holds the key or the code, what time the space must be vacated, and what happens if the renter runs over. Second, cleaning: the space must be returned as it was found, or a cleaning fee comes out of the deposit. Third, numbers: a maximum headcount, agreed in advance, so a quiet six-person workshop does not become a twenty-person gathering your neighbours and your landlord never agreed to.

Check your lease and your insurance before your first booking. Some salon leases restrict after-hours use or require the landlord's written permission for any subletting, even for a few hours. A quick message to your landlord now is far cheaper than a problem later. The same applies to your insurance: confirm it covers third parties using the space outside your normal hours, and if it does not, ask what an extension costs before you need it.

## Start small, then let the renters market you

You do not need to turn your salon into a venue overnight. Start with a single evening a week — often a Sunday or Monday, when the diary is quietest — and offer it to one or two renters you already know. A henna artist who already works with your clients. A photographer who shot your salon's opening. Starting with people you trust means the first bookings go smoothly.

You do not need an advertising budget to fill after-hours slots. A short post on your salon's Instagram, showing the space in its best light with a simple caption — available for evening hire, message for rates — reaches exactly the freelancers and trainers who already follow you. And the renters themselves become your marketing: every photographer who shoots in your salon tags the location, every trainer who runs a class there tells her students where it happened, and every small brand that films a video there shows your space to its audience for free.

## The maths that makes it worth it

Take a modest example. Two evening rentals a week at AED 200 an hour for two hours is AED 1,600 a week, or roughly AED 6,400 a month. The extra cost is small: a little more air conditioning, a little more cleaning, and the time you spend answering messages and handing over keys. Even after a cleaning fee and the occasional quiet week, that is meaningful money for most salons — enough to cover a chunk of the rent, fund a staff bonus, or pay for the new equipment you have been putting off.

Not every salon is suited to it. If your space is hard to access after hours, if your landlord says no, or if your team is already stretched thin, the honest answer is to skip it for now. But for most UAE salons, the space is already there, the demand is already out there, and the only thing missing is a price, a set of rules and a willingness to say yes to the first enquiry. Your salon does not stop being an asset when the lights go out. It just needs someone to use it.
