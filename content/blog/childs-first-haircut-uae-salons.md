For most parents, a child's first haircut is not just an appointment. It is a milestone, up there with the first day of school — and it usually arrives with a phone full of photos, a grandparent on a video call, and a small person who has no idea what is about to happen. In the UAE, where so many families live far from grandparents, that first cut carries even more weight. Get it right and you have won a whole family; get it wrong and they quietly never return. A great first haircut is not luck — it is a small system any salon can build.

## Ask the parent three questions at booking

The experience starts before anyone walks in. When a parent books a first haircut, ask three simple questions and write the answers on the booking: how old is the child, has she ever had her hair cut before (even at home), and is there anything that worries her — loud noises, strangers, sitting still? Those three answers tell your stylist almost everything she needs to know.

Also ask whether the parents would like a keepsake of the first cut. Many families want a small lock of hair saved, and some want a photo they can send to grandparents. Agreeing that at booking means nobody has to scramble for an envelope mid-haircut. ## Book it at the right hour

Timing decides more than technique does. A toddler who has just woken from a nap, eaten, and is in a good mood is a completely different client from the same child at six in the evening, tired after nursery and hungry for dinner. Offer first haircuts in the morning, ideally as the first appointment of the day, when the salon is calm and the stylist is fresh.

Keep the slot realistic. A first cut for a small child takes longer than an adult cut, not because the hair is complicated but because the child needs pauses. Book forty-five minutes for what is really twenty minutes of cutting, and never stack a first haircut into the middle of your busiest evening run.

## The chair, the cape and the first five minutes

The first five minutes decide the whole visit. Let the child arrive and look around before anything happens. A booster seat, a chair that goes low enough for little legs, or simply sitting on a parent's lap — any of these beats lifting a nervous child into a tall adult chair and hoping for the best.

Show her the tools before they touch her hair. Let her hold the comb and hear the spray bottle. If clippers are needed, switch them on away from her head first, so the buzz is not a surprise next to her ear — and for many first cuts, scissors alone are the kinder choice. Kneel to her eye level when you speak to her, and talk to the child, not just the parent. A stylist who asks a three-year-old which side she wants to see in the mirror has already won her trust.

## Go slow, and narrate like a story

A first haircut works best as a story the child is part of, not a procedure done to her. Talk through every step in a calm, ordinary voice: "Now we are going to make the front neat, so you can see your eyes properly." Small talk is not small — it keeps her attention on the stylist's voice instead of on the scissors.

Let her watch in the mirror, and let her hold something — a comb, a toy from home, the spray bottle. Children cope far better when their hands are busy. Take breaks without making a fuss of it. A thirty-second pause to look at a picture on the wall is not lost time; it is the difference between a child who stays in the chair and one who slides off it.

## When tears come — and sometimes they will

Even with the best preparation, some children cry. That is normal, and how the salon responds is the moment the parents will remember most. Do not panic, do not rush, and never hold a child still while she is upset. Pause the cut, let the parent comfort her, and offer water, a short walk around the salon, or simply five quiet minutes.

If the child truly cannot settle, say so kindly and offer to stop: "We have done the front beautifully. Shall we finish the rest another day, on us?" A free rebooking costs the salon one short slot. Forcing the cut costs the family.

## Make the ending the memory

A first haircut should end with a small ceremony. Put the saved lock of hair in a little envelope with the date on it. Take a photo — with the parents' permission — of the child in the chair, cape on, grinning or just relieved. A simple printed certificate with the child's name and the date costs almost nothing and ends up on fridges all over Dubai and Abu Dhabi.

Send the photo to the parent on WhatsApp before they leave, so they can forward it to grandparents the same day. That one message turns the visit into a family moment shared across time zones, and your salon's name travels with it.

## The follow-up that brings the whole family back

The visit is not finished when the family walks out. The next day, send a short message: ask how the little one liked her new haircut, and whether the fringe is sitting right. If anything needs a tiny tidy-up, offer it free within the week. That message tells the parents the salon remembers their child, not just their booking. Note the details for next time — the child's name, the booster seat or mum's lap, the toy she held. When the family returns in six weeks and the stylist greets her by name, the parents stop shopping around: they have found their salon.

A child's first haircut is fifteen minutes of cutting and years of loyalty. Build the system once, and every small client who walks through your door will feel like the most important appointment of the day — because to their family, she is.
