Walk into almost any salon in Dubai or Abu Dhabi on a busy evening and you will see the same quiet problem. A stylist is deep in conversation with someone who walked in asking about a colour correction, while a paying client with a confirmed booking waits in the chair next door, watching the clock. Nobody did anything wrong. The consultation never had a slot of its own.

Most UAE salons treat consultations as something that happens in the gaps: between appointments, at the front desk, over WhatsApp, whenever someone is free. It feels generous. In practice, it is one of the biggest hidden drains on a salon's diary. A consultation that is not booked cannot be planned for, confirmed, or protected — and when it overruns, the paying appointments around it pay the price.

## Why the "quick chat" costs more than you think

A proper consultation is not a quick chat. For colour work, extensions, keratin treatments or corrective work, it means examining the hair, discussing its history, managing expectations and agreeing a price — fifteen to thirty minutes of a senior stylist's time. When it happens informally, three things go wrong.

First, it steals time from the wrong place. Squeezed between clients, it makes the next appointment start late, and the delay ripples through the evening — especially damaging when peak hours run from late afternoon into the night.

Second, it is invisible to the diary. A consultation nobody recorded cannot be confirmed, reminded or followed up. If the client does not show up, nobody notices, because nothing was ever booked. If they do show up, nobody planned for them — and the free advice they receive often walks out the door to a cheaper salon, leaving the stylist with nothing to show for the half hour.

## What a real consultation slot looks like

Booking consultations like real appointments does not mean charging for them. It means giving them the same structure as any other service: a diary slot, a set duration, a named stylist, and a confirmation step.

Start by deciding which services need a consultation. Not every haircut does. Colour corrections, major colour changes, extensions, smoothing treatments and anything where the result depends on hair history are the obvious candidates. For these, the rule is simple: no consultation, no booking. Clients in the UAE are used to structured booking, and a salon that books consultations properly looks professional, not difficult.

Give each consultation a realistic length — fifteen minutes for a straightforward colour discussion, thirty for corrections or extensions — with a buffer after it, and assign it to the stylist who will actually do the work, not whoever happens to be free. Clients trust advice far more when it comes from the person who will hold the colour brush.

## Confirmation changes everything

The single biggest difference between a casual chat and a booked consultation is confirmation. When a client receives a WhatsApp message confirming the day, the time, the stylist's name and what to bring — such as photos of their hair now and the result they want — the appointment becomes real in their mind. They are far more likely to turn up, and to arrive prepared.

A reminder the day before does the same work again. It also gives the salon a clean moment to reschedule if the client cannot make it, rather than discovering an empty chair at the appointed time. Salons that already send reminders for regular appointments often skip them for consultations, because consultations were never in the diary. For high-value work such as extensions, a small refundable deposit at consultation stage, deducted from the final bill, acts as a commitment filter.

## The consultation is a selling opportunity, not admin

Once consultations have proper slots, the salon starts treating them as the selling opportunity they are. A consultation is the moment a client is most open to advice: they have a problem — damaged hair, a colour gone wrong, a wedding in three weeks — and they are sitting in front of the person who can solve it.

Structure pays twice here. A stylist who knows a consultation is coming can prepare: check previous visits, look at photos sent in advance, and have a realistic price range ready. A stylist ambushed between clients can only improvise. The prepared consultation ends with a booked service, a price agreed and a date in the diary. The improvised one ends with "let me think about it." A short note afterwards — hair history, what was agreed, price quoted, date offered — means the salon never starts again, and makes a follow-up message a few days later possible. That follow-up converts a surprising number of undecided clients.

## Making it work in a UAE salon week

The pattern of a UAE salon week makes consultation slots especially valuable. Weekends are intense, with Friday and Saturday carrying the heaviest demand, and evenings are the peak for working clients — the worst times to absorb an unplanned consultation. Instead, place consultation slots in the quieter parts of the day, late mornings and early afternoons on weekdays, when senior stylists have breathing room. Clients who are serious about a major service will happily come at 11am on a Tuesday. Clients who only wanted a free chat will not, and that is useful information too.

The same logic applies on WhatsApp, where most UAE salon enquiries now begin. When a client sends photos and asks whether a look is possible, the reply should end with a consultation slot, not an open-ended discussion. "We would love to look at this properly — we have a consultation with Sara on Wednesday at 12:30, shall I hold it for you?" turns a chat thread into a diary entry. Threads that stay open-ended tend to drift, and drifted threads rarely become bookings.

None of this requires new software or a bigger team. It requires one decision: that a consultation is a real appointment, with a slot, a duration, a stylist and a confirmation. Salons that make that decision stop giving away their most valuable asset — senior expertise — in the gaps between paying work, and start using it to fill the diary with the clients they actually want. Within weeks, the diary shows fewer unexplained gaps, stylists run late less often, and clients arrive better prepared, because the consultation set expectations and price.
