The message lands at around 11:40 in the morning. "Hi, can I get a blow-dry and my nails done in my lunch break? I need to be back at the office by 2." The client works in Business Bay, DIFC or JLT, and her lunch hour is her only free hour. What happens next depends entirely on your reply.

Lunch-break enquiries are a distinct kind of booking request, and UAE salons get more of them every year. Office workers often have a 60-minute lunch window, sometimes less. They are trying to solve a time problem, and they will book with whichever salon gives them a clear, honest answer first.

## The lunch-break client is a different kind of client

A Saturday client has the whole afternoon. A lunch-break client has a hard deadline, a desk to return to, and no patience for a vague reply. She will not wait an hour for an answer — by then her lunch break is gone. If your reply arrives at 1:30, you have already lost her.

There is a second difference owners often miss. The lunch-break client is usually a repeat client in waiting. Office workers eat lunch at the same time every day, work in the same tower for years, and tell colleagues where they go. The value of the enquiry is not the single blow-dry. It is the routine that follows.

## The first reply decides everything

The best replies have four parts, and they fit in one short WhatsApp message. First, confirm the exact service and the time it takes. Second, name a specific slot. Third, confirm the price. Fourth, tell her what to do next. A reply that does all four looks like this: "Yes — a blow-dry takes 30 minutes. I can book you 12:30 to 1:00 today, AED 120. Shall I hold it for you?"

Skip "What time suits you?" and "Let me check and get back to you" — both push the decision back to a client who is already short on time. When she asks for a blow-dry and a manicure in one lunch break, the honest answer is that the two together do not fit. Say so plainly, then offer the combination that does: "A blow-dry and a manicure together take about 75 minutes, so we can't fit both before 2. I can do the blow-dry today at 12:30 and the manicure tomorrow at the same time — shall I book both?" That reply protects your diary from a booking that would run late, and it turns one visit into two.

## Which services honestly fit in 45 minutes

The biggest mistake is saying yes to the wrong service. A root touch-up, a full colour or a bridal trial does not belong in a lunch hour — agreeing creates a late client, a rushed stylist and a stressed afternoon.

Work out in advance which services genuinely fit a 45-minute window, greeting and payment included. In most UAE salons the list is short: blow-dry, fringe trim, express manicure, brow shaping, express facial, a quick gel polish change.

Give the midday menu a name clients can ask for — a "lunch-break blow-dry" or a "45-minute refresh" — so booking it is easy and the promise is clear.

## The travel-time question nobody asks

Here is the question most salons forget to ask: how long does it take her to get from her office to your chair? A client who says her lunch break is an hour may spend fifteen minutes of it in a lift, a car park or a metro station. Inside her office tower, a 45-minute service fits comfortably. Across town by taxi at midday, it does not.

Ask the question in your reply: "Are you in the tower next door, or coming from further away?" It takes five seconds, and it saves you from booking a visit that was never going to work. For clients further away, suggest a different slot — the first appointment of the morning, or the last one of the evening — rather than squeezing them into a lunch hour that ends in a rush.

## When the honest answer is no

Some lunch-break enquiries should end in a no, and saying it well is a skill worth teaching your team. A full balayage in a lunch hour, a bridal trial at 1 PM, colour, cut and nails before a 2 PM meeting — these are disappointments waiting to happen.

The reply that keeps the client is a no with a better offer attached. "A full balayage takes about three hours, so a lunch break won't do it justice. I can book you a Saturday morning slot with our senior colourist, or a weekday evening at 6 PM — which works better for you?" Most clients book the alternative.

## Turn one lunch break into a standing slot

The smartest move happens after the first visit, not before it. If she came at 12:30 on a Tuesday and left happy, offer the same slot next week before she leaves the chair. "Same time next Tuesday? I'll hold it for you." Office routines repeat, and a client who has found a salon that fits her lunch hour rarely looks for another one.

Keep a note against her name — office tower, lunch window, preferred service — so the second visit needs no explanation from her. She never has to repeat herself, and the visit becomes part of her week.

## A reply template you can use today

Save this template where your team can copy it, and adapt the service and time to each enquiry:

"Hi! Yes, we can fit that into your lunch break. A [service] takes [X] minutes — I can book you [time] today for [price]. It takes most clients about [X] minutes to reach us from [area]. Shall I hold the slot for you?"

Then keep three habits around it. Reply within ten minutes during working hours. Never promise a service that does not fit the window. And always offer the next visit before she leaves. She is not asking for a one-off favour. She is asking whether your salon can fit into her life — answer well, and she will keep coming back, every week, at the same time, to the same chair.
