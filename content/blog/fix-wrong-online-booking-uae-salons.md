Online booking has been a gift to UAE salons. Clients book at midnight, after the salon has closed, without a single phone call. The diary fills itself while the team sleeps. But every salon owner who runs online booking knows the other side of it: sooner or later, a client books the wrong thing. She wanted a full balayage and booked a 30-minute express cut.

It is a small mistake with a big knock-on effect: a three-hour colour service squeezed into a one-hour slot either runs over and wrecks the day, or the client gets turned away at the door. Salons that handle wrong bookings well treat them as a normal part of online booking — they spot the mismatch early and turn the fix into a better booking.

## Why wrong bookings happen

Clients do not book the wrong service because they are careless. They book the wrong service because salon menus are confusing. A first-time client in Dubai does not know the difference between a root touch-up and a full colour, or between a classic facial and a hydrafacial. She picks the name that sounds closest, or the cheapest option that mentions the word she recognises, and taps book.

Regulars do it too: a client who books a cut and finish every month suddenly wants highlights as well, and books the usual slot out of habit. Sometimes the booking is right but the duration is wrong — a client with waist-length, thick hair books the colour slot priced for shoulder-length hair. If a booking can be made wrong, the system allowed it.

## Spot the mismatch before the client arrives

The worst time to discover a wrong booking is when the client is already in the chair. The best time is the moment the booking lands. Make a quick check of every online booking part of the morning routine: does this service match what this client usually has?

A few signals give it away: a brand-new client booking a 30-minute slot for a two-hour service, a colour booking with no consultation note when your salon always consults before colour, or a regular whose booking is half her usual duration. Any of these is worth a five-minute check.

Add a note field asking "Anything we should know before your visit?" — clients will happily write "I want balayage, first time" or "my hair is very long and thick". Add a one-line description under each service name, such as "Full balayage — allow 3 hours". A client who reads that rarely books the wrong thing.

## The correction message that keeps the client

When you spot a mismatch, tell the client the same day, while there is still time to fix it. A short WhatsApp message works best: it is fast, it is where the client booked from, and it lets her reply at her own pace. The tone matters more than the words. Never write anything that sounds like "you booked the wrong service". Write as if you are looking after her.

Something like this works: "Hi Sara, thank you for booking tomorrow at 5 pm. We wanted to check — the slot you booked is our 30-minute express cut, and a full balayage usually needs around three hours. Would you like the longer slot at 4 pm with the same stylist, or Saturday morning? Either way, we have you looked after."

It thanks her, explains the reason in terms of her result rather than the salon's diary, and offers two concrete alternatives instead of a problem. Most clients reply within minutes, relieved that someone caught it.

One rule: never charge more for the corrected service without warning her first. If the right service costs more, say so in the same message — "The balayage is AED 450 rather than the AED 120 express cut, is that alright?" A surprise at the till is the fastest way to lose a client you were trying to keep.

## When the mismatch is discovered at the door

Sometimes the check fails and the client walks in anyway. She is standing at reception, the diary says 45 minutes, and the service she wants takes two hours. Turning her away is the worst option; squeezing her in is the second worst.

The answer is a calm, honest conversation at the desk. Explain the timing plainly and give her a real choice: "The balayage needs about three hours, and your slot today is 45 minutes. We can do a gloss and tidy-up today and book the full balayage for Thursday, or start now and finish tomorrow morning. Which would you prefer?" A client who is offered a choice rarely leaves angry.

If the diary has a gap later the same day, use it. If another stylist is free, offer them by name. And if the client agrees to come back, make the rebooking before she leaves, with a small gesture that says you value her time. She should remember the recovery, not the mistake.

## Fix the system, not just the booking

Every wrong booking is a clue about your menu. If clients keep booking the express cut when they mean a full colour, your service names are too similar. Once a month, review the mismatches you corrected and rename the confusing services. Add durations to service names where it helps, and put the consultation step before the booking step for colour and chemical services.

Also think about who owns the fix. In many UAE salons, online bookings land in a shared inbox and nobody is responsible for checking them. Give one person per shift ownership of new online bookings: every booking gets a two-minute check within a few hours of landing, and any mismatch gets a message the same day. ## The booking is a promise, not a form

Online booking works because clients trust that the slot they pick is the slot they get. When a booking is wrong, that trust is tested. Salons that handle it well do not treat the booking as a contract the client broke — they treat it as the start of a conversation, and finish it with a better appointment than the one the client originally made.

A wrong booking caught early is a five-minute message. Caught late, it is a lost afternoon and a lost client. Build the check into your day, correct with warmth, and fix the menu that caused the confusion.
