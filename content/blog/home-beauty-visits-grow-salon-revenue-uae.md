Every salon owner in the UAE knows the same frustration. The chairs are full on a Friday evening, but there are clients who would happily pay for your work and never set foot in your salon. New mums who cannot leave the house. Brides getting ready at a hotel. Elderly clients who find the trip to the mall exhausting. Busy professionals who would pay extra for a stylist to come to them at 7 in the morning.

Done properly, home visits are a second revenue stream that uses the same team, skills and reputation you already have. The salons winning at this in Dubai and Abu Dhabi treat it as a proper service line, not a favour done quietly on the side.

## Start with the clients who already want it

The easiest mistake is to launch a home service and then wonder who it is for. Start with the people who have already asked. Look back through your WhatsApp enquiries for the last three months. You will almost certainly find messages like "Can you come to my place?" or "Do you do home service for brides?" Those messages are your first customer list.

Three groups book home visits most reliably. The first is bridal parties, especially in the winter wedding season, where hair and makeup happen at the hotel or the family home. The second is new mothers, who book blow-dries, manicures and facials at home because leaving the house takes a full operation. The third is elderly clients and clients recovering from illness, who value a familiar stylist coming to them. All three will pay a premium over your in-salon price because you are saving them the journey.

## Price it like a premium service, not a discount

Home visits cost you more than in-salon appointments, and the price should say so honestly. There is travel time, often forty minutes each way across Dubai. There is the stylist's time away from a chair that could be earning. There is the kit to carry and the risk of a cancelled visit when the client is not home.

A workable starting point is your normal service price plus a home-visit fee of AED 75 to AED 150, depending on distance, with a minimum booking value so a stylist is never sent across town for a single eyebrow threading. Be upfront about it in the first message; clients who value the service accept the fee without argument.

Packages work better than one-off bookings. A monthly package for a new mum, or a bridal party package covering the bride plus three family members, gives you predictable income. Take a deposit on every home booking: a no-show at a client's home costs you more than one in the salon, because you have already spent the travel time.

## Protect the salon diary first

The biggest risk is that home visits quietly steal your best stylists from your busiest hours. A stylist who leaves at 5 pm for a home visit is not there for the evening rush, where most UAE salons make their money.

Draw a hard line: home visits happen in quiet windows, weekday mornings, early afternoons, and the first hour after opening. Peak evening hours and weekends belong to the salon. One person per shift decides which home requests are accepted, and nobody promises a home booking in a WhatsApp chat before checking the diary.

Start small, with one or two stylists who actually want to do home visits, on one or two days a week. A stylist who resents the travel will deliver a tired service, and the client will feel it. When demand grows, add days. You never need to offer home visits seven days a week to make them profitable.

## The kit, the setup and the small details

A home visit lives or dies on professionalism. The client is inviting you into their home, and the service has to feel like a salon experience, not a favour from a friend. That means a proper kit: clean towels, a portable chair or cushion where needed, sanitised tools opened in front of the client, and products in labelled bottles rather than unmarked containers.

Agree the practical details in advance, in writing, on WhatsApp: how much space you need, whether there is a plug socket near a mirror, and who pays for parking in a paid-parking building. These questions take thirty seconds to ask and prevent an awkward fifteen minutes of setting up in a hallway. Send a short message on the morning of the visit confirming the time, services and total price.

Check that your salon's trade licence and insurance cover services delivered outside your premises. It is dull admin, but it is the difference between a growth channel and a liability problem.

## Market it to the people who already trust you

You do not need a big advertising budget to fill home-visit slots. A short WhatsApp broadcast to your regulars, a line on your service menu, and a story showing a real home setup will bring the first bookings. Bridal parties come through referrals: one calm, professional morning at a hotel produces three enquiries from the bridesmaids.

The strongest marketing is a simple before-and-after, shared with the client's permission, captioned with the service and the area, not the client's home. "Bridal hair and makeup, Jumeirah, 6 am start" tells a future client everything she needs to know. Keep the client's privacy absolute, and never share anything that identifies where the visit happened.

## Measure it like a business line

After a month, look at the numbers honestly. How many visits did you do, and what did they earn after travel time? Which stylists enjoyed it, and did any in-salon appointments get squeezed? If the answers are good, add another day. If the numbers are thin, raise the minimum booking value or narrow the list to the three or four treatments that travel well, like blow-dries, makeup, manicures and facials.

Home visits will not replace your salon. They are the revenue you are currently turning away every time a client asks whether you can come to her, and you say no. A small, well-run home service turns those enquiries into bookings, keeps your team earning in quiet hours, and reaches clients your competitors never see. That is growth without a single new chair.
