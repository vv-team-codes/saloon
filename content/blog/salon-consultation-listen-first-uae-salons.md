Most salon visits are decided before a single strand is cut. In the first five minutes, a client in Dubai or Abu Dhabi is quietly deciding whether this is her salon now, or just a one-off visit. The service matters, of course. But the consultation is the moment she feels heard or not, safe or sold to. Get it right and the rest of the appointment almost takes care of itself.

That is why the strongest salons in the UAE treat the consultation as a service in its own right, not as the small talk before the real work starts. It costs nothing extra, takes five minutes, and is the single best investment a salon can make in keeping clients.

## Listening comes before looking

The most common consultation mistake: the stylist looks at the hair and starts talking. The colour she would choose, the layers she would add, the treatment she would recommend. It sounds expert, but to the client it feels like a decision made without her.

Flip the order. Before touching the hair, ask two or three genuine questions and then actually wait for the answers. What do you like about your hair right now? What frustrates you about it? How much time do you spend on it in the morning? The answers tell you what the client values — and what she will judge the result against when she looks in the mirror at home the next morning.

Good listening also means selling nothing yet. Clients can tell instantly when a question is a setup for a product pitch. For quieter clients, ask early whether they prefer chat or quiet — a salon that asks once never has to guess again.

## The five questions that change everything

A strong consultation does not need a long script. It needs five questions, asked in a natural order, every single time.

First, what brought you in today? Second, what do you like about your hair as it is? Third, what do you wish was different? Fourth, how do you usually style it, and how much effort are you honestly willing to put in? Fifth, is there anything you have tried before that you did not like?

That fourth question is the one most salons skip, and it is the one that prevents the most disappointment. A low-maintenance client who gets a high-maintenance cut will be unhappy within a week, no matter how beautiful the result looked in the chair. In the UAE, where humidity and hard water shape how hair actually behaves, the honest answer matters even more than the inspiration photo.

## Show her, don't just tell her

Most clients arrive with a photo on their phone. Treat it as the start of the conversation, not the instruction. Hold the phone next to the mirror, look at the photo together, and talk honestly about what translates and what does not. Different hair thickness, different natural colour, different face shape — the same picture can mean three different results.

Use the mirror while you talk. Turn the chair, show her the back, lift a section to show where the colour will sit. Clients who can see the plan are far more relaxed during a long service. Agree the price and the time out loud, in plain words, before anything begins. No one enjoys a surprise at the till.

## The honest no is the most loyal answer

Every stylist knows the request that should not be agreed to: the bleach that will break already damaged hair, the shade that will wash out in a week, the dramatic cut on hair that will not hold it. The temptation is to say yes and hope for the best. The professional move is the opposite — a kind, clear no, followed by the closest honest alternative.

"I wouldn't do that blonde on your hair today, because it would snap. What I can do is this softer balayage, and in six weeks we can go lighter." That sentence loses nothing and gains everything. It proves the stylist is on the client's side, not the sale's side. In a market as competitive as Dubai's, the honest no is often the reason she comes back — and the reason she tells her friends about you.

## Write it down, every time

A consultation that lives only in the stylist's head disappears the moment the client walks out. The salons that keep regulars for years keep simple notes: the formula used, the length agreed, what she liked, what she asked to change, the date of the next visit. It takes thirty seconds after the appointment, and it changes the next visit completely.

"Last time you said the fringe was too short, so I've kept it longer today." One sentence like that tells a client she is remembered. It is the difference between a salon and her salon. Keep the notes in one shared place so any stylist can pick them up — on the day a regular's usual stylist is on leave, those notes stop the visit feeling like starting over.

## The consultation is the rebooking moment

Here is the part most salons miss: the consultation does not end when the service begins. It ends when the next appointment is already in the diary. The stylist who listened properly knows what to say at the mirror: "This colour will be at its best for about six weeks. Shall we put you in for the same Saturday?" The client who has been heard properly says yes far more often than the client who was simply served.

## Start tomorrow

You do not need new equipment or a new menu to fix your consultations. Pick your five questions, agree them as a team, and use them on every client for two weeks. Write the notes down. Say the honest no when it is needed. Then watch what happens to your rebooking rate and your reviews.

The cut is what they pay for. The consultation is why they come back.
