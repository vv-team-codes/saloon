A new mum in Dubai messages your salon at 11 pm. She has not had her hair cut in months, and she has exactly one window tomorrow: between the baby's morning feed and the next nap, about ninety minutes if everything goes to plan. Her message is short, but it carries four questions at once. Can she bring the baby? Will the appointment actually finish on time? Is it safe to colour her hair while breastfeeding? And will anyone judge her for cancelling at the last minute if the baby will not settle?

This is one of the most common enquiry types a UAE salon receives, and one of the most mishandled. The usual reply — a price list, or a cheerful "yes, come anytime" — answers none of it. A new mum does not need a menu. She needs to know whether the visit will work, in her real life, on her real day. Salons that answer that question well win a client who comes back every few weeks for years.

## What she is really asking

Every new-mum enquiry is three enquiries in one. The first is about time: her day runs on feeds and naps, and she cannot promise to be anywhere for three hours. The second is about the baby: can he come, where will he sit, and what happens if he cries. The third is about safety: colour while breastfeeding, treatments after a C-section, hair shedding in handfuls. If your reply only covers one of the three, she keeps looking.

The fastest way to lose her is to overpromise. Telling her a full balayage takes ninety minutes when it takes two and a half hours does not buy you a booking; it buys you a client who leaves halfway through with foils in her hair and never returns. New mums remember exactly which salons respected their time — and they tell their mum groups and nursery chats.

## The four-part reply that books her

The reply that works has four parts, and it fits in one WhatsApp message: name the service that fits her window, give the honest time including processing, answer the baby question plainly, and offer two specific slots.

It might read like this: "Yes, you can bring the baby — we have a quiet corner where the pram fits beside you. A root touch-up and blow-dry takes about 75 minutes, and we can split the drying so you can feed halfway through. Would tomorrow at 10:30 or Thursday at 11:00 work better?"

That message gives a real duration instead of a hopeful one, answers the baby question before she has to ask it twice, and ends with a choice between two times. A tired mum at midnight can tap "tomorrow 10:30" with one hand. She cannot negotiate a diary.

## Services that honestly fit a new mum's window

Not every service suits a ninety-minute window, and saying so is a strength, not a weakness. Root touch-ups, gloss treatments, express facials, brow and lash work, and blow-dries are honest fits. Full colour transformations, balayage and keratin are not — book those for a day when someone else has the baby, or split them across two shorter visits.

The salons that do this best keep a small "new mum menu" in mind, even if it never appears on the wall: a 30-minute express facial, a root touch-up with a blow-dry, a trim and style. Each one has a real duration attached, and the team knows which stylist is fastest at each. When she asks "what can I get done in an hour?", the answer is immediate and specific.

## Bringing the baby: say it plainly

The baby question is where most salons lose the enquiry. "Babies are welcome" sounds warm, but it tells her nothing. Answer the picture in her head: the pram in a narrow aisle, the baby crying mid-colour, other clients staring.

Say where the pram goes, and whether there is a quiet chair to feed in, away from the main floor. Say what happens if the baby cries — that the stylist will pause and she can step aside for ten minutes without losing her slot. And say the honest limit too: if your salon has no room for a pram during the 7 pm rush, say mornings are better for a visit with the baby, and offer one. "We will look after you both" is a sentiment, not a plan.

## Postnatal hair and skin: honest answers only

The second half of the enquiry is usually about her hair. Postnatal shedding is normal, and most new mums have read enough to be worried about it. She wants to know whether colour is safe while breastfeeding and whether a treatment will help the shedding.

Answer like a professional, not a doctor. Colour and breastfeeding is a question for her doctor, and the honest salon answer is to say exactly that, then offer what you can do: a patch test, a gentler option, a consultation before the booking. Never promise that a treatment will stop shedding. Offer what is true: a good cut that makes thinning less visible, a scalp treatment that feels wonderful, and a realistic timeline for regrowth.

## The follow-up that turns one visit into a routine

The visit itself is only half the job. A message the same evening — "How did the colour settle? Hope the little one slept well tonight" — costs nothing and is remembered for weeks. A note in her client record — feeds around 10 am, prefers mornings, baby comes along, partner has the baby on Saturdays — means the next booking starts from knowledge, not from scratch.

And the next booking should be offered before she leaves. "Your roots will need us in about five weeks. Shall I hold Thursday the 18th at 10:30 — you can move it if the week changes?" A held slot she can move is a gift when her week is unpredictable.

New mums are not a niche. In the UAE, where so many families are far from their own mothers, the salon is often the first place a new mum does something for herself again. Answer her first message with honesty, a real plan and a real time, and she will bring her friends, her sister and, in a few years, her daughter.
