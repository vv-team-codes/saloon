It usually arrives on a Thursday evening, as the weekend diary fills up. A regular client messages your salon on WhatsApp: "Can my friend come too?" She has a blow-dry booked for Saturday at 5 PM, and her friend would like the same. It looks like a small question, but it is one of the best messages a salon can receive: a new client arriving with a personal recommendation already attached. How you answer decides whether you gain a regular or lose a chair.

Most salons answer badly in one of two ways: they say yes without thinking and run late all evening, or they say no too quickly and the friend books elsewhere — often taking your regular with her next time. The right answer is a warm yes, with a clear plan, given fast.

## Why the plus-one message is a buying signal

A client who brings a friend is doing your marketing for you. In the UAE, so much of salon life runs on personal recommendation that a plus-one request means your salon has already passed the hardest test.

It also tells you about timing: friends want to come together, same day, same hour, side by side — a Saturday afternoon, a weekday evening, or a morning before a wedding, the hours your diary is already under pressure. Treat it as a two-person booking from the start, not an extra squeezed into a gap.

## The three questions before you say yes

Before you confirm anything, get three answers in one friendly message.

First, what does the friend want? A blow-dry, a manicure, a full colour? "The same as me" is not an answer — two blow-dries fit in one hour, a blow-dry and a full colour do not. Second, does the friend want the same time, or just the same day? Some are happy to come an hour later; others want the chairs side by side. Third, is this a one-off treat or the start of a routine? One question — "Is she thinking of coming regularly, or is this a one-off treat?" — tells you how much diary effort the pair is worth.

Ask these three things in your first reply and you will almost never have to correct yourself. Clients forgive a salon that asks a good question; they do not forgive one that confirms and then cancels.

## Pairing chairs without breaking the diary

If the services take the same time, book a linked pair with two stylists starting together. Say so in the message: "We have you both with Sara and Aisha at 5 PM, side by side, finishing together at 6." That sentence does more than confirm a booking — it tells the client you have actually thought about her visit.

If the services take different lengths, be honest about it. "Your blow-dry finishes at 6, and your friend's colour will need until 6:45 — she can relax with a coffee while she waits, or come at 5:45 and you will finish together." Clients accept a staggered plan when it comes with a solution.

One rule: never promise side-by-side chairs until you have checked two stylists are free at that hour. A promise made on WhatsApp at 9 PM, when the diary is not in front of you, is the most common way this goes wrong. Replying after hours? Say "Let me check in the morning and confirm both chairs by 10 AM" — and then do it. A fast honest hold beats a fast wrong confirmation.

## Pricing the second chair honestly

The awkward part is money, and WhatsApp makes it worse because everything is in writing. State the friend's price in the same message as the confirmation, with a range if the figure depends on hair length or the exact service. "Her blow-dry is AED 120 to 150 depending on length" prevents a difficult conversation at the till.

Resist the urge to discount the friend's service. A free blow-dry teaches her your work is worth less than the menu says, and teaches your regular that bringing a friend earns a discount she will expect every time. If you want a gesture, make it small and memorable: a complimentary express manicure while her colour processes, or a coffee and a head massage. The friend remembers how she was treated far longer than she remembers a price cut.

## The friend becomes a regular

The real prize is not the second booking on Saturday; it is the second regular by January. Treat the friend's first visit like the first visit it is. Take her details, note her preferences, and send her — not just your regular — a short WhatsApp message the same evening: "Lovely to meet you today — shall I keep the same Saturday slot open for you next week?" That one message turns a plus-one into a client of her own.

It also protects you: if the friend only ever books through your regular, she is your regular's guest, not your client. The day your regular moves away or changes salons, the friend goes with her. A direct relationship, built in one short message, keeps her with you.

## When the answer has to be no

Sometimes you genuinely cannot fit two people in: the diary is full, the stylist they want is on leave, or the chair is not free. Say so quickly and offer the nearest real alternative — a different day, a different time, or one chair now and one later the same day. "Saturday at 5 is fully booked, but I can do you both on Sunday at 11, side by side — shall I hold it?" keeps the pair together, which is what the client actually wanted.

Never leave the message unanswered until the morning, or reply with a bare "fully booked" and nothing else. A plus-one request is a client handing you her friend. Answer it like it matters, because it does.

Handled well, the plus-one message is the cheapest new-client channel a salon in the UAE has: it costs nothing, it arrives already trusted, and it doubles in value when the friend becomes a regular of her own. All it takes is a fast reply, three good questions, and a diary that treats two people as two bookings.
