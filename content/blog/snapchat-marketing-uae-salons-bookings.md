Ask a UAE salon owner where new clients come from and you will hear the same answers: Instagram, Google, word of mouth. Almost nobody mentions Snapchat. That is the opportunity. Snapchat reaches millions of people in the UAE every day, and its audience is almost exactly the clients salons want most: women aged 18 to 40 who discover beauty trends on their phones and decide quickly. While every salon in your area fights for the same Instagram feed, Snapchat has far fewer competitors.

Beauty is visual and fast, which is why the platform suits salons. A client does not need a ten-minute video to judge a balayage. She needs three seconds of a real result, a price, and a way to book. Snapchat is built for exactly that kind of moment: quick, candid, and gone tomorrow.

## Your Story is your shop window

The simplest place to start is your Snapchat Story. Post every day, but keep it real. A stylist sectioning hair before a colour, the mirror reveal after a facial, a finished set of nails under the salon lights — these show real work on real people, not stock photos.

A Story that appears every day, even with just two or three snaps, keeps your salon at the top of your followers' screens. Post in the morning, and again in the late afternoon.

Always end a Story sequence with a next step: a simple "DM us to book" or a swipe-up link to your booking page turns a viewer into an enquiry.

## Lenses and filters that sell the service

Snapchat's lenses and filters are the feature most salons ignore, and the one with the most potential. A sponsored geo-filter around your salon lets every client who snaps inside share your name with her friends, for free. It costs little, and it reaches the people most likely to visit: her friends nearby, who already trust her taste.

Branded lenses go a step further. A simple lens with a soft glow or a "fresh from the salon" frame turns a client's own selfie into an advert for your work. Clients love sharing these because they flatter them. Keep it tasteful: a lens that changes someone's face beyond recognition undermines the trust a salon sells. The best salon lenses enhance, they do not disguise.

## Snap Ads that send people straight to booking

When you are ready to spend, Snap Ads are straightforward: a short vertical video, six to ten seconds, showing a transformation, the price and the location, and a swipe-up that goes directly to your booking page or WhatsApp. No long captions, no complicated funnels.

Targeting is where the money is saved: a salon in Jumeirah can reach women nearby, in the age range that books its services, who already follow beauty content. Start with a small daily budget — the cost of one or two coffees — and run the ad for a week. Watch one number only: how many people swiped up and booked. If the cost of one booking is less than the profit on that service, the ad pays for itself. If it does not, change the video, not the budget: a dull first two seconds is almost always the problem.

Evening works best: clients browse after work, between seven and ten, and decide then to book for the next day or the weekend. Run your ads hardest in that window.

## Spotlight: free reach for your best work

Spotlight is Snapchat's short-video feed, and it is the closest thing the platform has to a discovery engine. A striking before-and-after, a satisfying hair wash, a nail transformation in fast motion — these clips travel. You do not need a big following for a Spotlight video to reach thousands nearby. You need one genuinely good clip and a caption that says what the service is, how long it takes, and what it costs.

Post your best work to Spotlight once or twice a week. The clips that perform best have a clear payoff in the first second: the reveal, the colour change, the finished nails.

## Turning a snap into a booking

Snapchat only works if the path from snap to chair is short. When someone messages after seeing a Story or an ad, reply within minutes. In the UAE that message usually arrives on WhatsApp, where the client expects the same speed. A reply that comes the next morning often finds she has already booked somewhere else.

Keep the reply simple: confirm the service, give the price range, offer two specific times, and ask which one suits her. Do not send a long menu and ask her to choose — clients book with the salon that makes it easy. ## A simple first-week plan

You do not need a strategy document. Set up a Business account under your salon's real name, with a clear profile photo of your logo or your best work. On day one, post three Story snaps from a real appointment, with permission, and end with a booking prompt. On day two, create a geo-filter for your location. On day three, post your best transformation to Spotlight. On day four, try your first small Snap Ad. By day seven, check what happened: which Story got the most views, which snap brought messages, whether anyone booked. Keep the winners, drop the rest, and repeat.

## What to measure

Ignore follower counts — the least useful number on Snapchat. Watch three things instead. First, Story views: are more people watching than last week? Second, messages and swipe-ups: is anyone acting? Third, bookings that mention Snapchat: ask every new client where she found you, and write it down. If after a month Snapchat has brought you even a handful of returning clients, it has earned its place in your marketing.

Snapchat will not replace Instagram or Google for your salon. It does not need to. It is the channel your competitors have not bothered with yet, full of clients who love beauty content and make quick decisions. A daily Story, a few honest transformations, and a booking link that works is enough to start. The salons that win new clients in the UAE are not always the ones with the biggest budgets. They are the ones that show up where their clients already are.
