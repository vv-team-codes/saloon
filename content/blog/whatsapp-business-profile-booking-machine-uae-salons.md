Most salon clients in the UAE never make a phone call. They find you on Instagram or Google, tap the WhatsApp button, and land on your business profile before they type a single word. That small screen — your photo, a line of text, your hours, your location — is doing the work of a receptionist, a shop window and a first impression all at once. And in most salons, it is completely neglected.

Think about it from the client's side. She has just moved to Dubai Marina and wants a balayage before the weekend. She finds three salons and taps each WhatsApp button. One profile has no photo, no description and no opening hours. Another has a clear photo, a short line saying "Ladies salon in JLT — colour, nails and facials", opening hours, and a catalogue she can browse right there. Which one gets her message? The second one, every time. The booking is often decided before the first message is ever sent.

## Your profile photo is doing more work than you think

The profile photo is the first thing a client sees, and in a chat list full of contacts it is also what makes your salon recognisable later. A blurry logo cropped from a flyer, a photo of an empty chair, or a picture of a stylist who left two years ago all quietly work against you.

Use a clean, bright photo that reads at thumbnail size: the salon name in a readable font on a plain background, or a warm photo of your actual salon interior. Real photos beat stock images because they tell the client she has found a real place.

## The description line that answers before they ask

The short description under your business name is the most underused space in salon marketing. Many salons leave it blank, or fill it with a phone number the client already has. Instead, use it to answer the three questions every new client is silently asking: what do you do, where are you, and who is it for?

A good line reads something like: "Ladies salon in Business Bay — hair colour, nails, facials and bridal. Open daily 10 am to 9 pm." That is one sentence, and it already tells a client whether you are the right salon. A ladies-only salon should say so here, plainly. A salon in a mall should name the mall. A salon that specialises in curly hair or keratin should say so, because the client searching for exactly that will stop the moment she reads it.

## Hours, location and the pin that saves the booking

Dubai clients plan around traffic, parking and prayer times, and they ask about location constantly. Your WhatsApp Business profile has fields for business hours and an address — fill them in properly, and they answer those questions before they are asked.

Set your real hours, including the Friday pattern and any difference between weekdays and weekends. Add your address with the building name, the area and the nearest landmark, because "near the metro" is not a direction. A client who can see the pin, the hours and the services in one place has no reason to hesitate, and hesitation is where bookings quietly die.

This matters most in the evening, when the peak enquiry wave runs from about 7 pm to 11 pm, long after the front desk has gone home.

## The catalogue is your silent receptionist

The WhatsApp Business catalogue is where your profile stops being a signpost and starts being a shop. Each item can carry a photo, a name, a price range and a short description — and clients can tap any item and message you about it directly, with the item attached to their message.

That changes the conversation completely. Instead of "How much is a balayage?", you get "How much is the full balayage listed here, and can I book it on Saturday?" The client has already chosen; she is asking you to confirm. Price your catalogue honestly, with ranges where the final price depends on hair length or condition, and keep the photos real — your own work, on your own clients, with permission. A catalogue with six well-photographed services will book more chairs than one with forty entries and no photos.

Keep the catalogue short and current. When a price changes, change it in the catalogue the same day. When you add a seasonal service — a Ramadan glow facial, a National Day bridal package, a summer keratin offer — add it to the catalogue first, because that is where clients who already have your number will find it. And remove services you no longer offer: nothing damages trust faster than a client booking a service from your catalogue and being told it does not exist.

## Let the profile do the routine work

Every time you find yourself typing the same answer — your location, your price for a gel manicure, whether you take walk-ins — ask whether that answer belongs in the profile instead. The profile handles the routine so your team has time for the real questions: can you match this colour, is this safe for my hair, can my sister come too?

And when a message does arrive after hours, a 24/7 AI receptionist like ZYNA can answer it on the spot, drawing on the same clear information your profile already shows, so the client gets a helpful reply at midnight instead of silence until morning.

## Keep it current: the monthly five-minute check

A WhatsApp profile is not a one-time job. Prices change, stylists leave, hours shift for Ramadan, and a new service launches. Put a five-minute profile check on the calendar once a month, ideally on the first Monday. Open your own profile the way a client would and look at it with fresh eyes: is the photo still accurate, are the hours right, does the catalogue show what you actually sell?

Your WhatsApp profile is the first page of your salon that most clients will ever see. Treat it like the front of your shop: clean, clear, honest and always open. The salons that win the most bookings in the UAE are rarely the ones with the biggest advertising budgets. They are the ones whose clients never have to wonder, never have to ask twice, and never have to wait to find out whether this is the right salon. Make your profile do that work, and the messages that follow will already be halfway to a booking.
