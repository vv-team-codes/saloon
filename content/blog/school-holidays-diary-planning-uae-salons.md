Every UAE salon owner knows the pattern. One week the diary is calm. Then the school holidays begin, and the same diary turns into a scramble: mums booking last-minute haircuts for the children, regulars squeezing in a colour before a flight, and a queue of messages asking whether anything is left on Thursday evening.

School holidays do not just bring more clients. They change who books, when they book, and what they book. A salon that plans for them fills chairs at premium times. One that does not spends the break turning people away while regulars book elsewhere.

## Know the calendar before your clients do

The UAE school year repeats its rhythm: a half-term break in autumn, a long winter break in December, a spring break, and the long summer holiday when many families leave the country. Add the shorter breaks around National Day and Eid. Families plan travel around these dates months ahead. Your diary should too.

Put every school break on your wall calendar a full term in advance. Mark the last three days before each break and the first three after it. Those six days are your real peak. Before the break, everyone wants to look good for the holiday: cuts, colour, nails, facials, the full routine. After it, the families who stayed behind want the same, plus the children need haircuts before school starts again.

## Open the diary earlier for break weeks

In a normal week, opening the diary four weeks ahead is plenty. For a school-break week, it is too late. Regulars start thinking about pre-holiday appointments six to eight weeks before the break, often the moment flights are booked. If those slots are not open, many will quietly book somewhere that can.

Open break-week slots eight weeks ahead, and tell your regulars you have done it. A WhatsApp message works: the half-term slots are open, and the days before the break fill first. Your best clients get first pick of the times they already wanted.

It also protects your team. A diary that fills gradually over six weeks lets you plan staffing, stock and breaks. One that fills in a panic over three days lets nobody plan anything.

## Plan for the family booking, not the single chair

School holidays change the shape of a booking. A mum who normally comes alone for a cut and colour now brings two children who need haircuts, or books a family block: her colour, her daughter's trim, her son's cut, all in one visit. These bookings are worth more than a single appointment — but only if the diary can hold them together.

Keep a few family-sized blocks in the diary for break weeks: two adjacent chairs for two hours, or a morning where one stylist handles the children while another does the colour. A family that books together and leaves happy will rebook together next break. One split across three days because the diary could not hold them will not.

Children's cuts deserve a thought of their own: quick, profitable, and they bring the parent into the salon. Put them in the mid-morning and early-afternoon gaps adults rarely want, and keep peak evening hours for longer adult services. A ten-year-old does not need the 7 pm slot. A working mum getting a balayage before her flight does.

## The empty week in the middle is real — plan for it

Here is the part owners forget. After the pre-break rush, many families leave the UAE, and for a week or two in the middle of a long break the salon can go quiet. Chairs sit empty and the owner wonders what went wrong. Nothing went wrong. The clients are simply abroad.

Plan for the quiet middle instead of being surprised by it. It is the best time of the year for the things a busy diary never allows: deep cleaning, a stocktake, team training, or staggered leave so everyone is fresh for the post-break rush. A quiet week you planned for is a useful week. One that catches you by surprise feels like a crisis.

The return week needs the opposite plan. Families come home, school starts, and everyone wants a fresh cut or a colour refresh. Open the diary for the return week at the same time as the pre-break week, and send one reminder the day before school restarts.

## Protect your team through the peaks

School-break peaks are intense, and they sit on top of an already busy UAE salon calendar. The week before a break is often followed by a festive or wedding season, with no real pause in between. A team that works flat out for three weeks makes mistakes, gets sick, and starts dreading the diary.

Stagger the team through the peak. Split the late finishes so each stylist gets at least one normal evening in the break week, and build a proper lunch break into the diary rather than letting it be eaten by walk-ins. Put one person in charge of the diary each day — the one who decides what can be squeezed in and what genuinely cannot. When everyone owns the diary, nobody does, and the day falls apart by noon.

## Keep the diary honest when demand spikes

When demand is high, the temptation is to promise everything: one more colour, one more walk-in, one shorter service. A few extra bookings feel like free money. They are not. A colour squeezed into a gap that does not exist runs over, the next client waits, and the one after that cancels. One over-promised day costs more in lost regulars than it earns in extra bookings.

Be honest about what the diary can hold in break weeks. When a slot is gone, say so quickly and offer a real alternative: a different day, a different stylist, or a shorter service that fits. Clients accept a no when it comes with a plan. What they do not forgive is a yes that turns into a forty-minute wait.

School holidays will come around again in a few months. The salons that do best are not the ones with the most chairs. They are the ones that saw the break coming, opened the diary early, shaped it around families, planned for the quiet middle, and protected the team. That is not luck. It is a diary planned one term ahead.
