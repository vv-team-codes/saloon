She did not find you on Instagram or message you on WhatsApp. She opened a booking app, typed "balayage near me", and your salon appeared between three others with a price, a star rating and a free slot at 6.30. She tapped, paid a deposit and arrived on a Thursday evening. She is a real client in your chair — and yet she is not quite yours. The app holds her number and her booking history. You hold a commission bill. If she liked the service, her next booking will probably start in the same app, and next time the salon at the top of the list might be someone else's.

Marketplace apps bring real new clients, but an app client is loyal to the app first. Winning her is about the hour she spends with you, and the days after she leaves.

## The first visit has to do the work of three visits

A regular gives you months to earn her loyalty. An app client gives you one visit. She has no history with you, no favourite stylist, no reason to return beyond the result in the mirror. So the first visit has to carry the weight of three: a great service, a personal connection and a clear next step.

Start before the service begins. A short consultation about her hair, her routine and what she did not like about her last salon tells her this is not a production line. If she mentions she works in DIFC and only has evenings free, that is the beginning of a client record the app will never build for you.

Finish the visit the same way. Do not let her leave with only a "see you next time" — give her a date: "Your roots will need attention in about five weeks. Shall we put the 7th of January in the diary now, same time?" A concrete date turns a pleasant visit into a plan.

## Ask for her details like a person, not a form

The app will not hand you her number. Ask when the reason is obvious: "I will send you the aftercare steps for this colour on WhatsApp so you have them at home — what number should I use?" She says yes because the message is useful to her.

Keep it to one channel and one promise. Ask for WhatsApp, explain exactly what you will send — the aftercare, the rebooking reminder, nothing else — and then keep that promise. One useful message builds trust; a broadcast list she never asked for destroys it. If she declines, accept it gracefully. Pushing for a number is the fastest way to make sure she does not come back.

## Give her one honest reason to book direct

Never ask a client to leave the app because you want to save the commission. She does not care about your commission. Give her a reason that benefits her: direct booking means she can reach the same stylist, ask a question before she books, and choose the exact slot she wants. The person answering knows her hair, her colour history and her last visit. That is a better service, and it is true.

Be careful with the rules. Marketplace apps have terms about moving clients off their platform, and some will penalise a salon caught soliciting. Do not hand her a card that says "book direct and save", and do not offer a discount for leaving the app. The honest route is slower but safe: deliver a visit worth returning for, and let the next booking happen naturally on WhatsApp when she messages you herself.

Make your own booking path as easy as the app's. If your WhatsApp replies take four hours, the app wins, because the app answers instantly. Speed is the app's advantage — take it away, and the salon's advantage, a person who knows her, starts to count.

## The follow-up that makes her yours

The message she gets the day after her visit decides whether you are a salon she used once or a salon she belongs to. Send it the next morning, while the result is still fresh. Keep it short and specific: the aftercare you promised, one line about how the colour will settle, and the date you suggested for her next visit. End with a question she can answer in one word. "Shall I hold Thursday the 7th at 6.30 for you?" is a booking. "We hope you enjoyed your visit" is a dead end.

If she replies, you have won what the app could never give you: a direct conversation. Answer fast, confirm the booking, and note the details — her stylist, her usual time, the products she uses. If she does not reply, wait. One gentle message a week later with a real slot is fine. A third message is chasing, and chasing pushes her back to the app.

## Make the second visit the loyalty moment

A client who comes back a second time is far more likely to become a regular than one who visits once. So treat the second visit as the real conversion, not the first. Greet her by name. Remember what you talked about — a wedding, a job interview, a holiday — and ask about it before you ask about her hair. The app knows her booking history. You know her life. That is the difference she can feel.

## Count them separately, or you will never see the leak

If you do not measure it, you will keep paying for the same client again and again. Once a month, count three numbers: how many clients arrived through an app, how many booked a second visit directly with you, and how many are still coming six months later. The gap between the first two is the real cost of the app.

Marketplace apps are not the enemy — in a city where thousands of new residents arrive every month, the app is often the first introduction. But an introduction is not a relationship. Treat her like a regular from the first minute, and the app's client becomes yours. She has seen every other salon in the list. She picked yours twice.
