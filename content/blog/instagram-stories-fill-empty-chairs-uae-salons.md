Reels bring your salon new eyes. Stories bring those eyes into your chairs. Most UAE salon owners pour all their energy into Reels and treat Stories as an afterthought — a repost here, a random photo there. That is the wrong way round for filling tomorrow's diary. A Reel is watched by strangers who may never visit. A Story is watched by the people already following you, the ones who live nearby, know your work and are one tap away from booking. And because a Story disappears in 24 hours, it creates exactly what a quiet diary needs: a reason to act now.

The evening scroll is your best friend here. Between 7 and 10 pm, your followers are on their phones deciding how tomorrow looks. A Story posted at 8 pm that says "one chair free tomorrow at 11 am for a blow-dry, reply to grab it" reaches people at the precise moment they can say yes.

## The "one chair left" Story

Start with the simplest Story that books: the gap. Every day, look at tomorrow's diary and find the one or two empty slots. Photograph the chair, the mirror, the station — the real salon, not a stock image — and write the time, the service and the price range in big, readable text. Add a "DM to book" line, and nothing else. No hashtags, no ten lines of caption, no link that takes people somewhere else.

This works because it is specific. "Blow-dry, tomorrow 11 am, from AED 120, reply to book" is a decision a client can make in five seconds. "We have appointments available this week" is not.

## Polls and questions that do the selling for you

Stories have built-in tools that Reels do not, and the poll sticker is the strongest of them. Ask "Blow-dry or colour this weekend?" on a Tuesday, and by Wednesday you know which service to promote — and you have a list of people who already raised their hand for it. Follow up personally: "You picked colour — we have Friday 4 pm free, shall I hold it for you?" That message converts far better than any broadcast, because the client chose the topic.

The question sticker works the same way. Once a week, post "Ask us anything about your hair" and answer three questions with short video replies.

## The countdown that creates a deadline

Urgency is the one thing a Story can do that a permanent post cannot. The countdown sticker turns a vague offer into a deadline. Set one to Friday 6 pm for a weekend package or the last Saturday morning slot, and Instagram reminds every follower who tapped it when the countdown ends — and reminds them of your salon.

Use countdowns sparingly, once or twice a month, so they stay meaningful. When the countdown ends, post the result — "all five slots gone" — because nothing sells the next offer like proof that the last one sold out.

## Behind the chair: the trust Stories

Not every Story should sell. The ones that build trust are the quiet ones: a 10-second clip of a stylist mixing colour, a finished blow-dry from three angles, the salon at opening time with the lights on. Clients choose a salon they feel they already know, and these small, honest glimpses answer the question every new client asks silently: will I feel comfortable here?

Keep these clips real and unpolished. A shaky phone video of a real transformation beats a perfect graphic every time. And never film another client without their clear permission.

## Highlights: your always-open shop window

A Story disappears in 24 hours, but a Highlight stays. Treat your Highlights as the front page of your salon. Five is enough: Services and prices, Transformations, Reviews, Offers, and Visit us. Every Story worth keeping goes into the right Highlight the same day, so a new follower who finds you at midnight can see your prices, your work and your happy clients.

Update the price Highlight whenever prices change. Nothing loses a booking faster than a client who arrives expecting last year's price because your Highlight still shows it.

## A simple weekly Story rhythm

You do not need to post ten Stories a day. You need a rhythm your followers can rely on, taking about fifteen minutes a day. Monday: one poll about the week ahead. Tuesday: a behind-the-chair clip. Wednesday: a before-and-after transformation, with the service and time it took. Thursday: the weekend diary — two or three open slots, named times, reply to book. Friday: a question sticker, answered with short video replies. Saturday: the salon in full swing. Sunday: a tidy of Highlights, and one gap Story for Monday morning. The diary becomes the content plan, and the content plan fills the diary.

## Measure replies, not views

Instagram will show you how many people viewed each Story. Ignore that number. The number that matters is replies, because a reply is a booking conversation starting. Track three things each week: how many replies your Stories got, how many turned into a booking, and which type of Story got the replies. Within a month you will know what your followers respond to — and you can do more of it and drop what does not work.

One warning: the replies will not all arrive during opening hours. Many will come at 10 or 11 pm, when your followers are scrolling in bed. A reply left until the next morning is often a booking lost to the salon that answered first. ## The mistakes that kill Story bookings

A few habits quietly destroy Story marketing. Posting a Story with no clear next step — no reply prompt, no time, no price — is the biggest one. So is reposting someone else's content all day; your followers followed your salon, not a mood board. Avoid tiny text that cannot be read on a phone, and avoid posting ten Stories at once. One good Story beats ten forgettable ones.

Stories are the closest thing a salon has to a conversation with the clients who already like it. Use them to show the real salon, ask real questions, name real times and prices, and answer fast when someone replies. The chairs you fill tomorrow are sitting in tonight's Story viewers.
