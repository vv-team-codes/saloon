She has not even sat down yet and the phone is already out. A quick photo of the entrance for her story, a selfie in the mirror while the colour develops, a short video of the blow-dry for her friends. In a Dubai or Abu Dhabi salon, this is no longer unusual. It is the normal way many clients experience their appointment.

For years, salon owners treated phones as a nuisance. The reality has changed: the phone is now part of the visit, and salons that handle it well get clients who leave happy, post about it, and bring their friends.

## The phone is part of the visit now

Start by accepting what is already true. Most clients arrive with their phone in their hand, and many will use it during the appointment. During a two-hour colour service, clients answer messages, watch videos and photograph their hair at every stage.

The client filming her transformation is not being rude. She is excited — and about to show her whole circle what your salon can do.

The mistake is fighting the habit. Asking clients to put phones away, or sighing when they take a photo, creates exactly the tension a good salon experience is supposed to avoid.

## Set a simple, kind phone policy

You do need rules, but they should be short, clear and kind — and they should protect other clients, not punish the one holding the phone.

The rule that matters most is about other people. No filming or photographing other clients without asking. A client taking a selfie of her own hair is fine. A client sweeping her camera across the room and catching the woman in the next chair mid-facial is not. Put it in the appointment reminder and mention it kindly when the camera comes out.

The second rule is about the service itself. Some moments need the client's hands free and her head still — a precise cut, a lash application, a brow treatment. Your stylists should be able to say, honestly and warmly, "Give me five minutes for this part and then you can film the reveal." Clients accept this easily when it is framed as care for their result, not as a ban.

The third rule is about calls. A client on a loud speakerphone call during a relaxing facial ruins the experience for everyone in the room. A quiet request — "Would you mind taking calls outside or on your earphones?" — solves it in seconds.

## Make the selfie moment easy

If clients are going to photograph your work, make it easy for them to get a good photo. This is one of the cheapest client experience upgrades a salon can make.

Good light is the whole game. A mirror with clean, flattering light — the kind you already use for colour checks — is where the best client photos happen. Keep that area tidy, keep the background clean, and keep clutter out of the frame. A beautiful photo taken in your salon is marketing you did not have to pay for.

Wi-Fi is the other half of it. Slow or password-locked Wi-Fi frustrates clients trying to share in the moment. Put the password where clients can see it — on the mirror or the reception desk — and make sure the connection holds through the whole salon. The same applies to charging: a charging point near the waiting area removes one more small irritation.

When the result is ready, do not rush the reveal. Step back, let her take her photo, and say "Take your time, the light is lovely here."

## When filming meets other clients' privacy

The trickiest part of the phone habit is not the filming client. It is the client in the next chair who did not agree to be in anyone's video.

Some clients value privacy deeply, whether for personal, cultural or professional reasons, and a salon that protects it earns real loyalty. Others are happy to appear in a video. The only way to know is to ask.

Train your team on one simple habit: before a client films anything that might include someone else, check. "Is it alright if you're in the background of her video?" takes five seconds and prevents the complaint that follows when you do not ask. For regulars, note their preference in the client record, the same way you note their colour formula or their drink order.

## Turn the habit into bookings

Once the basics are handled, the phone habit becomes an opportunity rather than a problem.

The simplest version costs nothing. When a client posts a photo or video from your salon, thank her — a comment, a repost with her permission, a message saying how lovely the result looks. Clients who feel appreciated for sharing will share again — and their followers are exactly the audience you want.

Make sharing easy without being pushy: a small card at the mirror with your salon's handle, a gentle mention at checkout. Never pressure a client to post, and never offer a discount for a post at the chair — the posts that bring bookings are the genuine ones.

## A small checklist to start this week

You do not need a new policy document or a salon refit. Start with five small changes this week.

First, agree on one line your whole team uses about filming other clients. Second, put the Wi-Fi password somewhere visible and check the connection in every corner of the salon. Third, tidy the mirror area where clients take their after photos, and keep it tidy every day. Fourth, add a note to client records about who is happy to be filmed and who is not. Fifth, thank the next client who posts about your salon.

None of this costs much. All of it tells your clients the same thing: we have thought about how you actually live your visit. That kind of attention is what clients remember — and what they come back for.
