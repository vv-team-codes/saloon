She landed at 7, checked into her hotel by noon, and by 3 o'clock she is messaging your salon. She is in Dubai for four nights and wants a blow-dry for tonight, maybe a manicure tomorrow. Underneath it all is one question: can you make this worth one of her few free holiday hours?

A regular gives you months to earn her trust. A tourist gives you one visit. There is no second appointment to fix a colour that went wrong. The greeting, the consultation, the result, the goodbye — all of it has to land in a single afternoon. Salons that design for this earn recommendations carried home to people planning their own Dubai trip.

## Why the holiday client is different

She is comparing you with the version of this trip she imagined, not the salon she left behind. She has already spent money on flights and a hotel, so price matters less than certainty — she would rather pay a little more for a blow-dry she can trust than gamble on the cheapest option and spend her evening fixing it herself.

She is also working to a timetable she did not choose. Dinner is at eight, her desert safari leaves at three tomorrow, her flight home is on Tuesday. When she asks whether you can fit her in, she is really asking whether you can fit her into the holiday, not just into your diary. And because she is far from home, every small friction feels bigger — a confusing address, a slow reply, a chair that is not ready. At home these are annoyances; on holiday they feel like the day going wrong.

The opportunity is just as real. A tourist who leaves delighted tells people and posts the photo with your salon tagged. One perfect visit can quietly produce a dozen enquiries from people who have never set foot in the UAE.

## The first message matters more than ever

Most holiday visitors find you the same way: a late-night search, a map listing, a friend's recommendation, a reel that caught her eye while she was still packing. The enquiry usually arrives on WhatsApp, often late in the evening, and often with a photo attached.

Speed matters, but clarity matters more. She does not know your neighbourhood, your building, or your parking situation. The reply that wins her answers everything in a single message: yes, we can do this; here is the price range; this is how long it takes; here is the nearest landmark and the parking answer; and here are two times that work. Three follow-up questions to get the basics, and she will message the next salon instead.

This is also where after-hours cover earns its keep. Holiday visitors message when they are free, which is often after your team has gone home — on the plane, in the hotel lobby, over breakfast before a day out. A 24/7 AI receptionist like ZYNA can answer those messages the moment they arrive, confirm the details, and hold the slot, so the client wakes up to a booked appointment instead of a read receipt and silence.

## Make room for the suitcase

The details that make a tourist feel welcome are small and physical. She may arrive straight from the airport with a cabin bag, or straight from the beach with damp hair. A salon that has thought about this has a place for her bag that is not the floor beside a stranger's feet, a hook for a jacket, and a freshen-up corner before the service begins. Starting with a proper wash, offered warmly and without comment, resets the visit and tells her she is welcome exactly as she arrived.

Language deserves the same quiet thought. A tourist's English may be her second or third language, so consultation cards with photos and a mirror check at each stage prevent misunderstandings a local regular would catch in conversation. Show, do not only tell.

## Honest results in one visit

The hardest part of the holiday visit is honesty. A tourist may ask for a transformation that needs two or three sessions: a full colour correction, a dramatic cut on damaged hair. With a regular, you can plan the journey. With a tourist, the temptation is to say yes and hope for the best, because there is no next visit to lose.

Saying the honest thing is what earns the recommendation. Explain what one visit can genuinely achieve, show her the closest version, and offer the best single-session result instead of the impossible one. She will remember the salon that protected her holiday photos far longer than one that attempted the impossible and left her hiding under a scarf.

The same honesty applies to time: if a service needs three hours, say so before she books, not when she is already in the chair watching the clock. A salon that starts on time, finishes on time, and warns her early if a service runs over is a salon she will describe as professional when she tells the story at home.

## The goodbye that travels home

The last five minutes are where a one-visit client becomes a story. Walk her to the door rather than waving from the till. Make sure she has the photo she wants — good light, a clean background — because that photo advertises you in her home city. Offer the small practical things a traveller values: a card with your salon's name and location, and a note of the exact shade or service so her own stylist at home can match it.

Then let her go without pressure. Do not push a package she cannot use or a membership she cannot attend. The loyalty you want from a tourist is not a second appointment. It is a recommendation, a tag, a message to a friend that says "go here when you're in Dubai" — worth more than a discount voucher she will throw away at the airport.

Dubai welcomes millions of visitors a year, and a good share of them will sit in a salon chair while they are here. The salons that win them treat a single visit as a complete experience — fast, honest answers, comfort from the moment she walks in, a result she can trust in one sitting, and a goodbye that makes her want to tell someone about it. Get that right, and every holiday season fills your chairs with clients found through the best marketing there is: a happy traveller telling the truth to people already planning their trip.
