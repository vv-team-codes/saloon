Walk into almost any busy salon in Dubai or Abu Dhabi this month and you will find the same pattern: the smoothing chair is booked solid, often weeks ahead, while ordinary blow-dry slots still have gaps. Keratin treatments, hair botox and other smoothing services have quietly become one of the most reliable revenue lines in UAE salons — not because of a viral trend, but because the local climate makes frizz a daily problem for almost every client with wavy, curly or colour-treated hair.

For a salon owner, this is a trend worth understanding: smoothing carries higher ticket prices than a cut or a blow-dry, brings clients back on a predictable cycle, and is exactly the kind of service clients research before they book. The salons winning this work explain it honestly, price it clearly and look after the client after the appointment ends.

## Why the UAE climate sells smoothing for you

Dubai's humidity is the best salesperson a smoothing service could ask for. A client can leave the salon with a perfect blow-dry at 10 in the morning and watch it puff up by lunchtime. Add hard water, daily heat styling and regular colour work, and hair that looked manageable back home becomes a constant battle here. That is why so many clients arrive already convinced they need smoothing — they have lived with the problem for months.

You do not need to convince anyone that frizz is frustrating. You need to show that you understand the difference between the options: a keratin treatment that relaxes the hair and cuts styling time, a hair botox treatment that fills and softens without straightening, and a full straightening service for clients who want a genuinely sleek finish. Explained in plain language, those differences build the trust that wins the booking.

## The consultation is the service

The biggest mistake salons make with smoothing is treating it like a standard booking. It is not. A keratin or botox treatment takes two to four hours, costs several hundred dirhams, and changes the hair for months. Clients who book it blind are the ones who leave disappointed.

The salons doing this well build a short consultation into the process — ten minutes at the visit, or a WhatsApp exchange the day before, covering what the client's hair is like now, what she has had done to it, what result she wants, and how much daily styling she will do. A client with fine, colour-treated hair needs a different product and a different expectation than a client with thick, virgin curls. Saying so before the treatment starts is what turns a one-off booking into a loyal client.

Honesty about results is part of the consultation too. A keratin treatment tames frizz and makes hair far easier to manage, but it does not turn tight curls into pin-straight hair, and it does fade after three to five months. A client who knows that, and what will make it last longer, will come back for the next one. A client who was promised permanent silkiness will not.

## Pricing it without the awkwardness

Smoothing is one of the services where vague pricing hurts most: a client who messages "how much is keratin?" and gets no answer will message the next salon.

The clearest approach is a price range by hair length and thickness, shared on WhatsApp before the booking is confirmed. Short hair from a stated figure, medium hair a little higher, long or very thick hair higher still — with the final price confirmed at the consultation. That range filters out clients who were never going to pay it, and makes the clients who do book feel the price is fair because nothing was hidden.

It is also worth pricing the aftercare honestly. Sulphate-free shampoo and a proper mask are not optional extras after a keratin treatment; they are what protects the client's investment. Salons that include a small aftercare kit in the price, or sell the right products at the chair with a short explanation, protect the result and add retail revenue at the same time.

## The diary problem smoothing creates — and solves

A three-hour treatment is a diary challenge — two back to back leaves a stylist no room for anything else. But smoothing services are usually booked in advance, rarely cancelled, and repeat on a predictable three-to-five-month cycle.

Smart salons use that predictability. They book smoothing appointments into the quieter weekday hours, when the chair would otherwise sit empty, and keep the busy evening and weekend slots for quicker services. They also book the next treatment before the client leaves — a simple "shall we put you in for February?" at the mirror turns a one-off visit into a recurring diary entry.

This is also where a fast reply matters. Smoothing enquiries often arrive late at night, when a client is scrolling and frustrated with her hair. A salon that answers that evening, with the price range and two real appointment options, wins the booking. One that replies the next afternoon often finds the client has already booked elsewhere. ## Aftercare is where the repeat booking lives

The treatment ends when the client leaves, but the result depends on the next six weeks. Clients who wash with the wrong shampoo, tie their hair up too soon or skip the mask will watch the smoothness fade early — and many will blame the salon rather than the aftercare.

The salons with the best smoothing retention send one short WhatsApp message the next day: a quick check-in, the three aftercare rules in plain words, and an open invitation to ask anything. It protects the result, shows the client she is remembered, and keeps the conversation open for the rebooking message a few months later. That message is also the moment to ask for a photo of the result — a real before-and-after from a local client is the most convincing smoothing advert a salon can post.

The frizz-free boom is not a passing fashion. It is a response to the climate your clients live in every day, and it rewards the salons that treat it as a proper service: honest consultation, clear pricing, careful diary planning and real aftercare. Get those four things right and smoothing becomes a steady, high-value part of your business.
