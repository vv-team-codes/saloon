Every salon owner in the UAE knows the pattern. For most of the year the diary fills steadily — and then, about ten days before Eid, the phone does not stop. Clients who have not visited in months suddenly need a blow-dry, threading, henna and a mani-pedi, all before the same weekend. The salons that come out of Eid week with full chairs are rarely the ones with the biggest discounts. They are the ones that started marketing the rush three weeks early.

## The pre-Eid rush is different from any other busy week

In the final ten days, henna demand explodes and peaks in the last two or three nights. Threading and waxing bookings double. Blow-dry chairs run from opening to closing. Mothers book for themselves and their daughters in the same visit, and many clients bring a sister or a friend along. Men book haircuts and beard trims in the final two days, often as walk-ins.

That mix is your marketing material. A salon that posts "Eid offers — 20% off" competes on price with every salon in the neighbourhood. A salon that posts "Henna nights from 7pm, family blow-dry packages, and the last threading slots before Eid" sells exactly what people are already searching for. Name the services, the days and the times. Clients in a rush do not want to decode an offer — they want to know whether you can fit them in.

## Start marketing three weeks out, not three days out

The most common mistake is waiting until the rush is visible. By then, the clients who plan ahead have already booked elsewhere, and you are left fighting over the last-minute crowd.

Three weeks before Eid, send one clear WhatsApp message to your regulars: Eid dates, your extended hours, and the three or four services that book out first. End with a booking link or a simple reply — "Reply EID and we will hold your slot." Two weeks out, post your Eid packages with real photos of last Eid's work. One week out, switch to urgency: which days are nearly full, and the last slots for threading.

Make booking effortless: put your booking link in every message. If bookings come through WhatsApp, make sure someone — or a 24/7 AI receptionist like ZYNA — answers late-night enquiries the same evening, because a large share of Eid enquiries arrive after 9pm, once the family has sat down and made its plans.

## Build Eid packages people can say yes to in one message

Packages work for Eid because clients are buying several services at once and want one price, one appointment and one decision. Build them around real combinations, not random bundles.

A family package might cover a blow-dry for mum, henna for two daughters and threading for everyone, booked as one back-to-back block. A "ready for Eid" package might pair a facial with a blow-dry and a manicure, scheduled across two visits so the facial has time to settle. A henna-only package can offer a simple design for younger clients and a fuller design for those who want it, with the price difference stated plainly.

Give every package a price range, so a client can forward your message to her sister without needing to ask a follow-up question. Resist deep discounts: Eid demand is real, and a salon that fills its diary at full price earns more than one that fills it at half price and burns out its team.

## Henna night is your best marketing event

If your salon offers henna, treat henna night as an event, not just a service. Announce the dates early, name the artist, and show her work. A short video of henna being applied, posted a week before, will do more than any discount poster — clients share it in family groups, and that is exactly the audience you want.

Run henna in the evenings, when demand peaks, and keep a few walk-in slots alongside the booked ones. Have a simple price list visible — hands only, hands and feet, bridal — so nobody has to ask. Capture the booking on the spot: a client who leaves saying "I will message you later" may message another salon first.

## Extended hours need a plan, not just longer days

Almost every salon extends its hours before Eid, and almost every salon regrets doing it without a plan. Longer days without staggered shifts mean tired stylists, rushed services and mistakes on the busiest nights of the year.

Decide your extended hours two weeks ahead and publish them. Stagger start times so the team is freshest in the evening, when the real rush hits, and protect at least one short break per shift. Hold back a few slots each day for regulars, released in the morning, so the clients who keep your salon alive all year are not turned away.

Publish the hours everywhere — Google profile, Instagram bio, WhatsApp Status and the salon door. A salon that states "open until 11pm from the 27th" wins bookings from one that simply says "open late".

## Capture the last-minute wave without chaos

No matter how well you plan, the final 48 hours will bring a wave of last-minute enquiries — clients whose original salon let them down, and people who only just decided they want a blow-dry for Eid morning. This is where a waitlist earns its keep.

Keep one simple waitlist, ordered by time of enquiry, and message people the moment a slot opens — with the exact time and service, not a vague "we have space". Post same-day availability on WhatsApp Status in the afternoon and remove it once the slot is gone. Keep answering after hours: the 11pm enquiries the night before Eid are often the most valuable, because those clients will take whatever slot you offer.

## After Eid, turn the rush into regulars

The marketing does not end when Eid does. The clients who visited for the first time during the rush are the most valuable outcome of the whole season — but only if you follow up. Send a short thank-you message the day after Eid, and offer them their next appointment before they drift back to their old salon. A simple "Lovely to see you before Eid — shall we book your next blow-dry for next week?" converts more first-timers than any discount voucher.

That is the real measure of a good Eid campaign: not how full the diary was for one week, but how many chairs are still filled a month later.
