She bought six sessions in January, full of good intentions — paid in full. Then life happened: school runs, work trips, a summer away. Now the package expires on Sunday, three sessions unused, and your client has no idea. That conversation could have been avoided with three short WhatsApp messages.

Prepaid packages are one of the healthiest things a UAE salon can sell. They bring cash in upfront and give you a reason to see a client again and again. But their value is only realised when the sessions get used. Every unused session is a missed appointment and a missed chance to sell home care. Worse, a client who feels she wasted money rarely buys another package. She quietly tries the salon down the road instead.

## Why packages expire unused

It is rarely about the money or the treatment. Most clients who let a package lapse actually liked the service. They simply forgot, and the salon never reminded them.

There are three usual reasons. First, the client bought in a burst of motivation and the habit never formed. Second, the gap between sessions is long enough to forget: four weeks between laser appointments or six weeks between facials is plenty of time for life to intervene. Third, the salon has no system at all. The expiry date sits in a notebook nobody checks, and nobody owns the job of chasing it.

## The three reminders that actually work

You do not need to bombard anyone. Three well-timed messages are enough.

The first goes out four weeks before expiry — a friendly check-in, not a warning: "Hi Sara, hope you're well! A quick note — you have three sessions left on your facial package, which runs until 15 December. Would you like me to book your next one? We have Thursday at 6 PM or Saturday at 11 AM free." She just picks a time.

The second goes out two weeks before expiry, only to clients who have not booked: "Hi Sara, a gentle reminder — your facial package has two sessions left and expires on 15 December. Shall I hold Saturday at 11 AM for you?" The word "hold" makes the slot feel reserved for her, and gives her a reason to reply quickly.

The third goes out three days before expiry — warm, but clear: "Hi Sara, your package expires on Sunday and you still have two sessions left. I can book you tomorrow at 5 PM or Saturday at 10 AM. Would either work, or shall we move your remaining sessions to a different treatment?" That final question gives her an honest way out if the original treatment no longer suits her.

## What every reminder message needs

Every reminder needs five things: the client's name, the package name, the sessions left, the expiry date, and two real appointment slots. Leave one out and the message gets weaker. Without the session count, she does not know what is at stake. Without the expiry date, there is no reason to act now. Without two slots, she has to start a back-and-forth to find a time — and every extra message is a chance to drift away.

Send them as personal messages, never as a broadcast. A broadcast about package expiry feels like marketing, and clients ignore marketing. A one-to-one message that mentions her name, her package and her dates feels like care, and clients answer care. Keep the tone human. No capital letters, no "URGENT" or "FINAL NOTICE". A client who feels scolded will not rebook. A client who feels looked after will.

## Timing it to the Dubai week

In the UAE, the best window is the early evening, roughly 7 PM to 9 PM, when clients are home, planning the next day and actually looking at their phones. Avoid Friday midday, when many clients are at prayer or with family.

Summer needs special handling: many UAE residents travel for weeks in July and August, and a package that expires mid-holiday will quietly die while your client is abroad. Flag packages expiring in summer before your clients fly, and offer to pause them or bring sessions forward. In Ramadan, send reminders earlier and offer the late-evening slots that fill up fastest.

## The expiry conversation, handled well

Have the policy decided before you need it, and state it clearly when the package is sold.

The fairest approach most UAE salons use is simple: one short extension, offered once, when the client asks in good time. A two-week grace period costs almost nothing and buys a great deal of goodwill.

## Running it in ten minutes a week

Once a week — Friday morning works well, before the weekend rush — one person opens a simple list. A spreadsheet is enough: client name, package, sessions remaining, expiry date. Sort by expiry date. Anyone expiring within four weeks gets the first reminder that week. Anyone inside two weeks who has not booked gets the second. Anyone inside three days gets the last call. Ten minutes a week is all it takes once the list exists.

## The quiet payoff

The return shows up in places you might not expect. Chairs fill: sessions that would have expired become appointments in quiet weekday hours, exactly the slots that are hardest to sell. Retail sells: a client in the chair for her fourth facial is far more likely to buy the serum you recommend than one who never came back after session two.

When a client gets a message that says, in effect, "we noticed, we remembered, and we saved you a slot," she feels like a regular, not a transaction. That feeling turns a package buyer into a client who books for years and recommends you in the group chat. In a city where a new salon opens every week, being the salon that remembers is a genuine advantage.

So this week, open the diary and look for the packages expiring in the next month. Your clients have already paid for those sessions. A few honest messages are all it takes to make sure they get to enjoy them.
