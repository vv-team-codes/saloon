It usually arrives on a Tuesday morning, on WhatsApp, from a name you do not recognise. "Hi, I'm the office manager at a company in Business Bay. We have a team of 18 ladies and we'd like to arrange hair and nails for them. Can you do our whole team?"

It is a wonderful enquiry and a slightly frightening one. Say yes badly and you double-book your regulars and undercharge by thousands of dirhams. Say no, or take three days to reply, and the company books the salon down the road instead.

Corporate and group beauty bookings are a real and growing enquiry type for UAE salons. Hotels, airlines, banks and offices all run staff events and wellness days, and many would rather send their team to one trusted salon than scatter them across the city. The salons that win this work are not the biggest ones. They are the ones that answer fast, ask the right questions and make the organiser's job easy.

## The first reply decides everything

The person messaging you is rarely the person getting the treatment. She is an office manager, a HR coordinator or a personal assistant, comparing you with two other salons while her boss waits for a quote.

Reply within the hour, and answer the three questions hiding inside her message before she has to ask them: can you take a group this size, when could you do it, and roughly what will it cost. A reply that says "Yes, we can look after 18 ladies. We can offer Thursday 10am to 2pm as a dedicated block, with hair and nails, from AED 180 per person. Shall I send a simple plan?" will beat a reply that says "Please call the salon" every single time. If you cannot take the whole group in one block, say so in that same message and offer the honest alternative: two smaller blocks on consecutive days. Organisers respect a clear no with a workable alternative far more than a vague yes that falls apart later.

## Five questions before you quote a price

Never quote a group price until you know five things.

First, how many people, and is that number confirmed or an estimate? "Around 15 to 20" is not a booking. Ask for a confirmed headcount and a date by which it becomes final.

Second, what does each person actually want? A group that all wants a blow-dry and a manicure is one morning's work. A group where half want balayage and a third want facials is a different salon day entirely. Ask the organiser to collect a simple list of names and services.

Third, when must everyone be ready? A team that needs to be at a gala by 7pm has a hard deadline, and your last appointment must finish by 5pm at the latest, with buffer. Work backwards from the ready-by time, not forwards from opening.

Fourth, where will it happen? Most corporate groups come to your salon, but some ask for a hotel ballroom or an office. Off-site work means travel, setup time and kit, and a different price. Decide in advance whether you offer it at all.

Fifth, who pays, and how? One company invoice, each person paying individually, or a mix of the two? Get it in writing before the day, with a named contact and a payment deadline.

## Price it like a package, not a pile of discounts

The biggest mistake salons make with corporate enquiries is treating the group rate as a discount for volume. A group of 18 is not 18 individual clients who happen to arrive together. It is a block booking that takes your chairs out of circulation for half a day and needs extra staff and product.

Price from your normal service menu, then build the group offer honestly. A simple structure works well: a per-person price for a set menu of two or three services, a clear list of what is included, and upgrades priced separately. "Blow-dry and classic manicure, AED 180 per person, with gel polish available at AED 40 extra" is a quote an organiser can approve in one email.

Avoid blanket percentage discounts. A 20 percent discount on 18 people is a large amount of money to give away for work you would have filled anyway during a busy week. Instead, add value that costs you little: a welcome drink, a dedicated coordinator, or priority rebooking for the team afterwards.

Confirm the price in writing, with the headcount, the date, the time block, the services and the payment terms in one message.

## Protect your regulars while you host the group

A corporate block can quietly damage the rest of your week if you let it. Your regulars have standing appointments, and nothing upsets a loyal client more than discovering her Thursday slot vanished because a company booked the whole salon.

Block the diary deliberately. Choose the quietest realistic window for the group, usually a weekday morning, and keep your peak evening hours untouched. If the group needs more chairs than you can spare, split it across two days rather than cancelling regulars. Assign one person to own the group from first message to final payment, and brief the team on names, services and timing the day before.

On the day itself, treat the group like 18 first visits happening at once. The organiser is watching how her colleagues are treated, and every one of those colleagues is a potential regular who lives or works nearby. A warm welcome, a named stylist for each person, and a smooth finish matter more than any decoration. Note who had what, so colleagues who return feel remembered.

## The follow-up that turns one booking into a contract

Most salons send a thank-you message and move on. The smart ones follow up twice. The evening of the event, message the organiser: thank her, ask how the team felt, and confirm the invoice and payment. A week later, message again with a simple offer for the team: a priority booking link for anyone who wants to return, or a standing monthly slot if the company runs regular events.

Keep it repeatable: one saved first reply, one checklist, one price structure, one owner per booking. When the next "Can you do our whole team?" message arrives, you will have an answer ready in ten minutes and a plan ready in an hour.
