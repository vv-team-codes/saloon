Every week now, the same message lands in the salon inbox. A client wants blonde, or lighter, or brighter — but she cannot come back every three weeks to keep it up. She works long hours, she travels, and she does not want a harsh regrowth line two weeks later. What she is asking for, whether she knows the name or not, is lived-in colour. And across the UAE, it is quietly becoming the most requested colour service of the year.

Lived-in colour is not one technique. It is a way of thinking about colour: soft, blended roots, gradual lightening through the lengths, and a finish that grows out gracefully instead of announcing itself. Roots stay close to the client's natural shade, brightness sits mostly from the mid-lengths down, and there is no hard line anywhere. Eight weeks later, it still looks intentional. That is the whole point.

## Why the UAE made this trend inevitable

The UAE climate does half the explaining. Sun, humidity, sea water and hard water all fade colour faster than a client expects. A solid, high-maintenance blonde that looks perfect on day one can look tired by week four. Lived-in colour is built to absorb exactly that kind of wear. Because the root is already soft, fading and regrowth blend in instead of standing out, and the colour keeps looking good for much longer.

Lifestyle does the rest. Plenty of UAE clients split their year between here and home, take long summer trips, or simply cannot book a colour appointment every three weeks around work and family. A service that needs touching up every eight to twelve weeks fits their real life. That is why enquiries keep coming from clients who used to book full-head colour — and why salons that offer it well are booking weeks ahead.

## What it does to the diary

For the salon, lived-in colour changes the shape of the working day. The first appointment is longer, often two and a half to three and a half hours, because a good result needs a careful consultation, a root melt or shadow, lightening through the lengths and a gloss to finish. That is a big block of diary, so it has to be priced and scheduled like the premium service it is.

But the payoff is a calmer diary afterwards. Instead of a client returning every three weeks for root touch-ups, she comes back every eight to twelve weeks, often combining the visit with a cut or a treatment. Chairs free up, the evening rush spreads out, and the stylist who did the original work has time to take on a new lived-in colour client rather than repeating the same maintenance slot.

## Pricing it honestly

The mistake salons make with this trend is pricing it like a normal colour. The first visit costs more — more product, more time, more skill — and clients accept that when they understand what they are getting. What they do not accept is discovering the price halfway through, or being charged for a full colour and then told to come back in three weeks anyway.

The honest way to price it is to quote the full service as a package: consultation, colour, gloss and a blow-dry, with a clear range rather than a single number, because hair length and thickness change the work. Then give the maintenance price separately, so the client can see that the follow-up visits are cheaper and further apart. Including a home-care set with the first appointment makes the higher price feel complete, and the right shampoo and weekly mask keep the tone from turning brassy in the UAE sun.

## Who it is not for

Lived-in colour is not right for everyone, and saying so is what makes clients trust you. A client who wants platinum blonde, a sharp solid colour, or a dramatic change in one sitting will not be happy with a soft, gradual result. Very dark hair that wants a lot of lift needs two or three sessions, or the result will be patchy and the hair will suffer.

The consultation is where this is decided. Ask to see photos of what the client likes, look at her hair in natural light, and be direct about how many sessions it will take and how the colour will grow out. A client who walks away because you were honest will often come back. One who was promised the impossible will not — and she will tell her friends why.

## How to sell it without overpromising

The trend sells itself on one image: a client four weeks after her appointment, hair still looking soft and blended, no harsh root line in sight. That is the photo to post, not the fresh-from-the-chair shot on day one. Real grow-out photos in daylight are the most convincing marketing for this service, because they prove the promise instead of just making it.

When a client messages to ask about it, answer with what she actually wants to know: how long the appointment takes, what it costs, how often she will need to come back, and whether it suits her hair. A short voice note or a few photos of recent work answer faster than a price list, and they start a conversation that ends in a booking. ## The maintenance conversation that keeps clients coming back

The service is not finished when the client leaves the chair. The clients who come back are the ones who were told exactly how to look after the colour: a sulphate-free shampoo, a purple or toning product used once a week rather than every wash, heat protection before styling, and a gloss or toner booked at the halfway point instead of a full recolour. A short message a week later, asking how the colour is settling, turns a one-off visit into a routine.

Lived-in colour is not a passing fashion. It is a response to how clients in the UAE actually live — busy, sun-exposed, travelling, and tired of colour that demands more time than they have. Salons that do it well, price it honestly and talk about it plainly are offering a service that fits their clients' lives — and that is the kind clients come back for.
