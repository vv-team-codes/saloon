It happens in every salon in the UAE. A regular messages at lunchtime to move her 4 pm colour to tomorrow. A bride's mother shifts the whole family's appointments by a day. A client who booked three weeks ago asks to come an hour earlier, then changes her mind again by evening. Life in Dubai moves fast, plans change, and a salon diary that cannot bend will eventually break.

The problem is not the reschedule itself. It is what a reschedule does to the rest of the day. A moved appointment leaves a gap that is hard to fill at short notice, or quietly turns a fully booked evening into a half-empty one. Salons that handle this well do not have fewer reschedules. They have a system for them.

## First, decide what a reschedule actually is

The first step is to stop treating every change the same way. A client who moves her appointment from Tuesday to Wednesday is very different from one who cancels at 6 pm and asks for 7 pm the same evening. Write down three simple categories and let your team use them.

A **planned move** is a change made more than 24 hours ahead. These are easy. The freed slot has time to be filled, and the client's new slot can be chosen properly. A **short-notice move** is anything inside 24 hours. These need a decision, because the gap may not fill. A **same-day swap** is a client asking to come earlier or later on the same day. These are usually the easiest to say yes to, as long as the stylist's diary has room.

When the team knows which kind of move they are dealing with, they stop guessing and start responding consistently.

## Answer fast, and answer with a new time

The worst reply to a reschedule request is silence, and the second worst is "let me check and get back to you" with no follow-up. In the UAE, most reschedule requests arrive on WhatsApp, often outside working hours. The client is already rearranging her day around your answer.

Reply quickly, even if the answer is not final yet. A short message — "Of course, let me find you the closest time" — keeps the client with you. Then come back with a concrete alternative, not an open question. "Would Thursday at 5 pm or Friday at 11 am work better for you?" is far stronger than "When would you like to come instead?"

## Protect the freed slot before you confirm the move

Here is the mistake most salons make: they confirm the client's new time first and only afterwards look at the hole left behind. Do it the other way around. Before confirming, check what the freed slot is worth. A 45-minute gap at 11 am on a Tuesday is hard to fill. A two-hour colour slot at 6 pm on a Thursday will be snapped up by your waitlist. The harder the slot is to fill, the more care the move deserves.

Keep a short waitlist. When a slot opens up, message the first person on it within minutes, not hours. "A 5 pm slot just opened with Sara today — shall I hold it for you for 30 minutes?" turns a reschedule into a booking instead of a loss. Salons that do this well rarely have a gap that stays empty for long.

## Set a fair limit, and say it kindly

Some clients reschedule often, and a few reschedule every single time. This is usually not rudeness. It is a busy life, a shifting work schedule, or a family that changes plans constantly. Still, a salon cannot run on a diary that is rewritten daily.

A simple, fair rule works well: two free moves per appointment, and after that, the next booking needs a small deposit to hold the slot. Frame it as protection for the client's own time, not as a punishment. "So we can keep your stylist and your slot reserved for you, we ask for a small deposit after two changes" is honest and easy to accept. Tell clients about the rule when they book, not when they break it — a line in the booking confirmation message is enough.

## When the client moves, move the whole appointment

A reschedule is not just a new time on the diary. It is a chain of small things that need to move with it. The reminder message must go out at the right time for the new slot. The stylist needs to know before she starts preparing for a client who is no longer coming. If a deposit was taken, it must follow the booking, not sit against a cancelled slot.

Small salons lose money on this detail more than on anything else. A client arrives for a rescheduled appointment and finds no one expecting her. A reminder goes out for the old time, and the client turns up on the wrong day. Build one habit: when a booking moves, the person who moves it checks the reminder, the stylist, and the deposit in the same minute. It takes thirty seconds and saves hours of repair work.

## Keep a weekly eye on the pattern

Reschedules are data. Once a week, spend ten minutes looking at them. Which clients move most often? Which services get moved the most? Do moves cluster on certain days — Thursday evenings, Saturday mornings, the week before a school holiday?

## A calm diary is a service in itself

Clients judge a salon by how it handles the awkward moments. Anyone can take a booking. The salons clients stay loyal to are the ones that handle a changed plan gracefully — a quick reply, a sensible alternative, no sighing, no guilt. The client who moved her appointment three times and was treated kindly every time will still recommend you to her friends.

Reschedules will never disappear. In a city where people work late, travel often and juggle family across time zones, they are part of the business. The goal is not to eliminate them. It is to build a diary that absorbs them without drama, and a system that fills the gaps they leave behind.
