Most salon owners put all their energy into the service itself — the cut, the colour, the facial — and then treat the goodbye as an afterthought. The client pays, the stylist says "see you next time", and the door closes behind her. But ask any regular what made her loyal and she will rarely start with the haircut. She will mention something small: a note in her bag, a sample of the serum used on her. In a market as crowded as Dubai, Abu Dhabi or Sharjah, a good service gets you into the running — the little extra is what gets you remembered.

## The moment most salons waste

The walk from the chair to the door is the last impression a client takes home, and most salons let it pass without a thought. The stylist is already turning to the next client, the receptionist is already answering the phone, and the client is left to find her own way out. Compare that with the salon that walks her to the door, hands her bag over with a smile, and slips in one small thing she did not ask for. Same service, same price — yet the second visit feels completely different.

The effect is strongest in the UAE because so many salon visits here are squeezed between other things: the school run, the office, the mall, the gym. A parting touch is the one part of the visit that slows her down for a second and makes the experience hers — and in a city where word of mouth moves fast, she will mention that note to a friend in a way she will never mention a haircut.

## What a good parting touch looks like

The best parting touches are small, personal, and connected to the visit she just had. These are the ones that work in UAE salons.

**A handwritten thank-you card.** A small card with her name, the service she had, and one line in the stylist's own handwriting. "Thank you for trusting us with your colour today — see you in four weeks." It takes twenty seconds to write.

**A sample of the product you actually used on her.** A sachet of the mask, the serum or the oil that is still in her hair right now. It answers the question she is already thinking on the way home: what did they put on my hair? When she tries it at home and likes it, she comes back for the full size — and she comes back to you.

**A note for her next visit.** A line on the card with the date her colour will need a refresh, or the week her nails will need a fill.

## Keep it small, keep it honest

A parting touch only works if it feels like a gift. The moment it feels like a sales pitch, it does the opposite of what you want. Never put a discount voucher in the bag and call it a gift — that teaches clients to wait for offers instead of booking because they love the salon. And never give every client the same thing; a colour client does not need a nail oil sample, and a facial client does not need a hair mask.

Match the touch to the visit. A first-time client gets the warmest version — a card and a sample — because the first visit is when she is deciding whether to come back. A regular gets variety, a different sample each time, so the gesture never becomes wallpaper.

## Make it part of the routine, not a favour

The reason most salons never do this is that nobody owns it. The stylist assumes reception will do it, reception assumes the stylist will, and the client leaves with nothing. Fix that by making the parting touch a written step in your closing routine, like wiping down the station or confirming the next booking. The stylist writes the card while the client is at the till, and the receptionist drops in the sample and hands the bag over with a smile.

Record what you gave her. A line in the client notes — "sample: argan serum, 25 November" — takes five seconds and stops you handing her the same sachet twice. ## What never to put in the bag

There are a few hard rules, and they matter more than the idea itself. Never include a sample of a product a client is allergic to, or one that clashes with a treatment she just had. Check the client notes first — a client who mentioned sensitive skin, a pregnancy, or a reaction last time should get a card only, never a product. Never include anything expired, opened, or half-used, and never anything that needs a long explanation. If the gift needs instructions, it is the wrong gift.

## The cost — and what it returns

A sample sachet costs a few dirhams; a plain card costs less than a dirham a visit. Even a salon seeing forty clients a day can run this for the price of one no-show a week. The return is not measured in the gift itself but in what it does to the next booking. Try it for one month: give the parting touch to every client for four weeks, and track the rebooking rate against the month before. The difference is largest among first-time clients — the group every UAE salon spends the most to win and loses the fastest.

The December party season is the perfect time to start. Chairs are full, regulars are bringing friends, and a client who leaves with a small gift in her bag is far more likely to mention your salon at the party she is getting ready for. That is marketing no advert can buy.

## Start this week

You do not need a new supplier, a new system or a team meeting to begin. Put a basket of samples and a stack of cards at reception, write the parting touch into your closing routine, and try it for one week. The salons clients remember are the ones that made her feel, right at the door, that she was more than an appointment in the diary.
