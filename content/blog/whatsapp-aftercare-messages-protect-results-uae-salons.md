She left your salon glowing. The colour was exactly what she asked for, the blow-dry was perfect, and she promised to tell her friends. Three days later she messages you, upset: the colour has faded and her hair feels dry.

Nothing went wrong in the chair. She washed her hair that evening, then went swimming. Nobody told her not to. That is the gap aftercare messages close: the result your client paid for is decided in the hours after she leaves.

## The result is decided after they leave

Most salons explain aftercare verbally, at the mirror, while the client is checking her phone and thinking about traffic. Half of it is forgotten before she reaches the car. A written message on WhatsApp stays on her phone — she can re-read it, and find it again the night she is about to wash her hair.

In the UAE this matters more than most places. The heat, the humidity, the hard water and the swimming pools all work against fresh colour, keratin and facials. One swim or one sunny walk can undo in an afternoon what took you two hours to build.

## The three messages that matter

You do not need a long sequence. Three short messages cover almost every service.

The first goes out the same day, within an hour of the appointment ending. It thanks her, names what was done, and gives the one instruction that matters most tonight: "Thank you for today, Sara — your balayage is set. Please don't wash your hair tonight."

The second arrives the next morning: "How is your hair feeling today?" It repeats the second rule — no swimming for 48 hours, no heat styling today, keep the lashes dry. 

The third comes on day three, or day seven for facials and nails. Now the advice shifts from protection to maintenance: the right product, the right routine, and a gentle note about when to come back. "Your top-up is due in about five weeks. Shall I hold your usual Thursday evening slot?"

Three messages, each under four lines. Anything longer gets skimmed; anything more frequent feels like marketing.

## What to write for each service

Aftercare only works when it is specific. Write one short template per service, and let the stylist add a personal line.

For colour and balayage: wait 48 hours before the first wash, use sulphate-free shampoo, and avoid pools and the sea for a few days. In Dubai, add the line clients actually need — rinse with fresh water right after any swim, because chlorine and salt water strip colour fast.

For keratin and smoothing treatments: no washing, no tying up and no tucking behind the ears for 48 to 72 hours. Clients break this one most often, usually at the gym. Say it plainly and they will remember it.

For facials: no sun, no sauna and no strong active skincare for 24 to 48 hours. In the UAE summer that means a real warning — a midday walk across a car park after a facial is enough to irritate freshly treated skin.

For nails, lashes and brows: keep them dry for the first 24 hours and avoid steam rooms and saunas. A client who knows why — "water and steam loosen the bond" — follows the rule far more willingly than one who is simply told not to.

## How to send it without sounding like a robot

The fastest way to kill an aftercare message is to make it read like a leaflet. Clients can tell when a message was copied and pasted to fifty people, and they skim it and forget it.

Start with a personal line from the stylist who did the work: "Hi Aisha, it's Mariam from the salon — your hair looked beautiful today." One sentence of real warmth, then the instructions.

Send it from the salon's business number, not the stylist's personal phone, so the client knows where to reply at 9 pm. A 24/7 AI receptionist like ZYNA can send that first message the moment the appointment ends and answer the "can I wash it now?" question at midnight.

Timing matters as much as wording. Send the same-day message while the visit is fresh, and the morning message before 10 am. Never send aftercare late at night — a beauty message at 11 pm reads as marketing, however helpful it is.

## What never to put in an aftercare message

Aftercare is advice, not a guarantee. Never promise a result in writing — no "your colour will last six weeks" and no "this facial will clear your skin." Bodies, water, sun and habits all vary, and a written promise becomes a refund demand the moment reality differs. Keep sales pitches out of it too: a gentle mention of the product you used is helpful, but a hard sell makes the whole message feel like marketing.

Never give medical advice. If a client reports redness, swelling, itching or pain, do not diagnose it over WhatsApp. Acknowledge it quickly, ask her to come in so you can look, and suggest she sees a doctor if it worries her.

## The quiet rebooking

Here is the part owners miss: aftercare is also the most natural rebooking tool a salon has, because it arrives at the exact moment the client is happiest with her result.

The day-three message already knows when she should come back. Colour needs a top-up in five or six weeks, keratin a repeat in months, lashes an infill in weeks. When the message names the date and offers a specific slot — "your usual Thursday at 7 pm is free" — the client does not have to decide anything. She just says yes.

A discount message asks a client to come back for a price. An aftercare message reminds her the result she loves needs looking after. One feels like selling. The other feels like care.

## A simple system the whole team can follow

Start small. Pick your three most common services — colour, keratin and facials will cover most UAE salons — and write the three messages for each one. Nine short templates, each under four lines.

Assign one owner per shift. Aftercare fails when everyone assumes someone else sent it. Make it part of closing the appointment.

The work in the chair gets the client out of the door happy. The message after it decides whether she stays happy, whether the result lasts, and whether she books again.
