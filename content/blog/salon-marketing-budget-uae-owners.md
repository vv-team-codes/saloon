Ask ten salon owners in Dubai how much they spend on marketing and you will get ten different answers. One spends nothing and relies on walk-ins. Another boosts an Instagram post every week. Most share the same problem: the spending is not tied to any plan, so nobody can say whether it is working.

A marketing budget needs three things: a number you can afford every month, a clear split between the jobs the money has to do, and a way to check whether each dirham brings a client through the door. Get those three right and a modest budget outperforms a big one spent blindly.

## Start with a number, not a feeling

The simplest rule for an established salon is to set aside five to eight percent of monthly revenue for marketing. If your salon brings in AED 60,000 a month, that is AED 3,000 to AED 4,800. A new salon still building its client base should budget higher, around ten to fifteen percent, because it has to buy awareness before it can earn loyalty. A salon with a full diary can sit at the lower end.

The percentage matters less than the discipline. Pick a figure and treat it like rent. The owners who struggle most are not the ones spending too little; they are the ones spending in bursts. A big push before Eid, then nothing for two months. Clients do not book in bursts, so marketing should not work in bursts either.

## Split the budget into three jobs

Every dirham you spend is doing one of three jobs. First, being found: making sure people searching for a salon near them actually see you. Second, being chosen: giving them a reason to pick you over the salon next door. Third, being remembered: staying in touch with people who already visited.

A sensible split for a typical neighbourhood or mall salon is roughly forty percent on being found, thirty-five percent on being chosen and twenty-five percent on being remembered. Being found covers Google search ads and your local listings. Being chosen covers your best photos and videos, before-and-after content and small paid campaigns. Being remembered covers the follow-ups that cost almost nothing: WhatsApp messages to regulars and a reminder when a client is due for her next colour or facial.

If your salon is brand new, tilt the split toward being found. If your diary is full of one-time visitors who never return, tilt it toward being remembered.

## The channels that earn their keep in the UAE

Google is where the ready-to-book client starts: someone typing "balayage near me" or "best facial in JLT" is already halfway to your chair. A small monthly search budget aimed at two or three services you do brilliantly will beat a large budget spread thinly across everything you offer.

Instagram is where the undecided client makes up her mind. She will check your grid before she messages you, and she is judging your real work, not your follower count. One genuine transformation reel a week, filmed on a phone with good light, will do more than a polished advert that looks like every other salon.

WhatsApp is where the booking actually happens, so a slice of the budget should protect it. That might mean a WhatsApp Business setup done properly, quick replies for your most common questions, or simply making sure someone answers within minutes. Marketing that brings a client to your door and then leaves her waiting for a reply is money thrown away.

## The traps that eat the budget

Three mistakes swallow most small salon marketing budgets. The first is boosting random posts. A boosted post with no clear service, price range or booking step might earn likes, but likes do not fill chairs. Every paid post should point to one service and one way to book.

The second is buying attention you do not need. Paying for followers or sponsoring an influencer with a huge audience but no local following will fill your feed with people who will never sit in your chair. A micro-creator with two thousand genuine followers in your neighbourhood is worth more than a celebrity with two hundred thousand followers elsewhere.

The third is the discount spiral. A thirty percent discount will fill chairs for a weekend, but it teaches clients to wait for the next discount. Spend the same money on a package, a gift voucher or a small extra for regulars instead.

## How to know if it is working

The only marketing number that truly matters for a salon is cost per booking: how much did you spend to get one new client into a chair? If you spent AED 1,000 on a campaign and it brought twelve bookings, each one cost about AED 83. Compare that with what the average new client spends on her first visit and you will know immediately whether the campaign was worth repeating.

Track it simply. Ask every new client one question: how did you hear about us? Keep a tally behind the desk. After a month you will see the truth: one or two channels do almost all the work and the rest can be cut.

Review the budget every ninety days, not every week. Keep the channels that book clients, cut the ones that only produce likes, and move the money to what works.

## A starter budget you can copy

Here is a monthly plan for a salon earning around AED 50,000 a month. Set aside AED 3,000, about six percent. Put AED 1,200 into Google search ads for your two most profitable services. Put AED 1,000 into Instagram content: one paid campaign a month showing a real transformation. Put AED 500 into remembering your clients: a monthly message to regulars and a small thank-you for referrals. Keep AED 300 in reserve for a good opportunity, like a local event or a gap you want to fill.
