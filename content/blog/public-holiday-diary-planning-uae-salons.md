National Day is a week away, and the diary is about to behave nothing like a normal week. For three or four days before it, everyone wants to look their best for the celebrations. Then the salon goes quiet — or closes — for a day or two. Then the phone starts ringing again, and the first days back are the busiest of the month.

Most salons lose money around a holiday, and it is rarely because of the closed days themselves. It is because the diary was never planned around the holiday at all: the rush overflows, the closure is decided at the last minute, and the reopening surge arrives with no plan. A public holiday is not a week off from diary management.

## The pre-holiday rush starts earlier than you think

The rush does not start on the last working day before the holiday — it starts three or four days earlier, and it builds. Clients want their colour refreshed, their nails done and their brows shaped before the family gathering or the National Day event — not after it. By the final day before the holiday, your diary is already full, and the clients who left it late are messaging you at ten in the evening.

Open the holiday week's diary at least three weeks ahead, and give your regulars first refusal on the days before it. A short WhatsApp message works: the dates you will be open, the dates you will be closed, and a nudge to book the pre-holiday slot now. The clients who book early would have booked anyway — the difference is they book with you, calmly, instead of panicking on the last day and landing in a queue you cannot clear.

Keep the last day before the holiday for quick finishes — a blow-dry, a brow tidy, a manicure. A four-hour colour service booked the day before Eid, running late, costs you the three appointments behind it. Move the long services to two or three days before, and protect the final day for finishes.

## Decide the closure honestly — and early

The most expensive diary mistake around a public holiday is indecision. If you are not sure whether you will close for National Day, your team cannot plan, your regulars do not know whether to book before or after, and the diary fills with tentative bookings that collapse when you finally decide.

Decide at least three weeks ahead. There are only three honest options: closed for the full holiday, open with a skeleton team for half a day, or open as normal. The middle option only works if you say exactly which hours and which services — "open, but maybe shorter hours" is a promise you will break.

Once you have decided, tell everyone in the same week: mention it at the chair, put it in a WhatsApp broadcast, and update your Google Business Profile hours, Instagram bio and booking link the same day. A client who arrives at a closed salon because nobody told her will remember that visit longer than any good haircut. A client told early, and offered a slot before or after, will barely notice the closure.

## The reopening surge is where the money is

Here is the part most salon owners miss. The first two or three days after a public holiday are often the busiest days of the month: roots that could wait a week ago cannot wait any longer, nails have grown out, and the client who skipped her regular appointment wants back in immediately — along with five other regulars with the same idea.

Protect those days before you close: offer every regular a pre-booked slot in the reopening week — a real appointment with a date and a time, written in the diary before closing. The regulars who leave with a confirmed reopening booking are not the ones messaging you at midnight on the first day back. They are already in the book.

Do not let walk-ins eat the reopening diary: the first morning back belongs to your regulars, and the walk-in who arrives at ten can be offered the afternoon or the next day. The order matters: regulars first, then the queue.

## Deposits protect the holiday slots

A no-show on an ordinary Tuesday costs you an hour you can usually refill. A no-show on the last day before Eid costs you an hour nobody can refill — the client who wanted it has already gone elsewhere. Holiday-week slots are the most valuable hours in your diary, and they deserve the strongest protection.

Take a small deposit on long services booked into the holiday week — colour, bridal trials, keratin, full sets of lashes — and say so when the booking is made, not when the client arrives. Confirm every holiday-week appointment forty-eight hours ahead and keep a short waitlist, so a freed slot fills within the hour. The deposit is not about the money. It is about making the booking real enough that the client treats it as real.

## The one-week checklist

A public holiday week runs on a checklist, not on memory. One week before, confirm every booking for the holiday week and the reopening week, message the clients who still owe a deposit, and check your stock of the fast movers — colour, gel polish, lash glue. Agree the team rota in writing: who works the pre-holiday days, who is off, and who opens on the first day back.

Then set your away message properly: it should say when you reopen and offer the next available slots, not just "we are closed". A 24/7 AI receptionist like ZYNA can keep answering while the salon is dark, taking bookings for the reopening week so the diary is already filling before you unlock the door.

Finally, use the quiet days well if you do close. A closed salon is the only time you can deep-clean the stations, service the chairs, count the stock and train the team without losing a booking. Treat a holiday closure as a maintenance day and you come back sharper; treat it as a surprise and you come back scrambling.

Public holidays in the UAE will keep coming — National Day, Eid, New Year — and the diary will behave the same way around each of them. The salons that plan the closure, protect the rush and pre-book the reopening keep their regulars and their revenue. The ones that wing it lose a week, three or four times a year, and wonder why the year never quite adds up.
