Every salon has a birthday, and most of them let it pass in silence. The date comes and goes, the team is too busy with the diary to notice, and a ready-made marketing moment slips away. An anniversary is one of the few occasions when a salon has a genuine reason to contact every client it has ever served and invite people back — without sounding like another sales message.

In the UAE, where new salons open in malls and on high streets every month, an anniversary says something no discount can say: we are still here, and our clients still trust us. That message lands best with the regulars who have been with you since the early days, and the ones who drifted away and need one good reason to return.

## Why an anniversary works when ordinary offers don't

Most salon marketing asks a stranger to trust you. An anniversary does the opposite: it reminds people who already know you why they liked you in the first place. A client who visited twice last year does not need convincing that your blow-dries are good. She needs a reason to think of you this week, and a small nudge to book.

Anniversaries also give your marketing a story. A "20% off" message is forgettable within minutes. A message that says "we opened in JVC three years ago with two chairs and a borrowed mirror, and now we have a team of seven" is something people remember — and mention to a friend.

## Start with the story, not the discount

Before you plan any offer, spend an hour gathering the story of your salon. Find the photo from opening day, even if the paint was still drying. Ask your longest-serving stylist what she remembers about the first week. Count how many clients have been with you since the beginning — you may be surprised by the number.

Then tell that story properly — a short Instagram post and a few lines on WhatsApp Status will do. Keep it honest and specific: the neighbourhood, the year, the people. A genuine anniversary story makes your salon feel established in a way a new competitor cannot copy overnight.

## Build one anniversary package, not a sale

The biggest mistake salons make with anniversaries is turning them into a blanket discount. A week of slashed prices fills chairs for seven days and teaches clients to wait for the next sale. A single, well-designed anniversary package does something better: it gives people a reason to try something they have never booked before.

Keep it simple. Pick one signature experience — a cut and gloss, a facial and brow ritual, a blow-dry and head massage — and bundle it at a fair price for about ten days. Give it a name tied to the milestone: "Three Years, One Ritual" reads better than "Anniversary Offer 3". Offer it first to regulars, then to everyone else, and place it in normally quiet slots such as weekday mornings, rather than the packed Thursday and Friday evening rush.

## Invite the people who matter most first

An anniversary campaign works best in layers. Start with your regulars, a week before anyone else hears about it. A personal WhatsApp message — not a broadcast — thanking them by name and inviting them to the anniversary package feels like recognition, not marketing. Regulars who feel appreciated are the ones who bring a friend, leave a review, and rebook before they leave the chair.

Next, reach the clients who went quiet. An anniversary is the softest possible reason to message someone who has not visited in six months. "We're three years old this week, and we'd love to see you again" is warm and easy to answer. Offer them the same package and a specific slot, so the reply is a booking rather than a conversation that fades away.

Only then take it public: Instagram, Google Business Profile, and a small sign in the window. By the time the public sees it, your regulars have already filled the first slots — and a half-booked diary is the best advertisement a salon can have.

## Make the week feel like a celebration

The salon itself should feel different during anniversary week, and it costs very little to make it so. Fresh flowers at reception, a small cake for the team photo, a handwritten thank-you card with every service, and a simple photo corner where clients can take a picture with their stylist all turn an ordinary visit into something worth sharing.

A client who posts a photo from your salon is advertising to exactly the audience you want — her friends, in your neighbourhood, who trust her taste. Make it easy: good lighting near the mirror, a clean backdrop, and a stylist who offers to take the photo.

## Capture the bookings while the goodwill lasts

Goodwill fades fast, so build the follow-up into the week itself. When a client enjoys her anniversary treatment, rebook her before she reaches the reception desk — offer two dates, and let her choose. Ask, while she is still happy, whether she would leave a Google review; a short request at the mirror works far better than a link sent days later.

When the week ends, do not let the momentum die. Send a short thank-you message to everyone who booked, with a photo of the team celebration. Note who came back after a long absence, and who brought a friend. The anniversary may last a week, but the clients it brings back should stay for years.

## A simple four-week countdown

Four weeks out, choose the package and set the dates. Three weeks out, gather the photos and write the story. Two weeks out, message your regulars first, then your quiet clients. One week out, go public on Instagram and WhatsApp Status, brief the team, and decorate the salon. On the day itself, celebrate with the team and photograph everything — a salon proud of its milestone is a salon clients want to belong to.

Your salon's birthday comes around once a year, whether you mark it or not. Use it. A thoughtful anniversary campaign costs less than a week of paid ads and reminds the people who already love you exactly why they chose you.
