Every salon owner in the UAE knows the feeling: you launch a new service, print the price list, train the team — and nobody books it. Or you open an extra Thursday evening slot and the chairs sit empty. The money was spent before anyone asked the people who actually pay for it.

There is a much cheaper way to find out what your clients want. It is already on their phones and takes three seconds to answer: the WhatsApp poll.

A poll is one question with two to four options. One tap and the client has voted. No typing, no phone call, no form. In a market where clients message in five languages and reply between meetings, that low-effort format is exactly why polls work.

## What to ask: the decisions that cost money

The best poll questions are the ones where a wrong guess costs real money. Before you spend on a new service, a new slot or a new offer, ask the people who will pay for it.

Start with services. Considering a head spa or a new facial? Put it to a vote: "Which treatment should we add next?" Give three real options and one honest "something else — tell us" choice. Fifteen votes for a head spa is a signal. Three votes is a warning.

Next, ask about times. Evening hours are the busiest part of a UAE salon's day, and the hardest to staff. A poll like "Which evening works best for you: 6–8 pm, 8–10 pm, or Friday morning?" gives you a real answer instead of a guess, and can settle whether clients want a Sunday slot or longer hours on Saturday.

Then there are offers. Instead of guessing which festive package will sell — a bridal bundle, a friends-together deal, a home-care hamper — ask. "Which National Day treat would you book?" turns a gamble into a decision with evidence behind it, and the clients who voted for the winning offer are the warmest audience for it when it launches.

## Where to run the poll

A poll only works if the right people see it. Your broadcast list is the natural home for a service or offer poll, because it reaches clients one-to-one and every vote is a small private conversation starter. Keep the list segmented if you can: regulars who come for colour will vote differently from clients who only book facials, and a poll sent to the wrong group gives you a misleading answer.

A WhatsApp group works when the group already exists for a real reason — a bridal party, a group of regulars, or your most loyal clients. A poll in a group feels social: people see each other's votes and it starts a conversation. Keep group polls light and fun, and keep business decisions on the broadcast list, where nobody feels watched.

One place never to run a poll is a cold list — people who have not heard from you in months. A poll from a salon they barely remember feels like spam, and it will earn you a block faster than a discount message. Polls work because the relationship is already there.

## Reading the result — and acting on it

A poll is only useful if you do something with the answer. First, count honestly. Fifty votes on a broadcast list of three hundred clients is a hint from your most engaged clients, not a mandate. Look at who voted, not just how many. If all fifteen votes for a new keratin service came from your colour clients, the demand is real but narrow.

Then close the loop, publicly. When the poll ends, send a short follow-up: "You voted for the head spa — it launches on 1 December, and voters get first pick of slots." That single message proves you listened, turns voters into bookers, and teaches every client that voting in your polls has a real result. Next time you ask, more of them will answer.

The same rule applies when the answer is no. If you asked about Sunday opening and the votes said no, say so: "You told us Sundays don't work — so we're extending Saturday instead." Clients respect an owner who asks and then visibly acts on the answer.

## Five polls every UAE salon should run

If you have never run a poll, start with these. The new-service vote — "Which treatment should we add next?" — run before you spend on training or products. The hours vote — "Which evening slot suits you best?" — run when you are thinking about extending hours. The festive vote — "Which package would you book this season?" — run three weeks before a festival, not three days. The experience vote — "What would make your visit better: faster check-in, a quiet chair, or a drink on arrival?" — run when you want to improve the salon without spending much. And the fun vote — "Blow-dry or braids for the weekend?" — run on a Thursday, when clients are already planning their weekend. The fun poll earns the attention that makes the serious polls work.

Keep each poll to one question and no more than four options. Write the options in plain words a client would use, not salon jargon. And never run two polls in the same week about the same thing — a client who is asked twice stops answering.

## A small habit with a big return

Most of the decisions that shape a salon — what to offer, when to open, what to charge for a package — are made on instinct. Instinct is useful, but it is expensive when it is wrong. A WhatsApp poll costs nothing, takes a minute, and replaces a guess with evidence from the people who matter most: the clients who will actually book.

Start with one poll this week. Ask about something real, count the answer honestly, and tell your clients what you decided because of it. Do it a few times and clients start volunteering opinions, regulars feel like insiders, and your next big decision is no longer a gamble.
