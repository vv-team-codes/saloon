Every salon in Dubai and Abu Dhabi knows the message. It arrives late in the evening, usually with three photos attached: hair meant to be cool ash blonde that came out orange at the roots and grey at the ends. The client is upset, she has an event coming up, and she is asking a salon she has never visited to fix it. How you answer decides whether she books with you, and whether the correction ends well for both of you.

Correction work is the highest-stakes enquiry a salon receives on WhatsApp. The client is already disappointed once. She is anxious, expects a miracle, and will leave a public review if things go wrong a second time. A careless reply — a quick "yes, come tomorrow, AED 400" — is how a salon inherits someone else's problem and makes it its own. A careful reply turns the most nervous client in your inbox into one of your most loyal regulars, because nobody forgets the salon that rescued their hair.

## Start with empathy, not a price

The first reply should acknowledge what she is feeling before it talks about money or timing. She has just paid another salon for a result she hates, and she is probably embarrassed about the photos. A warm, plain reply works: "I'm sorry this happened — I can see why you're unhappy with it. Let me look properly and we'll work out the safest way to fix it." That tells her she is not being judged, and buys you time to assess the hair properly instead of guessing from a thumbnail.

Resist naming a price in the first message. Correction pricing depends on what is on the hair, how much lifting or toning is needed, and how many hours the chair will be tied up. A price given too early is almost always wrong — too low, and you lose money on a four-hour correction; too high, and she never replies again. Tell her corrections are priced after a proper look, and the consultation is free.

## Ask for the right photos before you promise anything

Most correction enquiries fail at the photo stage: the client sends one dark, yellow-lit bathroom selfie, and the stylist has to guess. So ask for four specific photos, and explain why each matters: one of the front in daylight, one of the back, one of the roots close up, and one of the ends. Daylight matters in the UAE, because warm indoor lighting flatters brassy tones and hides banding. A quick voice note works well here — it feels personal, and it is faster than a list a stressed client may skim.

While you are asking for photos, ask three questions that protect you later. What was done, and when? Was bleach or lightener used, and has the hair been coloured before? And is there an event or deadline driving the booking? The answers tell you whether the hair can take another round of lightener, whether a patch test is needed, and whether her timeline is realistic. A client who needs a fix before a Saturday wedding needs a different conversation from one who can wait a week.

## Be honest about what a correction can and cannot do

This is the moment that separates good salons from busy ones. Some corrections take one session. Many take two, and the honest ones need the client to accept a darker or warmer result first while the hair recovers. Say so on WhatsApp, before she books, in plain language: "We can get you to a beautiful, even colour in one visit, but the exact shade in your photo will probably take a second session." Clients rarely argue with honesty.

The same honesty applies to the hair's condition. If the photos show breakage or stretchy, over-processed ends, offer the safer plan kindly — a cut, a bond-building treatment, and a tone-down now, with the lighter result later. A client who hears "your hair needs a rest first" may feel disappointed, but she will trust you far more than the salon that said yes to everything — and that trust brings her back for every colour appointment afterwards.

## Turn the consultation into the booking

Once you have the photos and the answers, move fast. Correction clients are usually messaging two or three salons at once, and the one that replies with a clear plan wins. Send a short summary message: what you can do, how long it will take, the price range, and two specific appointment times. Offer the consultation as part of the appointment if your diary is tight — a 15-minute in-chair assessment, with the plan and price confirmed before any colour touches the hair.

This is also where a deposit earns its place. A correction blocks a chair for two to four hours, often in the busy evening slots between 6 and 9 pm. A small deposit, explained as holding that long slot, protects the salon without sounding harsh: "Because a correction takes a longer appointment, we take a small deposit to hold the chair for you — it comes off your final bill." Most clients pay it without a second thought — they have already decided you are the salon that took them seriously.

## After the fix, close the loop on WhatsApp

The job is not finished when she leaves happy. Send a short message the next evening: how is the colour settling in daylight? Add one practical line about aftercare — which shampoo to use, when to come back for a toner refresh — and the date of her next appointment. Correction clients who are looked after this way become the easiest rebookings a salon has, because the memory of the rescue never fades.

Correction enquiries will keep arriving, because bad colour happens in every city and Dubai's salons are full of clients who want it fixed well. The salons that win this work are not the ones that reply fastest with the lowest price, but the ones that slow down just enough to look properly, tell the truth about what is possible, and deliver exactly what they promised. Answer like that, and the client with the colour disaster becomes the client who recommends you to everyone she knows.
