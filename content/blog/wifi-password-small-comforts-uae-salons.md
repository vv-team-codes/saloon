She had been in the chair for less than a minute when she asked for it. Not the price, not how long the colour would take — the Wi-Fi password. Her phone was on four percent, her earphones were in, and she had a two-hour appointment ahead of her. In that moment, the quality of her visit had little to do with the colour. It was about whether she could settle in, stay connected, and relax.

Owners focus on the big things — skill, products, the room. But a visit is also made of a dozen small comforts, and in the UAE — where appointments are long, evenings are busy, and almost every client arrives phone in hand — those small comforts carry surprising weight. A comfortable client stays longer, books the extra service, and comes back.

## The first thirty seconds set the tone

The moment a client sits down, she decides — consciously or not — whether this visit will feel easy. A warm greeting matters, and so does what happens next. Is she offered water without having to ask? Does someone tell her the Wi-Fi password before she has to ask for it?

None of this is complicated. It is the habit of anticipating what a client will need in the next hour and putting it within reach before she asks. In a busy Dubai salon at seven in the evening, the team with these small things ready looks calm and in control.

## Wi-Fi is no longer a perk — it is part of the service

Ten years ago, salon Wi-Fi was a nice extra. Today it is closer to a utility, like water or air conditioning. Clients use it to answer messages while their colour processes, to stream a show during a long treatment, to show their stylist a reference photo.

The details matter more than simply having Wi-Fi. A password printed on a small card at every mirror beats one the client must ask for and type from memory. And a connection that reaches the back chairs beats one that drops out past the washbasins. Test the signal in the farthest chair, not just at reception. Keep it simple: one clear name, one short password, printed neatly. Skip login screens and forms asking for an email address.

## A phone at four percent is a stressed client

A low battery changes how a person behaves. She stops scrolling, stops answering messages, and starts watching the percentage drop instead of enjoying the appointment.

The fix costs almost nothing. A short charging cable at each mirror, with the two or three connector types clients actually use, turns a source of stress into a small delight. And a client whose phone is charging stays in the chair, watches the result take shape, and says yes to the extra service she would otherwise have rushed past.

## Comfort for the long sit

Many UAE salon visits are long. A full colour, a keratin treatment, or a bridal trial can keep a client in one chair for two or three hours, and much of that time is waiting — for colour to develop, for a treatment to set, for a rinse.

A cushion behind the lower back, a light blanket when the air conditioning is doing its job a little too well, a fresh glass of water without being asked, a phone stand so she does not have to hold her device for an hour — each one is small, and together they change the visit from something endured into something enjoyed.

## A quiet corner for the client on a call

Plenty of UAE salon clients arrive straight from work, and some need to take a short call during a long appointment. A client whispering into her phone while colour processes, worried about disturbing the client next to her, is not relaxing — and neither is the client next to her. A chair near the back, away from the busiest mirrors, is enough.

The same thinking applies to clients who simply want quiet: some put their earphones in and watch a show, others close their eyes and enjoy the wash.

## Train the team to offer, not wait to be asked

The difference between a good salon and a memorable one is often timing. A charger offered at the start of the appointment is a thoughtful touch; a charger produced after twenty minutes of a client anxiously watching her battery is damage control. The same is true of water, Wi-Fi, and a blanket. Offer early, before the client has to ask.

When a client sits down, the stylist or assistant can run through three quiet questions: does she have water, does she know the Wi-Fi password, and is her phone charging if it needs to be? It takes ten seconds.

## What it all adds up to

Clients rarely leave a review saying "the Wi-Fi was excellent and my phone was charged." But they do leave reviews saying "I felt so looked after" and "I never want to go anywhere else." Those feelings are built from exactly these small moments.
