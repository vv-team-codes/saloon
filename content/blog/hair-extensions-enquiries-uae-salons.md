It is one of the most common messages a UAE salon receives, and one of the easiest to get wrong: "Do you do hair extensions? How much?"

The client has usually already decided she wants them. She has seen a transformation on Instagram and probably messaged two or three salons before yours. What she is really asking is whether you can give her that result, on her hair, at a price she can accept, without a bad surprise. The salon that answers well books a consultation. The salon that answers badly loses a client who would have booked with a little more care.

## Why extension enquiries are different

Most salon enquiries have a fixed answer. A blow-dry takes forty minutes and costs a set amount. Extensions do not work like that. The method, the amount of hair, the colour match and the price all depend on the client's own hair — its length, thickness, condition and history. Two clients asking the same question can need completely different answers.

That is why the best first reply is never a price. It is a short, warm message that takes the question seriously and moves the conversation toward a consultation.

## Four questions before you quote anything

Before any price is mentioned, gather four pieces of information. A few friendly questions in the same thread are enough.

First, length, volume, or both? A client who wants a little extra fullness needs far less hair than one who wants waist-length hair from a shoulder-length cut, and the price difference is large.

Second, what is her hair like now? Ask for a recent photo in natural light, front and back, with her hair down. It takes thirty seconds, saves the salon from guessing, and shows at once whether her hair is healthy enough.

Third, has it been coloured, bleached or treated with keratin recently? Extensions fitted onto freshly lightened hair behave differently, and a stylist who knows the history can plan honestly.

Fourth, when does she need it? Extensions often need to be ordered in, matched and fitted across two visits, and saying so early avoids the disappointment of a last-minute no.

These questions do more than gather facts. They show the client she is dealing with a salon that knows its work.

## Answering the price question honestly

Sooner or later, the client will ask what it costs. The honest answer is a range, tied to what you have learned, with a clear statement of what it includes.

Try: "For the length and fullness you described, most clients pay between X and Y dirhams, including the hair, fitting and a follow-up check. The exact price depends on how much hair we need, which we confirm at your consultation." A flat figure quoted before you have seen the hair is a promise you may not be able to keep.

Name what is not included too. Maintenance, removal and aftercare products are part of the real cost. Clients who hear about them during the enquiry feel looked after; clients who learn at the till feel misled.

## When the answer should be "not yet"

Some enquiries should end with a kind no, or at least a not yet. Badly damaged hair, very fine and short hair, or a length the client's own hair cannot support are all cases where fitting extensions would damage the hair and your reputation with it.

Saying no feels like losing a booking. In practice, the opposite is true. A client told honestly that her hair needs six weeks of conditioning first usually comes back — and brings friends. A client given extensions she should never have had leaves a one-star review.

Wording matters. "Your hair is too damaged" sounds like blame. "To get the result in your photo safely, we need to strengthen your hair first — here is a simple plan" sounds like care. A no with a plan is still a booking, just a later one.

## Book the consultation, not the fitting

The goal of every extension enquiry is a consultation, not a fitting. That is where the stylist sees the hair, feels it, matches the colour in person and agrees the method — tape, bonds, wefts or clip-ins — with the client.

End the enquiry with a specific invitation, not an open one. "We have a free fifteen-minute extension consultation with Sara on Tuesday at 6pm or Wednesday at 11am — which suits you?" Two named times, one named stylist, one clear next step.

Confirm the method, amount of hair, total price, maintenance schedule and expected lifespan, then send a summary on WhatsApp. That summary is the best defence against "Nobody told me."

## After the consultation: keeping the client warm

Not every consultation converts on the day. Some clients need to think about the price or wait until payday. That is normal, and it is where most salons lose the booking — not at the enquiry, but in the silence afterwards.

A short follow-up two days later keeps the door open without pressure: thank her, confirm the price and the next fitting slot, and offer to hold it for twenty-four hours. One more message a week later is enough. A salon that follows up once, warmly, looks professional.

## A better answer wins the booking

Hair extension enquiries reward the salons that slow down. Ask four questions, answer price with a range, say no kindly when the hair is not ready, and book a consultation with two named times.

That trust is the real product. A client who has a good first experience comes back every six to eight weeks for a year or more. The enquiry is where that relationship starts.
