Every salon owner in the UAE knows the feed. You post a reel, you boost it, you watch the views. But there is a whole audience your feed never reaches: the woman driving to work on Sheikh Zayed Road at 8 in the morning, the commuter listening to a podcast on the Metro, the mum doing the school run with the radio on. They are not scrolling. They are listening — and that is exactly where a smart salon can be heard.

Local radio and podcasts are the quiet corner of salon marketing in the UAE. Most owners ignore them because they feel old-fashioned or hard to measure. In reality, they are affordable, local by nature, and they reach people when a phone screen cannot compete. Here is how to use them properly.

## Why audio works for salons

A salon is a local business with a local audience. Radio stations in Dubai and Abu Dhabi are built around exactly that: a city, a commute, a community. Drive-time shows reach thousands of people in cars at the same hour your salon is opening its doors. A 30-second mention of a salon in Jumeirah or Deira lands with listeners who live ten minutes away — the same people a boosted post might show to someone in another emirate entirely.

Podcasts work differently, and that is their strength. A listener chooses a podcast, trusts the host, and listens for half an hour or more. When that host recommends a salon, it carries the weight of a personal recommendation, not an advert. For a beauty business, where trust decides everything, that kind of endorsement is hard to buy anywhere else.

## Start with radio: small, local, and timed well

You do not need a national campaign. Start with one local station and one time slot. The two slots that matter for salons are the morning drive, roughly 7 to 9, and the afternoon drive, roughly 4 to 6. Morning listeners are planning their day; afternoon listeners are thinking about what to do after work. Both are good moments to plant the idea of an evening appointment.

Ask the station for a short run — two weeks, a few spots a day — and negotiate. Stations in the UAE work with small businesses all the time, and a first-time advertiser can usually agree a trial package. What matters is consistency: the same message, heard several times, in the same week.

Your radio script needs three things and nothing more. First, the name of your salon and where it is — a landmark, not a full address. Second, one clear reason to book: a signature treatment, a new service, a seasonal offer. Third, one easy next step: a WhatsApp number or a simple instruction like "search for us on Google Maps." Keep it under 30 seconds and say the name twice.

## Podcasts: borrow trust instead of buying attention

Podcast advertising in the UAE is still young, which is good news for a salon owner. Rates are reasonable, hosts are open to local sponsors, and the audiences are loyal. Look for shows your clients actually listen to: lifestyle and wellness podcasts, mum-focused shows, business and self-development programmes, and community podcasts in your area. A show with 2,000 regular listeners in Dubai is worth far more to you than a show with 100,000 listeners spread across the world.

There are two ways to appear on a podcast. The first is a host-read advert, where the host talks about your salon in their own words, usually for 30 to 60 seconds in the middle of the episode. This works best because it sounds like a recommendation, not a commercial. Give the host a real experience first: invite them for a treatment and let them describe what they actually liked. A host who has sat in your chair will sell your salon far better than any script.

The second way is to be a guest. If you are an owner with a story — you built a salon from nothing, you specialise in a treatment nobody else offers — local podcast hosts are often looking for guests exactly like you. A 20-minute conversation about your craft reaches people who would never click on an advert, and it positions you as the expert in your neighbourhood. Prepare two or three useful tips to share, mention your salon naturally when the host asks where you work, and let the conversation do the marketing.

## Make audio measurable

The biggest objection to radio and podcasts is measurement. You cannot see clicks, so how do you know it worked? The answer is to give every audio campaign its own trackable hook.

For radio, use a simple phrase only listeners will hear: mention a code word, a named treatment, or a specific offer, and ask callers to quote it. "Mention drive-time and get a complimentary hair mask with any colour service this week" tells you exactly how many bookings the campaign produced. For podcasts, give the host a unique offer or a short link to share, and track every enquiry that mentions it.

Also watch your WhatsApp enquiries in the two weeks after a campaign starts. A rise in messages asking "where are you located?" or "do you do this treatment?" from people who have never messaged before is a strong signal, even before the bookings show up. Keep a simple note of how each new client found you — one line at booking is enough — and you will build a clear picture over a month.

## A small budget, used well

A sensible first test for a UAE salon is a two-week radio trial on one station plus one podcast host-read advert. That is a modest spend, and it buys you something a feed cannot: being heard by people at the exact moment they are deciding how to spend their evening. If it works, repeat it monthly with a fresh message — a new service, a seasonal treatment, a client story.

Your clients are not only on their phones. Some of your best future regulars are sitting in traffic right now, listening. Make sure your salon is one of the things they hear.
