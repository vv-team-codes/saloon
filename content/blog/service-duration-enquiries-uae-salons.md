"How long will it take?" It is one of the first questions a client asks on WhatsApp, and one of the easiest to get wrong. Answer too short and she sits in the chair watching the clock; answer too long and she books elsewhere. In a packed UAE salon diary, a broken promise about time is felt at once.

## Why the question matters

When a client asks about duration, she is planning her day around your answer: a parking meter, a driver waiting outside, a meeting at four, children to collect. In Dubai and Abu Dhabi, many also plan around prayer times and the evening rush.

An honest answer builds trust fast. A client told a balayage takes three to three and a half hours, who finishes in three, feels looked after. A client told ninety minutes, still under the dryer at two hours, feels misled — even if the result is beautiful.

## The real answer is a range, not a number

No salon service has one true duration. A cut on fine, shoulder-length hair is a different appointment from a cut on thick hair past the waist, and a root touch-up is not a full colour.

So stop answering with a single figure. Answer with a range, and say what it depends on: "A cut and blow-dry is usually forty-five minutes to an hour, depending on your hair length and thickness." Quote the time it takes on a normal day, not your best day. The client who finishes early is delighted; the client promised forty-five minutes who gets sixty is not.

## The four-part reply that works every time

A good duration answer has four parts, and it fits in one WhatsApp message: the range, what it includes, what could make it longer, and a concrete next step.

It might sound like this: "A full balayage with a toner and blow-dry is usually three to three and a half hours. That includes the colour, the processing time while it develops, and the finish. If your hair is very long or very thick, it can run a little longer. I have Thursday at 4pm or Saturday at 11am — would either of those work for you?"

That message answers the question, explains the processing time clients often forget exists, warns about the one variable that matters, and ends with a booking. It never says "it depends" — the answer that kills enquiries.

## Where salons get duration wrong

The most common mistake is quoting the hands-on time and forgetting the rest. A keratin treatment is ninety minutes plus processing, rinsing and a final blow-dry; a bridal trial is an hour plus the consultation, the photos and the pins coming out. When the quoted time only counts the part the stylist is holding a brush, the client experiences the rest as a delay.

The second mistake is letting every stylist quote their own number. If one says an hour and another says ninety minutes, the client hears the number she likes and holds you to it. Agree on one standard range per service, write it down for the whole team, and make sure whoever answers WhatsApp uses the same numbers as the stylist in the chair.

The third mistake is promising the impossible to win the booking. A client who needs to be out by six, told she will be done by five-thirty when the diary says otherwise, will remember the salon that made her late. Offer the next slot that genuinely fits.

## Standard times for the services clients ask about most

Some services cause more duration arguments than others, and they deserve one standard answer for the whole team. A cut and blow-dry: forty-five minutes to an hour. A root colour with blow-dry: two to two and a half hours. A full balayage or highlights with toner: three to four hours. A keratin or smoothing treatment: three to four hours including processing. A bridal hair trial: ninety minutes to two hours. A gel manicure: an hour. A facial: an hour to seventy-five minutes.

Adjust them to your team once, then use the same numbers everywhere — WhatsApp, booking page, front desk.

## Protecting the rest of the day

An honest duration answer also protects every appointment after hers. One service that overruns by half an hour costs you the next client's patience, the one after that's parking time, and the stylist's break. By evening, the whole diary is running late.

The person answering enquiries and the person running the diary need the same information. If a colour is booked for three hours, the diary should show three hours, not two and a half with a hopeful gap. When you confirm a booking, repeat the finish time back: "You are booked for 4pm, and we expect to finish around 7pm."

## When it runs over anyway

Sometimes it runs over anyway: hair is thicker than it looked in the photo, a colour needs a second round, a client arrives late and still wants the full service. Say so early — the worst version is a client who discovers at the time she expected to leave that she still has an hour to go.

Tell her as soon as you know, give her the new finish time, and offer something small: a coffee, a phone charger, a call to her driver. Clients forgive a salon that is honest about a delay. They do not forgive one that pretends it is not happening.
