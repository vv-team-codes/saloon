It usually arrives on WhatsApp in the evening, after the salon has closed. "Hi, I saw your balayage price. The salon near my building does it for 350. Can you match it?"

Every salon owner in the UAE has read some version of this message. Most salons answer badly in one of two ways: they cave with a quick discount to save the booking, or they reply with a cold "our prices are fixed" and never hear from the client again. There is a third way.

## Why the first reply decides everything

A discount enquiry is not really about the price. It is a test of confidence: the client is asking whether your price is real or just a starting point. Drop the price in the first message and you have answered that question — the price was never real. She will expect a discount on every visit, and she will tell her friends your prices are negotiable.

Refuse flatly and you have answered a question she did not ask. The reply that works does neither: it holds the price, respects the question, and moves the conversation toward a booking. Clients who book after a calm answer tend to stay full-price regulars; clients who book after a discount stay discount regulars.

## What the client is really asking

Most discount enquiries fall into one of four motives. The first is comparison: she has two or three salons in mind and is asking why you are worth more. The second is budget: she likes your work but the number is above what she planned to spend. The third is habit: in some of the UAE's markets, bargaining is normal, and some clients ask because asking costs nothing. The fourth is leverage: "I will come every week" trades a promise of future loyalty for a lower price today — a promise you cannot verify.

## The five discount asks you will hear in the UAE

The price-match ask is the most common: "The salon near me does it for less." Never argue about the other salon — you do not know their products or standards. Anchor your own value instead: your products, your stylist, the time it takes.

The friend ask is next: "My friend paid less." Prices change, and a friend's memory of a price is rarely exact. A gentle "prices have changed a little since then" is honest — and it lets you quote the real range for her hair.

The regulars ask — "I come every week, surely you can do something" — deserves the warmest answer, because it comes from a client you want to keep. Offer a package instead: a better rate for committing to a series of visits.

The occasion ask arrives around birthdays, Eid and wedding season. A small gesture — a complimentary blow-dry, a little extra time — costs far less than a percentage off.

Then there is the group ask: "There are four of us, what is your best price?" Four chairs filled at once is real value — but the answer should be a fixed group package, agreed in advance.

## A reply structure that holds the price

The strongest replies follow the same shape every time — teach it to everyone who answers your WhatsApp. First, acknowledge warmly: "Thank you for asking — I completely understand." That one line stops the client feeling judged for asking. Second, anchor the value in one specific detail: "Our balayage includes a bond treatment and a toner, and it is done by a senior colourist — it usually takes about three hours."

Third, offer a choice, not a discount: "Would you prefer Thursday evening with Sara, or Saturday morning with a junior stylist at a lower rate?" Fourth, close with a slot, not a question about the price: "I can hold Saturday at 11am for you until this evening — shall I?" Keep the whole reply to three or four sentences.

## What to offer instead of a discount

A discount is the laziest answer to a price question, and it is the only one that costs you money on every future visit too. A quiet-hour slot is the best alternative: your Tuesday morning chair is empty anyway, so offering that slot at the standard price, with a small extra such as a head massage, fills dead time without touching your rate card. 

A package works for the client who promises to come every week: three visits booked and paid in advance at a fair package price is a genuine commitment, and it is worth a better rate because it is real and paid for — unlike a promise in a chat message. A free add-on, given once as a welcome gesture, feels generous without changing your prices. A small concession is smart only when you choose it yourself — a launch week, a quiet month — made conditional and time-limited.

## Train the whole team on one answer

The biggest leak in most salons is the front desk: the owner holds the price, and then a receptionist, trying to be helpful at 8pm, types "ok, we can do 50 off" before the owner sees the message. Keep the reply as a saved message in WhatsApp Business, so everyone who answers uses the same words. If a client pushes back twice, the message goes to the owner — never a discount on the spot.

There is one more trap: most discount enquiries arrive after closing time, when the client is scrolling and comparing salons and nobody is at the desk. ## A simple rule to keep

When the next "can you do it cheaper?" message arrives, remember the rule: never say yes to a discount in the first message, and never say no to the client. Say yes to her visit, her budget, a service that fits — and hold the price while you do it. Salons do not lose clients because prices are too high. They lose them because the first reply felt like a negotiation instead of a welcome. Answer with warmth, anchor the value, and close with a slot. Those clients come back at full price — and those are the clients a salon is built on.
