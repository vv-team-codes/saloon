It usually arrives late at night. A WhatsApp message with a video attached and a short line underneath: "I saw this on TikTok — can you do it?" Sometimes it is a Korean head spa ritual, glass hair, a Russian manicure, a jelly pedicure, or a treatment with a name you have never heard before. The client is excited, the video has millions of views, and she wants it this week.

Viral trend enquiries are now a regular part of running a salon in the UAE. Clients scroll the same videos you do, often before you have seen them, and they message the salon they trust to make the video real. How you reply decides whether that excitement turns into a booking — or into a polite "thanks, I'll think about it" that never comes back.

## Why trend enquiries are different

A normal enquiry starts with a service the client already understands — roughly what it costs and how long it takes. A trend enquiry starts with a thirty-second video and almost no information at all.

The client does not know the real name of the treatment, the products involved, how long it takes, or what it should cost — only how it looked on camera. That gap between the video and reality is where salons lose these bookings — by promising the video exactly as shown, or by dismissing the trend and losing the client to a salon that says yes faster.

## The five questions before you say yes

When a viral trend enquiry lands, the reply should not be the first thing you write. Run a quick check inside the salon first — five questions:

**What exactly is the treatment?** Watch the video the client sent. Look past the music and the lighting and work out what was actually done. Many viral names are just new labels for services you already offer.

**Do we have what it takes?** The products, the equipment, and the space. A head spa needs a basin setup that works for a long, lying-down service. If you would have to buy everything for one client, say so honestly.

**Is anyone on the team trained for it?** A treatment done badly because nobody had practised it is worse than a polite no. If one stylist has the skill, the booking belongs with them.

**How long does it really take?** Viral videos compress an hour into thirty seconds. Tell the client the honest duration before she books, not after she is already in the chair.

**What should it cost?** Price it like a real service — products, time, skill — not like a favour. Clients who found you through a trend are often happy to pay a fair price for the real thing.

All five yes and you have a booking. One or two no and you still have a reply — just a different one.

## The reply that keeps the booking

The best reply has three parts, and it fits in one WhatsApp message.

First, acknowledge the trend. "Yes, we've seen this one — it's been all over our feed too" tells the client she is not the first person to ask, and that you are keeping up.

Second, give the honest answer. If you offer it, say so with a time and a price: "We do the full head spa ritual — 75 minutes, AED 350, and I can book you Thursday at 6 PM." If you offer something close but not identical, name the difference plainly: "We don't do the exact jelly pedicure from the video, but our luxury pedicure uses the same soak and massage steps — 60 minutes, AED 180." If you do not offer it at all, say so in one line and move straight to the alternative.

Third, end with a next step, not a question mark. Two real slots beat "let me know when you'd like to come" — give her something to say yes to while the excitement is fresh. What never works is the slow reply that says "let me check and get back to you" and then goes quiet for two days. By then she has asked three other salons, and the one that answered first has her booking.

## When the trend is not right for the client

Sometimes the honest answer is not about your salon — it is about the client. The treatment in the video was filmed on hair or skin very different from hers, with lighting and filters doing half the work.

A keratin-style smoothing treatment on hair that has just been bleached, a strong peel on skin that has spent the summer in the sun, a nail technique on nails that are already damaged — these are the moments where a good salon earns a client for life by saying "not yet" instead of "yes". Explain why in one sentence and offer the safer version: a gentler treatment now, the full version after the hair or skin has recovered, a consultation first.

## Turning one enquiry into a service on your menu

If the same trend enquiry arrives three or four times in a week, stop treating it as a one-off question and treat it as market research. Your clients are telling you what they want before your competitors have noticed.

Start small. Offer the treatment as a limited addition, with one trained stylist, on set days, at a price you have costed properly. Watch how it books, how long it really takes, and whether clients rebook it or just try it once. If it rebooks, it earns a permanent place on the menu.

## A simple system for trend enquiries

You do not need a new process for viral trends. You need the same discipline you use for every enquiry, applied a little faster. Watch the video the client sent before you reply. Check the five questions with the team. Reply the same day with an honest answer and two real slots. Log the trend and how often it is asked for, so a repeated enquiry becomes a menu decision instead of a surprise.

Viral trends will keep coming. The salons that win are not the ones that chase every video — they are the ones that answer honestly, price fairly, and turn a curious message into a client who trusts them next time.
