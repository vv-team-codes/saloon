A salon move is exciting for the owner and unsettling for the client. You see a bigger space, better light, a smarter location near a metro station or a busy mall. Your regular sees something else: the chair she knows, the stylist who remembers her, the ten-minute walk from her building, the parking spot she always finds — all suddenly uncertain. In the UAE, where clients choose between dozens of salons within a few blocks, that uncertainty is enough to lose people you spent years winning.

The good news is that clients are rarely loyal to an address. They are loyal to an experience. Carry the experience across with you, and the new location becomes an upgrade instead of a disruption. The salons that move well treat relocation as a client-experience project, not a logistics job.

## Tell them before the sign goes up

The biggest mistake is letting clients discover the move by accident — a closed door, a "we've moved" note taped to the glass, a confused WhatsApp message on the morning of her appointment. By then, the damage is done: she has already decided the move is inconvenient, before she has seen the new place.

Start telling regulars at least a month before moving day, in person first. The stylist mentions it during the appointment, while the client is relaxed and happy with the result: "Next time you'll see us in our new place, just five minutes from here." That sentence does more than any announcement post. It frames the move as news shared between people who know each other, not a corporate notice.

Follow it up on WhatsApp with the details that actually matter to her: the new address, a location pin, how to park, the nearest metro stop, and — most importantly — that her stylist, her usual time and her usual service are all moving with her. Send it once, personally. A broadcast says "we moved." A personal message says "we're still your salon."

## Make the first visit to the new place feel familiar

The first appointment at the new address is the moment a regular decides whether the move worked. She walks in slightly nervous, looking for something she recognises. Give her plenty to find.

Keep the rituals that cost nothing: the same greeting at the door, the same karak or water offered the same way, the same music at the same volume, the same scent if you use one. If her stylist always starts with the same question — "Same as last time, or shall we try something?" — keep that exact question. Familiarity is not about the walls. It is about the dozen small moments that tell her she is in the right place.

## Directions are part of the experience

In Dubai and Abu Dhabi, a salon's directions are part of its service. Clients navigate by landmarks, not street numbers: "behind the mall, next to the pharmacy, opposite the school." Your new address needs that same translation.

Every booking confirmation in the first two months should carry the new location pin, the nearest landmark, parking instructions and, if it applies, valet details. The first time a client has to circle a new building looking for parking, the move feels like a downgrade — even if the salon itself is twice as beautiful. Tell her where to park before she leaves home, and what the entrance looks like. A photo of the new shopfront in your WhatsApp message removes the last bit of doubt.

This is also where enquiries spike. During a move, clients message more than usual — "Are you open yet?", "Is it the same price?", "Where exactly are you now?" — and they message at all hours, including the evenings when your team is busy unpacking boxes. ## Protect the regulars' rituals

Your most loyal clients have rituals: the same stylist, the same chair, the same Saturday 11 AM slot, the same coffee order. A move threatens all of them at once, so protect them deliberately.

Transfer standing appointments to the new diary before you announce the move, and confirm each one personally. If a regular's usual slot no longer exists in the new rota, call her — don't message — and offer the closest alternative before she discovers the gap herself. If her stylist's days have changed, tell her early and offer a first visit with that stylist at the new place as a priority booking.

## The first week is a launch, not a move

Treat the first week at the new address as a reopening. A small welcome gesture — a hand-written note at her station, a complimentary blow-dry upgrade, a first-visit photo of the new space she can share — costs little and marks the moment. The point is not the gift. The point is that her first visit to the new salon feels like an occasion, not an errand she had to relearn.

Ask for feedback at the end of that first visit, while she is still in the chair: "Is the new place easy to get to? Anything we should change?" Clients who are asked become invested. They will tell you about the parking confusion or the cold corner chair before it becomes a reason to drift away.

## When someone doesn't follow

Even a well-managed move loses a few clients. Someone's new commute is genuinely longer, or she has found somewhere closer to home. When a regular goes quiet after the move, one personal message is worth more than any offer: "We miss you at the new place — the chair by the window is yours whenever you like." No discount, no pressure, just a door left open.

Resist the temptation to win her back with a heavy discount. A discount tells her the new salon needs bribing to visit. A personal invitation tells her she was missed. The first keeps a client for one visit; the second brings her back for good.
