Your salon's WhatsApp number is probably the most valuable thing in your business that you never think about. It holds years of client conversations, booking histories, price agreements, before-and-after photos and voice notes from regulars. It is the trust of every client who messages you first instead of calling around. If that number stopped working tomorrow, how many of those clients could you reach again?

Most UAE salon owners only find out when something goes wrong. A phone is stolen from a handbag in a mall. A receptionist leaves and takes the number she registered in her own name. A new phone arrives and the chat history never transfers. Each is a small, ordinary event — and each one can cut a salon off from its clients overnight.

## Your number belongs to the business, not a person

The most important rule is also the most commonly broken one. In many salons, the WhatsApp Business account was set up years ago on whoever happened to be at the front desk — on their personal SIM, in their name, with their email as the recovery address. It worked fine until that person left.

Fix this now, while nothing is on fire. The number should sit on a company SIM or a dedicated business number, with the recovery email an address the owner controls. If it currently sits on a staff member's personal SIM, plan the move: announce it a week ahead on WhatsApp Status and in the salon, move the account, and keep the old number active for a month with an away message pointing clients to the new one.

Staff should use the business number through linked devices, so a stylist's departure never takes your client list with her.

## Switch on two-step verification today

WhatsApp Business offers a six-digit PIN that is asked for whenever the number is registered on a new phone. It takes two minutes to set up, and it stops a stolen phone becoming a catastrophe. Without it, anyone holding your phone can move your number to another device and lock you out of your own business.

Set the PIN, write it down somewhere safe that is not on the phone itself — a notebook in the office safe, or a password manager the owner controls — and make sure one other trusted person knows where it is kept. A PIN nobody can find is almost as bad as no PIN at all.

While you are in the settings, check your linked devices. WhatsApp shows every phone, tablet and computer connected to the account. If you see a device you do not recognise, or an old phone a former employee still has linked, remove it immediately. Do this check once a month, the same way you would check the salon's cash float.

## Back up your chats — and check the backup

WhatsApp Business can back up your chats to Google Drive automatically. Turn on daily backup and confirm it is actually running. A backup that was switched on two years ago and quietly stopped when the phone ran out of storage is no backup at all. Once a month, check the date of the last successful backup. If it is not recent, find out why: usually storage space, a changed Google account, or a phone that has not been charged overnight on Wi-Fi.

The backup matters most for the things you cannot reconstruct. A regular's allergy note, the exact shade formula from her last colour, the deposit she already paid for Saturday — these live in chat history. Your booking system may hold the appointment, but the conversation holds the context. In a busy Dubai salon, that context is what makes a client who books months later feel remembered rather than processed.

## Plan for the day the phone disappears

Phones get lost, stolen and dropped in sinks. Three preparations keep your salon booking while it is sorted out.

First, keep a spare device in the salon — an old phone or tablet — with WhatsApp already linked to the business number. If the main phone dies at 10am, the spare takes over in minutes and the 7pm rush still gets its confirmations.

Second, write down your recovery steps somewhere the team can find them without the missing phone: the WhatsApp PIN, the Google account used for backups, and your telecom provider's number to block or replace the SIM. A laminated card in the office drawer costs nothing.

Third, know the order of operations. Block the SIM first through your provider, then register the number on the replacement device using your PIN, then restore from the latest backup. Bookings keep coming in through the linked spare device while this happens, so nobody has to tell a client to "just call back later."

## When a number has to change, move your clients with it

Sometimes a change is unavoidable: a telecom plan ends, a number gets recycled, or a salon rebrands. Handled well, a number change loses almost nobody; handled badly, it loses the clients who message least often — the quiet regulars who book every six weeks and never check your Status.

Announce the change three times, not once. Put it on your WhatsApp Status a week ahead, send a short broadcast message to your regulars, and put a small sign at reception. Keep the old number alive for at least a month with an away message giving the new number. Then send one warm message from the new number, inviting regulars to save it. Clients forgive a number change. They do not forgive discovering it only when they need an appointment and nobody answers.

## A small habit that protects everything else

None of this is glamorous work. But your WhatsApp number is where your salon's relationships live — the regular who messages "same as last time," the bride coordinating six appointments, the new client who found you at 11pm and booked before breakfast. Every booking system you rely on is only as safe as that number.

Spend half an hour on it this week: confirm who owns the number, switch on the PIN, check the backup date, and link a spare device. Then set a monthly reminder to check it again. It is the cheapest insurance your salon will ever buy.
