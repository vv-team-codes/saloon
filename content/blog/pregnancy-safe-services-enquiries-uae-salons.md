Every UAE salon hears it sooner or later. A WhatsApp message arrives, usually late in the evening: "I'm pregnant — is it safe to get my hair coloured?"

This is not an ordinary enquiry. She is not asking about price or availability. She is asking whether she can trust you with something that matters far more than her hair. The answer you give in the next few minutes decides whether she books, whether she returns after the baby, and whether she tells her friends your salon is the one that looked after her properly.

## Why this enquiry is different

The client wants to feel like herself again, and she is worried about doing anything that could affect her baby. She has probably read conflicting advice online — one site says hair colour is fine, another says avoid everything, and a relative has told her to stay away from salons entirely.

So she arrives anxious, listening carefully to your tone. Sound dismissive and she feels unheard. Sound uncertain and she feels unsafe. Sound calm, specific and honest about what you know and what you do not know, and she relaxes — and your reply should help her make a decision she feels good about, not just close a sale.

## What you can say, and what you must never promise

Never give medical advice, and never promise a service is completely safe. You are a beauty professional, not a doctor, and saying "it's totally fine" about a chemical service during pregnancy is a promise no salon should make.

Instead, share general, widely known information and guide her to her own doctor for the final word: "Many clients colour their hair during pregnancy, and most stylists avoid treatments with strong fumes during the first trimester. Your doctor is the best person to confirm for your situation — and once they have, we can choose the gentlest option together."

That shows you understand the concern, shares something useful, draws a clear line around your expertise, and keeps the booking alive.

## Know your menu before the message arrives

Enquiries go wrong when the person replying does not know which services carry a caution. Agree three lists with your team in advance.

First, services generally considered low concern: most haircuts, blow-dries, manicures and pedicures, gentle facials without strong acids, and head massages. Offer these confidently, with small comfort adjustments — a cushion for her back and a reminder she can ask for a break any time.

Second, services that need a conversation first: hair colour, highlights, keratin and smoothing treatments, chemical peels, and anything with strong fumes or prolonged lying flat. Here the honest answer is "let's talk about the options." Highlights and balayage touch less of the scalp than a full colour, ammonia-free colour is gentler than a standard formula, and keratin is usually better postponed until after the birth. Knowing these distinctions lets you offer an alternative instead of a flat no.

Third, services you will not perform during pregnancy. Write this list down; do not leave it to whoever happens to be on the phone. When the whole team gives the same answer, the client hears a professional salon, not a confused one.

## The trimester changes the answer

The right answer also depends on how far along she is. In the first trimester, most cautious guidance suggests avoiding chemical services and strong fumes, and nausea makes a long appointment with chemical smells genuinely unpleasant. Try: "In the first few months most clients prefer to wait for colour and keratin — shall we book a cut and blow-dry now, and put a colour in the diary for your second trimester?"

In the second trimester, clients usually feel more energetic and want the treatments they postponed — the moment to suggest gentler alternatives. In the third trimester, comfort is the main issue: shorter appointments, extra cushions, and a relaxed attitude to rescheduling matter more than any product choice.

## The reply your team can actually use

These messages often arrive after hours, when the person answering is not a senior stylist. Give your team a short structure. First, acknowledge warmly: "Congratulations! Thank you for asking — it's a really good question." Second, give the honest general picture for that service in one or two sentences. Third, draw the line: "Your doctor knows your pregnancy best, so please do check with them." Fourth, offer the next step — a gentler alternative, a consultation, or a low-concern service.

For a colour enquiry, that sounds like: "Congratulations, and thank you for checking! Many of our pregnant clients choose highlights or ammonia-free colour, because they touch less of the scalp, and most prefer to wait until after the first trimester. Your doctor is the best person to confirm what's right for you — and once you've checked, we can book you in with our senior colourist and choose the gentlest option together. Shall I hold a consultation slot for you this week?"

It does not say the treatment is safe. It does not say it is dangerous. It gives her a path forward and ends with a concrete next step.

## Turn the enquiry into a relationship

Handle a pregnancy enquiry well and it becomes the start of a long relationship. The client who trusts you during her pregnancy comes back for her first post-baby cut and tells every new mother she knows where to go.

Keep a simple note on her file, with her permission, about the pregnancy and the services you discussed. If she postponed a treatment, message her when the time feels right: "We hope you're feeling well — whenever you're ready, your colour appointment is here for you." The few minutes you spend answering properly are the cheapest client retention your salon will ever do.
