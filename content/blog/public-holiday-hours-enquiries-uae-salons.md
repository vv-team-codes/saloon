Every year, about ten days before National Day, the same messages start landing in UAE salon inboxes. "Are you open on the 2nd?" "What time do you close on Eid?" They arrive on WhatsApp at midnight, on Instagram at breakfast, and by phone while your stylists are mid-colour. Each one looks like a small question. Together, they are one of the busiest enquiry waves of the year — and most salons answer them far worse than they realise.

The problem is not that clients ask. The problem is that the salon has not decided yet. When the person on the phone has to check with the owner, or the WhatsApp reply says "let me confirm and get back to you", the client hears uncertainty. In the UAE, where a public holiday often means a long weekend and last-minute beauty prep, an uncertain answer sends the client straight to the next salon on Google Maps. Holiday enquiries are not admin. They are bookings wearing a question mark.

## Decide early, decide once

The single biggest improvement you can make costs nothing: decide your holiday hours at least three weeks before the holiday, write them down, and tell every member of the team the same answer. National Day, Eid Al Fitr, Eid Al Adha, New Year's Day — the UAE holiday calendar is published well in advance, so there is no excuse for deciding the week of the holiday.

Once the decision is made, put it everywhere before the enquiries start: a pinned WhatsApp message, your Instagram highlights, a line on your Google Business Profile, and a short note at reception. Many of the "are you open?" messages will simply never arrive, because the client already found the answer.

## Answer the real question behind the question

When a client asks "are you open on National Day?", she is rarely asking only about your hours. She is really asking: can I get the service I need before the holiday, on the holiday itself, or the day after? A reply that only says "yes, we're open 10 to 6" answers none of those properly, and the conversation stalls.

The best replies answer all three at once. Confirm the hours, name the services available, and offer a concrete slot. "Yes, we're open on National Day, 10am to 6pm. We have hair and nails all day, and colour until 3pm. I have 11:30 or 2:00 free for a cut and blow-dry — shall I hold 11:30 for you?" That message takes thirty seconds to write and it closes the loop.

Speed matters here more than polish. Holiday enquiries cluster in the evenings, after work, when clients are planning the long weekend. A reply that arrives the next morning often arrives too late. ## One message that does the whole job

Holiday enquiries are repetitive, and repetitive questions deserve prepared answers. Write three short saved replies before each holiday and give them to everyone who answers messages.

First, the open-day reply: your hours, the services available, and two real slots with a question that invites a yes. Second, the closed-day reply: a warm confirmation that you are closed, the exact reopening time, and an offer to book the first slot when you reopen. Third, the fully-booked reply: an honest "we're full on that day", the nearest available alternative, and a place on the waitlist if a chair frees up.

## Staff the holiday diary without burning out the team

Holiday demand in the UAE is real but uneven. The day before a public holiday is often busier than the holiday itself, as clients prepare for gatherings and family photos. The morning of the holiday can be quiet, with a surge in the late afternoon as people head out for the evening. A salon that staffs every holiday hour at full strength wastes money; one that staffs it like a normal Tuesday turns clients away.

## When you are closed, say so proudly

Some salons close on public holidays, and that is a perfectly good decision — especially for small teams that have worked flat out through the festive season. The mistake is hiding it. A salon that leaves its hours unanswered, or replies vaguely to avoid disappointing anyone, loses more bookings than a salon that says plainly, "We're closed on National Day and reopening on the 3rd at 10am — shall I book you in for the first slot?"

A confident closed message respects the client enough to give her a straight answer, protects your team, and converts the enquiry anyway, because the client who wanted a blow-dry on the 2nd will often happily take the 3rd at 10am if you offer it before she goes looking elsewhere. Closure is only a lost booking if you let the conversation end at "we're closed".

## Turn the holiday rush into regulars

The clients who message you about holiday hours are disproportionately valuable. Many are new residents, visitors staying with family, or lapsed clients returning for a special occasion. They are asking because they do not yet have a regular salon — or because their regular salon has not answered them.

Treat the holiday enquiry as the start of a relationship, not a one-off transaction. Note the occasion in the client's record: National Day blow-dry, Eid henna, New Year colour. When she returns, the stylist who remembers why she first came in wins her loyalty far faster than any discount. A short follow-up after the holiday visit — "hope the celebrations went well, shall we book your usual slot for next month?" — turns a seasonal booking into a standing one.
