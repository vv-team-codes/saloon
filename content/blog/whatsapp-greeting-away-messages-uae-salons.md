Most salon enquiries in the UAE do not arrive between 10 am and 6 pm. They arrive at 9:40 pm, when a client scrolling in bed decides she wants her hair done before the weekend. They arrive on a Friday morning, while the salon is still closed.

What do those clients get? Silence until the next morning — or a blue tick and nothing at all. By the time the salon opens, she has messaged two other salons. The first one to reply wins the chair.

WhatsApp greeting and away messages fix this. They are the two automatic messages inside WhatsApp Business, free to set up. One greets a client the first time she messages you. The other answers when you are closed or away from the phone. Done well, they keep the conversation alive until a human can take over.

## The first message sets the tone

A greeting message is the first thing a new client ever hears from your salon. It fires automatically when someone messages you for the first time, or after fourteen days of silence. That makes it your digital front door.

The default says something like "Hello, thanks for contacting us." It tells the client nothing. A good greeting does three small jobs in two or three lines. It welcomes the client, tells her what she can do next, and sets a realistic expectation for when a person will reply.

Try this: "Hello, and welcome to [salon name] in [area]! Tell us the service you'd like and your preferred day, and we'll confirm a time and price. We reply within a couple of hours during opening times." It is warm, it is specific, and it asks for the two details you need — the service and the day. Do not stuff the greeting with your full price list and three links. A greeting is not a brochure.

## Away messages that don't lose the booking

The away message is the more valuable of the two, because it works while you sleep. It fires automatically outside your opening hours. In a UAE salon, that window is long: evenings after closing, Friday mornings, and the quiet hours before the team arrives.

The mistake most salons make is writing an away message that says nothing useful: "We are currently closed. We will get back to you soon." The client learns nothing, and "soon" could mean tomorrow morning or next Tuesday. She has no reason to wait.

A strong away message does four things. It states the opening time, so the client knows exactly when to expect a reply. It invites her to leave the details now, so the booking is half done before you open. It names your two or three most requested services, because that nudges her toward a decision. And it gives her a next step that needs no reply from you, such as your service menu or booking link.

Here is a version a Dubai salon could use: "Hi, thank you for messaging [salon name]! We are closed right now and open again tomorrow at 10 am. Leave us the service you'd like and your preferred day, and we'll confirm your slot first thing. Our most booked services are hair colour, balayage, and manicure and pedicure."

## The handover matters more than the message

An automatic reply is only useful if a human follows up quickly. The most common failure is a salon that sets up a beautiful away message and then nobody checks the inbox until noon. The client was promised a reply "first thing" — and by noon she has booked elsewhere.

Make the handover a habit. Whoever opens the salon should spend the first ten minutes of the day on WhatsApp, answering the overnight messages in the order they arrived. Start each reply by referring to what the client actually wrote. "Good morning! You asked about balayage on Saturday — we have 11 am or 4 pm with Sara" is a world away from "Hi, how can I help?"

The evening peak in UAE salons runs roughly from 5 pm to 9 pm, so a final inbox check at 8:30 pm, just before closing, is worth more than a perfect away message.

## A thirty-minute setup plan

You can set both messages up in half an hour, and you only need to do it once. Open WhatsApp Business, go to Business Tools, then Greeting Message and Away Message, and switch both on. Set the away message schedule to your real opening hours, so it switches itself on and off without anyone remembering.

Then write the two messages in your salon's voice. Read them out loud before saving them. If they sound stiff, rewrite them until they sound like something your receptionist would actually say. Keep the promises honest: never promise a reply time you cannot keep, and never put prices in unless they are real and current.

Finally, test them. Message the salon number from a personal phone after closing time and see what arrives. If anything looks wrong — a typo, a wrong opening time, a link that does not open — fix it on the spot. Revisit the wording once a month, and check every Monday that the away message is not still switched on from a public holiday.

## How to know it is working

You will know the messages are working when the morning inbox looks different. Instead of a pile of "hi" and "are you open?", you will see messages that already contain the service and the day. "Hi, do you have balayage on Saturday morning?" is a booking waiting to happen. "Hi" is a conversation that has not started yet.

Watch two simple numbers for a month. First, how many overnight messages turn into a confirmed appointment the next morning. Second, how quickly the first human reply goes out after opening. If the first number climbs and the second stays under an hour, the system is doing its job.
