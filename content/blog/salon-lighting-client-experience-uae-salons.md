A client leaves your salon delighted with her new colour. The next morning she messages you a photo from her bathroom, worried it looks brassy, dull or nothing like what she saw in your mirror. Nothing went wrong with the colour. The light did it.

Lighting is one of the most powerful — and least noticed — parts of the client experience. It decides how hair colour reads, how skin looks in the mirror, how relaxed a client feels, and how good the photos look that clients post afterwards. Getting it right takes a little thought, not a renovation.

## Why light changes everything your clients see

Hair colour is not a fixed thing. The same shade can look rich and warm under one light and flat or orange under another. A salon lit mostly with warm yellow bulbs will make ash tones look dull and golden tones look richer than they really are. Cool white light can make warm colours look washed out and skin look tired.

This matters most at the mirror, where a client decides whether she loves the result — under your lights, not daylight. If the colour was mixed to look perfect under flattering salon lighting but misleads at home, the disappointment arrives later as a message, a bad review or a client who quietly books elsewhere.

The same is true for skin: facials, makeup and brow work are judged in your mirror first, and soft, even light from the front shows the real result while harsh overhead light makes skin look uneven and tired.

## The daylight test every salon should do

The simplest check costs nothing. On a bright afternoon, stand a client in front of your main mirror, then walk her to the nearest window and look again. If the colour shifts noticeably, your salon lighting is doing too much of the talking.

Daylight is the honest reference point, and in the UAE there is plenty of it. After a colour service, invite the client to check the result in natural light before she leaves — it takes thirty seconds and clients remember the honesty.

For salons in malls or basements without windows, the answer is not more light but the right light: bulbs with a colour rendering index (CRI) of 90 or above, which measures how faithfully light shows true colours. Swapping the bulbs over your colour and cutting stations is one of the cheapest upgrades a salon can make.

## Warm, cool or in between?

Light colour is measured in Kelvin. Lower numbers are warmer and more golden; higher numbers are cooler and more blue-white. Most salons need a middle ground, around 4000 Kelvin, often called neutral white. It is close enough to daylight to show colour honestly, but warm enough that the salon still feels welcoming rather than clinical.

Mixing temperatures without thinking creates three different versions of the same client as she moves through the salon — a warm pendant over reception, cool tubes over the colour station and a yellow lamp in the treatment room. Pick one temperature for the client-facing areas and keep it consistent, adding warmth only where relaxation matters more than colour accuracy — the waiting area, the wash room and the treatment rooms.

## Mirrors, selfies and the phone test

Every client photographs her result now. The after photo goes on WhatsApp, Instagram and TikTok within minutes — free marketing for your salon, or a quiet warning to her friends.

Front lighting is what makes a good selfie. Light from behind the client or directly above her casts shadows under the eyes and chin and flattens the hair. Light from the front, at face height, softens everything. A vertical light strip on either side of the mirror, or a ring light at the styling station, transforms the photo and the client's mood when she sees it.

Do your own phone test: photograph a finished style under your current mirror lighting, then again with a small ring light or a repositioned lamp. If the second photo looks noticeably better, your mirror lighting is costing you marketing — clients share the photo they love and tag the salon that made them look that good.

## Lighting by zone, not one switch for everything

At reception, warm and welcoming wins — the light should feel like a hotel lobby, not an office. A dimmer here is worth its weight in gold: bright at 10 in the morning, softer by 8 in the evening.

At the wash basin, clients lie back with their eyes closed — bright overhead light in their face ruins a relaxing wash. Use soft, indirect light, or a dimmer the stylist can lower.

At the colour and cutting stations, honest neutral light is non-negotiable. At the nail station, focused task lighting matters most. In treatment rooms, low and warm is the rule — bright clinical light tells a relaxing body the opposite.

## Small fixes and a one-evening audit

You do not need an electrician to start. Replace the bulbs over your mirrors with high-CRI neutral white bulbs, add a dimmer to reception and the treatment rooms, and clean your light fittings — dust dims a bulb far more than most owners realise. Move one lamp to light the client's face from the front, and replace any flickering tube the same day.

Then walk through at three times — mid-morning, late afternoon and 8 in the evening. A room that looks perfect at noon can feel harsh or gloomy after dark.

Set aside one quiet evening and audit your salon the way a new client would see it: walk in from the street, sit in each chair, look in each mirror, take a photo in each zone. Write down three fixes — one free, one cheap, one worth budgeting for — and do the first two within the week.

The payoff is quiet but real. Clients who love what they see in your mirror rebook faster, photograph their results and recommend you with confidence. Colour corrections drop, because you caught the true tone before the client left.

The experience your clients remember is never one big thing — it is the sum of small details. Get the light right and every other detail looks better in it.
