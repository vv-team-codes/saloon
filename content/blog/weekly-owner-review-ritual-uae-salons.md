Most UAE salon owners are busy every hour of the week. They are cutting, colouring, answering WhatsApp messages, ordering stock and fixing the diary — and somehow still feel behind. The irony is that the busiest owners are often the ones whose salons grow the slowest, because nobody ever steps back to look at the whole picture.

The fix is not a new app or a management course. It is one protected hour a week, spent on the business instead of in it. Owners who do this make better decisions than owners who don't, and the difference shows up in the diary within a month. Here is how to run it.

## Pick a slot and protect it

A review that moves around never happens. Pick one hour that survives the week: Sunday evening after close, Monday morning before the first client, or the quiet stretch on a Tuesday afternoon. Put it in the diary as a real appointment with yourself, the same way you would block a colour appointment. Tell the team it is your planning hour and ask them to hold non-urgent questions.

Keep it to one hour. Longer than that and it becomes a chore you will dodge. Shorter than that and you will only skim the surface. Set a rough timer for each part of the review so one problem does not swallow the whole hour.

## Start with the diary, not the bank balance

Look at next week's diary first, because it is where the money is made. How many chairs are still empty on Saturday, the day that matters most? Which evening slots between 5 PM and 9 PM are still open? Are there any three-hour gaps in the middle of a weekday where a stylist will be standing around?

When you find the gaps, act on them in the same hour. Message two or three regulars who usually book that service and offer the empty slot. Put one last-minute offer on your WhatsApp Status. If a gap keeps appearing at the same time every week, that is a diary design problem, telling you to change your opening hours or your shift pattern.

Then look backwards at last week's diary. Which stylist had the most rebookings? Which service ran over its time every single day? One colour service that always takes forty minutes longer than booked is quietly costing you a chair every day, and a weekly review is where you finally notice it.

## Five numbers, no spreadsheets degree needed

You do not need a dashboard to run a salon well. You need five numbers, written down in a notebook every week so you can see the trend: total revenue for the week, the rebooking rate, the average bill per client, how many WhatsApp enquiries turned into bookings, and retail sales.

Watch the direction, not the precision. If rebooking is drifting down for three weeks in a row, something in the client experience has slipped and you have caught it early. If the average bill is falling, somebody has stopped suggesting add-ons or the diary is filling with short services at the expense of longer ones.

## One team conversation, not a lecture

Spend fifteen minutes of your hour on the team. Pick one thing you noticed during the week — a stylist who handled a difficult client beautifully, a front-desk reply that lost a booking, a station that was never reset properly — and turn it into one clear, kind conversation.

Praise in public, correct in private, and always name the specific behaviour. "You were great this week" changes nothing. "The way you offered the blow-dry at the basin on Tuesday is exactly how we keep chairs full" teaches the whole team.

Also ask one team member a week what they need. A stylist quietly struggling with a new service, or a receptionist drowning in WhatsApp at 7 PM, will not volunteer it in the middle of a busy Saturday. Your planning hour is the moment you hear it in time.

## Stock, suppliers and the small leaks

Salon profit leaks through small holes, and a weekly check finds them before they become expensive. Count the three or four products you use most — colour, shampoo, your bestselling retail item — and note whether you are about to run out or sitting on too much. Running out mid-week means emergency orders and lost bookings. Overstocking means cash sitting on a shelf.

Check one supplier detail a week. Is there a product you order every week that you could buy monthly for a better price? Is a delivery consistently arriving late and messing up your Friday?

## Look one month ahead, not one day ahead

The last fifteen minutes are for the calendar, not the diary. What is happening in the UAE in the next four to six weeks that should change what your salon does? National Day, a school holiday, the start of wedding season, the summer slowdown — every one of these shows up in your diary whether you plan for it or not. Planning for it is what turns it into revenue instead of chaos.

Write down one action for the coming month: a small party-season package, a reminder to pre-book regulars before the festive rush, or a quiet-week training session for the team. One action a week adds up to fifty-two improvements a year, and that is how a good salon becomes a great one.

## A business that grows while you breathe

Running a salon in the UAE means competing for attention in one of the most crowded beauty markets in the world. The salons that pull ahead are rarely the ones with the biggest budgets. They are the ones whose owners know what is happening in their own business — which hours are empty, which clients are drifting, which services actually pay — and act on it while there is still time to fix it.

One hour a week is a small price for that. Put it in the diary, protect it like a paying client, and let the ritual do its quiet work.
