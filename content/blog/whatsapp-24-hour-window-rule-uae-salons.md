If you run a salon in Dubai or anywhere in the UAE, most of your bookings start on WhatsApp. A client messages at 11pm about a balayage, you see it the next morning, and you reply — or try to. Sometimes the reply goes through. Sometimes WhatsApp quietly blocks it, and you never find out until the client books somewhere else.

The reason is a rule most salon owners have never heard of: the 24-hour window. Once you understand it, a lot of lost bookings suddenly make sense. And once you work with it, your WhatsApp stops leaking clients.

## What the 24-hour window actually is

WhatsApp Business lets you reply freely to a client for 24 hours after their last message. Inside that window, you can write anything — answer a price question, send photos, suggest a slot, confirm a booking. The moment the window closes, free-form replies stop working. You can only message that client again using an approved template message, which costs money and has to follow Meta's rules.

This is not a glitch. It is how WhatsApp protects people from spam, and it applies to every business, from a one-chair home salon to a large salon in a Dubai mall.

The practical consequence is simple: every hour you wait to reply costs you. A client who messages at 9pm and hears nothing until the next afternoon may already be outside your window.

## Why this hits UAE salons harder than most

The UAE salon day does not run nine to five. Clients message late at night after the kids are asleep, during the school run, and in the quiet minutes between meetings. The busiest enquiry hours are often the hours your salon is closed. A client messages at midnight about a Saturday slot; your team opens the phone at 10am and replies at noon. That is already twelve hours gone. If the conversation then pauses for a day — she is deciding, she is checking with her husband, she is waiting for payday — the window closes before you ever get to close the sale.

Weekend patterns make it worse. Friday and Saturday are peak salon days in the UAE, and they are also the days your team is busiest on the floor. Messages pile up while stylists are with clients, and by Sunday evening half your unread chats are past the window.

## The three habits that keep you inside the window

You do not need new software to fix this. You need three habits, and they cost nothing.

First, answer fast, even briefly. A short reply inside the window is worth more than a perfect reply outside it. "Hi Sara, yes — balayage is AED 650 and takes about three hours. Shall I hold Saturday 4pm for you?" takes twenty seconds and keeps the conversation alive for another 24 hours, because every message the client sends reopens the window. Train your team that the first reply is a holding reply, not a final answer.

Second, end every reply with a question. A conversation only stays open while the client keeps talking. "Would morning or evening suit you better?" is not small talk — it is a window extender. Every answer she sends buys you another day.

Third, run a ten-minute end-of-day sweep. Before the salon closes, one person opens the WhatsApp inbox and checks every chat older than a few hours. Anything unanswered gets a short reply that evening, not tomorrow morning. This single habit rescues more bookings than any marketing campaign.

## Template messages: your safety net when the window closes

Sometimes the window closes anyway. A client goes quiet for a week, then your quiet Tuesday needs filling. This is what template messages are for. They are pre-written, pre-approved messages you can send outside the 24-hour window — appointment reminders, booking confirmations, and a limited range of promotional messages.

Templates have rules. They must be approved before you use them, promotional templates have content limits, and every template costs a small fee per message. They also read more formally than a normal chat message, so write them like a human: one clear purpose, one clear next step.

The smartest use of templates is not marketing. It is recovery. A template that says "Hi Aisha, your usual colour slot is free this Thursday at 5pm — shall I book it for you?" sent to a regular who has gone quiet will outperform any broadcast offer, because it is personal, timely and easy to answer with one word. Build three or four templates you actually use — a reminder, a rebooking nudge, a slot-opening alert, and a gentle check-in.

One warning: never use templates to blast your whole client list. Clients can block and report you, and enough reports can restrict your number.

## What to do when a client has gone cold

A chat that has been silent for weeks is not dead, but it needs a different approach. Do not reopen with "Hi, just checking in" — it says nothing and earns nothing. Reopen with something specific to that client: the service she had last time, the stylist she asked for, or a slot that fits her usual pattern. "Hi Mariam, Sara has Thursday 6pm free — your usual cut and blow-dry slot. Want it?" gives her a reason to answer, and her answer reopens the window.

If she does not answer the template, leave it. One message is a reminder; three in a week is pressure. Note the date, try again in a month with a genuinely new reason, and move on.

## Making the rule work for your salon

The 24-hour window is not an obstacle to work around. It is a discipline that makes your salon better. Salons that reply fast, ask questions, and sweep their inbox every evening book more and lose fewer clients to competitors.

Start this week. Put one person in charge of the inbox for each shift, agree on a first-reply time of under an hour during opening hours, and write your four recovery templates now, before you need them. Check your unread chats tonight and count how many are already past 24 hours. That number is your lost bookings for the week — and it is entirely fixable.
