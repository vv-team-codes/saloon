She loved the cut. She loved the colour. She took a photo in the mirror, hugged her stylist, and floated toward the reception desk. Then she saw the bill — and her face changed. It was more than she expected, by enough that the whole visit suddenly felt different.

That moment at the till is one of the most dangerous in a UAE salon. The client does not complain. She pays, smiles tightly, and simply never comes back — no review, no message, no second chance. The salon only notices weeks later, when a regular quietly disappears from the diary. Yet a bill surprise is almost always preventable, and when it still happens, the way the team handles it decides whether she returns or walks away for good.

## Why the surprise happens in honest salons

Most bill surprises are not caused by dishonesty. They are caused by small gaps between what the client imagined and what actually happened in the chair.

A client books a haircut and, mid-visit, agrees to a gloss, a treatment and a blow-dry. Each addition feels small in the moment. Together, they add up. She never heard a running total, so the final number feels like a jump.

Or the price she saw online was a starting price — "balayage from AED 450" — and her hair needed two rounds of lightening, extra product and an extra hour. The starting price was honest. Her hair simply needed more. But nobody explained that before the foils went in.

In Dubai and Abu Dhabi, where clients move between salons in malls, on high streets and inside hotels, these gaps are common. A client who has lived here for years still cannot guess your prices from your address. The cure is to say the number out loud, early, and more than once.

## Say the price before the service starts

The single most effective habit is also the simplest: confirm the total before the service begins. Not a range, not a starting price — the total for what she is actually having today.

At the consultation, once the stylist knows the plan, the words are plain: "Your cut, colour and blow-dry today will be AED 620. Shall we go ahead?" That sentence removes almost every bill surprise a salon will ever face.

It also protects the team. When a client asks mid-service for an extra treatment, the stylist can say yes and add the price at the same time: "Of course — that adds AED 80, so your total becomes AED 700. Is that alright?" Clients almost never object to a price they have agreed to. They object to a price they first meet at the till.

Make this a rule, not a suggestion. Put the price confirmation into the consultation script, and have the receptionist repeat it when she confirms the booking the day before. A client who hears the number twice never feels ambushed by it.

## When the bill still surprises her

Even with good habits, it will happen. A regular asks for "the usual" and the usual has quietly grown. A price list changed last month and nobody told her.

The first rule at the till is to slow down. Do not rush the payment, do not look embarrassed, and do not immediately offer a discount. A rushed discount tells the client the price was wrong; a calm explanation tells her it was fair and she simply did not know it.

Walk her through the bill, line by line. "Your cut is AED 180, the full colour is AED 350, and the gloss we added is AED 90. That is AED 620 in total." Most clients relax once they see the parts. The number stops being a shock and becomes a list of things she chose.

Then ask: "Does that match what you were expecting?" If the answer is no, listen before defending. Once you know her expectation, you can answer it — with the right information, not with a fight.

Never argue about the price at reception, blame the client for not checking, or cut the price in a panic — each of those turns a small surprise into a lost client. What she will not forgive is the feeling of being tricked; what she will forgive is an honest mistake, explained calmly.

If the salon genuinely made a mistake — quoted the wrong price, or added a service she never agreed to — fix it on the spot. Remove the charge, apologise simply, and move on. One corrected bill costs far less than a lost regular.

## Fix the system, not just the moment

One bill surprise is a moment. Three in a month is a system problem, and it is worth an hour of the owner's time to find it.

Start with the price list. Is it visible — on the wall, on the website, in the WhatsApp catalogue — with real prices, not just "from" prices? A client who can check the price before she books never gets surprised.

Next, check the booking message. When a client books on WhatsApp, does the confirmation include the price of the service she booked? Adding one line with the total costs nothing and prevents most surprises before the client even arrives. Finally, review price changes. When a price goes up, regulars deserve to hear it before their next visit, not discover it at the till. A short WhatsApp message — "Just to let you know, from next month our full colour will be AED 380" — turns a shock into a small, accepted fact. Clients accept price rises they were told about. They resent price rises they discover.

## The clients who come back anyway

Here is the part owners forget: a bill surprise handled well can actually strengthen loyalty. A client who felt heard and was told honestly where the gap came from has seen how the salon behaves when things go wrong — and she liked what she saw.

That is why the till deserves as much care as the chair. The cut and the colour are the reason she books; the honesty at the till is the reason she books again. Confirm the price before the service, explain it calmly if it still surprises her, own the gaps, and fix the system so it happens less next month.
