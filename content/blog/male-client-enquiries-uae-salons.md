It usually arrives on WhatsApp in the evening, short and a little cautious. "Hi, do you take male clients?" Sometimes it comes from the man himself. Sometimes it comes from his wife, booking a haircut for him while she is already a regular. Either way, the salon's reply decides whether a new client walks through the door or quietly moves on to the next salon on Google Maps.

This is one of the most common enquiries UAE salons receive, and one of the most badly answered. Too many replies are a single word — "yes" or "no" — with nothing after it. That tells the man nothing about whether he will feel comfortable, what it will cost, or when he can come. That silence is lost revenue.

## What he is really asking

"Do you take male clients?" sounds like a yes-or-no question. It is actually three questions in one.

First, am I welcome here? Many men have walked into a salon and felt like they were in the wrong place — the only man in a room full of women, nobody quite sure where to seat him. He is checking that he will not be an awkward exception.

Second, will I be comfortable? He wants to know whether the stylist has experience with men's hair, and whether the visit will be quick and straightforward.

Third, what will it cost and how long will it take? A good reply answers all three, in one message, without making a fuss of any of them.

## Say the policy first, clearly and warmly

Do not make him guess. The first line of your reply should state your position plainly.

If your salon welcomes men, say so: "Yes, we welcome male clients — we have a dedicated men's haircut and beard service, and our stylist Ahmed has ten years of experience with men's cuts." That one sentence answers the welcome question, names the service, and gives him a person to picture.

If your salon is ladies-only, say that too, kindly and without apology: "We're a ladies-only salon, so we don't offer men's services — but we'd be glad to recommend a good men's barber nearby." A warm no with a useful referral leaves a better impression than a cold one.

What never works is a hedge. "We mostly do ladies" or "Sometimes, it depends" leaves him exactly where he started. He will not ask a second time. He will message the next salon.

## If you are unisex, prove it on the menu

Plenty of UAE salons say they are unisex, but their Instagram, price list and Google profile all speak to women. A man who has to hunt for evidence that he belongs will assume he does not.

Put men's services on the menu by name, with their own prices and durations: men's cut, beard trim and shape, men's facial, grey blending. Show real photos of male clients, with their permission, on your Instagram and your Google profile.

If only one stylist on your team cuts men's hair well, say so and book him with that stylist. A man who gets a great cut from the right person becomes a regular every three weeks; a mediocre cut from whoever was free loses him for good.

## Comfort works both ways

Welcoming male clients is not only about the man. Your female regulars matter just as much, and some chose your salon precisely because it feels like their space. A thoughtful owner protects both.

That means small, practical choices: seat male clients where they do not face the ladies' section directly, and use a separate room or quieter corner for any client who prefers privacy. Train the team so nobody stares, nobody giggles, and nobody treats a male booking as an event. The fastest way to make a man comfortable is to treat his visit as completely normal, because it is.

## Price it honestly, and say the time

Men's services are often quicker than women's, and clients know it. If a men's cut takes thirty minutes and costs AED 80, say so in the reply. Do not quote a range so wide it means nothing, and do not leave the price for the chair — that is where trust breaks down.

A skin fade with a beard shape takes longer than a simple trim, and grey blending is a colour service with a colour price — say that plainly too. Then end with two real slots: "We have today at 6:30 PM or tomorrow at 11 AM — which suits you?"

## The first visit decides everything

A man trying a new salon decides in the first ten minutes whether this becomes his regular place. Greet him by name at the door, offer water or karak, and ask two questions before the cape goes on: how he likes it cut, and when he last had it done. Show him the result in the mirror before you finish.

Before he leaves, book the next visit at the chair — three weeks out for a cut, two for a beard trim — and send the confirmation on WhatsApp the same evening. That small routine turns a one-off enquiry into a client who books twelve times a year and recommends you to his colleagues. Male regulars are among the most loyal clients a salon can have, because they value a place that gets it right without a fuss.

## A reply you can copy

Keep one saved reply ready for this enquiry, and let the whole team use it:

"Hi, thanks for asking! Yes, we welcome male clients. A men's cut is AED 80 and takes about 30 minutes, and a cut with beard shaping is AED 120 and takes 45 minutes. Our stylist Ahmed specialises in men's cuts. We have today at 6:30 PM or tomorrow at 11 AM — would either suit you?"

Adjust the names, prices and times to your salon. The shape is what matters: welcome, service, price, time, and a booking question. Five lines, no hedging, no waiting for a second message.

Every enquiry is a client deciding whether to trust you. The men messaging your salon have already decided they like what they see. Answer them like you mean it, and they will keep coming back.
