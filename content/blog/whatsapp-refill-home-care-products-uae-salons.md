Every salon owner in the UAE knows the pattern. A client leaves with a perfect blow-dry or fresh colour — and six weeks later she is back with dull hair, because her shampoo ran out in week three and she bought a random replacement from the supermarket. The service was excellent. The home care in between was not.

That gap between visits is where salons quietly lose money twice: once in retail sales they never made, and once in results that fade before the next appointment. The fix is not a bigger shelf by the mirror. It is a short WhatsApp message, sent at the right moment, offering the exact product the client already uses.

## Why refills work better than shelf sales

Selling a product at the chair is the hardest sale in the salon. The client is still processing the bill, and she has to decide on the spot. A refill message arrives later, when the need is real. Her bottle is nearly empty. She remembers how her hair felt after your salon.

Refills also suit how UAE clients actually live. Many travel for weeks in summer or over the holidays, and a bottle bought in May is finished by July. A WhatsApp message reaches them wherever they are, and a product they can pick up at their next visit — or have delivered — removes every excuse to buy elsewhere.

## What to sell on WhatsApp — and what not to

The refill list should be short: five to eight products at most, all of them products you already use in the salon and genuinely recommend. Think shampoo and conditioner in the exact range you used on the client, a leave-in treatment, a heat protectant, a face wash from your facial line, and a nail oil for regular manicure clients. Every item should be something a client has already tried on her own hair or skin, in your chair.

Do not try to sell products a client has never used through a message. New products belong in the chair, where you can show the result. Refills belong on WhatsApp, where the result is already proven.

Price refills honestly. A small discount on a bundle — shampoo plus conditioner, for example — is fine. A deep discount is not. The value is convenience and the right product, not a bargain.

## Setting up the refill flow

WhatsApp Business already has everything this needs.

First, build a small catalogue: each product with a clear photo of the actual bottle, the price, and one line saying who it is for.

Second, note what each regular uses — "Aisha: purple shampoo + keratin mask" — so the message can name her product. "Your purple shampoo" is personal. "Our shampoo range" is an advert.

Third, decide how the client gets the product. The simplest option is pickup at the next appointment: she replies yes, you put the bottle aside with her name on it, and it is waiting at her chair. For clients who will not be in for weeks, offer delivery through a local courier. Charge the delivery cost honestly, and never promise same-day delivery you cannot keep.

## The message itself: short, personal, and timed

Timing is what makes the refill message work. The right moment is when the product is running out — usually three to four weeks after the visit for a shampoo, two to three weeks for a facial product used daily. Send it in the early evening, when clients are at home and actually read their messages.

Keep it to three or four lines, and always end with a simple choice. A message that works sounds like this:

"Hi Sara, hope the colour is still looking lovely. Your purple shampoo usually runs out around now — would you like me to keep a bottle aside for your visit on Thursday, or shall I send one over?"

That message does four things: it remembers her, it names her product, it gives her two easy answers, and it connects to a visit that is already booked. It is not a broadcast. Send refills as individual messages, never as a broadcast list blast. A broadcast that says "20% off shampoo this week" will be ignored by almost everyone and will train clients to ignore your messages. A personal message to twelve regulars will get ten replies.

If a client does not reply, leave it. One message is a service. A second message about the same bottle is pressure.

## Payment without the awkwardness

A payment link sent in the same chat is the cleanest option: she taps, pays, and the order is confirmed in seconds. If you do not use payment links, take the payment at pickup — "I will add it to your bill on Thursday" — and never chase a client for a bottle she has not collected.

Keep a short record of every refill order: client, product, date, paid or pending. A notebook or a simple spreadsheet is enough.

## The routine that keeps it going

A refill routine only earns money if it happens every week. Set aside ten minutes on a quiet morning — a Tuesday or Wednesday, when the diary is light — to look at the week's clients from three or four weeks ago and send their refill messages. Ten clients, ten short messages, ten minutes. That is the whole system.

Review it once a month. Which products sold? Which messages got replies? Drop the products nobody orders, and add the one clients keep asking about. Over a few months, a pattern appears: a handful of products, a handful of regulars, and a small but steady income that arrives between appointments instead of only on appointment days.

There is a second benefit beyond the margin: a client who buys her home care from you stays connected to your salon between visits. When her friend asks where she gets her shampoo, she names your salon.
