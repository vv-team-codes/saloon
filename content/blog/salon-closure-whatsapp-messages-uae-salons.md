Every UAE salon closes for a while at some point. A renovation. A full-team annual leave. An Eid break. Landlord works that leave the shop unusable for a week. The closure itself is rarely the problem. The problem is the silence around it — and what clients do in that silence.

A regular who cannot reach you does not wait patiently. She messages once, gets no answer, and by Thursday she has booked somewhere else. A client with an appointment inside the closure week assumes she has been forgotten and starts looking for a replacement. Handled badly, a closure quietly costs you the clients who took years to win. Handled well, the reopening week is full before the doors open. The difference is almost entirely in the WhatsApp messages you send, in what order, and to whom.

## Tell them before they hear it from someone else

Your clients should hear about a closure from you, not from a locked door or a friend who walked past the shop. Announce early — two weeks ahead for a planned closure, and the same day for an unplanned one. Early notice turns a closure from a shock into a plan clients can work around.

Use more than one channel. A WhatsApp broadcast to your client list carries the news, a WhatsApp Status post catches the clients who read Status before messages, and your WhatsApp greeting message should mention the closure dates so anyone who messages during the break gets the facts immediately instead of silence.

Keep the announcement short and specific. Say the dates, say the reason in one line, and say what happens next. "We are closed from Monday 8 December to Sunday 14 December while we renovate the salon. Your appointments are safe — we will message you personally with a new time." Clients need dates, reassurance, and a promise of a personal follow-up.

## Sort your clients into three groups

Not every client needs the same message. Before you announce anything, sort your client list into three groups.

The first group is clients with appointments inside the closure dates. Message them first, individually, before the general announcement goes out. Nobody should learn from a broadcast that her Saturday bridal trial has moved. A personal message that names her appointment and offers two new times shows her she has not been forgotten.

The second group is regulars with no appointment booked. They need the announcement and a simple invitation: book before the closure, or book the reopening week now. Give them a reason to act early — first pick of reopening slots — rather than a vague "see you soon".

The third group is everyone else: occasional clients and people who enquired but never booked. They get the general announcement only. There is no need to offer them priority slots you should be saving for regulars.

## The rebooking message is the one that matters most

The announcement tells clients you are closing. The rebooking message is what protects your revenue. For every affected appointment, send a personal message with two concrete alternatives, not an open question.

"Hi Aisha, your colour appointment on Tuesday 9 December falls inside our renovation week. I can move you to Saturday 6 December at 4 PM with Sara, or to Monday 15 December at 11 AM when we reopen. Which suits you better?" It names the client, it names the original appointment, and it makes replying easy — she only has to pick one of two times.

Never write "let us know when you are free". An open question puts the work on the client, and busy clients answer open questions by not answering at all. No reply in two days? Follow up once, kindly, and then leave it.

One more detail owners forget: move the deposit with the appointment, and say so. "Your deposit moves with you to the new date." Clients who have paid a deposit worry silently about losing it. One sentence removes the worry.

## Answer while you are closed

A closure week is not a week of silence. Enquiries keep arriving — someone's roots need doing, someone is in Dubai for three days and wants a blow-dry, someone is asking about January prices.

This is where a 24/7 AI receptionist like ZYNA earns its keep: it can answer WhatsApp enquiries, explain the closure dates, and take bookings for the reopening week while the whole team is away. Even without one, the minimum is a WhatsApp away message that states the dates, says when you reopen, and invites the client to leave her request so you can confirm on your return. Then check the inbox once a day during the closure and answer the genuinely urgent ones. Clients forgive a closure. They do not forgive being ignored.

## Make the reopening week the event

Treat reopening as an event, not a return to normal. Three days before you reopen, message your regulars with the reopening date and first pick of slots. The day before, post a WhatsApp Status with a photo of the finished salon — the new chairs, the fresh paint, the appointment book filling up. On reopening morning, send a short message to everyone you rebooked: "We are back today, and your chair is ready."

Some owners add a small reopening gesture for the clients whose appointments moved — a complimentary blow-dry with their colour, or a head massage with their facial. The diary for reopening week should be full before the doors open, not filled slowly over the following fortnight.

## What not to do

A few mistakes turn a manageable closure into a client exodus. Do not announce the closure the day before it starts, unless it is genuinely an emergency. Do not send the same long message to every client — the bride with a trial inside the closure week is not the same as someone who visited once in March. Do not cancel appointments without offering new times in the same message. Do not go silent for the whole week and hope clients come back on their own.

Closures are a normal part of running a salon in the UAE. Clients understand that. What they do not understand is being left without a new appointment. Tell them early, sort them into groups, rebook them personally with two real choices, answer while you are away, and make reopening worth coming back for. Do that, and a closed week becomes proof that your salon looks after its clients — even when the doors are shut.
