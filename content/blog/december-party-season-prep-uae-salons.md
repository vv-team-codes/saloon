December is the month every UAE salon owner both loves and dreads. Office parties, National Day gatherings and New Year's Eve plans all land in the same few weeks, and suddenly everyone wants a blow-dry, a fresh colour and a manicure — ideally on the same Friday evening. The salons that make real money in December are rarely the ones with the biggest discounts. They are the ones that planned for the rush while November was still quiet.

## Start with the diary, not the decorations

The most common December mistake is filling the diary on a first-come, first-served basis and discovering too late that the peak evenings are blocked by long colour appointments while the clients who wanted a quick blow-dry have gone elsewhere.

Block your peak hours — typically 5pm to 9pm on Thursdays, Fridays and Saturdays — for the services that earn the most per hour and that clients actually want before a party. Blow-dries, express facials, manicures and party-ready styling belong there. Long colour corrections, which tie up a chair for three hours, are better placed in weekday mornings when the diary is quieter.

## Pre-book your regulars before the rush arrives

Your regulars are the clients most likely to be squeezed out by December demand, and they are also the clients you most want to keep. In early November, message your regulars with their usual slot and a simple question: would you like the same time in December?

This is not a sales push. It is a service. A client who gets her usual Saturday 6pm slot secured in advance does not need to compete with party-season walk-ins, and you get a diary that is partly filled before the rush even starts. Offer two or three dates rather than one, and confirm the booking with a WhatsApp message the day before.

## Staff the month in waves, not in one long push

December breaks teams that try to run at full speed for four straight weeks. The salons that get through it in good shape plan staffing in waves. The first two weeks of December are busy but manageable, so keep normal rotas and use that time to build a buffer of rest. The third and fourth weeks are the real peak, and that is when you need your strongest team on the floor.

Talk to your team now about extended hours. Agree in advance which days the salon will stay open later, who works those evenings, and how the extra hours are compensated — whether that is overtime pay, time off in January, or a mix of both. Stylists who feel the plan was made with them will carry the busy weeks; stylists who first hear about a 10pm finish on the day itself will be exhausted by the second weekend.

Keep one contingency in mind. Someone will call in sick during the busiest week of the year. Decide now who covers and which appointments can be moved without a phone call. A plan made in November is calm. The same decision made at 9am on a packed Saturday is not.

## Stock for the services clients actually ask for

Party season changes what clients buy. Blow-dry products, shine sprays, setting products, express facial kits and party-ready nail colours move fast, while the slow sellers of October sit untouched. Check your stock in mid-November and order the fast movers in quantities that match December demand, not your usual monthly order.

This is also the month to check your retail shelf with fresh eyes. Clients who come in for a party blow-dry are more open than usual to taking home the product that created the result — but only if it is in front of them, with a price they can see, and if the stylist mentions it naturally at the mirror rather than at the till.

Watch one number through the month: how many days of stock you have left for your top five products. When it drops below a week, reorder immediately. UAE suppliers slow down around the holidays, and a stock-out in the last week of December is revenue you cannot recover.

## Make booking easy when the phone will not stop

December enquiries arrive at all hours. A client finishing a work dinner at 10pm decides she wants a blow-dry for the next evening and messages the salon before she forgets. If nobody replies until the next morning, she has already messaged two other salons.

This is where the booking path matters more than the marketing. A client should be able to see real availability, pick a service and a time, and get a confirmation without waiting for a human reply. Salons that offer online self-booking alongside WhatsApp capture the late-night enquiries that phone-only salons lose. For everything else, a quick reply that names a real slot — "Thursday 6:30pm with Sara is free, shall I hold it for you?" — beats a friendly "we'll check and get back to you" every time. ## Protect January while you are busy in December

The salons that struggle most in January are often the ones that had a wonderful December. They spent the whole month serving new party clients, then January arrives, the new clients disappear, and the regulars they squeezed out have found another salon.

The fix is simple and it costs nothing. When a new client books for a party, rebook her before she leaves. Not a hard sell — a simple question at the mirror: "Shall we book your next visit now, so you keep this time in January?" Clients who leave with a January appointment are far more likely to return than clients who leave with a vague promise to call.

## A short checklist for the next two weeks

December rewards preparation more than effort. Before the rush arrives, make sure your peak-hour diary is shaped around short, high-demand services; your regulars have been offered their December slots; your team has agreed extended hours and rest days; your fast-moving stock is ordered; your booking path works after hours; and every client who visits in December leaves with a January appointment.

Party season will be busy either way. The difference between a December that grows your business and a December that simply exhausts it is the planning you do now.
