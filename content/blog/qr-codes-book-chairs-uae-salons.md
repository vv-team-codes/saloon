Walk through any mall in Dubai and you will see people scanning little black-and-white squares without thinking twice. They scan to see a menu, to pay for parking, to open a loyalty app. In the UAE, scanning a QR code is now as ordinary as tapping a card.

That habit is an opportunity most salons have not properly used. A salon is full of quiet moments when a client is sitting still, phone in hand. She is waiting for colour to develop. She is sitting under the dryer. She is standing at the mirror, admiring the finished result. Each of those moments is a chance to make the next booking easier — and a well-placed QR code is the simplest way to do it.

The mistake is treating a QR code as a decoration. A code that leads to a generic homepage, or one printed too small to scan, does nothing. A code that does one clear job, placed exactly where that job matters, quietly books chairs.

## One code, one job

The first rule is simple: every QR code should do exactly one thing. A code that opens your Instagram is not the same as a code that books a blow-dry, and a client who scans expecting one and gets the other will not scan again.

The most useful jobs for a UAE salon are booking a specific service, leaving a Google review, rebooking the next visit, starting a WhatsApp chat, viewing a price list, and reading aftercare instructions. Six jobs, six codes, each placed where its job makes sense.

A booking code belongs on the mirror and on the receipt. A review code belongs where the client is happiest, which is the mirror at the end of the appointment, not the till where she is already thinking about paying. A WhatsApp code belongs on the business card and the shopfront window, where someone walking past after closing time can still reach you. An aftercare code belongs on the card you hand over with the treatment, so the instructions live on her phone instead of a piece of paper she will lose before she reaches the car park.

## The booking code is your quietest salesperson

Your salon closes at nine or ten in the evening, but your clients do their thinking much later. A woman lying in bed at midnight, scrolling through photos of the balayage she wants, is not going to call you. She might, however, scan a code she saw on your Instagram story or on the card in her bag, and land straight on a booking page for that exact service.

That is why a booking code should never land on your homepage. It should land on the service itself, with the price range, the duration and the next available slots already visible. Every extra tap is a chance to lose her. One code, one service, one short path to a confirmed slot.

Mall salons have an extra advantage here. A code on the shopfront window works all night, long after the shutters come down. Someone walking past at eleven, on her way back from dinner, can scan it and book tomorrow's first blow-dry slot before she has even reached the lift.

## Ask for the review at the happiest moment

Most salons ask for Google reviews at the wrong time. The receptionist mentions it while the client is paying, when her mind has already moved on to the parking meter. Or the salon sends a message the next day, when the excitement of the new haircut has already faded.

The best moment is the mirror moment. She has just turned, seen the result from every angle, and smiled. That is when a small, elegant code on the mirror frame, with three words beside it — "Loved it? Tell Google" — gets scanned. She is already holding her phone, and the review takes thirty seconds.

Keep it honest: never offer a discount in exchange for a review, and never write it for her. A short, genuine review in her own words is worth far more than a polished one she was paid to leave.

## The rebooking code, before she leaves

Every salon owner knows the pattern: the client leaves delighted, says she will book again soon, and then three months pass. The cure is to make rebooking happen before she walks out the door, and a QR code on the mirror makes that a thirty-second job instead of a conversation at a busy reception desk.

The code can open a simple page: her usual service, her usual stylist, and the next two dates that match her normal pattern. She taps one, the slot is held, and a confirmation arrives on WhatsApp. No phone call, no queue.

For clients who come every four weeks for roots, this is a small kindness. They do not have to remember — the diary starts filling itself weeks ahead, which makes the quiet weekday mornings far easier to plan.

## Make it look like your salon, not a sticker

A QR code can look cheap, and in a premium salon that matters. A curling, faded printout taped to a mirror will do more harm than good. Treat the code like any other part of your branding: print it properly, on card that matches your colours, with your logo beside it and a short line saying what happens when she scans.

Test every code yourself before it goes on display: a code that leads to a broken link teaches clients that scanning your codes wastes their time. Check them monthly too — window codes fade in the UAE sun, and mirror codes get covered in hairspray.

## Small squares, steady bookings

QR codes will not transform your salon overnight. They are not a campaign or a launch or a grand gesture. They are something quieter and more useful: a way to catch the bookings that would otherwise slip away in the gaps — the midnight browser, the happy client at the mirror, the regular who means to rebook and forgets.

In a market like the UAE, where clients message first and call second, and where a salon's evening hours are its busiest, anything that turns a passing moment into a confirmed appointment is worth doing properly. A few well-placed codes, each doing one clear job, can quietly fill chairs that would otherwise have stayed empty.
