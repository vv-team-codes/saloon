Every year in the UAE, the same thing happens in salon WhatsApp inboxes. National Day approaches, then Ramadan, then Eid, then Christmas — and every salon sends the same glittery forwarded image and generic greeting. Clients glance, smile politely, and delete. Nobody books.

Most festive messages ask for nothing, offer nothing and say nothing personal. A greeting that lands well does the opposite: it reminds a client why she liked your salon, arrives when she can act, and makes booking effortless. Done right, it is one of the cheapest bookings a salon ever sends. Done badly, it gets your number muted.

## Start with the calendar, not the card

The UAE festive calendar is generous, which is exactly why it needs planning. National Day on 2 December, the winter wedding season, Ramadan and the two Eids, Christmas, New Year and Diwali all arrive within a few months of each other. A salon that messages for every one of them becomes noise. A salon that picks three or four moments that fit its clients becomes a familiar, welcome voice.

Choose your moments now. A ladies' salon in a family neighbourhood might pick National Day, Ramadan and Eid al-Fitr; a salon with many European clients might pick Christmas and New Year. Plan them a month ahead, and decide for each one what the message should do: wish, remind, or offer. A message should only ever do one of those three things.

## Write to one person, not to a list

The fastest way to kill a festive message is to write it like a poster. "Dear valued customer, on the occasion of…" is the language of a billboard, not a conversation, and WhatsApp is a conversation. Your clients chose to message you there because it feels personal. The festive message should feel the same way.

Use the client's name and mention something real: the colour she had done last time, or the fact that her roots will be due around the holiday. "Aisha, your balayage will be about six weeks old by National Day weekend — shall we get you in on the Thursday before?" is a message that gets a reply. "Happy National Day from all of us!" gets a polite emoji and nothing else.

Broadcast lists beat group messages here: a broadcast arrives as a private message from you, and the reply comes back to you alone. Keep each list small and grouped by something that matters — colour clients, bridal clients, monthly regulars — so the message can be specific to them.

## Timing is half the message

A festive greeting sent on the morning of the holiday is too late. By then your client has already made her plans, booked her appointment elsewhere or decided to skip the salon this time. The booking window is three to five days before the occasion, when she is still deciding what the week looks like.

Never send one in the evening rush either. Between 6 and 9 pm your clients are finishing work, collecting children and cooking dinner — a message then is read in a hurry and forgotten. Send it late morning or early afternoon, when there is a quiet moment to reply — and be ready to reply fast. A festive message that earns a reply and then sits unanswered for six hours has wasted its effort.

For Ramadan, the rhythm shifts: messages sent just before iftar or in the calm hour after it land better than mid-afternoon ones. For National Day, the Thursday before the long weekend is your booking window.

## Give her a reason to book, not a reason to feel sold to

The line between a thoughtful festive message and a sales blast is thinner than most salon owners think. A discount shouted in capital letters feels like a flyer. A useful, time-limited suggestion feels like care.

Frame the offer around the occasion itself. Before National Day, that might be a blow-dry and styling slot reserved for the long weekend. Before Eid, a henna and manicure pairing that saves her a second trip. The offer should solve a problem the festive week actually creates — looking good for gatherings, fitting beauty into a busy few days — rather than inventing a discount for its own sake.

Keep the ask small. One message, one suggestion, one way to book. End with a specific slot, not an open question: "I have Thursday 11 am or Friday 4 pm open — would either suit you?" A client who has to think about when she is free will reply tomorrow. A client who only has to pick between two times replies now.

## What never to send

A few festive habits cost salons clients every year, and they are all avoidable. Never forward a greeting image you did not make, especially one with another salon's logo still on it. Never send the same message twice because you forgot who already received it. Never message clients who have asked to be contacted only about their own bookings. And never chase a festive message with a second one the next day asking why she did not reply. One warm message, sent at the right moment, is enough.

## Make it repeatable, not a one-off effort

The salons that do festive messaging well do not reinvent it every season. They keep a short note of what worked: which message got replies, which offer filled chairs, which list responded best. Next season, they reuse the structure and change the words.

They also decide in advance who sends what. One person owns the festive messages for each shift, checks the replies, and books the slots the messages create. Nothing damages a festive campaign faster than three stylists all messaging the same client, or a client who replies "yes, Thursday please" and hears nothing until the following morning.

The festive season in the UAE is full of occasions when clients want to look their best. A salon that messages like a thoughtful friend — personally, at the right moment, with a real suggestion and an easy way to book — will fill chairs other salons leave empty. And because the message arrived as a conversation, not an advertisement, the client does not just book once. She remembers who thought of her, and she comes back.
