It usually arrives as a short, slightly nervous WhatsApp message: "Hi, can I buy a gift voucher for my wife? Her birthday is this weekend." Or: "My friend is getting married next month — do you do gift cards?"

Gift voucher enquiries are some of the warmest messages a UAE salon ever receives. The person writing is not shopping around. They have already chosen your salon, and they want to spend money. Yet many salons answer these messages slowly, vaguely, or not at all — and the buyer quietly goes somewhere else. A little structure turns a casual question into new revenue and, often, a brand-new client.

## Why gift enquiries deserve a fast, confident answer

A gift buyer is different from a regular client. They are usually buying under a deadline — a birthday this weekend, an anniversary, Eid, or a wedding — and they are buying for someone else, which makes them anxious about getting it right. They do not need a long explanation of your services. They need to know three things quickly: that you sell vouchers, how much they cost, and how the recipient actually uses one.

If your first reply takes a day, the moment passes. Birthdays do not wait for a slow inbox. A reply within minutes keeps the sale alive: "Yes, we do! We have vouchers from AED 150, and I can send one on WhatsApp today. Shall I help you pick one?"

That speed matters even more in the evening. Many gift buyers message after work, once they have remembered the occasion. A salon whose inbox is still being answered at 8 or 9 pm wins the sale while competitors reply the next morning, too late. ## Ask four questions before you recommend anything

The biggest mistake in gift enquiries is jumping straight to a price list. A list of ten voucher values is not helpful to someone who does not know what their wife, mother, or friend would enjoy. Instead, ask four short questions — in one message, so the buyer does not have to wait through a long back-and-forth:

1. Who is it for, and what do they usually enjoy — hair, nails, skin, or a full pampering session?
2. Is there an occasion, and when is it?
3. Do you have a budget in mind, or would you like a suggestion?
4. Would you like the voucher sent to you, or directly to the recipient?

These questions make the buyer feel looked after, and they give you the information to recommend something specific instead of a generic amount. "A AED 300 voucher for a blow-dry and manicure" sounds like a real gift. "A voucher" does not.

For weddings, ask whether the voucher is for the bride or a bridesmaid, and whether the recipient might want a trial before the big day.

## Sell the experience, not the amount

A voucher is not a product; it is a promise of an experience. The way you describe it changes how generous the gift feels and how much the buyer is willing to spend.

Instead of listing denominations, name two or three ready-made gift packages with clear prices. A "Glow Hour" — a facial and a blow-dry. A "Bridal Treat" — a trial session and a manicure. A "Relax Evening" — a head massage and a pedicure. Packages remove decision fatigue, and they quietly lift the average voucher value, because choosing between AED 200 and AED 350 is easier than inventing a number.

Add one honest detail to every package: how long it takes. "About two hours, and she can book any evening" helps the buyer picture the gift.

## Make buying and sending effortless

The buyer is often not even in Dubai at the moment — a husband travelling for work, or a friend in Abu Dhabi ordering for someone in Dubai. The whole purchase must work without anyone visiting the salon.

A simple WhatsApp flow is enough: confirm the package, send a payment link, and deliver a digital voucher with the recipient's name, the service, the value, and an expiry date. A digital voucher sent on WhatsApp within minutes of payment is far better than a paper card that has to be collected.

Validity matters, so state it plainly in the first reply: "Valid for six months, any day except public holidays." Surprises about expiry are the fastest way to turn a generous gift into a complaint. Six months is a fair window in the UAE, where summer travel and busy schedules mean the recipient may not book immediately.

## The recipient is your next client

Here is the part most salons miss: a gift voucher enquiry is not one sale, it is two. The buyer pays, and the recipient walks through your door — a brand-new client who arrived already trusting you, because someone they love chose your salon for them.

Treat the redemption visit like a first visit that matters. Welcome the recipient warmly, and never make them feel like a "voucher client" who deserves less attention. A small touch goes a long way: a cup of karak or a few extra minutes of unhurried consultation. When the visit ends, do what you would do for any happy new client — invite them to rebook, and take their number with permission for a gentle follow-up.

Track it, too. A simple note in your client records — "gift voucher from Sara, redeemed 12 November" — tells you which gifts actually bring people in, and which packages get redeemed fastest.

## Prepare for the festive rush

Gift enquiries spike at predictable moments in the UAE calendar: National Day, Eid, Valentine's Day, Mother's Day, and the winter wedding season. In the two weeks before each one, your team should already know the answers by heart — the packages, the prices, the validity, and how to send a voucher on WhatsApp in under five minutes.

A short message to your regular clients before these occasions helps too: "Looking for a gift? Our vouchers are ready on WhatsApp." Regulars who love your salon are your best gift ambassadors.

Handled well, a gift voucher enquiry is the easiest sale a salon ever makes. The buyer has already decided — your job is to answer quickly, ask the right questions, make the purchase effortless, and give the recipient a visit worth coming back for.
