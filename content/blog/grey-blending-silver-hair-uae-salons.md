For years, the conversation in the colour chair went one way. A client spotted her first grey hairs, booked a full colour, and returned every three or four weeks to keep the roots covered. It was reliable income, but it was also a treadmill. Now a growing number of UAE clients are stepping off it. They are not giving up on colour. They are asking for something different: help growing their grey out gracefully, with the silver woven in rather than painted over.

Grey blending is the service behind that shift. Instead of covering every grey hair with a solid shade, the colourist uses fine highlights, lowlights and toners to weave the natural grey into the existing colour, softening the line between the two until the client's own silver becomes part of the look. It is a gradual, deliberate transition — and it is becoming one of the most requested colour services in salons that offer it well.

## Why the grow-out moment is arriving now

Several forces are meeting at once. Clients are more open about ageing than they were a decade ago, and silver hair has moved from something to hide into something people wear with pride, helped by silver-haired celebrities who treat grey as a style choice rather than a surrender. Many clients are also tired of the upkeep: a full root colour every three weeks is expensive and time-consuming, and in the UAE heat it is hard on hair already dealing with sun, salt water and hard water.

The UAE makes the case stronger. Many residents travel for long stretches in summer, and a solid colour that needs a touch-up every few weeks looks tired by week five of a holiday. A blended grow-out, by contrast, still looks intentional after six or eight weeks away. Many salon clients here are also in their forties, fifties and beyond — loyal clients who value expertise, book longer appointments and stay when treated well.

## What grey blending actually involves

The first thing to get right is the consultation, because grey blending is not a single service. It is a plan, usually spread over several appointments. The colourist needs to see the client's natural grey pattern and colour history: hair coloured dark for years needs a different route from hair that has only ever had a few highlights.

The technique itself is unglamorous work done well. Fine babylights break up the solid line of old colour, a toner cools the warmth that lifting creates, and a gloss evens the finish. Over successive visits, the share of blended silver grows and the share of old colour shrinks. The honest timeline matters: most transitions take six to twelve months, and a client who expects silver by Friday will be disappointed. Texture matters too — grey hair is often coarser and drier, so a good plan also covers care: a silver shampoo, a regular gloss and honest advice about heat styling. This is where the service becomes a programme rather than a one-off, and programmes retain clients.

## How salons are pricing and packaging it

Grey blending breaks the old pricing habit. A root touch-up is a quick, repeatable ticket; a blending appointment is longer, more skilled and harder to rush. Salons doing it well price it accordingly — as a specialist service with its own menu entry, not as a variation of a standard colour. Many package it as a transition programme — consultation, first blending session, six-week follow-up and review — with the total cost and timeline agreed up front. Clients accept premium pricing when they can see the plan.

Blending clients visit less often, but each visit is longer and higher value, filling quiet weekday hours — and a client who has spent six months growing out her grey with one colourist is not going to try the salon next door.

## Marketing a trend clients are slightly nervous about

The biggest barrier is not skill. It is nerves. Most clients considering the switch have spent years covering their grey, and letting it show feels like a public statement. The salons winning this work show real transitions, not finished silver hair on models who were never coloured. Before-and-after photos taken over months — the same client at visit one, visit three and visit six — do more persuading than any polished campaign shot.

The other smart move is language. Clients respond to words like soft, natural and low-maintenance, and they recoil from anything that sounds like giving up. Salons that frame blending as an upgrade — a modern, intentional look that suits the client's life — book the consultations. Many of these enquiries arrive as a photo sent late at night, a screenshot of a silver-haired stranger with the message "can you do this?", so the first reply matters enormously. A fast, warm answer that asks about her current colour and offers a consultation slot beats a bare price every time. ## The mistakes that spoil it

There are three ways to get this wrong, and they are all avoidable. The first is promising instant silver. Lifting years of dark colour in one session damages hair and disappoints the client; the honest salon says no to the shortcut. The second is ignoring tone: untoned lifted hair goes brassy in the UAE sun within weeks, and a client who sees orange in the mirror will not come back. A gloss at every blending visit is not an upsell, it is part of the service. The third is treating blending as a one-off. A single session without a plan leaves a client with a half-finished transition and no reason to return.

Many salons already have the skills, but the service is not on the menu, not in the WhatsApp quick replies, and not in any photo on the salon's feed. Clients cannot book a service they do not know exists. The salons booking out grey blending are the ones that named it, priced it and photographed it.

Grey is not a problem to solve. For a growing number of UAE clients it is the look they want — and for the salons that guide them through it, it is a premium service and a loyal client.
