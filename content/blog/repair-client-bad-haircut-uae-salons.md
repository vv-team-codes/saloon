She walks in quietly, hair tucked under a cap or tied back tight. She has already rehearsed what to say, and she is embarrassed before she says a word. Somewhere else — another salon, maybe in another emirate, maybe on holiday — a cut went wrong or a colour turned out nothing like the picture. Now she is in your salon, hoping you can fix it, and half expecting to be judged for it.

Repair clients are some of the most valuable clients a UAE salon can win. They arrive anxious, they have already spent money once, and if you handle the next hour well they will not just come back — they will tell every friend who has ever had a bad haircut where to go.

## Listen first, look second

The instinct is to look at the hair immediately and start diagnosing. Resist it. Sit her down, offer her water, and ask her to tell you what happened in her own words. What did she ask for? What did she get? How long ago was it done? Is she unhappy with the shape, the length, the colour, or all three?

These questions matter practically — a colour done yesterday needs a very different approach from one done three weeks ago — but they also matter emotionally. She has already felt unheard once. The fastest way to earn her trust is to prove that this salon listens before it acts.

Then look, honestly and without flinching. Part the hair, check the layers, look at the colour in natural light if you can. In Dubai's bright light, a colour that looked fine under salon spotlights can tell a different story by the window. Take the extra minute. It is the difference between a fix and a second disappointment.

## Tell her the truth, kindly

This is the hardest part of the repair visit, and the part that decides whether she becomes a regular. Some repairs are quick. Some are not possible in one visit. And some should not be attempted at all today — hair that has been bleached twice in a week, or a colour that needs to rest before anything else touches it, will only get worse if you rush.

Say so, plainly and kindly. "I can fix the shape today, and we can start correcting the colour, but the full result will take two visits" is a sentence a client can trust. "No problem, we can fix everything in one hour" is a sentence she has probably already heard once — from the salon that caused the problem.

Honesty here does something no discount ever will: it resets her expectations to something real. Clients do not leave because a fix took two visits. They leave because they were promised one and got a surprise. If the honest answer is that the hair needs a rest and a return visit, say it, and book that return visit before she leaves.

## Price the fix like a fix, not a punishment

Repair work takes longer than a standard service. It needs more consultation, more product, and often more than one pair of hands. It should cost more than a normal cut or colour, and most clients expect that. What they do not expect — and what loses them — is a price that appears halfway through, or a bill that feels like a penalty for someone else's mistake.

Give her the full price before you start. Break it down simply: the correction work, the treatment, the finish. If the fix genuinely needs two visits, quote both, so there is no second shock. And resist the temptation to discount the repair to win her over. A discount says the work is worth less. A clear price, explained with confidence, says the work is worth it — and that this salon knows exactly what it is doing.

## The reveal matters twice as much

For a normal client, the mirror moment is a happy ending. For a repair client, it is a test. She has been disappointed once already, and she is looking for the problem, not the result. So slow the reveal down. Show her the shape from the front first, then turn her gently, then show her the back with a hand mirror. Ask what she sees before you tell her what you did.

If something is not quite right yet — a tone that needs one more session, a layer that will settle over the next wash — say it before she spots it. Pointing out the remaining step yourself turns it from a flaw she discovered into a plan she agreed to.

## The enquiry often arrives after hours

Repair enquiries rarely come at a convenient time. They come at 10pm, when she has just taken the cap off and looked in the mirror, or on a Friday morning before a weekend event. She messages with a photo and a short, worried question: can you fix this, and how soon?

Salons that answer that message fast — with a calm reply, a couple of honest questions and a real slot — win the client before she messages the next salon on her list. ## A repaired client is your loudest advocate

Here is the part owners forget: a client you rescued does not just come back. She talks. She tells her sister, her colleagues, her group chat. "Go to this salon — they fixed what the other place did to me" is the strongest recommendation in the business, because it carries a story, and stories travel further than any offer.

That is why the repair visit deserves the same care as your most expensive service. It is not a difficult client or an awkward appointment. It is a client who has already learned what a bad salon feels like, and is giving yours one chance to show her the difference. Handle that hour well, and you have not just fixed her hair — you have won a regular, and probably her friends too.
