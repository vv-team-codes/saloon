Every UAE salon owner knows the feeling. You have booked your flight home, or a week away with family, and instead of excitement you feel a knot in your stomach. Not about the travel. About the salon. Your regulars come every two weeks, every month, like clockwork — and many of them come because of you. You remember their colour formula, their daughter's exam results, the way they take their karak. What happens to that loyalty while you are five thousand kilometres away?

Here is the honest answer: nothing has to happen to it. Regulars do not drift away because an owner takes a holiday. They drift away when the salon suddenly feels different — when nobody greets them by name, when their usual slot is given away, when a message goes unanswered for two days. Keeping regulars loyal while you are away is not about being present. It is about making sure your presence is felt even when you are not in the building.

## Tell them before you go, not after

The single biggest mistake owners make is treating a holiday like a secret. A regular arrives for her usual Thursday appointment, asks where you are, and hears "she is on leave" from a stylist who was never told what to say. In that moment the client does not think you are on holiday. She thinks something has changed, and change makes loyal clients nervous.

Tell your regulars a week or two before you travel. A short, warm message is enough: you will be away from this date to that date, the salon is open as normal, and one named person will be looking after everything while you are gone. Say it at the chair, send it on WhatsApp, or both.

## Name one person they can trust

While you are away, your regulars need one clear answer to a simple question: who do I ask for? Not "the team" and not "whoever is free". One name.

Pick your most experienced stylist or your front-desk lead and make them the stand-in owner for the week. Tell your regulars by name who that person is, and tell that person exactly what their job is: greet the regulars, solve small problems on the spot, and call you — not the client — when something is genuinely uncertain. If your client notes live in your head, they are useless while you are away. Write them down before you travel: the formula, the timing, the little things. A stand-in who can say "the usual, and shall I keep the extra ten minutes for the blow-dry like last time?" keeps a regular feeling known.

## Keep your voice in the salon while you are away

Being away does not mean going silent. A short message from you, sent personally, carries more weight than any amount of delegation. Not a broadcast, and not a sales message — just a human check-in. A voice note to a long-standing regular, or a quick WhatsApp message saying you hope the colour settled well, reminds her that the relationship is with you, and that you are still thinking about the salon even from another country.

Set a simple rhythm before you leave: ten minutes a day, at a time that works with the time difference, to check the diary and answer anything only you can answer. Everything else stays with your stand-in. The point is not to run the salon from a beach. The point is that your regulars never feel abandoned, and your team never feels unsupported.

## Protect your regulars' slots

Here is where loyalty is quietly lost during an owner's absence: the diary drifts. A stand-in, trying to be helpful, squeezes in a walk-in over a regular's usual slot. A new client is given the Saturday 6 pm chair that has belonged to the same woman for two years. The regular arrives, finds her rhythm broken, and says nothing — she just books somewhere else next time.

Before you travel, mark your regulars' recurring slots as protected. Tell your team clearly: these times belong to these clients, and if a regular calls to move, she gets first choice of the replacement. When they return after your trip and everything is exactly as they left it, that is what loyalty feels like.

## The return visit matters most

The most important day of your holiday is not the day you leave. It is the day you come back. Regulars who visited while you were away deserve a personal welcome on your return — a greeting by name, a genuine question about how their last visit went, and a small acknowledgement that they kept coming while you were gone. A "thank you for trusting us while I was away" said with real warmth does more for retention than any loyalty card ever will.

Use your first week back to listen. Ask your stand-in which regulars came in and whether any small complaint was handled. If a regular had a less-than-perfect visit while you were away, that is your moment to fix it personally — a quick message, a small gesture at her next appointment, an honest word. Clients forgive a wobble far more easily than they forgive being ignored.

## What not to do

Do not close the salon just because you are away. In the UAE, where clients plan around weekend patterns and peak evening hours, a closed week is a lost week — and a regular who is forced to try another salon for a fortnight may discover she likes it there. And do not check the diary every hour from abroad. Constant remote control tells your team you do not trust them, and the stress travels back to the clients through the staff.

Plan the absence the way you would plan any other part of the business: in advance, with named people, protected slots and a clear return. Most UAE salon owners travel several times a year, whether to visit family or to take a proper break. The salons that keep their regulars through every trip are the ones that treat loyalty as something to protect deliberately, not something to assume.

Your regulars chose your salon, and many of them chose you. A holiday does not change that, as long as the salon they come back to still feels like yours.
