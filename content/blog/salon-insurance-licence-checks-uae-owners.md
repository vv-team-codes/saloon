Most salon owners in the UAE think about growth in terms of bookings, chairs and marketing. Insurance and licences rarely make the list, because they feel like paperwork rather than profit. But one allergic reaction, one slip on a wet floor, or one lapsed licence can cost more than a whole month of good trading. The busy December season is exactly when the risk is highest: more clients, more treatments and longer hours. November is the right moment to check that the business is properly covered, while there is still time to fix anything that is out of date.

## Start with your trade licence, not your insurance

Everything else depends on the licence being right. Check that your trade licence is current, that the listed activities actually match the services you offer, and that the details on the licence match the business as it operates today. A salon that started with haircuts and quietly added nails, facials or laser treatments may be offering services its licence does not cover. That gap usually only becomes visible when something goes wrong — a client complaint, an inspection, or an insurance claim that gets refused because the activity was not on the licence.

If you run a ladies salon, a gents salon or a mixed salon, confirm the licence reflects that too. And if you have added a new treatment room or a home-visit service since the licence was issued, check whether any of that needs an update. The renewal process is far cheaper and calmer than dealing with a problem after the fact, so put the licence renewal date on your calendar with a reminder a month before it falls due. ## Public liability: the cover you hope never to use

Public liability insurance protects the salon if a client is injured or their property is damaged on your premises. A client who slips near the wash basin, a bag knocked off a hook into a basin of colour, a reaction to a product — these are everyday salon risks, not rare disasters. The question is not whether something can happen, but whether the business can absorb the cost if it does.

Read your policy rather than trusting the renewal email. Check the cover limit, what is excluded, and whether the policy covers all the services you offer, including newer treatments such as lash lifts, semi-permanent makeup or LED therapy. Some policies exclude specific treatments or require the stylist to hold a particular qualification. If you have changed your menu in the last year, ask your insurer whether the policy still fits.

## Professional indemnity for the work itself

Public liability covers accidents in the salon. Professional indemnity covers the work your team does — a colour that goes wrong, a cut that a client says was not what was agreed, a treatment that causes a reaction. In a beauty business, where results are personal and subjective, this is the cover that matters most, and it is the one owners most often skip.

If you already have it, check the limit and the excess. A policy bought when the salon had two chairs may no longer fit a busier salon with higher-value services. If you do not have it, get a quote before December, when appointment volumes peak and the diary is full of one-off clients who have no history with your team — and therefore less tolerance for a mistake.

## Your team, your equipment and your stock

Staff cover is easy to overlook in a small salon. Check whether your policy covers your employees, including part-time and freelance stylists working on your chairs, and whether it includes employers' liability where required. A freelance stylist may carry their own cover, but if they are working under your salon's name, confirm whose policy answers first when something goes wrong. Put the answer in writing with your team so nobody is guessing.

Then look at the physical business. Equipment cover for chairs, dryers, laser machines and nail stations is worth reviewing every year, because the value of what you own creeps up with each new purchase. Stock is the same: the shelves of retail products and professional colour may now be worth several thousand dirhams. Photograph your equipment and keep supplier invoices in one folder, digital or paper, so a claim can be evidenced quickly.

## The small print that catches salons out

Three details cause most of the trouble. The first is the patch test: many policies expect a patch test to have been offered or recorded before colour or chemical treatments, and a missing record can weaken a claim. Keep a simple log — client name, date, product, result — and make it part of the booking routine, not an afterthought.

The second is hygiene and sterilisation records. Insurers and inspectors both look for evidence that tools are cleaned and products are stored correctly. A short daily checklist signed by the person on duty is enough, and it doubles as proof that the salon takes safety seriously.

## A one-hour checklist before the rush

Set aside one hour this week and work through it. Licence current and accurate. Public liability and professional indemnity in force, with limits that fit today's turnover and menu. Staff and freelance cover clear. Equipment and stock values up to date. Patch test and hygiene records in place. Renewal dates on the calendar. Then file everything — policies, licences, certificates — in one place where you can find it at 9pm on a Saturday when a client asks to see it, because that is when it will be needed.

Insurance and licences will never bring a single new client through the door, and that is exactly why they get ignored. But growth is not only about adding chairs and services. It is about making sure the business you have built can survive a bad day. A salon that is properly covered can handle a complaint, a claim or an inspection calmly, fix the problem and carry on trading. A salon that is not covered can lose a month of profit, or much more, to one incident. Before the December diary fills up, give the foundations an hour of your attention. It is the cheapest growth work you will do all year.
