Every December, Dubai changes gear. The malls stay open later, the streets fill with visitors, and shoppers arrive ready to spend. For most retailers, Dubai Shopping Festival is the biggest trading period of the year. For salons, it can be too — but only if the salon treats it as a campaign, not as background noise.

Too many salons watch the festival crowds walk past their door and do nothing different. The salons that win during DSF are the ones that plan for it the way a retailer plans a sale: they shape the diary, the offer and the message around what festival shoppers actually want.

## Why the festival weeks are different

DSF shoppers are not your usual clients. A large share are visitors — tourists staying in nearby hotels, residents hosting family from abroad, and shoppers who came to the mall for a deal and stayed for the atmosphere. They are in spending mode, they have time on their hands, and they are open to trying something new.

The other difference is the clock. During the festival, malls run extended hours and the busiest shopping happens in the evening, often well after 8 PM. A salon that closes at 9 PM while the mall hums until midnight is leaving its best hours on the table.

## Start with the diary, not the discount

The first mistake salons make is reaching for a blanket discount. A 50 percent off poster brings in bargain hunters who never return, and it trains your regulars to wait for the next sale.

Look at your quiet hours first. Most UAE salons have the same shape: slow weekday mornings, a packed Saturday, and a dead patch between lunch and the evening rush. During DSF, fill those gaps with festival-specific slots. A "festival glow" express facial at 11 AM, a lunchtime blow-dry for shoppers on a break, a 30-minute express manicure for someone between stores — services designed for the festival crowd's schedule, not yours.

Hold back a small number of evening slots each day for same-day festival bookings. Shoppers decide late. Two or three protected slots per evening, released on WhatsApp Status an hour before they open, can fill chairs that would otherwise sit empty.

## Sell the gift, not the service

The single best-selling product in a salon during DSF is not a treatment. It is a gift voucher. Shoppers are buying presents for mothers, sisters, wives and friends, and a salon voucher is the rare gift that feels personal without requiring the buyer to guess sizes or tastes.

Make vouchers effortless to buy. A ready-made festival voucher — one named package, one price, one message — sells far better than "any amount you like." Put it on a small stand at reception and on your WhatsApp catalogue. The buyer should be able to pay and receive the voucher on their phone in under two minutes, while standing in the mall.

Name the packages for the season. "The Festival Glow" or "A Morning of Her Own" tells the recipient what she is getting in a way a price list never will. When the recipient arrives to use her voucher, treat her like the next regular, not a one-off redemption. Take her details, note her preferences, and book her next visit before she leaves.

## Catch the after-hours shopper

Festival enquiries arrive at hours your team is not working. A tourist messages at 11 PM asking whether you have a slot tomorrow morning. A resident messages at midnight asking about a henna appointment for her visiting cousin. If nobody answers until 9 AM, she has already found a salon that did.

Whatever you post during DSF — a Status, a Reel, a mall poster — it must lead somewhere a shopper can act on immediately: a WhatsApp message that gets a real answer, or a booking link that shows live availability. A beautiful post that ends in a silent inbox is money wasted.

## Partner with the shops next door

During DSF, the mall is one big shared audience. The boutique two doors down, the perfume shop across the walkway and the cafe on the corner are all talking to the same shoppers you want. A simple partnership costs nothing and reaches people you could never afford to advertise to.

Offer the simplest version first. A card at the boutique till: "Show your receipt from us and get a complimentary express treatment upgrade at the salon." A joint offer with the cafe: coffee and a blow-dry before the evening's shopping. A small display in a hotel lobby near the mall, offering festival-week slots to guests.

Keep the tracking simple. One code word per partner, written on the card, so you know which partnership actually sent clients. After the first week, double down on the one that works and quietly drop the rest.

## A simple DSF plan you can run this week

You do not need a big budget or an agency. You need a plan that fits on one page and starts this week.

First, shape the diary. Add two festival express services for quiet hours, protect two evening slots a day for same-day bookings, and extend closing by an hour on the busiest shopping nights if your team can manage it.

Second, build the gift. One named voucher package, one price, ready to buy on WhatsApp in two minutes. Tell your regulars about it first — they buy gifts for the same people the festival brings in.

Third, fix the after-hours gap. Make sure every post, poster and Status leads to a booking path that answers at midnight, not just at 9 AM.

Fourth, knock on two neighbouring doors. One partnership, one shared offer, one code word to track it.

Fifth, plan the follow-up before the festival starts. Decide now what message goes out the morning after a first visit, and who sends it.

DSF rewards the salons that show up prepared. The crowds are coming to the mall either way. The question is whether they walk past your door, or walk in and sit down.
