It usually arrives as a short WhatsApp message, late in the evening: "Hi, can I book a haircut with a female stylist please?" Sometimes it is a man asking for a male barber, or a mother booking for her teenage daughter. Sometimes there is no explanation at all — just the request, sent with a politeness that suggests the client half-expects to be told no.

In UAE salons, this is one of the most common enquiries a front desk will ever receive, and one of the most mishandled. A clumsy reply costs more than a single booking. It costs the client who was already nervous about walking into a new salon. Handled well, the same enquiry does the opposite: it turns a cautious first-timer into a regular who books the same stylist every few weeks.

## Why clients ask — and why the answer matters

The reasons are rarely about skill. A client may prefer a female stylist for reasons of comfort, culture or faith. A new resident may come from a place where salons are strictly separated and simply wants to know the rules here.

None of these clients is asking for a debate. They are asking a yes-or-no question, and the way you answer it tells them everything about how your salon treats people. A warm, specific answer says: we understand. A vague or defensive one says: you are an inconvenience.

## The reply that books: four parts

The best replies follow the same shape every time: four parts, in one message, so the client never has to ask a follow-up question to book.

First, say yes or no plainly. "Yes, of course" beats a paragraph of hedging. If the answer is yes, name the stylist. A named person is reassuring in a way a policy statement never is: "Sara is our senior stylist for cuts and colour, and she is available on Thursday at 6 PM or Saturday at 11 AM." Two real slots, not an open invitation to negotiate.

Second, add one honest detail. "Sara has been with us for four years and does most of our ladies' cuts" tells the client she is in experienced hands. Keep it to one line — the client does not need a biography.

Third, close with the booking. "Shall I hold Thursday at 6 PM for you with Sara?" A question with a specific time is easy to answer with a yes. An open "let us know when you'd like to come" is easy to ignore, and every hour that passes is an hour in which another salon replies faster.

Fourth, note the preference. Write it in the client's record the moment the booking is made: "Prefers female stylist — Sara." The next time she messages, your team already knows, and she never has to ask again. Clients who never have to repeat themselves come back.

## When you can't say yes — honest alternatives

Sometimes the honest answer is no — your only female stylist is on leave, or your ladies' section is fully booked that day. This is the moment most salons lose the client, because "no" on its own feels like a door closing.

Never end on the no. Offer the closest alternative in the same message, and make it a real one. "Sara is on leave until Monday, but Aisha is available on Saturday at 5 PM — she is also a senior stylist and does the same cuts. Or I can book you with Sara on Monday at 11 AM." Two options, both specific, both easy to accept.

If you genuinely cannot meet the request at all, say so kindly and offer the next available day. "Today we only have Omar on shift, but Sara is in tomorrow from 10 AM. Shall I book you in with her?" A client who is told the truth, with a date attached, will usually wait a day. A client who is given a vague "we'll see what we can do" will book elsewhere.

## Put the answer on your profiles before they ask

The smartest salons answer this enquiry before it is ever sent. Your Instagram bio, your Google Business Profile and your WhatsApp greeting message are all places where a single line saves dozens of messages a week.

"Ladies' services by female stylists available" or "Female and male stylists on the team — just ask when you book" tells a browsing client everything she needs to know. For ladies-only salons, state it clearly: "A ladies-only salon — all services by our female team." The same applies in reverse for men's grooming: "Men's cuts and grooming by our male barbers" brings you the clients you serve best.

## Train the team on one answer

Gender-preference enquiries go wrong most often when the person replying has not been told what to say. A receptionist who hesitates, or who answers with "I think so" or "let me check and get back to you", has already made the client feel like an unusual request. The client is not unusual. In a Dubai or Abu Dhabi salon, this question arrives every single day.

Agree on one answer as a team and write it down. Who are your female stylists, and which services does each one do? Which days is each one in? What do you offer when the preferred stylist is away? A one-page note at the front desk, reviewed whenever the rota changes, is all it takes. New team members should be able to answer this enquiry confidently on their first day.

It is also worth agreeing on tone. This is a question about comfort, so the reply should sound comfortable — warm, matter-of-fact, and never surprised.

Salons that grow steadily in the UAE treat every enquiry as the start of a relationship, not a transaction.
