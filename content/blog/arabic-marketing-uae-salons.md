Walk into almost any salon in Dubai, Sharjah or Abu Dhabi and you will hear three or four languages before the first blow-dry is finished. Yet open the same salon's Instagram, Google profile or ad account and you will usually find only one: English. That gap costs salons bookings every week, and most owners never notice — because the clients they are missing never send a message at all. They search in Arabic, scroll in Arabic, and book with the salon that spoke to them first.

Arabic is not a niche language in the UAE. It is the official language, the first language of Emirati clients and a large share of the Arab expat community, and the language many residents default to when they are searching for something close to home. A client looking for a ladies' salon in Al Barsha or a barber in Deira is far more likely to search in Arabic. If your salon only exists in English online, you are invisible to that search — not second on the list, but absent from it entirely.

## Start where clients search

Google is the cheapest place to begin, because it costs nothing to fix. Open your Google Business Profile and check whether it carries an Arabic version of your salon name, your services and your description. Google shows the profile in the language of the person searching, so an Arabic searcher who finds an English-only profile sees a business that feels foreign, even if it sits two streets away. Add your services in Arabic — haircut, colour, manicure, henna, bridal packages — using the words clients actually type, not a formal translation from a dictionary. The same applies to your website: a single Arabic landing page, or even Arabic headings on your services page, is enough to start appearing in Arabic searches for "salon near me" in your area.

Once a week, type one of your own services into Google in Arabic and see who appears. If a weaker salon outranks you, it is almost never because their work is better. It is because they wrote the words the client typed.

## Instagram and TikTok speak Arabic too

Social media is where the gap is widest. A salon can post beautiful Reels every day and still miss the Arabic-speaking audience, because the captions, the on-screen text and the hashtags are all in English. You do not need to rebuild your feed. Start by writing captions in both languages — English first, Arabic second — and adding Arabic text to the covers of your best Reels. A transformation video needs no translation, but the caption underneath decides who finds it and who messages about it.

Arabic hashtags matter more than most owners realise: they put your work in front of people browsing beauty content in Arabic, a feed your English hashtags will never reach. The same goes for creators: the UAE has a large community of Arabic-speaking beauty creators with loyal local followings, and their rates are often far lower than the big English-language influencers. One honest collaboration — a real service, a real result, filmed in Arabic — can bring in clients who had never heard of your salon, because until that video they had never seen a salon speak their language.

## Your shopfront and price list are marketing too

Marketing in Arabic is not only about ads and posts. It is about every place a client meets your salon. A price list that exists only in English quietly tells an Arabic-speaking client that the salon was not built with her in mind — and the same goes for the sign outside, the WhatsApp catalogue and the service menu pinned in your stories. Adding Arabic to these is a one-afternoon job: a translator, or a bilingual member of your team, can produce clean versions of your twenty most-booked services in a couple of hours, and those versions keep working for months.

This matters most for the services clients feel nervous about: a first-time laser client, a bride choosing a salon for her wedding, a mother booking her daughter's first haircut. These clients read carefully before they message. When they can read your prices, timings and policies in Arabic, the fear of a misunderstanding disappears and the message gets sent. When they cannot, they move on without ever telling you why.

## You do not need to speak Arabic to market in it

The objection every owner raises is the same: "But I don't speak Arabic." You do not need to. Most salons that market well in Arabic have nobody fluent on the team. What they have is a system. One bilingual freelancer translates the captions and the price list once, and the salon reuses them. One Arabic-speaking stylist or receptionist — hired for her skills first, with language as a bonus — becomes the voice of the salon's Arabic replies. A translation app on the front desk phone handles the occasional question in between.

The rule is to translate once and reuse everywhere. Write five Arabic message templates — prices, timings, location, booking confirmation, aftercare — and save them where the whole team can find them. A client who messages in Arabic and receives a warm, correct reply in Arabic within minutes will not care whether the person typing speaks it as a first language. She will care that she was understood.

## Measure it before you spend more

Arabic marketing earns its keep quickly, but only if you track it. For one month, ask every new client one question at the chair: "How did you find us?" Write the answers down. Within a few weeks you will see whether Arabic searches, Arabic Reels or a friend's recommendation brought them in. A salon that gains even four or five extra clients a month from Arabic marketing has paid for the translation many times over — and those clients tend to stay, because a salon that speaks their language feels like theirs.

The UAE rewards salons that meet clients where they are. For years that has meant WhatsApp, evening hours and honest prices. Increasingly it also means Arabic. The salons that add it first will not just win the clients their competitors never saw — they will keep them, because being understood is the fastest way to be trusted.
