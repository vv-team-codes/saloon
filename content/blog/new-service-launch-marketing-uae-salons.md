Most UAE salons launch a new service the same way: they add it to the price list, mention it on Instagram once, and wait. Then they wonder why the diary stays empty for weeks. The problem is not the service. It is the launch. A new treatment, a new colour technique or a new ritual only earns its place when clients already know about it, want it and have a reason to book it on day one. That takes a short, deliberate plan — not a big budget.

The salons that fill a new service fast treat the launch like a small event with a beginning, a middle and an end. Here is how to run one.

## Start with the client, not the service

Before you announce anything, decide exactly who this service is for. A scalp ritual aimed at stressed professionals in DIFC is a different launch from a bridal hair trial package aimed at engaged women in JVC. Write one sentence: who it helps, and what changes for them after one visit. If you cannot write that sentence, the service is not ready to be marketed.

Then check the diary. A launch works best when it lands in a quiet pocket you can actually fill — a Tuesday morning, a Sunday afternoon, the week before a busy period. Launching into a Saturday that is already full wastes the excitement, because interested clients hear "we are booked" and move on. Pick a launch window of two to three weeks and protect a few slots in it for the new service only.

## Tease it before you name it

The week before launch, start dropping hints. A short video of the new equipment arriving, a stylist training on the technique, a close-up of the product range with the name hidden. Post it on Instagram Stories and WhatsApp Status, where your regulars already watch you. Do not reveal the price yet. Curiosity is the point.

Regulars who reply to a teaser are your warmest launch audience. Answer each one personally, and note their names. These are the people you will message first when booking opens, and they will feel like insiders rather than an audience.

## Give the first bookings a reason to happen now

A launch needs a reason to book this week rather than "sometime". The simplest reason is scarcity with a real limit: the first twelve appointments at a founding-client price, or a launch-week bonus such as a complimentary add-on that genuinely fits the service. Keep the offer modest and time-bound. A small, honest incentive beats a deep discount, because it protects your price for the months after launch.

Make the offer easy to claim in one message. "Reply YES and I will hold Thursday 6pm for you" converts far better than a link to a booking page with six steps. Your WhatsApp replies should be ready before launch day: the price, the duration, who it suits, and two or three real time slots.

## Let one great result do the talking

Your first few clients are your marketing. Ask their permission before the service begins — a simple "may we take a photo at the end?" — and capture the result honestly, in the same light, without filters. One strong before-and-after, posted the same evening with the price and the time it took, will do more than a week of graphics.

Share it everywhere your clients already are: Instagram Reels, your Google Business Profile, WhatsApp Status and the salon's window display if you have footfall. Each place should carry the same message: what it is, what it costs, how long it takes, and how to book. Repetition across channels is not annoying when the message is useful — it is how a new service becomes familiar.

## Turn the launch into a routine, not a one-off

The biggest launch mistake is stopping after week one. A new service needs about six weeks of steady attention before clients start asking for it by name. After launch week, keep one mention of it in your weekly rhythm: a Tuesday Status update with the next free slot, a Friday Reel answering the most common question about it, a line in your monthly email. Small, regular reminders compound.

Track three numbers and nothing else: how many launch appointments were booked, how many of those clients rebooked the service, and how many came from a referral or a post. If the rebooking number is low, the service or the experience needs work, not more marketing. If rebooking is strong but bookings are slow, the problem is visibility, and another round of honest result photos will fix it faster than a discount.

## What a good launch looks like in practice

Imagine a salon in a Dubai mall adding a 45-minute express facial for lunch-break clients. Two weeks out, the team posts a short video of the new treatment bed being set up. One week out, regulars get a WhatsApp message: twelve founding slots at a launch price, Thursday and Friday lunchtimes only. On launch day, the first client agrees to a photo, and by evening the Reel shows a real result with the price and the 45-minute duration on screen. By the end of week two, the lunchtime slots are the ones clients ask for by name — and the salon did not spend a dirham on ads.

That is the whole secret. A launch is not a poster. It is a sequence: choose the client, tease the service, give a reason to book now, show one real result, and keep mentioning it for six weeks. Do that, and the next time you add a service, your clients will already be asking about it before you announce it.

A new service should grow your salon, not sit quietly on a price list. Plan the launch, and the diary will follow.
